abstract: Contact-rich manipulation requires a robot to regulate the force it applies, not only where it moves. Existing force-aware policies learn this force from demonstrations and therefore reproduce the force levels they were trained on, which fails once the interaction demands more force than any demonstration. We present Force2Act, a hybrid force-position policy built on two complementary mechanisms. A global force policy plans the motion and the interaction force once per action chunk, with force represented relative to the measured interaction, so that the planned force scales with the load. Between planning steps, a reactive compliance policy, termed Mixture of Force Experts, identifies at every control step the force expert that governs the interaction and regulates the force through it, so that the contact holds when it changes within a chunk. We further introduce Force2Act-Bench, a simulation benchmark of five contact-rich tasks that gives the community a common and repeatable way to test force-aware policies and measure how well they regulate the interaction force. Force2Act achieves an overall success rate of 93.8%, compared with 77.0% for the strongest baseline, and its reactive regulation reduces the failures on block pivoting from 30 to 10 of 150 trials. On a real robot, Force2Act reaches an average success rate of 94.2% over four tasks, against at most 27.5% for the baselines.

## What they do
Force-aware policies usually learn how hard to press from demonstrations, so they reproduce the demonstrated force levels. When the load grows, for example a heavier block or a steeper incline, they press too lightly. Force2Act is a hybrid force-position policy whose force follows the measured load instead. The authors also release Force2Act-Bench, five contact-rich tasks simulated in Genesis.

## How it works
- **Global force policy.** A flow-matching diffusion transformer reads camera images, language, end-effector poses and wrist force/torque history. At 15 Hz it predicts 16-step chunks of poses and wrenches, of which 8 (0.53 s) are executed. Wrenches are divided by the recently measured force level, so the planned force scales with the load even though training uses a single load.
- **Mixture of Force Experts (MoFE).** Seven experts, each an affine law (normal force = slope × load + offset) derived from Coulomb friction for sticking, sliding, pushing or insertion contacts. Geometric gates rule out experts whose contact condition does not hold, and a router (image plus tangential force and speed) picks one at every step. The router does not see the normal force or the force direction, since those reflect the robot's own command.
- **Training and execution.** The only supervision is the demonstrated normal force, with no contact labels. A hybrid controller applies a blend (0.8 expert, 0.2 plan) of normal force along the contact normal and tracks the planned pose elsewhere.

## Results
- Simulation, 305 trials: 93.8% against 77.0% for ACP. No VLA or position-only baseline pivots the block at any mass. From 0.4 to 0.9 kg, Force2Act stays at 88-100% while ACP falls to 0% at 0.9 kg. USB insertion is its weakest task (76% vs 96% for ACP and ForceVLA).
- Ablations: the global policy alone scores 86.9% and MoFE alone 67.9%. Planning absolute instead of relative force drops the global policy to 56.7%, and removing the gates gives 48.5%.
- Real Flexiv arm, 20 trials per condition: 20/20 with the 0.418 kg block (ACP 3/20), 20/20 on the 10° incline, 16/20 on plug insertion. That averages 94.2% against 26.7% (ACP), 22.5% (ForceVLA) and 27.5% (ManiFlow).

## Limitations
Force is regulated along one direction only, the contact normal, so compliance in several directions or in rotation is left to future work. All contacts are rigid. On the real robot, objects stay at their demonstrated positions.
