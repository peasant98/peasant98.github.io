abstract: Tactile sensing is critical for robotic grasping and manipulation of objects under visual occlusion. However, in contrast to simulations of robot arms and cameras, current simulations of tactile sensors have limited accuracy, speed, and utility. In this work, we develop an efficient 3D finite element method (FEM) model of the SynTouch BioTac sensor using an open-access, GPU-based robotics simulator. Our simulations closely reproduce results from an experimentally-validated model in an industry-standard, CPU-based simulator, but at 75x the speed. We then learn latent representations for simulated BioTac deformations and real-world electrical output through self-supervision, as well as projections between the latent spaces using a small supervised dataset. Using these learned latent projections, we accurately synthesize real-world BioTac electrical output and estimate contact patches, both for unseen contact interactions. This work contributes an efficient, freely-accessible FEM model of the BioTac and comprises one of the first efforts to combine self-supervision, cross-modal transfer, and sim-to-real transfer for tactile sensors.

## What they do
The BioTac turns skin deformation into fluidic impedances read at 19 electrodes, which is hard to simulate. The authors rebuild their experimentally validated ANSYS finite-element (FEM) model of the BioTac in Isaac Gym. They do not simulate the electrodes; instead they learn a mapping between simulated deformations and real electrode signals.

## How it works
- **FEM model.** A roughly 4000-node mesh of the skin around a rigid core, linear-elastic and without the internal fluid. Stiffness, Poisson's ratio and friction are fitted to the ANSYS force profile of one indentation.
- **Self-supervised latents.** A graph-convolutional VAE compresses meshes to 128 dimensions and a fully connected VAE compresses electrode signals to 8. Both train on unlabeled contacts with 6 pegs and 3 table features.
- **Latent projection.** With the VAEs frozen, two small MLPs map between the latents in both directions, trained on 359 labeled indentations.

## Results
- Over 358 held-out indentations from 8 indenters, the mean force error against ANSYS is 0.153 N.
- 359 indentations take 33 min on 1 GPU versus about 42 h on 6 CPUs in ANSYS.
- Median electrode RMS error is 0.010 for latent projection vs 0.012 for a fully supervised PointNet++ on unseen trajectories, and 0.015 vs 0.016 on the unseen ring indenter (31 vs 32 raw units of 4095). A prior method reported 305 units on unseen objects.
- The inverse projection recovers contact patches from real signals, judged visually. For the ring it misses bimodal patches, which the authors attribute to the mesh VAE bottleneck.

## Limitations
One 6 mm indentation still takes about 5.6 s to simulate, too slow for model-predictive control. Evaluation used a single BioTac, and manufacturing variation may require data from more sensors. In the paper, unseen-object results cover only the ring.
