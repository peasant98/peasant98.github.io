abstract: 'Reliable proprioceptive state estimation for legged robots fuses inertial sensing with kinematic sensing from leg encoders. The latter assumes a foot is stationary during the stance phase; however, physical foot touchdown does not necessarily provide a stationary reference for odometry, particularly during impact and load transfer. Repeatedly applying kinematic corrections throughout the same contact phase can introduce redundant or unreliable information. We present SG-ODOM, a proprioceptive odometry framework that uses stability-gated contact selection to schedule kinematic updates. Following physical touchdown, a stability gate accepts the contact for state estimation only after the foot reaches a sufficiently stable support condition. The resulting validated touchdown event triggers an estimator correction, while additional timeout updates bound the maximum duration of uninterrupted IMU propagation. The framework is evaluated on multiple legged platforms, both quadrupedal and humanoid robots, against representative state-of-the-art proprioceptive estimators. Across the evaluated sequences, SG-ODOM demonstrates competitive or superior estimation accuracy, reducing Relative Pose Error (RPE) by up to 25% compared to the baseline estimator on outdoor sequences from the GrandTour dataset. ## What they do
Proprioceptive odometry for legged robots fuses IMU propagation with leg kinematics and assumes a foot in contact is stationary. Right after touchdown, though, a foot can still bounce or settle, and repeated corrections within one contact reuse strongly correlated measurements. SG-ODOM changes when kinematic corrections happen: only once a contact has stabilized, and then at a bounded, lower rate. It is tested on Spot, ANYmal D and a Unitree G1 humanoid.

## How it works
- **Stability gate.** Ground reaction force is estimated from joint-torque residuals. A contact counts only once the vertical force is large enough and steady for a set time, so the delay after impact varies per step.
- **Event-driven updates.** A validated touchdown triggers an immediate correction. Otherwise a timeout update fires once IMU-only propagation reaches a maximum interval (0.1 s). Contacts are still classified at full rate.
- **Two back ends.** An invariant EKF (SG-ODOM-InEKF) and a fixed-lag smoother (SG-ODOM-FL), both in GTSAM, share one robot-specific front end.

## Results
- Error is lowest for update intervals of 0.05-0.10 s. On ANYmal the smoother's translational error rises from 0.157 m to 0.418 m at 0.30 s and to 0.406 m at 0.01 s.
- Stability gating beats a force threshold and, where available, the manufacturer's contact state for both estimators on all three robots (ANYmal smoother: 0.157 m vs 0.259 m and 0.210 m).
- ANYmal (seven GrandTour missions): SG-ODOM-FL averages 0.157 m, against 0.208 m for the onboard ANYbotics estimator, and is best on 5 of 7. Spot: 0.185 m vs 0.226 m for Pronto. G1: 0.109 m, about half of Pronto and Unitree's odometry.

## Limitations
The G1 reference comes from LiDAR-inertial odometry rather than absolute ground truth, and the G1 only walked on paved concrete, where gating helped least. The smoother's IMU bias parameters were chosen empirically per robot, without calibration data. The filter version averages 0.252 m on ANYmal, worse than the ANYbotics estimator.
