abstract: 'Humans can accomplish complex contact-rich tasks using vision and touch, with highly reactive capabilities such as fast response to external changes and adaptive control of contact forces; however, this remains challenging for robots. Existing visual imitation learning (IL) approaches rely on action chunking to model complex behaviors, which lacks the ability to respond instantly to real-time tactile feedback during the chunk execution. Furthermore, most teleoperation systems struggle to provide fine-grained tactile / force feedback, which limits the range of tasks that can be performed. To address these challenges, we introduce TactAR, a low-cost teleoperation system that provides real-time tactile feedback through Augmented Reality (AR), along with Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP), a novel slow-fast visual-tactile imitation learning algorithm for learning contact-rich manipulation skills. RDP employs a two-level hierarchy: (1) a slow latent diffusion policy for predicting high-level action chunks in latent space at low frequency, (2) a fast asymmetric tokenizer for closed-loop tactile feedback control at high frequency. This design enables both complex trajectory modeling and quick reactive behavior within a unified framework. Through extensive evaluation across three challenging contact-rich tasks, RDP significantly improves performance compared to state-of-the-art visual IL baselines. Furthermore, experiments show that RDP is applicable across different tactile / force sensors. Code and videos are available on https://reactive-diffusion-policy.github.io.'

## What they do
Imitation-learning policies with action chunking, such as Diffusion Policy and ACT, execute each chunk open-loop, so they cannot react to touch mid-chunk. The paper adds two pieces: TactAR, a teleoperation system that shows tactile / force feedback in AR on a Meta Quest 3, and Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP), a slow-fast visual-tactile policy.

## How it works
- **TactAR.** The marker displacement field of an optical tactile sensor, or a force sensor's wrench, is drawn as 3D arrows on the end-effector in AR. The headset costs $499.
- **Fast policy.** An asymmetric tokenizer: a 1D-CNN encoder compresses an action chunk into a latent, and a GRU decoder rebuilds the actions from the latent plus the current tactile features (PCA of the marker field, or the 6-D wrench). Only the decoder sees touch, so it carries the reactive corrections. It runs at 24 Hz in under 1 ms.
- **Slow policy.** A latent diffusion policy predicts latent action chunks at 1-2 Hz from images, touch and proprioception.
- Relative end-effector trajectories and latency matching (dropping predicted steps already in the past) both matter for performance.

## Results
- Overall scores on Peeling / Wiping / Bimanual Lifting: Diffusion Policy 0.44 / 0.57 / 0.00, RDP with force input 0.95 / 0.87 / 0.70. Adding tactile embeddings to Diffusion Policy did not help (0.39 / 0.50 / 0.08).
- When the cucumber is moved after contact, RDP (GelSight) scores 0.80 against 0.15 for Diffusion Policy with tactile embeddings. GelSight and MCTac give similar Peeling scores (0.90 vs 0.88).
- Ablations: on Peeling, keeping only normal force lowers RDP from 0.95 to 0.48, a symmetric tokenizer to 0.58. On Wiping, shrinking Diffusion Policy's chunk from 8 to 2 drops its grasp rate from 100% to 20%.
- TactAR user study (10 users, 200 trials): peel length rises from 0.72 to 0.91 and the share of stable contact force from 0.58 to 0.87.

## Limitations
AR feedback is less intuitive than working with one's own hands. Only two-finger grippers are supported, the fast policy reacts to touch but not to high-frequency vision, and each policy learns a single task.
