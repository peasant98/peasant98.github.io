abstract: Humanoid robots can reduce personnel exposure in electrical switching, but safe control is challenging because the tool must reach a target switch next to similar devices, where an imprecise operator command can contact a non-target switch. This study proposes a runtime safety filter that checks each operator command during virtual-reality teleoperation of a humanoid robot. The filter integrates a scene model estimated from the robot head camera and a target-conditioned control barrier function quadratic program that keeps the tool away from the non-target switch while passing safe commands unchanged. In real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, with six rounds at each of two standing positions, target operations succeed in all ten rounds with an unchanged target and no non-target actuation. A simulation study achieves 9/9 safe completions, compared with 5/9 for raw teleoperation, demonstrating the feasibility of the method for humanoid switching operations.

## What they do
A Unitree G1, teleoperated through a VR headset, operates one of two neighboring wall-mounted safety switches. The authors insert a safety filter between the existing teleoperation stack and the robot. It keeps a wrist-mounted tool at least 5 cm from the non-target switch and the walls, leaves the operator-selected target reachable, and passes safe commands unchanged. No learning is involved.

## How it works
- **Scene model.** Before control, the head camera records about 900 RGB-D frames. Planes are fitted to hand-selected regions (cabinet fronts, walls), and each becomes an oriented box of declared size. The target's box is removed; the non-target and the two walls remain as constraints.
- **CBF-QP filter.** At 50 Hz, a control barrier function on the eight corners of the tool box gives constraints on the 14 arm-joint velocities, and a QP finds the command closest to the operator's. If the nominal command already satisfies them it is sent directly; otherwise cheap fallbacks are tried while a full QP runs in the background and the arm holds.
- **Boundary return.** If the tool is at the 5 cm boundary and the command still pushes inward, the arm returns to its initial pose and waits for recalibration and operator re-authorization.
- **Simulation extensions.** In MuJoCo, a finger-press task adds part-region permissions (only the index fingertip pad may touch the button) and phase-dependent motion directions.

## Results
- On the real G1, 12 rounds at two standing positions: 10 target operations completed, with no non-target actuation or accidental contact. Both failures were rounds where the target changed to the other switch, which the one-arm tool could not reach from that position.
- Deliberately approaching the non-target triggered an automatic return in 11 of 12 rounds. The commanded gap never fell below 50 mm; the measured gap reached 49.04 mm at worst, attributed to tracking lag.
- The filter changed the operator's command in 8.45% and 26.78% of eligible cycles at the two positions. All 180 background QP calls succeeded (mean about 0.5 ms), and 71.4% of cycles needed no solver.
- In simulation, 9 paired runs: 9/9 safe completions with the full filter, 5/9 for raw teleoperation and 6/9 for each ablation.

## Limitations
The switches were not energized, and they are general-duty wall switches rather than substation switchgear. The robot uses a rigid tool on one arm, so the finger-level permissions and phase constraints are tested only in simulation. A hold has no timeout: in one round the arm stalled for 61.38 s until the operator requested a manual return. Scene regions are chosen by hand, and the model must be rebuilt when the layout changes.
