tldr: A gripper with GelSight fingertips follows a free-hanging cable by sliding along it, using only touch to control grip force and cable pose. With an LQR pose controller it follows 98.2% of the length of five different cables, and it inserts a headphone plug in 85% of 20 trials.
abstract: 'Cables are complex, high dimensional, and dynamic objects. Standard approaches to manipulate them often rely on conservative strategies that involve long series of very slow and incremental deformations, or various mechanical fixtures such as clamps, pins or rings. We are interested in manipulating freely moving cables, in real time, with a pair of robotic grippers, and with no added mechanical constraints. The main contribution of this paper is a perception and control framework that moves in that direction, and uses real-time tactile feedback to accomplish the task of following a dangling cable. The approach relies on a vision-based tactile sensor, GelSight, that estimates the pose of the cable in the grip, and the friction forces during cable sliding. We achieve the behavior by combining two tactile-based controllers: 1) Cable grip controller, where a PD controller combined with a leaky integrator regulates the gripping force to maintain the frictional sliding forces close to a suitable value; and 2) Cable pose controller, where an LQR controller based on a learned linear model of the cable sliding dynamics keeps the cable centered and aligned on the fingertips to prevent the cable from falling from the grip. This behavior is possible by a reactive gripper fitted with GelSight-based high-resolution tactile sensors. The robot can follow one meter of cable in random configurations within 2-3 hand regrasps, adapting to cables of different materials and thicknesses. We demonstrate a robot grasping a headphone cable, sliding the fingers to the jack connector, and inserting it. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first implementation of real-time cable following without the aid of mechanical fixtures.'

## What they do
Most cable manipulation keeps the cable quasistatic or constrains it with tables, pegs or clamps. Here a free-hanging cable is followed in real time: a gripper fixed to the table holds its start, and a second gripper on a UR5 slides along it to the tail end, regrasping by feeding the cable back to the fixed gripper when it is about to lose it. Only touch is used, no vision. Following reaches 6.5 cm/s.

## How it works
- **Gripper.** A parallel gripper driven by one servo through a tendon and slider-crank, with a flexure parallel-guiding mechanism that replaces 28 rigid parts with one. GelSight fingertips; grip force is controlled at 60 Hz.
- **Perception.** Depth from the GelSight image, a thresholded contact region and PCA give the cable's offset and angle in the grip. Mean marker displacement approximates the friction force; contact area serves as a grasp-quality check.
- **Grip controller.** A PD controller holds the mean marker displacement at a target; a leaky integrator raises the target when the imprint is too small.
- **Pose controller.** A linear model of the cable state (offset, angle, pulling angle) is fit to about 2000 samples from one cable, collected with a noisy P controller. LQR on this model chooses the pulling direction.

## Results
- Open-loop motion follows only 36% of the cable. Adding emergency regrasps finishes the task but needs many regrasps; LQR needs the fewest of the four controllers, with little gain in speed.
- Fit at 0.025 m/s, the controller still works at 0.045 and 0.065 m/s, with more regrasps per distance but less total time.
- Across five cables and ropes it follows 98.2% of total length; the thin, light nylon rope needs the most regrasps.
- The robot follows a headphone cable to the plug, detects the plug by touch and inserts it. Insertion alone, with the plug handed over in a random pose, succeeds in 85% of 20 trials.

## Limitations
The tactile signal runs at 30 Hz and the gripper loop at 60 Hz. The convex GelSight surface makes it hard to pull the cable back once it reaches the finger edge. The dynamics model is linear, the cable's start is held by a fixed gripper, and the jack position is calibrated in advance.
