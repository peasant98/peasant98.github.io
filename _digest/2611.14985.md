abstract: Humanoid motion tracking is fundamental to reproducing human motions on humanoid robots and serves as an important tool for teleoperation and motion data collection. However, directly tracking reference motions can be unsafe in constrained environments or during physical human--robot interaction, motivating the need for compliant motion tracking. We present ILA, an Interface-Level Adapter for compliant humanoid motion tracking without dedicated interaction sensors. ILA adapts motion references from the robot's observation history, allowing the robot to respond to physical guidance while preserving the capabilities of a pretrained motion tracker. As an interface-level module, ILA can be directly plugged into different pretrained motion trackers without retraining either the adapter or the tracking policies. ILA is trained entirely in simulation to predict reference corrections induced by physical interaction. We evaluate ILA with three humanoid motion trackers and achieve a 63.6--75.4% reduction in guidance-target error compared with their original policies. Matched simulation studies and hardware experiments further show that ILA improves responsiveness to human guidance while preserving nominal motion-tracking performance. These results demonstrate compliant guidance as a reusable, sensor-free extension to pretrained humanoid motion trackers.

## What they do
Whole-body motion trackers follow their reference closely, so they resist a person guiding the robot's arm. ILA (Interface-Level Adapter) adds compliance without retraining the tracker, estimating wrenches or adding interaction sensors. It edits the reference the tracker receives, and the frozen tracker keeps handling balance and whole-body execution.

## How it works
- From the nominal reference and a history of proprioception and past tracker actions, ILA predicts a 32-step residual for the 14 arm joints. Trunk and leg references stay nominal.
- Training pairs nominal motions (LAFAN1, AMASS, 100STYLE) with modified ones in which an arm moves toward a temporary pose, holds and returns. A simulated spring wrench pulls the hand accordingly.
- A privileged teacher sees both references. A student that sees only the history is distilled from it and also predicts the interaction phase (none, yield, hold, recover) and per-arm intensity.
- Fixed mappings of joint order, coordinates and timing plug the same adapter into other trackers.

## Results
- Simulation (243 matched guidance rollouts per method): target error drops 68.1% on MotionTracking, 63.6% on MimicLite and 75.4% on Sonic, with lower fall rates. No-force tracking error rises 0.5% on MotionTracking.
- ILA gives stronger guidance and better recovery after release than finetuning each tracker. GentleHumanoid gets closer to the target (2.6 vs 5.7 cm) but moves the uninvolved arm more (10.6° vs 2.8°).
- G1 hardware, 10 trials per task: the original trackers never reach the wrist-pull target and fail all disturbance and handshake trials. With ILA all three succeed 10/10, as do native MotionTracking compliance and GentleHumanoid.
- Inference takes 0.51 ms on the G1's CPU.

## Limitations
Only the arms adapt. On Sonic, no-force tracking error grows more (5.36° to 5.85°), and how much nominal motion changes depends on the tracker. On hardware ILA matches, but does not beat, existing compliance methods.
