However, using recurrence to explicitly model dynamics necessitates sequential processing of data, slowing real-time applications such as brain-computer interfaces. Here we introduce the Neural Data Transformer (NDT), a non-recurrent alternative. ## What they do
Models that turn noisy spike trains into single-trial firing rates, such as LFADS, rely on recurrent networks. These process a window of data step by step, which slows real-time uses like brain-computer interfaces. The Neural Data Transformer (NDT) is a non-recurrent alternative: a Transformer encoder that reads the whole window of binned spikes in parallel and outputs a firing rate for every channel and time bin.

## How it works
- **Architecture.** A BERT-style Transformer encoder (6 layers in most experiments, 1 or 2 attention heads) with learned position embeddings. A linear layer outputs log firing rates, which are exponentiated and scored against the observed spikes with a Poisson likelihood.
- **Masked training.** A random subset of timesteps is masked and the model must reproduce the spikes there from the unmasked context. Masked inputs are zeroed out instead of being replaced by a special mask token.
- **Heavy regularization.** Dropout ratios are swept between 0.2 and 0.6, which matters most for small datasets.
- **Evaluation.** NDT is compared with AutoLFADS on two synthetic autonomous systems (Lorenz, chaotic RNN) and on recordings of 202 motor and premotor neurons from a monkey doing a delayed reaching task (2296 trials).

## Results
- Synthetic data: inferred rates match ground truth with R2 of 0.934 (Lorenz) and 0.846 (chaotic RNN), against 0.921 and 0.869 for AutoLFADS.
- Monkey reaching: linear decoding of hand velocity from inferred rates reaches 0.918 R2 for NDT and 0.915 for AutoLFADS.
- Speed: a forward pass takes 3.9 ms on a CPU and grows only slightly with sequence length, against 26 ms for LFADS on 70 bins (6.7x faster).
- Smaller models: a 2-layer NDT trains in 45 minutes, infers in 2.2 ms and decodes at 0.921 R2; trained on only 92 trials it reaches 0.866 R2, ahead of AutoLFADS. The 6-layer model took 9.4 hours to train.

## Limitations
NDT cannot model non-autonomous dynamics, where unpredictable inputs from unrecorded areas perturb the population: LFADS outperforms it by over 20% R2 in a preliminary test on a synthetic dataset with inputs. Timing was measured offline, not in a closed-loop interface. Without log-rate outputs and strong dropout, performance is much worse and more variable. The Transformer also lacks the recurrent structure that dynamical-systems analyses of RNNs rely on.
