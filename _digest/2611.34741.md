abstract: 'Robot trajectories naturally decompose into variable-length phases with distinct semantic intents, such as reaching, grasping, and lifting. However, training policies to minimize MSE treats all actions as equal, obscuring their semantic structure. While latent action models attempt to create a structured prediction target by compressing action chunks into latent representations, existing approaches rely on two-stage training and context-blind decoders. Consequently, their representations are context-agnostic motion codes (e.g., "move down") which fail to capture behavioural intent (e.g., "pick up the block"). To overcome these bottlenecks, we introduce the Emergent Latent-action MOdel (ELMO), an end-to-end framework that encodes robot trajectories as grounded semantic codes -- representations that ground task-level behavioural intent in the current environment. ELMO jointly trains a VLA planner and an observation-conditioned decoder over dynamically chunked trajectories, learning a semantically grounded action space without separate pretraining. Across standard benchmarks, ELMO outperforms comparable prior methods by 9.6 percentage points on LIBERO-Plus and by 0.75 in average task length on CALVIN. Furthermore, on Blocks -- our shortcut-free simulation and real-world generalization benchmark -- ELMO reaches a 38.6% success rate while all baselines remain below 5%, demonstrating its ability to capture the semantic structure of demonstrations. ## What they do
Latent action models compress action chunks into codes that a VLA predicts. The authors probe two of them, MINT and EDAR, and find mostly "motion codes": the latents predict end-effector displacement and gripper state, not the target object or the task. ELMO instead trains the planner and decoder together in a single stage and lets the decoder see the scene. The latent can then carry the intent ("push the red block") and leave the exact motion to the decoder.

## How it works
- **Dynamic chunking.** As in H-Net, a recurrent encoder runs at the control rate on pooled wrist-camera features and proprioception. A learned router starts a new chunk when consecutive encoder states differ, aiming for about 12 steps per chunk.
- **Planner.** Only at chunk boundaries, a causal transformer over earlier chunks reads frozen SmolVLM2 image and language tokens and emits a continuous latent code.
- **Decoder.** At every step a recurrent decoder combines the active code with the encoder state, cross-attends to the wrist camera, and drives a flow-matching action head. Only 22M parameters are trained.
- **No history shortcuts.** Training sequences are spliced from different demonstrations of the same instruction, and episodes whose opening the planner gets wrong are sampled more often.

## Results
- CALVIN ABCD→D: average chain length 4.08, against 3.33 for the best baseline. LIBERO: 90.2%, tied with MINT-30M. LIBERO-Plus zero-shot: 64.0%, 9.6 points above the best method without image augmentation.
- Blocks, a simulated bimanual ALOHA task with five randomly placed coloured cubes: 38.6% success in distribution and 31.7% on held-out positions, against at most 5.0% and 2.0% for baselines.
- Real ALOHA, fine-tuned on 1,200 teleoperated demos: 13/25 push and 8/25 placement trials, against 2/25 and 0/25 for SmolVLA.

## Limitations
Chunk boundaries line up with grasps reliably only with an early kinematic warm-start loss (82% within 2 steps, 39% without it) and a preset compression ratio. ELMO picks the right block but struggles with sub-centimeter grasping and stacking (7.2% stacking success on Blocks). Cross-embodiment pretraining and learning from unlabeled video are left to future work.
