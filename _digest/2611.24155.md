abstract: 'Robot navigation requires both an understanding of the scene and an efficient adaptation to new robots, yet current methods rely on expensive LiDAR sensors, long mapping and modular pipelines. We present BEV-Diffusion, a camera-only model that learns a bird''s-eye view (BEV) traversability map, its uncertainty and the path in a single network. A frozen DINOv2 backbone encodes consecutive RGB frames, which inverse perspective mapping and Bayesian fusion lift into the BEV, and a ranking loss keeps outdoor traversability graded rather than binary, separating asphalt from grass (median 0.94 vs. 0.75). On this map, a transformer denoiser generates polar waypoints in a few DDIM steps, and uncertainty-aware guidance refines the path and ends it where the map is no longer reliable. At deployment, a metric BEV memory keeps obstacles after they leave the camera view and warm starts each sample from the previous, ego-motion compensated path. Because the planner jointly learns the path, the map, and its uncertainty, it selects routes through terrain it can confidently traverse. In an unseen courtyard, a wheeled-legged robot autonomously traveled 120 m, completing 96% of the route without intervention, compared with 75-88% for the baselines. Even under low-light conditions after sunset, the proposed model completed the route without requiring any intervention. Retraining only the BEV head, about 3% of the parameters, adapts the model to a small legged robot in 2.3 min. Indoors, the memory gets this robot out of maze-like traps: it retains 62-66% of the nearby out-of-view obstacles, against at most 3% for the baselines, and the robot achieves 67-100% task progress, compared with 20-40% for the evaluated baselines.'

## What they do
Navigation for legged robots from a single camera, with no prior map or localisation. One network predicts a top-down (BEV) traversability map with its uncertainty and generates a path on it by diffusion. LiDAR, odometry and IMU are used only to make training labels.

## How it works
- **BEV from two frames.** A frozen DINOv2 with small adapters encodes two RGB frames 0.1 s apart; the features are projected onto a flat ground plane and fused by adding log-odds into a polar grid. A head predicts graded traversability (0 impassable to 1 free) with epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty, and a ranking loss keeps asphalt and grass apart.
- **Path diffusion.** A 26M-parameter transformer denoiser generates 50 polar waypoints in five DDIM steps. Traversability and uncertainty gradients refine the path, which is cut where confidence falls below 0.5. A plan takes 42 ms on an RTX 5090.
- **BEV memory.** At deployment, an ego-centric grid shifted by ego-motion estimated from the images keeps obstacles that left the view, warm-starts each sample and turns the robot towards remembered free space when it is stuck.
- **Adaptation.** A new robot retrains only the BEV head, 1.1M of 40M trained parameters.

## Results
- On a 41 kg Agibot D1 Max wheeled quadruped, over 120 m in an unseen courtyard, 96% of the route passed without intervention versus 75-88% for BEVFormer + A*, BEVFormer + diffusion and TG-Diff, with no intervention after sunset.
- The learned BEV detects 69.5% of blocked cells, against 5.6% for image-space traversability projected onto the ground.
- Adapting to the small Dogzilla S2 took 2.3 min instead of 19.1 min from scratch, for 89% versus 96% indoor task progress.
- In the round room, the memory keeps 62-63% of out-of-view obstacles versus 2-3% for the BEVFormer baselines; across the two trap-like scenes progress is 67-100% versus 20-40%.

## Limitations
The flat-ground projection limits it to mostly flat terrain. In unfamiliar scenes the map detects fewer obstacles: blocked recall in the unseen courtyard is 37-40%, below the BEVFormer baselines' 55-69%. Fusion treats frames as independent, so more frames hurt calibration.
