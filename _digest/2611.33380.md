abstract: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has recently gained popularity as a faster alternative to Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) in 3D reconstruction and view synthesis methods. Leveraging the spatial information encoded in 3DGS, this work proposes FOCI (Field Overlap Collision Integral), an algorithm that is able to optimize trajectories directly on the Gaussians themselves. FOCI leverages a novel and interpretable collision formulation for 3DGS using the notion of the overlap integral between Gaussians. The formulation presented applies to different robot morphologies (e.g., fixed and mobile base manipulators, legged robots, UAVs) and handles both static and dynamic Gaussian-Splat environments. Contrary to other approaches, which represent the robot with conservative bounding boxes that underestimate the traversability of the environment, we propose to represent the environment and the robot as Gaussian Splats. This not only has desirable computational properties, but also allows for orientation-aware planning, allowing the robot to pass through very tight and narrow spaces. We extensively test our algorithm in both synthetic and real Gaussian Splats, showcasing that collision-free trajectories for legged, aerial and manipulator robots can be computed in seconds, even with hundreds of thousands of Gaussians making up the environment. The code is available at https://github.com/leggedrobotics/foci

## What they do
Earlier planners on Gaussian splats treat the robot as a sphere or ellipsoid with a fixed orientation, which is conservative in tight spaces. FOCI (Field Overlap Collision Integral) represents the robot as a set of Gaussians too. Collision then becomes a closed-form, differentiable overlap between the two sets of Gaussians, and a trajectory optimizer can rotate the robot to fit through narrow passages. The same formulation covers legged robots, UAVs and fixed-base manipulators, as well as scenes whose Gaussians move over time.

## How it works
- **Collision cost.** For each robot-environment Gaussian pair, the overlap integral reduces to a normal density on the Mahalanobis distance. It decays to zero away from obstacles, grows with overlap, and is differentiable in the joint angles.
- **Trajectory.** Joint trajectories, plus virtual prismatic joints for a mobile base, are cubic B-splines. The cost sums obstacle overlap, jerk and distance to the goal. Velocity and acceleration limits are enforced on the control points through the MINVO convex hull.
- **Solver.** A* or RRT* on a voxelized occupancy grid gives the initial guess. IPOPT solves the problem via CasADi, with the collision sum and its gradients computed in parallel on the GPU with NVIDIA Warp.

## Results
- ANYmal, modeled as 3 Gaussians: solves take 0.45-0.47 s in synthetic scenes and 2.12 s in Machine Hall (243k Gaussians). On hardware, ANYmal followed plans made on a splat built from UAV imagery, rotating through narrow passages.
- On Stonehenge (138k Gaussians) FOCI averages 0.78 s for a normalized path length of 0.88. RRT* with a 100 s budget reaches 0.92, and Splat-Nav reaches 1.01 in 0.76 s.
- UR5 in Bonsai (about 569k Gaussians): 3.06 s, against 29.3 s for CHOMP (whose shorter path collides) and 440 s for STOMP, which did not converge.
- The paper compares CasADi optimization on one CPU core, on many cores with OpenMP and on the GPU, and reports the GPU version as 320 times faster. Solve time grows linearly with the number of robot Gaussians.

## Limitations
Jerk and obstacle costs are traded off rather than constrained, so the optimizer occasionally returns a colliding trajectory. Splat density does not always mean solid geometry, and with no interior density, a path that penetrates a surface can get stuck. The CHOMP and STOMP baselines are the authors' own adaptations.
