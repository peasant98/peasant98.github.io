tldr: A five-fingered, tendon-driven hand whose fingers are rigid 3D-printed skeletons overmolded in silicone, so it stays compliant yet can be run with linear controllers. It performs 28 of the 33 GRASP-taxonomy grasps and every teleoperated grasping task succeeded at least 80% of the time.
abstract: 'Adding compliance to robot hands results in higher grasp quality and robust contact with the environment. Soft hands are inherently compliant, but are typically more complex to control and model than rigid equivalents. We extend prior work on soft-rigid hybrid robot fingers into a five-fingered dexterous gripper --- an integrated tendon-driven hand that is highly compliant and controllable. Structural compliance combined with a rigid skeleton, when realized in a five-fingered hand, allow us to use linear control to achieve soft-grasping generality. We evaluate the use of this hand in a variety of teleoperated manipulation tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness of the overall mechanical design and control strategy. Our contributions include: (1) Mechanical design of the a five-fingered Soft-Rigid Hybrid Hand, composed of 12 active degrees of freedom, demonstrating a high degree on generalization enabled by the soft-rigid architecture, with our gripper, able to complete 28 of the 33 (84.8\%) grasp postures in the human range. (2) Precise, high bandwidth (up to 1432deg/s extension speed, compared with 700deg/s for the equivalent human joint). (3) Position and task space controllers for the soft-rigid hybrid finger and whole hand. (4) A teleoperation framework for control of this hand, as well as demonstrations of its efficacy in a variety of grasping and manipulation tasks Further tests illustrate the hand''s advantages in completing non-rigid manipulation tasks, such as picking up thin deformable objects (such as a dollar bill or folding cloth) as well as in interfacing with soft or delicate targets.'

## What they do
Soft hands conform to objects but are weak, imprecise and hard to model; rigid hands are easy to control but not compliant. The authors turn their earlier soft-rigid hybrid finger, a rigid skeleton cast inside a silicone shell, into a complete five-fingered hand with 12 actuated degrees of freedom. They give it kinematic models and linear controllers, mount it on a UR5 and run it by teleoperation.

## How it works
- **Hardware.** Each finger is a four-part Nylon 12 skeleton overmolded in Ecoflex silicone, with three 16-bit magnetic joint angle sensors read at 250 Hz. Dyneema tendons in Bowden tubes run to 10 Dynamixel motors in the base, and two more motors in the palm add index abduction and an angled thumb rotation.
- **Choosing joints.** DOFs were picked by how many GRASP-taxonomy grasps need them; little-finger abduction (needed in 4 grasps) was left out.
- **Control.** Sweeping each tendon at constant speeds showed joint velocities depend piecewise linearly on tendon speed. Joint-space and task-space feedback controllers are built on that model and run at 40 Hz.
- **Teleoperation.** A glove with six magnetic trackers sets the UR5 wrist pose and fingertip targets through kinematic retargeting.

## Results
- 28 of 33 GRASP-taxonomy grasps (84.8%); the five failures need DOFs the hand omits.
- Ten teleoperated grasping tasks, 20 trials each, all succeeded at least 80% of the time with only visual feedback; contact-heavy picks such as a dollar bill or a disc were among the most reliable.
- Fast fingers: extension reaches about 1432 deg/s and flexion about 860 deg/s, against 700 deg/s for the human MCP joint.
- Grip force 25.26 N and extension force 2.15 N.

## Limitations
Force output is low, roughly an order of magnitude below a human hand, which the authors attribute mainly to Bowden-tube friction. Underactuated fingers cannot reach every pose, so joint control can leave steady-state errors. Each grasp type was tested with a single object.
