abstract: Collaboration promises to extend humanoid capabilities beyond what individual robots can achieve. However, realizing this potential requires coordinated whole-body interactions, coherent long-horizon planning, and precise synchronization across robots. We introduce Heave-Ho, a decentralized framework for long-horizon collaborative multi-humanoid loco-manipulation. At the low level, we develop a multi-agent post-training framework that adapts a humanoid foundation tracking model to physically coupled interactions between humanoids, enabling coordinated decentralized control from local observations alone. At the high level, we train a vision-language planner and design a speech-based leader-follower coordination mechanism to synchronize decentralized execution without network communication. Trained with two identical humanoids, our system scales to teams of up to ten robots and generalizes to heterogeneous teams without retraining. To benchmark these capabilities, we introduce HH-Bench, comprising 12 tasks involving teams of 2 to 10 humanoids, including heterogeneous teams. Systematic evaluations show that Heave-Ho consistently outperforms baseline methods, while real-world experiments demonstrate robust decentralized collaboration across challenging tasks.

## What they do
Tables, sofas and large boxes often need several humanoids lifting together. Centralized controllers grow with team size and need network links between robots. Heave-Ho is fully decentralized: each Unitree G1 runs the same policy on its own computer from its own observations, and the robots exchange no network messages. The authors also introduce HH-Bench, 12 collaborative tasks for teams of 2-10 humanoids.

## How it works
- **Composable team skills.** Lift, put down, steps and in-place rotations all start and end at two hub poses (standing empty-handed or holding the object), so any skill can follow any other. Reference motions come from retargeted videos of two people and from inverse kinematics, conditioned on object size and mass.
- **Residual multi-agent post-training.** A frozen humanoid foundation tracker (SONIC) supplies balance and stepping. A residual actor shared by all robots learns the load sharing; it is trained with MAPPO and a centralized critic in Isaac simulation with two G1s. Partners affect each robot only through the object.
- **Planner and speech sync.** A LoRA-tuned Qwen3-VL-2B picks the next skill from the leader's head-camera image. The leader speaks it, followers recognize it by microphone, and all robots start on a coarse shared tick grid so acoustic delay does not shift the start. A person can follow or lead.

## Results
- Real world: 92 of 100 trials succeed across 10 team and task combinations; stair carry with a person is hardest (7/10). Teams unlike the training pair (4 G1s, G1s with people, an EngineAI PM01) succeed in 37 of 40 table carries.
- Simulation: 94.41% average success over the 12 tasks, versus 28.56% for Multi-HDMI, 22.05% with independent actors and 0.22% for the frozen tracker alone.
- Planner: 86% success on five long-horizon tasks, versus 42% for zero-shot GPT-6 (Astra), 12% for CHORUS and 98% for a human operator. One decision takes 52.5 ms, against 6.46 s for GPT-6 (Astra).

## Limitations
Synchronization needs a reasonably quiet environment and reliable sound pickup. Training the planner for a new task needs teleoperated demonstrations. Teams of eight and ten robots were tested only in simulation, and real trials start from a nominal configuration, so real and simulated success rates are not directly comparable.
