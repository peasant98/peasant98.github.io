abstract: 'Whole-body humanoid manipulation remains bottlenecked by high-dimensional state-action spaces, contact-rich environments, and long task horizons. This trilemma is a challenge that state-of-the-art reinforcement learning (RL) methods are yet to resolve reliably. We address it with three contributions that add just enough structure to guide exploration: Sparse Interaction Edges (SIE) for whole-body manipulation, Interaction Gated Transport (IGT) for sustained transport over long horizons, and a Hinted Object Path (HOP) that resolves collision and search complexity bottlenecks. ## What they do
Pure RL rarely solves whole-body humanoid manipulation: the robot has 35 DoFs plus the object's own, contact makes the reward landscape jagged, and tasks run through distinct phases (lift, carry, place). The authors add three small pieces of task structure to the observation and reward, leave PPO untouched, and train the AIREC humanoid to carry a ball into a box and to move an articulated human model from a bed into a wheelchair.

## How it works
- **Sparse Interaction Edges.** Hand-picked keypoints on the robot (base, chest, arms, hands) and object (one for the ball, five for the human) are joined by edges that the policy both observes and is rewarded for shortening, so it uses its whole body instead of just its hands.
- **Interaction Gated Transport.** A sigmoid gate zeroes the transport reward unless the selected edges stay short (0.3 m for the human), ruling out throwing or pushing.
- **Hinted Object Path.** A coarse Bézier path per object keypoint, with waypoints reached in order, steers the reward around obstacles; the policy may later leave it.
- Training uses Isaac Lab with 2048-4096 parallel environments on one RTX 5090.

## Results
- Ball trains in about 30 min; the human tasks reach competitive performance in about 60 min. The ablated baseline (joint states, end-effector edges, plain object-to-goal reward) never lifts the ball in 5 seeds and succeeds in 1 of 5 seeds on Human (far), otherwise throwing or flipping the human; their method moves the human the same way in every seed.
- Policies trained with human mass varied over 11-33 kg, or height over 115-180 cm, complete the transfer in most cases.
- On the real robot, policy trajectories replayed open-loop at 10 Hz lift a 155 cm, 15 kg mannequin, carry it to a wheelchair at two placements, set it down and disengage.

## Limitations
The hardware result is open-loop replay, identical replays sometimes failed, and the authors call it a demonstration rather than a hardware validation. Torque and joint-error safety stops fired often during placement. Keypoints and object paths are designed by hand. Real people are out of reach for now: holding 50 kg needs about 375 Nm per shoulder against AIREC's rated 220 Nm, and the policy has no safety certificate.
