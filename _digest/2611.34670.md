abstract: Autonomous robotic palpation offers repeatable, quantitative estimates of tissue properties for stiff inclusion localization. However, existing methods often rely on prior mesh models or planar surface assumptions that do not generalize to complex human anatomy. We propose an ergodic palpation framework that operates directly on curved soft surfaces using point cloud data. Central to our approach is the Laplace--Beltrami operator, which grounds both online estimation and exploration in the surface intrinsic geometry. We estimate local viscoelastic parameters using an Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) and fuse them into a surface-aware Gaussian Process (GP). This probabilistic model drives a diffusion-based ergodic motion planner, generating a continuous exploration trajectory that feeds the new informative samples back to the GP in a closed loop. Throughout this process, a hybrid force-impedance controller regulates interaction forces to maintain contact. We evaluate the approach against existing methods in simulation and validate it in physical experiments on tissue-mimicking silicone phantoms with varying geometries and viscoelastic properties, demonstrating geometry-consistent mapping and stiff inclusion localization under contact-rich conditions.

## What they do
Autonomous palpation methods usually assume a flat, rectangular patch or a mesh of the anatomy. This paper palpates curved soft surfaces given only a point cloud from a wrist depth camera. A Franka arm slides a 5 mm spherical indenter over the surface under force control, estimates local stiffness as it moves, and uses the growing stiffness map to decide where to go next.

## How it works
- **Local stiffness.** An unscented Kalman filter with a viscoelastic contact model estimates penetration, elasticity and viscosity online from the wrist force and the normal velocity. A fading-memory factor lets it react when the probe crosses a stiffer inclusion.
- **Mapping on the surface.** The point cloud's Laplace-Beltrami operator gives a heat-kernel GP, which correlates points by distance along the surface rather than in 3D. Kernel values for 500 eigenpairs are precomputed offline.
- **Exploration.** The GP mean and standard deviation form an information density, with α weighting stiffness peaks against uncertainty. The planner (PC-HEDAC) diffuses the gap between this target and past coverage over the surface and follows its gradient along the tangent plane at 500 Hz; the GP updates at 1 Hz.
- **Stopping.** Exploration ends when the ergodic metric drops below 0.3 and stops decreasing.

## Results
- Simulation, ten runs: PC-HEDAC reaches RMSE 0.213 after 4.0 m of travel; global BO-EI and BO-UCB need 19.8 and 20.7 m. With matched budgets it beats BO (0.302, 0.311), adaptive LSE (0.251), spectral ergodic control (0.244, 0.257) and a precomputed coverage path (0.255).
- The intrinsic kernel lowers RMSE by 11-14% on a folded surface but is 3-4% worse than a Euclidean kernel on a bunny.
- Real robot, eight trials per phantom: stopping saves 26% and 35% of the fixed budget, with RMSE of 7.6 and 16.1 kPa. Running the full budget did not consistently lower RMSE.
- Mean localization errors of the stiff regions range from 1.9 to 5.4 mm.

## Limitations
The point cloud is captured once and assumed not to deform. The online and reference maps share sensor and estimator, so the results measure the extra error from exploration rather than absolute stiffness accuracy. Stopping thresholds were tuned on the same trials. Only surface response is mapped, not inclusion depth, and only on silicone phantoms.
