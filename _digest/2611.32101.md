abstract: 'Learning dexterous manipulation from egocentric human demonstrations offers a scalable path toward robot skill acquisition. Vision alone, however, provides limited information about physical contacts and the demonstrator''s force intent, leading to ambiguities in interpreting hand-object interactions. Capturing human touch via tactile sensing is therefore crucial for reliable human-to-robot dexterous skill transfer. We present Ego2Real, a framework that leverages egocentric demonstrations and tactile gloves to transfer bimanual dexterous manipulation skills to robotic hands. Ego2Real incorporates tactile supervision at two complementary stages: (1) tactile-guided hand-object interaction reconstruction, which uses measured tactile signals to refine hand poses and contact geometry; and (2) contact-informed reinforcement learning, which translates human contact patterns into robotic contact rewards. By combining refined motion references with physical contact rewards during training, Ego2Real guides anthropomorphic robotic hands to emulate demonstrated contact behaviors while adapting to their own morphology and actuation. Experiments show that incorporating tactile supervision into both reconstruction and policy learning yields higher manipulation success rates, underscoring the importance of enriching egocentric demonstrations with tactile observations to improve their quality and transferability.'

## What they do
Egocentric video of people manipulating objects is cheap to collect, but it shows contact poorly: occluded fingers leave gaps or penetration in the reconstructed hand-object motion, and vision gives no force. Ego2Real adds a pair of tactile gloves to the recording and uses the touch signal twice: to correct the reconstruction, and to reward the robot for reproducing the human's contacts during RL.

## How it works
- **Capture.** A static first-person RealSense D435i (RGB-D, 30 Hz) plus two gloves with 1,123 taxels each (125 Hz), calibrated to normal force with a six-axis force/torque sensor.
- **Tactile-guided reconstruction.** SAM 3, FoundationPose and HaMeR initialize object and MANO hand trajectories. Glove readings are pooled into 54 regions, each tied to an anchor on the hand surface; active regions pull their anchor onto the object with opposing normals, and a preservation term stops the hand sliding during sustained contact.
- **Retargeting.** An interaction mesh between hand keypoints and object surface points is matched to OmniHand 3 (10 actuated, 6 passive joints).
- **Contact-informed residual RL.** PPO in mjlab learns wrist and finger residuals, rewarded for object tracking, matching contact states and total force over 8 regions per hand, and not touching before the human did. Touch goes only to the critic, so the deployed policy uses object pose and proprioception.

## Results
- Real robot (Marvin Pro arms, OmniHand 3 hands, 15 trials each): Bottle Handover succeeds 12/15 versus 2/15 for REGRIND with the same tactile-refined references. Box Rotation reaches 15/15 for both with refined references and 0/15 for both without.
- Policies trained from bare-hand demonstrations (no gloves) succeed 1/15 and 4/15 on the two tasks.
- Simulation: 100% success on both tasks, like REGRIND, against 0% for SPIDER and 68.5% / 49.6% for MANIPTRANS; object tracking error is slightly higher than REGRIND (4.73 vs 3.98 mm on Bottle Handover).

## Limitations
One policy per demonstration, object meshes and simulation are required, and only two rigid-object tasks were tested. The robot has no tactile feedback at execution.
