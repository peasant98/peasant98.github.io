abstract: Haptic interactions, such as touching an object in virtual reality, involve detecting a plethora of sensations through the skin. Whilst tactile displays exist to simulate and manipulate all of these cues individually, little progress has been made constructing devices that can control multiple cues simultaneously. This paper presents a tactile display technology that offers both softness and temperature change. A prototype display, based on particle jamming and the thermal transfer of heated and cooled water, is evaluated in terms of its ability to change both stiffness and temperature. Mechanical evaluations demonstrate controllable stiffness increase of 300%, whilst thermal characterisation showed strong surface uniformity and a range of achievable temperatures from 10°C up to 50°C, with closed-loop control maintaining a ±0.2°C error from a setpoint. This work advances the design of multimodal tactile displays with applications to perceptual research, medical simulation, and affective haptics.

## What they do
Most tactile displays render one cue at a time. This paper builds a 150 mm round display that renders softness and temperature together. A particle-jamming core sets stiffness, and a thin silicone skin carrying heated or chilled water sets the surface temperature. Both channels are characterized with bench measurements; no user study is reported.

## How it works
- **Softness.** Constrictive jamming: particles in a fabric pouch are twisted from one end by a brushless motor under closed-loop torque control, so more torque means a stiffer surface. No pump or airtight container is needed.
- **Temperature.** Water flows through channels cast in silicone under a touch layer of silicone mixed 60:40 with copper powder to spread heat. The plain silicone insulates the jamming core. A 100 W Peltier module heats or cools the water.
- **Control.** An Arduino uses bang-bang control when the temperature is more than 5 °C from the setpoint and PID inside that band, with thermistors under the skin.

## Results
- Stiffness, measured with an 8 mm indenter over 0.5-1 N, rises linearly from 0.63 N/mm (unjammed) to 2.27 N/mm at 0.5 Nm of torque, then levels off at 2.38 N/mm at 1 Nm. Hysteresis falls from 84% to 2.1%.
- At full power the surface reaches 50.2 °C in 20 minutes (τ = 290 s) and cools to 9.3 °C (τ = 325.5 s). Neighboring channels differ by under 0.3 °C, and the pouch exterior stays at 26 °C when the surface is above 50 °C.
- Commanded from about 33 °C to 15 °C, it took 12 minutes, then held within ±0.2 °C for 15 minutes.

## Limitations
Temperature changes are slow: every test showed a 30 s lag between command and response, and reaching the maximum took 20 minutes, which the authors attribute to the Peltier module and the fluid's thermal mass. The closed-loop test was preliminary, and recovery from perturbations was not tested.
