## What they do
Robot learning still leans on classical optimization for teleoperation, data generation and planning. RoboKit is one GPU toolkit for these optimization tasks: inverse kinematics, trajectory optimization, hand and humanoid retargeting, dexterous grasp synthesis, rendering-based hand-eye calibration and physics-based MPC. Its cost terms are differentiable, so the same objectives can train policies.

## How it works
- **Parallel solver.** Built in Warp, it pairs multi-seed particle sampling with a choice of optimizer per task: Adam, L-BFGS, Gauss-Newton/Levenberg-Marquardt, or MPPI. Cost terms are composable and support sparse Jacobians.
- **Acceleration structures.** A voxel SDF switches to exact mesh queries only near the surface, and one bounding sphere per link prunes collision checks. Calibration uses a coarse-to-fine rendering pyramid, and physics rollouts start coarse.
- **Distillation.** Offline distillation trains on filtered solver outputs. Online distillation backpropagates the cost residuals straight into the policy. Grasping needs an offline warm-up before online fine-tuning.

## Results
- IK is 7.1 vs 86.2 ms for PyRoKi at batch 2,000. Trajectory planning is 0.016 vs 0.057 s for cuRobo, at comparable success.
- Grasp synthesis succeeds 46.83% of the time vs 23.63% for Dex1B, though Dex1B has higher grasp quality (Q1 0.109 vs 0.077). From a 35 cm / 45 degree initial error, calibration reaches 1.10 cm in 2.0 s, while EasyHEC does not converge.
- Physics-based hand retargeting raises ADD-AUC on OakInk from 51.3 to 65.8 over SPIDER and runs 5.6x faster.
- A distilled trajectory policy takes 0.63 vs 150.61 ms per frame, and a distilled hand-retargeting policy lowers position error from 7.79 to 4.80 mm. RL reaches mjlab's final score 1.3-2.1x sooner.

## Limitations
RoboKit targets batched GPU workloads. For single-instance kinematics on a CPU, kernel-launch overhead dominates and a C++ solver would be faster. Trajectory optimization has no graph-search stage, and the toolkit lacks inverse dynamics. All evaluations use existing simulated benchmarks and datasets; the paper reports no real-robot experiments.
