## What they do
A vision-based touch sensor sliding over an object sees only a small patch, so a single touch is ambiguous: a sharp edge could lie anywhere along a mug's lip. MidasTouch keeps a full distribution over the sensor's 6D pose on a known object and narrows it as the sensor slides over distinctive geometry, without visual priors. The authors also release YCB-Slide, real and simulated DIGIT sliding data on 10 YCB objects with ground-truth poses.

## How it works
- **Tactile depth network.** A ResNet-50 maps each DIGIT image to a heightmap (0.135 mm RMSE on simulated test data), trained on TACTO renders of 40 YCB objects with the test objects held out. The masked heightmap becomes a point cloud.
- **Tactile code network.** MinkLoc3D, a sparse 3D convolution network pretrained for LIDAR place recognition and fine-tuned with a triplet loss, compresses the point cloud into a 256-d code.
- **Codebook.** Per object, 50k simulated sensor poses are rendered and encoded once. A pose-space KD-tree gives any particle its code without rendering.
- **Particle filter.** Robot odometry moves the particles, cosine similarity between codes sets their weights, and DBSCAN clusters them into pose hypotheses.

## Results
- Simulation, 500 trials: average final error 0.74 cm and 9.43°, from an initial spread covering the whole object. The full stack runs at about 10 Hz.
- Real world, 500 trials with a tighter initial prior: 1.97 cm and 21.48°. Error drops on every object except the baseball, where all trials fail. Tools and intricate objects are easiest; symmetric ones are hardest.
- Error of the closest hypothesis: median 0.28 cm and 2.03° in simulation, 1.11 cm and 10.76° in the real world.
- 3D codes beat image embeddings on single-touch localization (normalized error 0.473 vs 0.546) with a 300x smaller codebook.

## Limitations
The object must stay fixed and its mesh must be known. One contact only; breaking contact hurts the on-surface assumption, and shear is ignored. Deformable and articulated objects are out of scope.
