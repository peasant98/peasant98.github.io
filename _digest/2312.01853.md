abstract: Executing contact-rich manipulation tasks necessitates the fusion of tactile and visual feedback. However, the distinct nature of these modalities poses significant challenges. In this paper, we introduce a system that leverages visual and tactile sensory inputs to enable dexterous in-hand manipulation. Specifically, we propose Robot Synesthesia, a novel point cloud-based tactile representation inspired by human tactile-visual synesthesia. This approach allows for the simultaneous and seamless integration of both sensory inputs, offering richer spatial information and facilitating better reasoning about robot actions. The method, trained in a simulated environment and then deployed to a real robot, is applicable to various in-hand object rotation tasks. Comprehensive ablations are performed on how the integration of vision and touch can improve reinforcement learning and Sim2Real performance. ## What they do
In-hand rotation needs vision and touch, but touch is sparse and low-dimensional while vision is dense. Robot Synesthesia turns each triggered tactile sensor into points on that sensor's mesh, so contact appears in the same 3D point cloud as the camera data. A policy trained in simulation runs on the real hand without real-world data on three tasks: turning a four-way wrench, rotating two balls around each other, and rotating varied objects about the x, y or z axis.

## How it works
- **Hardware.** An XArm6 arm with a 16-DoF Allegro hand, 16 force-sensing resistors (thresholded to binary contact) and an Azure Kinect depth camera, mirrored in Isaac Gym.
- **Tactile point cloud.** Each active sensor contributes 8 points from its mesh. These join 512 camera points and 8 points per hand link sampled from the robot mesh at the current pose. A one-hot tag marks each point's source, and all points are expressed in the palm frame.
- **Teacher-student.** A PPO teacher sees object position, velocities and a shape embedding. It is distilled into a PointNet student by behavior cloning on 5120k transitions, then DAgger.

## Results
- Simulated teachers beat a proprioception-plus-touch baseline on every task (double balls: reward 1045.3 vs 620.9), while visual RL from scratch barely learns (127.8).
- Simulated students: adding tactile points to camera points, robot-mesh points and the touch vector raises the wrench reward from 344.1 to 504.0 and the double-ball reward from 148.6 to 407.7. On x- and y-axis rotation it does not beat the best baseline.
- Real robot, 5 trials per policy: the full model rotates the most on the wrench (1.5 vs 0.25 rounds), double balls (22.9 vs 18.8), x-axis (2.1 vs 0.7) and z-axis (10.2 vs 8.6), but not the y-axis (0.9 vs 1.4).
- On average, 42.7% of the points the encoder selects are tactile points.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. Touch is binary contact, and every task rotates the object about a fixed axis. The authors leave goal-conditioned rotation and optical tactile sensors to future work. On the wrench, the full model drops the object sooner than the baselines (43.0 s vs 60 s).
