abstract: 'World-Action Models (WAMs) are a promising new paradigm of robot manipulation policies that jointly generate future video and actions. Successful manipulation requires both identifying where to interact and modeling how the robot and objects move. Yet standard adaptation methods, such as full fine-tuning and LoRA, do not explicitly select parameters aligned with these manipulation-specific capabilities. Motivated by this challenge, we discover two functionally specialized families of attention heads: Next-Interaction Heads, which localize upcoming grasp-and-release targets, and Motion Heads, which track movement across frames. Furthermore, we introduce HeadLight, a parameter-efficient refinement method that jointly adapts Next-Interaction Heads and Motion Heads. On RoboTwin tasks unseen during fine-tuning, HeadLight achieves 62.28% success, compared with 57.89% for full fine-tuning and 58.66% for LoRA, while updating only 1.1% of parameters. Inference-time ablations further validate the functional importance of these heads: ablating Motion Heads reduces manipulation success from 57.89% to 0.94%, while ablating Next-Interaction Heads lowers it to 40.61%, both substantially more than ablating equally many random heads. Finally, we show that our approach is effective across multiple WAM architectures and real-robot manipulation settings.'

## What they do
World-action models (WAMs) generate future video and robot actions together. Full fine-tuning and LoRA adapt them without regard to what each part of the network does. The authors look inside two WAMs (Zero-WAM and LingBot-VA) for attention heads tied to two manipulation skills, finding where to interact next and tracking motion, and then adapt only those heads.

## How it works
- **Next-Interaction Heads.** For each video head, mask the token covering the upcoming grasp or release target and measure how much the head's output changes, relative to four control tokens (random, equidistant, previous target, after-next target). The top 24 are kept.
- **Motion Heads.** Turn each head's cross-frame attention into a displacement field and score its cosine agreement with optical flow. The top 48 are kept; the two sets are disjoint among 720 video heads.
- **HeadLight.** Fine-tune the 24 Next-Interaction video heads. Keep the 48 Motion video heads frozen and fine-tune their paired action heads, so actions follow the predicted motion. That is 113.3M trainable parameters versus 10.0B for full fine-tuning.

## Results
- Unseen RoboTwin tasks: 62.28% on Zero-WAM (Full FT 57.89%, LoRA 58.66%) and 38.17% on LingBot-VA (Full FT 32.61%, LoRA 34.11%).
- Ablating Motion Heads at inference drops success from 57.89% to 0.94%; ablating Next-Interaction Heads drops it to 40.61%. Random heads of the same count drop it to 47.4% and 53.9%.
- Each pathway alone gives about 56%; both together, 62.28%.
- Real Franka, 20 rollouts per task: on unseen tasks HeadLight reaches 68.75% (Zero-WAM) and 72.50% (LingBot-VA), 8.75 and 11.25 points above LoRA.

## Limitations
Finding Next-Interaction Heads needs data annotated with upcoming interaction targets. Finding Motion Heads needs attention from each frame to the previous one, which not every WAM architecture has.
