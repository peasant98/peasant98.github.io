## What they do
VLN-AR lets a small drone follow open-vocabulary instructions such as "head to the black chair, then the front circular pillar, and finally land on the carpet", with all inference on an onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX. A vision-language model decides where to go and a classical planner decides how to fly there. Most training data come from an automated pipeline that flies a simulated drone through 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstructions of real scenes.

## How it works
- **Hierarchy.** A post-trained Qwen3-VL model sees the current frame and 8 history frames (4 s) and outputs a 3D waypoint with yaw, a 2D bounding box or chain-of-thought, plus replan and finish flags. An action expert built on Ego-Planner turns the target into a collision-free trajectory at over 200 Hz; the VLM runs at 2 Hz.
- **Data.** Videos of real indoor and outdoor scenes become 3DGS scenes in Unity. Tasks are sampled from free space and flown by an optimization-based planner, and an MLLM rewrites the instructions. In total about 4 million samples, including VQA, grounding and spatial-reasoning data.
- **Training.** Grounding-and-reasoning SFT, then navigation SFT, then GRPO reinforcement fine-tuning in closed-loop simulation with a sparse success reward.
- **Agent and speed-up.** An agent splits long instructions into subtasks, keeps a memory of frames and trajectories, and rotates 360° or reasons about where to explore when the target is not visible. Instruction-guided pruning keeps 25% of vision tokens, and quantization plus kernel optimizations make decoding 7.1x-9.4x faster.

## Results
- Simulation: 78.15% success on AerialVLN and 76.88% on OpenFly (neither used for training), against 67.37% and 64.67% for the authors' earlier VLA-AN and under 50% for every other baseline, including π0, GR00T N1.5 and Gemini 3 Pro.
- Real world: 91 of 101 tasks succeed on two drones in homes, offices, logistics facilities, parks and shopping districts, with no failures caused by collisions. Robustness tests (dim light, people walking into the path, unseen scenes) succeed 17-19 times out of 20.
- Without the agent, long-horizon success drops from 88.0% to 57.4%. Training on 3DGS data works about as well as training on real-world data (long-horizon 88.0% vs 88.7%, both mixed with diverse data).
- Onboard inference time per step drops from about 4100 ms to 494 ms.

## Limitations
Most real-world failures come from inaccurate goal positions: projecting small or distant targets from 2D to 3D, ordinal instructions such as "the fifth tree from near to far", and ambiguous instructions in scenes with look-alike objects. Motion blur from the RealSense D435 also causes failures.
