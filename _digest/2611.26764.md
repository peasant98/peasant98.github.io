abstract: Egocentric videos offer a scalable source of human demonstrations for robot learning. However, recovering metric object geometry and motion from monocular video is hindered by scale-depth ambiguity and hand-induced occlusion, while image-derived contact and shape priors alone cannot uniquely determine metric scale. We introduce TacHOI, a training-free framework that integrates tactile contact with monocular vision for joint hand-object reconstruction and tracking, without requiring object templates or depth sensors. Starting from pretrained visual estimates, TacHOI uses tactile contact to constrain object depth relative to the estimated hand, while spatially separated contacts on a metrically calibrated hand provide a physical ruler for object extent. It incorporates these constraints into a joint optimization of hand poses, object trajectories, and anisotropic object scale alongside visual alignment. Across 100 ARCTIC and HO3D sequences with synthesized tactile observations, TacHOI achieves the lowest median ADD-S* among the evaluated baselines, reducing error from 15.6 to 3.6 cm on ARCTIC and achieving 2.8 cm on HO3D. Additional experiments on OpenTouch demonstrate its applicability to measured tactile observations.

## What they do
Recovering metric object geometry and motion from monocular egocentric video is ambiguous: an object k times larger and k times farther away projects to the same image, and hands hide much of the object. TacHOI is a training-free optimization that adds touch from a pressure glove. Contact ties the object's depth to the hand, and contacts on a hand of known size measure how wide the object is. It needs no object template or depth sensor.

## How it works
- **Initialization.** Off-the-shelf models give the starting point: HaMeR for MANO hands, SAM 3 and Diffusion-VAS for amodal object masks, SAM 3D Objects for a canonical mesh, Do-As-I-Do for the initial object trajectory and ViPE for camera poses.
- **Joint refinement.** Adam optimizes per-frame object pose, a three-axis scale correction and both hands' MANO parameters against silhouette, mask-attraction and point-track terms, plus penetration and smoothness priors.
- **Touch terms.** When the 16x16 pressure map signals contact, the object surface must reach the hand somewhere among 84 candidate vertices. Contacts on opposite sides of the object act as a caliper on its width, and any two simultaneous contacts set a floor on its size.

## Results
- ARCTIC (55 clips, no depth sensor, synthesized touch): ADD-S* 3.6 cm against 15.6 cm for the best baseline. Error along the camera ray falls from 17.5 to 1.8 cm.
- HO3D (45 clips): lowest ADD-S* at 2.8 cm, though BundleSDF, using sensor depth, has lower centroid error on the 77% of frames it tracks.
- Scale error falls from 12% to 4% (ARCTIC) and from 11% to 5% (HO3D) relative to the SAM 3D prior. Shape gains are small (+2.3 and +3.5 F@5 points).
- Removing the contact terms raises median along-ray error from 0.1 to 0.9 cm on ARCTIC.

## Limitations
The benchmark touch signals are synthesized from ground truth. On real glove data (OpenTouch) the results are only qualitative, and the method often struggles with clutter and fast motion. Object shape comes from SAM 3D and is only rescaled, and rotation error stays high (40-58°).
