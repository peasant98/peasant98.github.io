## What they do
Training tactile policies at scale needs fast simulation of the specific sensors on a hand, but most tactile simulators cover one sensor family or a generic taxel layout. RevoSim simulates both tactile modalities of the BrainCo Revo3 hand in Isaac Lab. These are 285 piezoresistive taxels in eleven pressure regions on the palm and finger pads, plus five visuotactile fingertips. At every step it returns whole-hand pressure, fingertip depth and RGB images (240x320) and the 3D displacement of 100 markers per fingertip. Both modalities are calibrated against real sensor responses, including sensitivity, drift, hysteresis, dead taxels and crosstalk.

## How it works
- **Pressure.** Each taxel's penetration into the object's signed distance field, and how fast that penetration changes, drive a calibrated spring-damper response with its own gain, threshold and saturation. A normal-aware kernel then spreads the response over the curved surface without leaking onto parts that face the other way.
- **Adaptive fingertip depth.** A coarse 32x24 penetration map finds the contact. A fixed 25x40 lattice of rays is then cast only around it, and the result is resampled onto the full 240x320 grid, so the ray count follows the contact rather than the image resolution.
- **RGB and markers.** GPU-Taxim renders the contact on top of a real no-contact image. Marker motion combines a dilation field from the indentation with a shear term that builds up over time.

## Results
- Depth only: 9.2 GiB at 1,024 environments, about 70% less than TacMap's 30.6 GiB.
- Marker displacement: 4,096 environments fit in 31.36 GiB. At that scale FOTS and HydroShear run out of memory, and TacSL already hits the limit at 256.
- Adding pressure to depth costs little memory. With markers and RGB added as well, capacity tops out at 1,024 environments.
- The sim-vs-real comparisons of fingertip images, markers and taxel readouts are qualitative. Ball-rotation and grasping policies are shown without success rates.

## Limitations
The paper has no policy sim-to-real transfer and no quantitative sim-to-real error, and the memory plots measure capacity, not simulation speed. RevoSim models one specific hand. Deformable objects and richer temporal dynamics are left for future work.
