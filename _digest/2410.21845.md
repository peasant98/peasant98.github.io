abstract: Reinforcement learning (RL) holds great promise for enabling autonomous acquisition of complex robotic manipulation skills, but realizing this potential in real-world settings has been challenging. We present a human-in-the-loop vision-based RL system that demonstrates impressive performance on a diverse set of dexterous manipulation tasks, including dynamic manipulation, precision assembly, and dual-arm coordination. Our approach integrates demonstrations and human corrections, efficient RL algorithms, and other system-level design choices to learn policies that achieve near-perfect success rates and fast cycle times within just 1 to 2.5 hours of training. We show that our method significantly outperforms imitation learning baselines and prior RL approaches, with an average 2x improvement in success rate and 1.8x faster execution. Through extensive experiments and analysis, we provide insights into the effectiveness of our approach, demonstrating how it learns robust, adaptive policies for both reactive and predictive control strategies. Our results suggest that RL can indeed learn a wide range of complex vision-based manipulation policies directly in the real world within practical training times. We hope this work will inspire a new generation of learned robotic manipulation techniques, benefiting both industrial applications and research advancements. Videos and code are available at our project website https://hil-serl.github.io/.

## What they do
HIL-SERL trains vision-based manipulation policies with reinforcement learning directly on real robots, for tasks that are hard to learn from scratch: precise insertion, dual-arm assembly, a deformable timing belt, and dynamic skills such as Jenga whipping and pan flipping. The main addition over the earlier SERL system is human corrections during training.

## How it works
- **Learning.** Off-policy RL with RLPD, sampling each batch equally from a demo buffer (20-30 demonstrations) and an online RL buffer. Images are 128x128 crops from wrist and side cameras, encoded by an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-10. A grasp critic trained with DQN picks discrete gripper actions.
- **Reward.** A binary success classifier per task, trained on about 200 positive and 1000 negative teleoperated frames (around 5 minutes of collection, over 95% accuracy).
- **Human corrections.** An operator takes over with a SpaceMouse when the policy gets stuck or fails. Corrections go into both buffers, the policy's own transitions only into the RL buffer.
- **Control.** Proprioception and actions are expressed relative to the end-effector, with randomized start poses. Contact tasks send 6D twists to an impedance controller that bounds the force; dynamic tasks command feedforward wrenches.

## Results
- 100% success on all 13 tasks over 100 trials each (10/10 for the chained IKEA assembly), mostly within 1-2.5 h of training; the timing belt took 6 h. HG-DAgger with the same human data averages 49.7% (BC with more demonstrations for Jenga and flipping).
- Average cycle time is 5.4 s against 9.6 s for the baseline.
- On RAM insertion, dashboard assembly and object flipping, RL from scratch gets 0%. SERL with 200 demonstrations and no corrections averages 49%, and 0% on the dashboard. Diffusion Policy, IBRL, Residual RL and DAPG, each given 200 demonstrations, average 32-57%.
- Policies recover from perturbations: a moving RAM target, a perturbed belt, a gripper forced open.

## Limitations
Untested on much longer-horizon tasks, where sample complexity grows. Little randomization was used and generalization to unstructured environments was not tested. Each task is trained from scratch.
