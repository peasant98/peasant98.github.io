## What they do
A plausible-looking humanoid command sequence can still drop a held object or leave a task unfinished. This paper samples several body-motion and hand command sequences, predicts in latent visual space where each leads, and runs the first commands of the best one before replanning. Tested on ten SIMPLE simulation tasks and seven real Unitree G1 tasks.

## How it works
- **Proposals.** Qwen3-VL-2B encodes the egocentric image and instruction; an Action DiT with flow matching samples 8 command chunks. On the real robot, each command is a 64-dimensional SONIC motion latent plus 14 hand joint targets.
- **Visual progress.** A V-JEPA 2.1 encoder, fine-tuned on 500+ hours of G1 teleoperation in homes, embeds images. An endpoint predictor estimates the features reached after the first 20 commands and scores the predicted reduction in distance to the current subgoal, one of 5 subgoal images extracted from demonstrations.
- **Temporal consistency.** A GRU predicts each next motion and hand command from the state and previous commands; its prediction error penalises inconsistent sequences.
- **Action consistency regularization.** During training, a decoder must recover the first demonstrated command from both observed and predicted feature changes, so the predictor keeps action-relevant information.

## Results
- SIMPLE (ten tasks, 30 trials each): 89.7% success, versus 82.0% for DiT4DiT and 73.7% for Psi-0.
- Real G1 (seven tasks, 10 trials per condition): 85.7% standard and 65.7% with unseen objects, versus 70.0% and 48.6% for GR00T N1.7. Load Washer accounts for most of the gap: 10/10 versus 2/10.
- Ablations on four SIMPLE tasks: action consistency regularization raises success from 55.0% to 65.8%; the joint temporal prior reaches 80.8%, versus 65.8% for visual-only scoring and 70.0% for a second-order smoothness penalty.
- Onboard the G1's Jetson Orin NX, inference runs at 0.73 Hz using 12.84 GB of memory.

## Limitations
Failures include partly opened articulated mechanisms (faucet, bin lid) and losing grip on a soft toy. Scoring depends on task-specific subgoal images from demonstrations, each task trains its own policy, and onboard inference is slow at 0.73 Hz.
