abstract: 'Egocentric human recordings are far cheaper than dexterous robot demonstrations, promising stronger pretraining and direct skill learning without task-specific robot data. Yet humans and robots differ in morphology and kinematics, so human recordings lack the joint positions and actuator commands that supervise robot policies, providing only retargeted end-effector (EEF) actions. This raises two questions: how human data should supervise the policy, and how a dexterous robot should read out actions at deployment. We address both in SHAPE (Supervision from Human Action tokens with Parallel Executable streams), a vision-language-action (VLA) model whose backbone predicts discrete action tokens of EEF chunks as an auxiliary task, while a shared flow-matching expert outputs two executable streams, EEF and joint. ## What they do
Egocentric human recordings are much cheaper than dexterous robot demonstrations, but give only retargeted end-effector (EEF) poses, not the joint commands a robot hand needs. SHAPE, a vision-language-action model for a dual-arm robot with two 22-DoF hands, addresses how human data should supervise the policy and which action space to execute.

## How it works
- **Data.** A Qwen3-VL-4B backbone is pretrained on 2.8k hours of egocentric human recordings and 228 hours of robot teleoperation, sampled 1:1.
- **Discrete supervision.** The backbone predicts FAST tokens of each EEF action chunk for human and robot samples alike, so human data reaches the backbone directly, not only through the action decoder. The tokens are dropped at deployment.
- **Two executable streams.** A shared 0.6B flow-matching expert outputs an EEF stream (wrist pose and fingertips, turned into joint targets by IK) and a joint stream trained on robot data only. An attention mask keeps them independent, so either can drive the robot.

## Results
- Fine-tuned on robot demonstrations, the joint stream scores 0.80 across six real tasks (20 trials each), against 0.63 for π0.5 and 0.51 for GR00T N1.7. The same model pretrained without human data scores 0.64.
- Fine-tuned on human demonstrations only, SHAPE succeeds in 21/60 trials (35%) on three tasks, against 7/60 without FAST pretraining and 0-4/60 with robot-only pretraining. Joint readout never succeeds here (0/240 across models).
- On 24 simulated RoboCasa tasks with the GR-1 humanoid, absent from pretraining, it reaches 72.2% against 64.8% for π0.5.

## Limitations
Human-to-robot transfer reaches 35%; most failures are grasps that close too loosely, from morphology gaps that retargeting does not bridge. Millimeter-tolerance connector reinsertion stays hard (2/20 full reinsertions per connector). Each stream is better in only one regime: joint readout with robot demonstrations, EEF readout with human ones. With robot data alone, FAST tokens raise error on held-out robot tasks.
