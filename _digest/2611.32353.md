tldr: A policy that performs a new task from one demonstration, whether teleoperated, simulated, a human video, UMI or another robot, after translating it into the executor robot's own views and actions; it is pretrained on same-task pairs drawn from 1.2M real trajectories. abstract: Robot in-context learning (ICL) enables a policy to perform unseen tasks by following contextual demonstrations, without task-specific fine-tuning. This is increasingly valuable as large language models reasoning at low frequency can specify tasks through demonstrations and delegate high-frequency execution to such a policy. However, methods conditioned on human videos are bounded by scarce human-robot pairs, while those conditioned on robot demonstrations accept only one or two source types at limited scale. We present Any2Act, a foundation model for robot ICL. Our key insight is that a demonstration should be expressed in the camera layout and action space of the robot that performs it (the executor), not of whoever provided it (the demonstrator). This unified embodiment-grounded representation decouples learning to follow from admitting a new source. Any2Act learns to follow once, from robot-to-robot pairs that already take this form in 1.2M real trajectories spanning 10K tasks. Human videos, simulation, UMI, and other robots are then translated into this representation, and source-routed adapters trained on only a few hundred pairs each absorb the residual gap while the pretrained model stays frozen. For long horizons, a training-free tracker feeds only the demonstration window around the executor's progress, bounding the context and rolling back to redo failed steps. From a single demonstration, Any2Act achieves 57.5% success on unseen tasks without fine-tuning, exceeding VLAs fine-tuned on 50 demonstrations per task; other demonstrators retain 74-91% of the success achieved with the executor's own demonstrations.

## What they do
Any2Act is an in-context policy: given one demonstration of a new task, it performs it on a Galbot G1 dual-arm mobile robot (the executor) without fine-tuning. The demonstration can come from teleoperation on the same robot, a simulator, a human video, a UMI handheld gripper or another robot; each is first converted into the executor's head and wrist views and joint/base actions. Following is learned once, from robot-to-robot pairs (two recordings of the same task in different scenes) drawn from 1.2M real trajectories, 10K hours over 10K tasks.

## How it works
- **Model.** A world action model on the 2B Cosmos-Predict2 video model predicts the frame 2 s ahead; a 0.5B action expert cross-attending to it outputs a 2 s, 30 Hz action chunk, in 180 ms on one H200.
- **Demonstration window.** The policy sees only a few frames and dense proprioception around its current progress, located by a training-free tracker that matches DINOv3 features of the head and wrist views. If an earlier phase matches the scene clearly better, as after a missed grasp, the window rolls back and the step is redone.
- **Translation and adapters.** An LLM agent generates simulated rollouts, human videos are re-enacted on a simulated executor (ego2sim), and UMI and robot motion is remapped. Per-source low-rank adapters, trained on 200 pairs and applied only to demonstration tokens, absorb the remaining gap while the base model stays frozen.

## Results
- Six unseen real tasks, 100 trials each, no fine-tuning: 57.5%, vs 47.5% for GR00T N1.7 and 43.0% for π0.5 (each fine-tuned on 50 demonstrations per task), and 15.7% and 19.7% for the in-context policies HOST and Zero-WAM retrained on the same pairs.
- Six tasks needing skills beyond pretraining, adapted with 20 demonstrations per task: 72.7%, vs 47.5% for GR00T N1.7 with 50.
- Unseen-task success grows with pretraining data: 13.3%, 32.3% and 57.5% at 400, 2,000 and 10,000 hours.
- Other sources with adapters: simulation 59.1%, human video 57.9%, UMI 55.1%, other robots 51.0% and 48.2%, against 65.1% with the executor's own demonstrations.

## Limitations
An LLM agent can pass only one demonstration of the whole task; composing or editing demonstrations is left open. Views and joint trajectories record no force or touch, so a demonstration cannot convey how hard to press in contact-rich tasks such as inserting or wiping. Each new source needs its own translation pipeline and 200 paired trajectories.
