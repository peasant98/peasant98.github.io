abstract: Imitation learning methods need significant human supervision to learn policies robust to changes in object poses, physical disturbances, and visual distractors. Reinforcement learning, on the other hand, can explore the environment autonomously to learn robust behaviors but may require impractical amounts of unsafe real-world data collection. To learn performant, robust policies without the burden of unsafe real-world data collection or extensive human supervision, we propose RialTo, a system for robustifying real-world imitation learning policies via reinforcement learning in "digital twin" simulation environments constructed on the fly from small amounts of real-world data. To enable this real-to-sim-to-real pipeline, RialTo proposes an easy-to-use interface for quickly scanning and constructing digital twins of real-world environments. We also introduce a novel "inverse distillation" procedure for bringing real-world demonstrations into simulated environments for efficient fine-tuning, with minimal human intervention and engineering required. We evaluate RialTo across a variety of robotic manipulation problems in the real world, such as robustly stacking dishes on a rack, placing books on a shelf, and six other tasks. RialTo increases (over 67%) in policy robustness without requiring extensive human data collection. Project website and videos at https://real-to-sim-to-real.github.io/RialTo/

## What they do
Imitation policies trained on a few demos break under clutter and disturbances, and running RL on a real robot is slow and unsafe. RialTo builds a digital twin of the deployment scene, uses sparse-reward RL there to robustify a policy learned from about 15 real demos, and transfers it back to a Franka arm that sees through one depth camera.

## How it works
- **Real-to-sim scenes.** The scene is scanned with Polycam, AR Code or NeRFStudio, and users cut out objects and add joints in a GUI that exports a USD for Isaac Sim. All objects get the same default mass and friction.
- **Inverse distillation.** A point-cloud policy trained on the real demos is rolled out in simulation. Its successful runs become 15 sim demos that also record object and joint poses.
- **RL fine-tuning.** PPO with an added BC loss on those demos trains a state-based policy from a sparse success reward, with robot, object and goal poses randomized.
- **Sim-to-real.** The state policy is distilled into a point-cloud policy with DAgger. Training mixes in the 15 real demos and simulated distractor objects.

## Results
- On book on shelf, RialTo with 15 demos reaches 90/70/60% (pose randomization/distractors/disturbances), against 40/30/20% for BC with 50 demos. The 15 demos take 30 minutes to collect, the 50 take 1 h 45 min.
- Co-training on real rather than sim demos gives 3.5x (book on shelf) and 2x (plate on rack) higher success under disturbances.
- A policy trained on 4 Objaverse drawers opens the real drawer 10% of the time, against 90% when trained on its scanned twin.
- PPO from scratch scores 0% on three of five tasks, against 81-96% when fine-tuned from demos. On drawer opening, RL from point clouds reaches 1% after 35 h, against 96% after 12 h from state.
- Six users built an articulated scene in 25 minutes on average, 14 min 40 s of it active work.

## Limitations
Depth sensing struggles with thin, transparent and reflective objects. The method covers only articulated rigid objects and quasistatic tasks with slow controllers, and physical parameters are never identified. Training one policy takes about 2 days of wall-clock time.
