abstract: 'Egocentric human video offers a scalable source of manipulation priors for robot learning, yet translating raw data scale into generalizable robot policies demands rigorous data curation and training recipes. We introduce Open-HumanWAM, the first fully open recipe for scaling egocentric pretraining to 50,000 hours, anchored by Open-HumanWAM-50K---a curated egocentric video corpus densely annotated with hand motion and natural language. Leveraging this corpus, we systematically investigate pure human pretraining via closed-loop simulation evaluations and uncover an empirical log-linear scaling law linking pretraining data hours to downstream robot success rates. Grounded in these findings, we develop HumanWAM-Vanilla, a world--action model pretrained exclusively on 50k hours of egocentric human video. Upon robot post-training, HumanWAM-Vanilla reaches 92.08% success on RoboTwin 2.0 Clean2Clean and 68.88% on Clean2Random, alongside a 14.17% mean success rate and a 19.00 mean progress score on RoboDojo. To push the capability frontier further, we explore joint human--robot pretraining with HumanWAM-Goal, a visual-goal-conditioned world--action model. Hybrid data scaling unlocks broad zero-shot generalization across both simulation and physical benchmarks, propelling post-trained RoboTwin 2.0 performance to new state-of-the-art levels: 92.70% on Clean2Clean and a substantial leap to 84.60% on Clean2Random. On physical robots, HumanWAM-Goal further exhibits emergent embodied in-context learning, dynamically adapting its manipulation behavior to unseen visual goals without parameter updates. To support reproducible scaling research in embodied AI, we open-source the Open-HumanWAM-50K corpus, data curation and pretraining pipelines, alongside the pretrained and post-trained HumanWAM checkpoints, inference code, and simulation evaluation suites.'

## What they do
An open recipe for pretraining robot policies on egocentric human video. The authors curate Open-HumanWAM-50K (50,000 hours from five public datasets, with hand-motion and language labels) and measure robot success as pretraining grows from 100 to 50,000 hours. Two models result: HumanWAM-Vanilla, pretrained on human video only, and HumanWAM-Goal, co-trained on human and robot data and conditioned on a goal image.

## How it works
- **Curation and alignment.** Clips are filtered for hand visibility, smooth pose tracks and actual manipulation. Wrist poses from every source go into one 16-D bimanual end-effector space: camera frame, canonical wrist axes, and gripper openness taken from the thumb-index distance.
- **HumanWAM-Vanilla.** Wan2.2-5B video model plus an action expert. It predicts future video and actions; with inverse-dynamics attention, actions attend to the predicted future frames.
- **HumanWAM-Goal.** Built on Cosmos3-Nano-16B with a 49-D action space covering joints, base and end-effectors. Trained on 14.6k robot hours plus up to 45.9k human hours, with goal images sampled from future frames.

## Results
- RoboTwin 2.0 success follows SR = a + b log10(hours). From 100 to 50k hours, scene-ood success rises from 49.1% to 73.2% and random from 21.5% to 42.9%. With 1k hours, human pretraining beats 5k hours of robot pretraining.
- Real Cobot Magic, 4 tasks: in-distribution success 57.5% → 87.5%. Under new tablecloth and lighting it goes 2.5% → 67.5%.
- Real Astribot S1, 20 tasks, zero-shot: 20.0% robot-only, 30.5% with 45.9k human hours. A 1:1 robot-human batch ratio raises hard-task success from 19% to 27%.
- After post-training, HumanWAM-Goal reaches 92.70% Clean / 84.60% Random on RoboTwin 2.0, with extra augmentation beyond the scaling recipe.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. HumanWAM-Vanilla trails the strongest RoboDojo entries (14.17% mean success); the authors attribute this to having no memory or reasoning modules. Zero-shot success on hard real tasks stays below 30%. In-context goal following and long-horizon runs are shown only as qualitative rollouts.
