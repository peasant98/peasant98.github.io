abstract: 'Dexterous grasping policies are usually trained for one arm paired with one hand. Every new pairing then needs its own policy, and the number of policies grows with every arm and hand added. We ask whether a single policy can control many arm-hand combinations and transfer to pairings and hands it has never seen. This is hard because robots differ in the number, layout, and dynamics of their joints, so their observations and actions share no common interface. We present DexFormer 2.0, a single reinforcement learning policy for cross-embodiment dexterous grasping. It maps every robot to a canonical joint interface with validity masks, encodes each joint''s morphology with attention biased by the kinematic tree, and uses a Transformer-XL memory to infer embodiment-dependent dynamics from recent interaction. Trained on 18 combinations of four arms and six hands, it reaches 89% stable success on seen combinations and 51% on six held-out arm-hand pairs, outperforming GRU and DexFormer 1.0 baselines. Without fine-tuning, it also grasps with four new hand types and, through retargeting, with parallel grippers. Ablations show that memory, the kinematic-tree bias, and broader pairing coverage each matter for transfer. On a physical Flexiv arm, distilled students transfer zero-shot from simulation with two hands, including a held-out pair. Project website: https://davidlxu.github.io/dexformer2-web/.'

## What they do
Dexterous grasping policies are usually trained for one arm with one hand, so every new pairing needs its own policy. DexFormer 2.0 is a single RL policy trained on 18 of the 24 combinations of four arms (Flexiv, Franka, KUKA, xArm7) and six hands (Revo3, Sharpa, LEAP, Allegro, RAPID, Shadow). It is tested on the six held-out pairs, on four new hand types and on parallel grippers.

## How it works
- **Canonical joint slots.** Every robot is mapped to 7 arm and 22 hand slots with validity masks, so missing joints affect neither actions nor learning. Each joint gets a 16-D descriptor: axis, attachment position, limits, semantic role and kinematic depth.
- **MorphNode encoder.** Arm, hand and mount MLPs are combined with an attention residual over joint and context tokens. The attention logits are biased by kinematic-tree distance and joint role.
- **Memory and training.** A 3-layer Transformer-XL with a 16-step memory infers embodiment-specific dynamics from recent interaction. A shared actor head outputs relative joint targets, and the policy is trained with PPO in Isaac Lab on a mixture of embodiments.
- **Real robot.** The state-based policy is distilled with DAgger into a student that sees depth from two stereo cameras. The student is deployed zero-shot, and a scripted routine then lifts the object to a basket.

## Results
- Simulation: 89.01% stable success on seen pairs and 51.18% on held-out pairs, versus 64.82%/31.28% for GRU and 66.02%/14.15% for DexFormer 1.0. With ten unseen YCB objects, held-out pairs reach 48.85%.
- Removing memory or the tree bias cuts held-out success to 24.45% and 24.15%. Training on 12 instead of 18 pairings, at the same interaction budget, drops it to 5.65%.
- Four unseen hand types average 17.45%. Parallel grippers driven through a virtual LEAP hand reach 7.55-16.62%.
- Real Flexiv arm: 23 of 25 trials with LEAP (a training pair) and 17 of 25 with Revo3 (a held-out pair).

## Limitations
Held-out pairs succeed only about half the time in simulation, and unseen hand types far less often. The gripper results come from retargeting a virtual LEAP hand, which the authors say is not zero-shot transfer to gripper morphology. Real-world tests cover one arm with two hands, and a scripted routine handles the lift and release after the grasp.
