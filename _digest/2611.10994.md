abstract: 'Humanoid robots must coordinate locomotion and manipulation to navigate complex environments and perform everyday tasks. Learning such behaviours from human demonstrations requires accurate whole-body motion paired with egocentric vision, particularly for lower-body movements and foot placements that are frequently invisible from a first-person perspective. However, existing egocentric datasets often lack high-fidelity optical motion capture or systematic coverage of diverse loco-manipulation behaviours. To address this gap, we introduce RoboMoCap, a large-scale multimodal dataset combining egocentric vision with marker-based optical motion capture, specifically designed for locomotion-centric humanoid learning. RoboMoCap comprises over 200 hours of recordings from 14 participants, featuring six head-mounted cameras, wrist-mounted visual and inertial sensing, and optical full-body motion capture at 120 Hz. The dataset systematically covers diverse locomotion primitives, dynamic whole-body movements, and loco-manipulation tasks, including variable-speed walking, turning, constrained navigation, terrain traversal, object transportation, and manipulation during locomotion. It provides temporally aligned multimodal observations and metric whole-body motion representations, enabling the study of human movement beyond directly observable body regions. We systematically evaluate existing methods on three benchmark tasks: egocentric full-body motion reconstruction, foot contact and placement estimation, and human-to-humanoid motion retargeting. By pairing egocentric vision with high-fidelity human motion across diverse locomotion and interaction scenarios, RoboMoCap provides a foundation for egocentric motion understanding and humanoid learning from human demonstrations.'

## What they do
Head-mounted cameras rarely see the wearer's legs and feet, so egocentric datasets give weak supervision for locomotion. RoboMoCap records people in an optical motion-capture studio while they wear a six-camera headset and wrist cameras, so every egocentric frame comes with measured whole-body motion. It holds over 200 hours from 14 participants across 291 predefined tasks, and the authors benchmark existing methods on it.

## How it works
- **Capture.** A RoboCap headset (six synchronized 1080p cameras at 30 Hz, two IMUs), a RoboWrist camera on each wrist, one third-person camera, and a NOKOV system tracking 53 body markers at 120 Hz in a 7 x 7 m area. Scanned objects carry four markers for 6-DoF pose.
- **Alignment.** Headset and mocap clocks are aligned by cross-correlating gyroscope angular speed with that of a marker cluster on the headset; camera poses come from a fixed transform to that cluster.
- **Annotation.** SMPL-X is fit to the markers. Hand poses are vision-based estimates aligned to the SMPL-X wrists and are marked as estimated. Each task has a text description and episode timestamps.
- **Protocol.** Eight action categories; locomotion primitives, posture and dynamics, and loco-manipulation make up about 80.6% of the hours.

## Results
- Quality: 99.81% valid skeleton frames and 4.78 mm median SMPL-X marker error.
- Egocentric body reconstruction with pretrained models: EgoAllo has the lowest MPJPE (80.8 mm) and AGRoL the lowest world-frame error (95.4 mm).
- Feet: EgoAllo is best with 90.5% contact F1, yet its footprint error is 85.1 mm and step displacement error 154.1 mm.
- Retargeting to a 29-DoF Unitree G1 over 543 test sessions: OmniRetarget has the lowest global body error (34.8 mm against 90.7 mm for GMR) but 53.6% clip success; PHC reaches 79.6% and GMR 7.4%. All methods are near joint limits in over 70% of frames.

## Limitations
Everything is recorded in one instrumented studio, which limits environments and terrain. Marker occlusions and body-model fitting errors can affect annotations, and finger motion is estimated from vision. The benchmarks stop at kinematic retargeting; validating the data through downstream humanoid execution is future work.
