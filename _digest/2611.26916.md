## What they do
Egocentric human video is the cheapest manipulation data to scale, but turning it into action labels depends on metric 3D estimates whose accuracy is often unknown. RoboCap-10k is 10,000 curated hours (1.38 M episodes) of unscripted work recorded by employees during their paid shifts in 11 countries, from garment sewing and onion harvesting to PCB soldering and engine repair. Every hour comes from the same device and the same processing stack, and the raw six-camera streams are released with the labels under CC BY 4.0 in LeRobot format.

## How it works
- **Hardware.** The RoboCap is a 250 g hat with six hardware-triggered global-shutter cameras (front and eye fisheye stereo pairs, two lateral wide-angle cameras) at 30 Hz and two IMUs at 200 Hz.
- **Per-session calibration.** Field drift is mostly rotational, so the rotations of all six cameras are re-estimated jointly for every session with the factory translations held fixed. Sessions above 1 px (front) or 1.5 px (side) reprojection error are rejected.
- **Labels.** Multi-camera visual-inertial SLAM with offline bundle adjustment gives gravity-aligned 6-DoF head poses. A feed-forward multi-view ViT predicts one MANO pose per hand that is consistent across all cameras.
- **Language and privacy.** Each episode has one caption plus dense sub-task segments, generated by a VLM whose pipeline was tuned on 1,000 human-reviewed hours. A face detector trained on RoboCap footage blurs bystanders.

## Results
- Coverage: 18 task families, 200 task types and 2,000 tasks; median episode length is 17.8 s.
- Calibration: every device averages below 1 px Sampson error, with a median of 0.62 px on both stereo pairs.
- Head trajectories: median ATE of 3.0 mm and median scale error of 0.17% against motion capture. On the LaMAria benchmark the SLAM scores 72.4, compared with 75.9 for Aria's SLAM.
- Hands: PA-MPJPE of 4.5 mm on a held-out motion-capture test split (9.2 min), down from 8.0 mm for a tracker trained without that dataset's training split.

## Limitations
All 3D labels are estimates, and hand errors concentrate on fingers hidden at contact. Captions are model-generated and were not corrected by hand. India and the Philippines supply about two thirds of the hours, and curation leaves out one-handed work and the hardest tracking conditions. There is no touch or force data, and the paper trains no robot policy on the corpus.
