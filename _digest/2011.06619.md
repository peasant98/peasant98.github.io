tldr: LILI learns a latent embedding of another agent's changing strategy from the ego agent's own observations, plus how that strategy reacts to the ego agent, and trains an RL policy that steers the other agent toward strategies it can exploit. abstract: Seamlessly interacting with humans or robots is hard because these agents are non-stationary. They update their policy in response to the ego agent's behavior, and the ego agent must anticipate these changes to co-adapt. Inspired by humans, we recognize that robots do not need to explicitly model every low-level action another agent will make; instead, we can capture the latent strategy of other agents through high-level representations. We propose a reinforcement learning-based framework for learning latent representations of an agent's policy, where the ego agent identifies the relationship between its behavior and the other agent's future strategy. The ego agent then leverages these latent dynamics to influence the other agent, purposely guiding them towards policies suitable for co-adaptation. Across several simulated domains and a real-world air hockey game, our approach outperforms the alternatives and learns to influence the other agent.

## What they do
The other agent in a repeated interaction (a driver, an air hockey opponent) changes its policy in response to the robot. LILI (Learning and Influencing Latent Intent) skips modeling the other agent's actions. It learns a latent strategy that is fixed within an interaction and changes between interactions, and how the robot's behavior drives that change. Optimizing reward across interactions then makes the robot steer the other agent toward useful strategies.

## How it works
- **Problem.** Each interaction is a hidden-parameter MDP whose dynamics and reward depend on the unobserved strategy z. The next z depends on the current z and the ego agent's trajectory.
- **Representation.** An encoder maps interaction i's (s, a, s', r) tuples to the z of interaction i+1. A decoder reconstructs interaction i+1's transitions and rewards from z, so no strategy labels are needed.
- **Policy.** SAC actor and critic conditioned on state and z. The encoder also gets critic-loss gradients.
- **Influence.** The objective sums discounted reward across interactions, so influence emerges without an extra reward. LILI (no influence) optimizes one interaction at a time.

## Results
- Point Mass: LILI overshoots or undershoots on purpose to keep the hidden target near its start, almost matching an Oracle that sees it. The best baseline, LILAC, moves to the circle's center.
- Lunar Lander, 2D driving and CARLA: LILI beats SAC, SLAC and LILAC and nearly matches the Oracle.
- Air hockey with two Franka arms: over the last 100 interactions a random blocker stops 18% of shots, SAC 44%, LILI 91%. With a bonus for left blocks, LILI gets the striker to shoot left 41% of the time.
- Against an expert human, LILI (no influence) blocks 29/40 shots, SAC 18/40.

## Limitations
Full LILI was too brittle against humans: it blocked with the paddle's edges and missed imperfect shots, and the planned user study did not happen. Strategies must be inferable from a short history; with an 11-interaction history LILI does no better than SAC.
