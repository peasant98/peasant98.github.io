tldr: A single RL policy lets a Unitree G1 humanoid "pivot walk" a 14.9 kg box, rocking it between its bottom corners while stepping, with no box sensing at deployment. In five real-robot trials the box advanced 1.93 m on average before each trial hit the robot's tilt safety stop.
abstract: Through pivot walking, humans can transport objects that are too heavy to lift. Previous humanoid studies have relied on planned sequences in which object manipulation and stepping are executed separately and the object is brought to rest between successive rotations. However, few studies have developed a single learned controller for humanoid pivot walking. In this paper, we propose a single reinforcement-learning policy that jointly coordinates stepping, balance control, and object manipulation. The policy is trained using rewards that promote alternating pivot motions and a phase reference that progresses according to the displacement of the object. In simulation, we confirmed that the humanoid can pivot a box. For deployment, we fine-tuned the policy into an object-blind version that follows a periodic clock instead of the progress reference. On the real robot, the deployed policy transported the box forward by more than 1 m in all five trials; each trial ended at a base-tilt safety stop. These results demonstrate that coordinated humanoid pivot walking can be learned with reinforcement learning and transferred to a physical humanoid robot without direct object-state sensing.

## What they do
A box too heavy to lift can be "pivot walked": rocked onto one bottom corner, swung forward, then rocked onto the other. Earlier humanoid pivoting used planned, quasi-static sequences with stepping and box rotation as separate phases. This paper trains one RL policy for a 29-DoF Unitree G1 that steps, balances and rocks a 14.9 kg box with both hands as a single continuous gait. Lifting that box would put more than twice the G1's 3 kg per-arm payload on each arm.

## How it works
- **Box-level skill rewards.** On top of a forward-progress reward, a yaw-tracking term and a reward for tilting the box between 12 and 30 degrees push the policy toward alternating rocking. In six outcome-only training routes, the policy instead learned to drag or push.
- **Progress-paced reference.** The gait is first discovered at 4 kg with a periodic yaw target. The target is then indexed by the box's actual forward progress (its sign flips every 0.10 m), so it pauses when the box stalls. A mass curriculum then goes 4, 8, 14.9 kg.
- **Object-blind transfer.** Box-state inputs are cut from the actor and replaced by a 1.4 s clock and the robot's yaw. RL then continues with randomized mass, friction, center of mass and PD gains while the critic keeps the privileged box state.
- PPO with 4096 parallel environments in mjlab (MuJoCo Warp).

## Results
- Simulation, 14.9 kg: 4.12 m in 20 s with zero terminations over 128 initial box orientations and 41.2 yaw swings per episode. In a contact-force replay of a posture-refined checkpoint, the support corner alternates 34 times in 20 s, with one corner dominant 99.5% of the time.
- A hand-chosen 4 s clock reference reached only 0.84 m. A from-scratch 1.1 s clock reached 4.83 m but toppled the box 963 times across 256 environments.
- At 24 kg the pivot policy moved the box 0.76-0.81 m with no terminations; failure-free outcome-only policies managed at most 0.33 m.
- Hardware: the gait appeared in 5/5 trials, with an average of 15.6 yaw reversals per trial and a tape-measured advance of 1.93 ± 0.41 m.

## Limitations
Every hardware trial ended 12-22 s after gait onset when base tilt crossed the safety threshold, after which the robot collapsed; the box toppled in 2 of these endings. The object-blind policy cannot sense or correct its drift relative to the box, and no controlled stop or set-down is shown. The contact corner was not measured on hardware, and no joint or IMU data were logged to explain the growing tilt.
