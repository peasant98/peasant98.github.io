abstract: Understanding 4D scenes is a core challenge in visual intelligence, requiring models to jointly capture scene geometry, appearance, and dynamics from partial observations. However, existing approaches are subdivided into feedforward models for direct geometric prediction, and generative models for visual synthesis. We introduce UGG, a unified framework for 4D scene understanding that jointly models RGB video and geometric representations such as depth and camera pose. By assigning different noise levels across modalities and time, UGG formulates visual generation and geometric prediction as conditional completion within a single generative model. This flexible formulation enables the model to learn from diverse, heterogeneous datasets with different subsets of geometric and visual annotations. Furthermore, our generative formulation enables inference-time search over multiple plausible scene hypotheses, using geometric evidence extracted from observed RGB frames to improve geometric prediction without ground truth geometry. We demonstrate UGG's effectiveness over non-unified baselines across camera-controlled video generation, depth estimation, and camera pose prediction, including in challenging sparse observation settings. Code and visualizations are available at https://ugg-model.github.io/

## What they do
Feedforward models predict depth and camera pose from video; generative models synthesize new views. UGG treats both as queries to one generative model of a 4D scene. RGB video, depth and camera pose are three aligned modalities, and whichever ones are observed serve as context while the model denoises the rest. The same weights do camera-controlled video generation, depth estimation and pose estimation.

## How it works
- **Aligned modalities.** Camera pose is encoded as a dense map of Plücker rays, so it is image-like and lines up with RGB and depth tokens. A mixture-of-transformers backbone, initialized from Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, shares self-attention across modalities and keeps separate projections and feed-forward layers for each.
- **Noise as masking.** Diffusion Forcing is extended across modalities and time: observed tokens get zero noise, missing ones are denoised. Losses are masked for missing annotations, so RGB-only, RGB-pose and RGB-depth datasets train one model.
- **Test-time search.** The model samples 16 scene completions and keeps the one that best agrees with RAFT correspondences between the observed frames. No ground-truth geometry is used.

## Results
- Dense pose estimation: lowest ATE and translation RPE of all baselines on all five datasets, e.g. ATE 0.087 against 0.190 for Geo4D on ScanNet++. Sparse pose (first and last frame only): lowest errors on all five.
- Depth: dense Abs Rel of 0.073 on ScanNet++ against 0.106 for Geo4D; in the sparse setting it beats the baselines on 4 of 5 datasets. Geo4D stays ahead on dense Sintel and 7-Scenes.
- Camera-controlled generation from one view: 21.79 dB PSNR on RealEstate10K against 19.58 for SEVA. SEVA is slightly better on two-view DL3DV.
- Test-time search improves sparse depth and sparse pose on all five benchmarks. Removing RGB generation from training raises KITTI depth Abs Rel from 0.152 to 0.322.

## Limitations
Not uniformly better than specialized methods: task-specific view-synthesis models win on pixel-aligned metrics and specialized geometry models on several indoor depth metrics. The search criterion, RGB reprojection error, is only a proxy for accuracy and is not uniformly reliable. Training ran at 288x384 on 50-frame clips, and the experiments are on vision benchmarks, not robots.
