abstract: Predicting long action chunks, often spanning dozens of timesteps, has become a key ingredient in increasingly capable robot policies. In this paper, we find that adapting these long-chunk policies with existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods can require many online samples due to the high dimensionality of the action chunks being optimized, making such adaptation impractical in real-world scenarios where interaction data are costly to collect. Our key insight is to shift adaptation from optimizing actions to optimizing how long to execute each predicted action chunk. This keeps the adaptation space low-dimensional while its expressivity grows with the chunk size. We thus introduce Execution at the Right Length (ExRL), a simple approach which augments the base policy with a lightweight adapter that predicts the execution length of each chunk and is trained using off-policy RL algorithms. Across simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic tasks, ExRL enables sample-efficient adaptation of a diverse range of long-chunk policies with minimal task-specific hyperparameter tuning.

## What they do
Policies such as pi0.5 predict chunks of up to 50 actions. RL methods that adapt them by optimizing whole chunks, or their latent noise, face a search space that grows with the chunk length and need many online samples. ExRL (Execution at the Right Length) leaves the predicted actions alone and learns one discrete decision per query: how many actions of the chunk to execute before asking the policy again.

## How it works
- **Execution-length adapter.** A small network takes the current state and the predicted chunk and picks k in {1, ..., H}. The base policy stays frozen, so the adapter works with any action-chunked policy.
- **Duration-aware off-policy RL.** The critic Q(s, chunk, k) is trained with a TD target whose accumulated reward and discount depend on k, as in a semi-Markov decision process. ExRL-Q acts greedily on the critic; ExRL-SAC adds a categorical actor. Both use a CQL regularizer.
- **Online BC (optional).** Successful rollouts are distilled back into the base policy, so it can improve beyond what execution length alone allows.
- **Offline data.** Any recorded H-step segment is treated as a virtual chunk whose prefixes become replay transitions for different execution lengths.

## Results
- LIBERO: pi0.5 goes from nearly 0% to over 90% success within 200,000 environment steps. On Robomimic Tool Hang with Diffusion Policy, ExRL-SAC exceeds 90% while all other baselines stay around 20% or below.
- Real AgileX Piper arm, frozen pi0.5 with H=50, ExRL-Q against the best of three fixed execution lengths (1, 10, 50): stack cube 17/20 vs 9/20, clothing folding 17/20 vs 12/20, power insertion 22/25 vs 10/25, parts organization 104/125 vs 69/125 stages.
- During training, stacking approaches 100% success after about ten minutes of physical interaction and folding reaches nearly 90% within 85 minutes.
- The adapter replans more near contact: mean execution length roughly halves near the object in simulation and falls to about a quarter on real insertion.

## Limitations
The real-world runs keep the base policy frozen and do not use online BC, so performance is bounded by what the base policy proposes and several tasks stay below 100%. Each task is adapted separately, with no multi-task or unseen-task evaluation. ExRL has not been tested with asynchronous inference such as real-time chunking.
