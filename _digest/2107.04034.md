## What they do
Simulation-trained walking policies break when real terrain, payload or motors differ from the simulator, and collecting real data to adapt risks falls. RMA trains, entirely in simulation, a walking policy plus a module that infers the environment from the robot's recent motion, so a Unitree A1 adapts in fractions of a second with no real-world fine-tuning or reference gaits.

## How it works
- **Base policy.** An MLP maps proprioceptive state, the previous action and an 8-dim latent "extrinsics" vector to 12 target joint angles. An encoder compresses 17 privileged parameters (payload, motor strength, friction, terrain height) into the extrinsics; both train jointly with PPO on fractal terrain in RaiSim.
- **Natural rewards.** Bioenergetics-inspired terms penalize work, ground impact, jerk and foot slip, ramped up by a curriculum so the robot does not learn to stand still.
- **Adaptation module.** A 1-D CNN regresses the extrinsics from the last 0.5 s of states and actions, trained by supervised learning on on-policy rollouts.
- **Deployment.** The module runs at 10 Hz and the base policy at 100 Hz, asynchronously, on board.

## Results
- Simulation: 73.5% success, close to an expert given true extrinsics (76.2%), versus 62.4% for domain randomization, 56.5% for system identification and 52.1% without adaptation.
- Indoors: carries up to 12 kg (its body weight) and steps down 15 cm with 80% success; A1's built-in controller fails on uneven foam and degrades from 8 kg of payload.
- Outdoors: no failures on sand, mud and dirt or in tall vegetation, 70% down trail stairs never seen in training, 80% across cement and pebble piles, 90% across an oily patch.

## Limitations
Proprioception only: sudden drops on stairs or several legs caught on rocks sometimes cause falls, and the authors name vision as the next step. Real-world comparisons use two baselines and 5 trials to spare the hardware.
