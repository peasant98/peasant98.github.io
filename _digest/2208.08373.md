abstract: Dynamic locomotion in rough terrain requires accurate foot placement, collision avoidance, and planning of the underactuated dynamics of the system. Reliably optimizing for such motions and interactions in the presence of imperfect and often incomplete perceptive information is challenging. We present a complete perception, planning, and control pipeline, that can optimize motions for all degrees of freedom of the robot in real-time. To mitigate the numerical challenges posed by the terrain a sequence of convex inequality constraints is extracted as local approximations of foothold feasibility and embedded into an online model predictive controller. Steppability classification, plane segmentation, and a signed distance field are precomputed per elevation map to minimize the computational effort during the optimization. A combination of multiple-shooting, real-time iteration, and a filter-based line-search are used to solve the formulated problem reliably and at high rate. We validate the proposed method in scenarios with gaps, slopes, and stepping stones in simulation and experimentally on the ANYmal quadruped platform, resulting in state-of-the-art dynamic climbing.

## What they do
Dynamic locomotion over gaps, stepping stones and stairs needs precise footholds, leg-terrain collision avoidance and planning of the underactuated torso at the same time. Instead of choosing footholds first and fitting the torso afterwards, this work optimizes the torso and all 12 joints jointly in one nonlinear MPC. To keep the terrain tractable for a gradient-based solver, it is reduced to one local convex foothold region per step plus a signed distance field (SDF).

## How it works
- **Terrain processing (20 Hz).** The 4 cm elevation map is inpainted and filtered, cells are classified as steppable by slope and roughness, and steppable regions are segmented into planes with holes. An SDF is precomputed around the previous plan.
- **Model.** Centroidal torso dynamics evaluated at the actual joint configuration rather than a single rigid body, with joint velocities and contact forces as inputs. A frequency-dependent cost penalizes inputs the series-elastic actuators cannot track.
- **Constraints.** A heuristic foothold is projected onto the best reachable plane, and a convex polygon grown around it becomes linear foot-placement constraints. Joint limits, friction cones and SDF collision spheres on knees and swing feet are soft barrier penalties.
- **Solver.** Multiple shooting with one SQP step per update and a filter line-search that accepts a step if it lowers either cost or constraint violation. A whole-body controller tracks the plan at 400 Hz.

## Results
- Hardware: a ramp, gap and high step in sequence, uneven stepping stones, and indoor and outdoor stairs. An MPC iteration takes 6.6 ms on average (9.8 ms max).
- In simulation, knee collision avoidance is needed to step the hind legs onto a 35 cm box. A single-rigid-body model has a 2.6 cm center-of-mass error at 30° pitch and nearly tips the robot over.
- iLQR diverged on the 0.5 m and 0.75 m stepping-stone scenarios; multiple shooting stayed within 10% of the cost of a solver run to convergence.
- A perceptive RL controller, not trained on stepping stones, fell on the simulated obstacle course this controller crossed.

## Limitations
The gait pattern is fixed externally and reactive behaviors are hardcoded. Foothold regions are chosen per leg and are not consistent over time. Planning is local, not navigation. Thin, tall obstacles can trap the optimizer in a local minimum.
