abstract: 'Existing agentic manipulation systems treat pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) policies as opaque, language-only primitives: when a policy call fails under unfamiliar instructions or scene perturbations, the agent cannot steer its behavior and is forced into futile retries or costly manual takeover. We present InteractVLA, a framework that turns pretrained flow-based VLAs into geometrically steerable and inspectable tools through a bidirectional geometric interface with vision-language agents. In the forward direction, test-time geometric guidance translates an agent-specified end-effector target into input gradients through the action expert via a differentiable command-to-pose mapping, while soft-time target alignment treats arrival time as a latent execution index to match the target pose without distorting the policy''s approach motion. Making the VLA controllable not only boosts online agentic tool use, but also unlocks effective recursive self-improvement (RSI), achieving state-of-the-art performance of 99.4\% on LIBERO-Plus, 48.62\% on RoboDojo, and 89.4\% on RoboCasa365. ## What they do
Agent systems usually call a pretrained VLA with a language instruction only. When a call fails, a retry gives nearly the same motion, and rephrasing pushes the VLA out of distribution. InteractVLA gives the agent a geometric interface instead: it passes the VLA an end-effector target (position, orientation, gripper state) and reads back the action chunk the VLA generates. The VLA is not retrained.

## How it works
- **Test-time geometric guidance.** A differentiable map from actions to end-effector poses lets the pose error be backpropagated through the action expert during flow-matching denoising, steering the chunk toward the target. If a confidence score is low after the first guided step, generation stops and the agent replans.
- **Soft-time target alignment.** The arrival step is not fixed: a log-sum-exp over per-step pose errors spreads guidance over compatible steps and sharpens onto one as denoising proceeds, so the approach motion is preserved.
- **Action-informed refinement.** The agent sees the generated chunk and its predicted poses reprojected into the calibrated camera, then either revises the target and calls the VLA again or edits the motion locally (approach offset, lift height, release timing). Over practice episodes in scenes kept separate from evaluation, the agent also accumulates strategies (recursive self-improvement, RSI).

## Results
- LIBERO-Plus, online tool use (MR-0 VLA, GPT-6.1 Sol agent): 93.1% with language-only calls to 98.3%; under layout shifts 89.5% to 98.4%. With RSI: 99.4%.
- RoboDojo: 18.0% to 26.0% online; with RSI and GPT-6 Astra, 48.62% against 46.85% for the best listed agent. RoboCasa365 with RSI: 89.4% against 55.0% for the base policy.
- Real Dexmal robot, 30 trials per task: task score against a language-only agent rises from 65.5 to 92.5 (tidying), 37.9 to 74.6 (upright bottle), 38.5 to 79.2 (pouring) and 86.7 to 97.6 (move cube, with time down from 165.2 s to 67.5 s).
- Soft-time alignment beats uniform (34.7%) and fixed-final-step (39.1%) alignment on RoboDojo (48.6%).

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. On RoboDojo precision tasks, online tool use drops slightly (9.7% to 8.3%). With an agent in the loop, real tasks take much longer than the standalone VLA (295.8 s against 97.4 s on tidying). The method needs a flow-based VLA, calibrated cameras and a commercial LLM agent.
