abstract: 'Vision-language-action (VLA) policies perform well on fixed-instruction robotic manipulation benchmarks, but their ability to adapt to instruction changes during execution remains understudied. ## What they do
VLA policies are benchmarked on instructions fixed before the episode starts. DTU-Bench asks what happens when the instruction changes while the robot is already acting. Built on LIBERO, it defines 40 task revisions in seven update types (spatial referent, object identity, destination, placement relation, terminal state, subgoal replacement, compound goal), delivered at three execution stages, and evaluates 13 released VLA checkpoints without retraining.

## How it works
- **Protocol.** The policy starts on the original instruction. At a progress event (approach, stable grasp or transport) the pending actions are discarded, the revised instruction is given without a reset, and the robot gets 300 more actions.
- **Controls.** Each scenario is also run as the original task and as the revised task given from the start, over the 50 official LIBERO initial states: 10,000 scheduled trials per configuration.
- **Metrics and diagnostics.** Ever, Held-10 (goal holds for 10 consecutive observations) and Final, plus matched continuations from the same restored state, posture and layout swaps, and a geometry-based reference controller.

## Results
- Runtime Final success is 36.4-68.8% for destination updates, but at most 13.5% for object-identity and 1.2% for spatial-referent updates.
- Success from the start does not carry over: OpenVLA-OFT shows a 96.7-point gap on compound-goal updates.
- Policies often finish the old task first: among successful object updates, DM0.5 completes the original goal first in 95.8% of cases. On terminal-state updates DM0.5 reaches 64.9% Held-10 but 0.22% Final.
- Releasing the object and retreating raises stable-grasp object-update success from 2% to 38% (π0.5) and 8% to 48% (DM0.5), but the released object falls unsupported in 49 of 50 trials per policy, and retreating lowers some destination updates (π0.5 approach: 84% to 64%).

## Limitations
Simulation only. Update categories come from different LIBERO suites, so scene layout and task difficulty also differ between them. The diagnostics use selected tasks and five initial states, and the helper is an external scripted controller rather than learned recovery.
