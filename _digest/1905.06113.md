abstract: With growing numbers of intelligent autonomous systems in human environments, the ability of such systems to perceive, understand and anticipate human behavior becomes increasingly important. Specifically, predicting future positions of dynamic agents and planning considering such predictions are key tasks for self-driving vehicles, service robots and advanced surveillance systems. This paper provides a survey of human motion trajectory prediction. We review, analyze and structure a large selection of work from different communities and propose a taxonomy that categorizes existing methods based on the motion modeling approach and level of contextual information used. We provide an overview of the existing datasets and performance metrics. We discuss limitations of the state of the art and outline directions for further research.

## What they do
A survey of human motion trajectory prediction: ground-level 2D trajectories of pedestrians, plus cyclists and vehicles, for service robots, self-driving vehicles and surveillance. It covers more than 200 methods from robotics, intelligent vehicles and computer vision, proposes a two-axis taxonomy, reviews metrics and datasets, and asks three questions: is evaluation good enough, does the modeling approach still matter, and is prediction solved?

## How it works
- **Modeling approach.** Physics-based methods (sense-predict) forward-simulate explicit dynamics, with one model or several. Pattern-based methods (sense-learn-predict) learn the dynamics from data, sequentially or over whole trajectories. Planning-based methods (sense-reason-predict) reason about goals, with a given cost (forward planning) or a cost learned from observations (inverse planning).
- **Contextual cues**, a separate axis: target-agent cues (motion state, articulated pose, semantic attributes), the dynamic environment (unaware, individual- or group-aware) and the static environment (unaware, obstacle-, map- or semantics-aware).
- **Evaluation.** Geometric metrics (ADE, FDE, DTW, modified Hausdorff, LCS) and probabilistic ones (negative log-likelihood, predicted probability, minimum-of-K ADE/FDE); 12 datasets used by at least two independent teams, plus 4 recent ones.

## Results
- Publications rose sharply in 2018-2019, led by pattern-based methods.
- Reported accuracy on ETH and UCY is 0.19-0.4 m ADE at 4.8 s, against 0.53 m for linear velocity projection; on the harder ATC dataset it is 1.4-2 m at 9 s.
- Q1 and Q2 are answered no: benchmarking has no common practice (differing metric names and formulas, single arbitrary horizons, little robustness testing), and too few studies compare modeling approaches directly.
- Q3: only specific use cases, such as basic emergency braking for intelligent vehicles, are judged solved.

## Limitations
The comparison is qualitative: datasets, metrics and horizons differ across papers, so the accuracy figures are ranges reported by different works, not a common benchmark. Articulated motion, video-frame and action prediction are out of scope. Open problems the authors name: short, noisy trajectories with few interactions in the datasets, rare robustness testing, unclear transfer of learned models to new sites, and planning-based methods that need a given set of goals.
