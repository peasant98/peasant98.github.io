abstract: Humanoid loco-manipulation requires coordinating whole-body motion to perform manipulation tasks in complex environments. Yet egocentric vision-language-action (VLA) policies can miss critical body–scene relationships, and action imitation alone provides no explicit supervision for future scene evolution. We propose BeyondEgo to extend VLA perception in space and time. Spatially, we encode a synchronized exocentric view with frozen DINOv3 and concatenate the resulting tokens with egocentric VLM tokens to condition the action expert. Temporally, we use future observations from both views as privileged supervision for a residual adapter on task-conditioned VLM features. A foresight expert regresses DINOv3 representations of both views 0.6s ahead. At deployment, the foresight expert is discarded and the 4.2M-parameter adapter is retained. We also introduce HELM, to our knowledge, the first closed-loop humanoid loco-manipulation benchmark with synchronized egocentric and exocentric views across five whole-body tasks. On HELM, BeyondEgo improves success from 48.0% to 92.0% in distribution and from 23.5% to 65.0% on 200 held-out scenes with layout, asset, material, and lighting shifts. Ablations show that dual-view DINOv3 foresight yields the best transfer results.

## What they do
A humanoid VLA policy that sees only through its head camera misses body–scene cues: whether its shoulders clear a narrow gap, which branch of a corridor lies behind it, or low obstacles near its feet. BeyondEgo adds a fixed external camera and, during training only, asks the model to predict what both cameras will see 0.6 s later. The authors also build HELM, five simulated pick-carry-place tasks for a 29-DoF Unitree G1 with Dex3 hands, each designed so the deciding cue is outside or ahead of the head view.

## How it works
- **Base policy.** Ψ0's three-level humanoid VLA: Qwen3-VL-2B reads the head image and instruction, a 500M-parameter flow-matching action expert outputs 0.6 s chunks of 36-D whole-body commands at 50 Hz, and the AMO controller maps body commands to leg and waist joints.
- **Spatial extension.** Frozen DINOv3 encodes the external image, and its tokens go straight to the action expert next to the VLM tokens instead of through the VLM.
- **Temporal extension.** A zero-initialized 4.2M-parameter adapter refines the VLM features. A 19.2M-parameter foresight expert reads them and regresses DINOv3 features of both views 0.6 s ahead. At deployment only the adapter remains.
- **Data.** 500 demonstrations (100 per task) planned with cuRobo, simulated in MuJoCo and rendered in Isaac Sim.

## Results
- In distribution (50 trials): 92.0% success, against 48.0% for head-camera-only Ψ0, 74.0% for feeding both views to the VLM and 84.0% for feature fusion without foresight.
- On 200 held-out scenes with layout, asset, material and lighting shifts: 65.0% against 23.5%, 49.5% and 55.0%. Feature fusion is slightly ahead on one task (87.5% vs 85.0%).
- On the back-out-and-turn task, predicting DINOv3 features of both views succeeds in 31 of 40 shifted scenes, versus 24 (head only) and 27 (external only); Qwen3-VL features as targets do worse in every view setting.
- An external camera covering the whole scene gets 77.5% on that task, against 72.5% and 60.0% for side placements.

## Limitations
All results are in simulation, on one robot and five tasks. The method needs a calibrated, fixed external camera with synchronized images, large movements can leave its view, and dual-view demonstrations cost more to collect.
