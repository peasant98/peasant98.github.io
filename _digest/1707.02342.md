## What they do
The paper derives a sampling-based model predictive controller (IT-MPC) from information theory and uses it to race a 1:5 scale AutoRally car around a dirt track. The free energy gives a lower bound on the optimal control cost, and an optimal control distribution attains it. Control then means pushing the applied control distribution toward that optimum. Unlike path integral control, this does not require control-affine dynamics.

## How it works
- **Update law.** Minimizing the KL divergence to the optimal distribution makes the best control sequence its expected value. Importance sampling estimates it by averaging sampled control perturbations, each weighted by its exponentiated negative cost.
- **Practical fixes.** Costs are shifted so the best sample costs 0, and control limits are clamped inside the dynamics. A Savitzky-Golay filter smooths the output. Under 1% of samples are zero-mean noise, so the controller can recover from strong disturbances.
- **Real time.** It runs at 40 Hz with a 2 s horizon, entirely on the car's GTX-750ti GPU.
- **Models and cost.** A 25-term basis-function regression and a 1412-parameter neural network, both fit to 30 minutes of human driving. The cost combines a track map plus an impulse penalty for leaving the track, a speed target and a side-slip penalty.

## Results
- Of 24 settings, 17 reached 100 laps: over 1700 laps and more than 100 km of driving.
- IT-MPC with the neural network succeeded on 100% of laps everywhere except 11 m/s clockwise (76%). At 11 m/s counter-clockwise it lapped in 9.27 s with a top speed of 9.06 m/s.
- The cross-entropy baseline (CEM-MPC) was faster but less reliable. It completed 66.32% of laps at 11 m/s counter-clockwise, and with the basis-function model it hit a barrier on over 50% of trials at 8.5 m/s.
- At high speed the controller slides slightly into turns and counter-steers out of them.

## Limitations
IT-MPC is conservative: at the 8.5 m/s target its top speed stays about 1 m/s below the target. All of its failures with the neural network came at 11 m/s clockwise, where the model predicted over-steer, the car under-steered and it hit the barrier. Neither model captures hidden variables like track condition or battery voltage. Highly unstable systems are hard to sample useful trajectories from.
