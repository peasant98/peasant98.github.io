abstract: Kinodynamic motion planning in large-scale, cluttered environments remains challenging, as dynamically feasible, collision-free trajectories must be computed efficiently over long horizons. Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) can strategically explore long-horizon alternatives but faces large branching factors in continuous control spaces and often relies on random default-policy rollouts, whereas Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) efficiently optimizes local trajectories yet remains susceptible to obstacle-induced local minima. To overcome these challenges, we propose Search-Informed MPPI (SI-MPPI), a unified framework that embeds MPPI trajectory optimization within MCTS-based tree search. The core concept is to fuse the expansion and default-policy simulation stages of MCTS into a single MPPI expansion stage guided by structured intermediate targets, termed baits. During each expansion, an adaptive bait-handling rule retains geometrically valid baits as local targets for GPU-batched MPPI optimizations that generate dynamically feasible candidate motion segments. Feasibility gating then checks these candidates for collision safety and sufficient progress, and top-$k$ selection admits the lowest-cost accepted candidates as tree edges, yielding sparse, directed tree growth; thereafter, effective sample size (ESS)-guided adaptation warm-starts subsequent expansions, while cost-based backpropagation updates tree statistics. Extensive simulations across four large-scale, cluttered 2D occupancy-grid maps demonstrate that SI-MPPI consistently produces the smallest search trees and smoothest angular control profiles compared with three kinodynamic baselines, while maintaining competitive planning times and path lengths. Ablation results further highlight the importance of GPU batching, adaptive bait handling, and retaining multiple expansion candidates for planning efficiency and robustness.

## What they do
SI-MPPI is a long-range planner for a mobile robot on a 2D occupancy grid. It combines Monte Carlo tree search, which decides which routes deserve more effort, with MPPI, a sampling-based optimizer that produces short trajectories that respect the robot's motion model. Every tree edge is a dynamically feasible motion segment, so no boundary-value steering or fixed motion primitives are needed.

## How it works
- **Selection.** UCB descends the tree, with each node's value seeded by the cost of the segment that created it.
- **Bait-guided expansion.** Eight "baits" (intermediate targets) sit on a 5 m forward semicircle. Baits whose robot footprint would collide are pulled inward or dropped, and one GPU batch optimizes an MPPI trajectory toward each remaining bait (1120 samples, 12 s horizon). Near the goal, the goal itself becomes a bait.
- **Admission.** Candidates must be collision-free and make enough progress; at most the three cheapest become child nodes.
- **Adaptation.** Candidate weights warm-start the next MPPI mean, and the expected cost is backed up toward the root.

## Results
Five trials on each of four 100 m maps for a differential-drive robot; all planners reach the goal every time.
- Against kinodynamic MCTS, averaged over the maps: 521 vs 8033 tree nodes and 138.9 vs 177.6 m paths, but 8.38 vs 5.97 s planning time.
- 26.8x and 9.7x less planning time than LQR-RRT* and Informed Kinodynamic RRT*, with paths 14.7% shorter and 2.7% longer, respectively.
- Smoothest steering on every map: RMS angular acceleration 4.8-7.9x lower than MCTS and 22-37x lower than the RRT* planners.
- Ablations: sequential instead of batched MPPI raises planning time from 8.38 to 18.53 s; keeping one child per expansion cuts it to 1.57 s but success falls to 80%.

## Limitations
Simulation only, with a differential-drive model on static, known maps. Paths are not guaranteed shortest: on the Moon-Gate map SI-MPPI's path is about 18% longer than IK-RRT*'s and planning takes 19.98 s. Planned speeds are low (about 0.43 m/s average against a 2 m/s limit), so fast execution needs a separate tracking controller.
