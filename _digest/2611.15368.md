abstract: 'World Action Models (WAMs) can improve robot learning by jointly predicting future visual states and actions, but generating a visual future at every replanning step is expensive and sometimes unnecessary. This raises a critical question: when should a robot policy imagine the future? In this paper, we propose Bind-WAM, a binding-uncertainty-guided WAM for selective future imagination. Bind-WAM introduces language-object binding uncertainty, selectively invoking joint future-action generation when the current observation does not support stable task-relevant action generation. An offline labeling pipeline uses complete demonstrations and action context to identify task-relevant objects and detect reference mismatch, invisibility, and identity drift. The resulting pseudo-labels supervise a lightweight router that predicts binding uncertainty from the current observation and language instruction. At inference, a shared WAM policy invokes selective future imagination according to the predicted uncertainty and its change between consecutive replanning steps. Simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate that Bind-WAM can establish binding uncertainty as a strong, interpretable signal for selective future imagination.'

## What they do
World action models (WAMs) predict future video and actions together, but imagining the future at every replanning step is slow and not always useful. Bind-WAM imagines only when *language-object binding uncertainty* is high, meaning the objects the instruction refers to are hard to identify in the current image.

## How it works
- **Offline labels.** On demonstrations, Qwen3-VL-32B picks out the manipulated object and the reference objects, SAM 3.1 tracks their masks and DINOv3 features check that each object keeps its identity. A frame counts as uncertain if a reference is mismatched, an object is invisible or its identity drifts.
- **Predictor.** A 6.65M-parameter model reads one RGB image and the instruction and predicts this uncertainty. The labeling models are not used at run time.
- **Routing.** One shared WAM (Wan2.2-5B video backbone) has two attention masks. On the direct path, actions attend only to the current frame; on the joint path, they also attend to denoised future video. The joint path runs when the uncertainty or its change since the last replan crosses a threshold.

## Results
- LIBERO: 98.6% mean success (Fast-WAM 97.1%, always-imagining Fast-WAM-Joint 98.4%), with imagination at 40.6% of replans. Completion time is 11.71 s, against 15.73 s for Fast-WAM-Joint (25.6% less).
- RoboTwin 2.0, seven bimanual tasks: 88.3% in clean scenes (AdaWAM 88.4%) and 87.0% in randomized scenes, the highest of the compared methods.
- Real bimanual robot, 3 tasks, 11 trials each: 66.6% average success, against 57.6% for Fast-WAM-Joint, 42.4% for Fast-WAM and 51.5% for Motus, with imagination at 28.4% of replans.
- With the same share of joint steps, uncertainty routing beats random (97.35%) and uniform (97.50%) routing and takes about 20% less time.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. The real-robot test covers three tasks on one platform with 11 trials each. In simulation, success is only 0.2-0.9 points above always imagining; the main gain is speed. The router learns from perception labels, not from whether imagining actually helped the task, which the authors list as future work.
