abstract: Many manipulation tasks require carefully controlled contact forces for successful execution, yet large-scale, force-aware datasets remain scarce. Handheld interfaces can help by transmitting interaction forces directly through the operator's arm, but most existing designs are tailored to simple linear grippers and do not match the nonlinear mechanics of widely used robotic grippers. We present RUMI, a handheld universal manipulation interface matched to the Robotiq 2F-85 with exchangeable fingertip sensors that preserves the target gripper geometry while enabling natural input before contact and force measurement after contact. By integrating the tracking system into a virtual reality headset, our method provides real-time augmented reality feedback about the kinematic feasibility of motion on the target robot platform. We further introduce a haptic feedback mechanism that supports force-aware teleoperation for simulated and real robots. A user study shows fewer episodes containing joint-limit violations with live robot visualization and pose initialization (3/50) than without augmented reality (16/50). We additionally train imitation policies from demonstrations, collected using two different fingertip modalities enabled by the gripper-matched hardware, and assess the haptic interface's capability for collecting force-aware teleoperation datasets in simulation.

## What they do
RUMI is a handheld data-collection gripper that copies the Robotiq 2F-85's nonlinear finger kinematics, finger mount and wrist-camera pose, so demonstrations match a common industrial gripper. The same device serves robot-free collection, with an AR view of the target robot in a Quest 3 headset, and teleoperation of simulated or real robots with haptic feedback from a mechanical blocker.

## How it works
- **Hardware.** A scissor linkage derived from the 2F-85 CAD model couples thumb and index finger to the fingers; an encoder reads the aperture and an index-pad CoinFT sensor measures the operator's squeeze. Fingertips swap between stock Robotiq, CoinFT force/torque and DIGIT tactile. A D405 sits at the robot's wrist-camera pose; an attached Quest 3 controller gives the 6-DoF pose.
- **AR digital shadow.** The IRIS app renders a MuJoCo model of the target robot, registered with QR codes, and solves IK for the tracked pose so the operator sees infeasible poses while recording. A catch-to-teleop step aligns each start pose with the projected end effector.
- **Haptic teleoperation.** Before contact the linkage moves freely and sets the aperture. Once the remote gripper touches something, a micro servo locks the linkage and further squeeze becomes a grasp-force command.

## Results
- User study (5 novices, 50 box pick-and-place episodes per condition): joint-limit violations in 3/50 episodes with AR against 16/50 without, joint-velocity violations in 11/50 against 29/50.
- ACT policies on a Franka FR3, 20 trials each, with against without fingertip sensing: gear assembly 20/20 vs 7/20 and whiteboard wiping 16/20 vs 7/20 (CoinFT); screw tightening 9/20 vs 3/20 and box sorting 17/20 vs 9/20 (DIGIT).
- Simulated packing with the blocker: demonstrators used 5.66 N on a fragile bulb and 22.82 N on a heavy block, and policies succeed 94% and 97%. Without it, bulb demonstrations average 18.46 N and every bulb rollout breaks the 10 N limit; the block still succeeds 99%.

## Limitations
Observations come from the wrist camera only. Beyond the binary blocker cue and controller vibration there is no feedback of external forces. Handheld jitter makes the screw-tightening policy slip. The haptic evaluation is in simulation only.
