abstract: Learned world models summarize an agent's experience to facilitate learning complex behaviors. While learning world models from high-dimensional sensory inputs is becoming feasible through deep learning, there are many potential ways for deriving behaviors from them. We present Dreamer, a reinforcement learning agent that solves long-horizon tasks from images purely by latent imagination. We efficiently learn behaviors by propagating analytic gradients of learned state values back through trajectories imagined in the compact state space of a learned world model. On 20 challenging visual control tasks, Dreamer exceeds existing approaches in data-efficiency, computation time, and final performance.

## What they do
Model-based agents that maximize imagined rewards over a fixed horizon tend to be shortsighted, and many rely on derivative-free planning. Dreamer learns a world model from images and then learns behavior purely inside that model's compact latent space: an actor and a value model are trained on imagined rollouts, and the actor is updated with analytic gradients through the learned dynamics.

## How it works
- **World model.** PlaNet's recurrent state space model (RSSM) encodes 64x64 images and actions into latent states and predicts rewards, trained by image reconstruction with a KL regularizer. Contrastive and reward-only objectives are also tested.
- **Imagination.** Starting from latent states of replayed sequences, the model rolls forward H=15 steps with actions sampled from a tanh-Gaussian policy.
- **Actor critic.** The value model regresses λ-returns, a weighted mix of k-step returns bootstrapped with the value model, so rewards beyond the horizon count. The actor maximizes these values by backpropagating through value, reward and transition predictions (reparameterization; straight-through gradients for discrete actions).
- **Interaction.** Training alternates 100 gradient steps with collecting one episode; one hyperparameter set covers all continuous tasks.

## Results
- 20 DeepMind Control tasks from pixels: average score 823 after 5×10^6 steps, versus 332 for PlaNet and 786 for D4PG after 10^8 steps.
- About 3 h per 10^6 environment steps on one V100, versus 11 h for PlaNet's online planning and the 24 h D4PG uses to reach similar performance.
- At horizon 20, Dreamer beats an action model without values and PlaNet on 16 of 20 tasks, with 4 ties.
- Pixel reconstruction outperforms contrastive estimation on most tasks; reward prediction alone was not sufficient.

## Limitations
Everything is evaluated in simulation. Dreamer trails D4PG on some tasks (Finger Spin 499 vs 986). On Atari and DeepMind Lab it learns successful behaviors only on a subset of tasks, and the authors note that agents learning purely through world models are not yet competitive there. Results depend on the representation objective, which the authors name as the path to visually more complex environments.
