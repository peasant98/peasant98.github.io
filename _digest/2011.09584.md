## What they do
ACRONYM is a dataset for learning 6-DOF parallel-jaw grasping. It labels 2000 grasps on each of 8872 ShapeNetSem objects with the outcome of a physics simulation instead of an analytical quality measure, and includes a generator for cluttered multi-object scenes. To show its value, the authors retrain two existing grasp planners on it without changing the models.

## How it works
- **Objects.** Single-component ShapeNetSem meshes, made watertight, with uniform density and friction 1.0, scaled so the longest bounding-box side is uniformly between 6 and 35 cm.
- **Grasp sampling.** Antipodal sampling with a Franka Panda gripper model at its 8 cm maximum aperture. Rejection sampling keeps 2000 proposals per object that are collision-free and have the object between the fingers.
- **Labeling.** In FleX without gravity, the fingers close until a force threshold, then the hand shakes along its approach direction and rotates. A grasp succeeds if both fingers still touch the object. Compared with labels obtained under gravity, shaking has 91% precision and 93% recall; 59.21% of the 17.744M grasps succeed.
- **Clutter.** Objects are placed on support surfaces of furniture meshes; single-object labels are reused, with grasps that collide with the scene counted as failures. Images are not shipped but rendered with provided pyrender code.

## Results
- On ACRONYM's test objects, area under the success-coverage curve rises from 0.08 to 0.21 (6-DOF GraspNet, c-VAE), 0.14 to 0.26 (c-IMLE) and 0.02 to 0.04 (GPD) when training on ACRONYM instead of the smaller 6-DOF GraspNet dataset.
- On unseen instances of the smaller dataset's own categories, ACRONYM-trained models still score higher: 0.29 vs 0.11 (c-VAE) and 0.41 vs 0.31 (c-IMLE), although ACRONYM training contained no boxes or cylinders.
- Every method scores lower on ACRONYM than on the smaller dataset, which the authors read as headroom for a long-term benchmark.

## Limitations
Labels come purely from simulation. The paper argues for real-world transfer by citing prior work (FleX reached 86% average precision over 2625 real grasps) but runs no robot experiments of its own. Only the Panda parallel-jaw gripper is modeled, and all objects share one density and friction. Clutter labels reuse single-object results and only remove colliding grasps, which the authors note can produce false positives and negatives.
