## What they do
UMI handheld grippers collect demonstrations without a robot, but their moving, close-range fisheye cameras leave scene scale and distances implicit in RGB. Spatial-UMI-Dream is a 5B World Action Model (WAM), built on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video model, that jointly generates future RGB, metric depth and bimanual actions. The authors also build RoboTwin-UMI, a 20-task simulation benchmark with UMI fisheye views that tests transfer across lighting and robot embodiments without fine-tuning.

## How it works
- **Metric depth.** Each GenRobot DAS UMI gripper has three cameras. Two form a stereo pair; Fast FoundationStereo estimates disparity, and the depth is mapped back into the native fisheye view.
- **RGB-D canvas.** Depth is Turbo-colormapped so the pretrained video VAE can encode it, and both grippers' RGB and depth panels are tiled into one 320×320 frame, with no depth-specific branch.
- **Two-stage training on 605 h.** World-only adaptation first predicts future RGB-D. Action and state encoders are then added, and the transformer jointly denoises video and action chunks with flow matching.
- **Deployment.** Demonstrations the Dexmate Vega1 robot cannot execute are filtered out by IK replay in ManiSkill, and gripper commands are compensated for delay with a first-order response model.

## Results
- Real world (Vega1, six tasks, 20 episodes each): 85.0% average success against 60.8% for π0.5; on Bimanual Handover 80.0% against 45.0%.
- RoboTwin-UMI in domain: 79.10% against 74.45% for π0.5 and 61.20% for Fast-WAM. After transfer to the ARX X5 arm the gap over π0.5 widens to 14.00 points; under combined embodiment and lighting shift it scores 72.15% against 61.85%.
- Ablations: an RGB-only variant with matched token count loses 5.75 points in domain, and real-world success drops from 85.0% to 80.8%. World-only adaptation raises simulated success from 39.50% (Wan2.2 without UMI pretraining) to 61.25%, joint pretraining to 79.10%.

## Limitations
The paper does not discuss limitations. Real-world tests use one robot with 20 to 40 demonstrations per task, and the simulated generalization tests vary only light color and two arm embodiments that share the same gripper. Each pretraining stage ran on 24 GPUs.
