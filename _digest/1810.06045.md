However, such hands pose a major challenge for autonomous control, due to the high dimensionality of their configuration space and complex intermittent contact interactions. In this work, we propose deep reinforcement learning (deep RL) as a scalable solution for learning complex, contact rich behaviors with multi-fingered hands. Deep RL provides an end-to-end approach to directly map sensor readings to actions, without the need for task specific models or policy classes. We show that contact-rich manipulation behavior with multi-fingered hands can be learned by directly training with model-free deep RL algorithms in the real world, with minimal additional assumption and without the aid of simulation. We learn a variety of complex behaviors on two different low-cost hardware platforms. ## What they do
Multi-fingered hands are hard to model and control because of their many joints and intermittent contacts. This paper trains neural-network policies for them with model-free deep RL directly in the real world, without a simulator or a model. It uses two low-cost hands: the 3-finger, 9-DoF Dynamixel claw (Dclaw, under $2,500) and the 16-DoF Allegro hand (about $15,000). It also shows that a few human demonstrations speed training up.

## How it works
- **Tasks.** Rotate a valve 180°, flip a box 180° about its long axis, and open a door with the hand mounted on a Sawyer arm. The state is joint angles, object angle, distance to goal and last action; actions are joint position targets.
- **Instrumented objects.** Dynamixel servos attached to the valve, box and door measure the object's angle and reset it between episodes.
- **Learning.** Truncated natural policy gradient from scratch. DAPG instead pretrains with behavior cloning on 20 kinesthetic demonstrations, then adds a weighted term to the policy gradient that pulls toward the demonstrated actions.
- **Design choices** were picked in simulation: position control vibrates least and learns best, and a velocity penalty in the reward slows learning.

## Results
- From scratch, the deterministic policy reaches 100% success over 10 rollouts after 7.4 h (valve), 4.1 h (box) and 15.6 h (door).
- DAPG cuts this to 3.0 h (valve) and 1.5 h (box), and its motions are gentler.
- With the same algorithm and hyperparameters, the Allegro hand learns the valve in time comparable to the Dclaw, and the Dclaw also learns to turn a deformable foam valve.
- In a sim-to-real comparison on the valve, randomizing PID gains and friction was crucial for transfer.

## Limitations
Object state comes from servos built into each object, not from vision, and each skill is learned separately; the authors list both as future work. The door task, rewarded only once the door opens, took 15.6 h. DAPG policies were more robust to the valve's initial angle but less robust to injected action and observation noise than policies trained from scratch.
