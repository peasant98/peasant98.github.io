abstract: Real-time human-robot social interaction requires continuous coordination between speech and physical behavior. Existing systems often separate interaction agents from motion generation, complicating coordination as conversational states change. We propose \textbf{Embodied Interaction Policy (EIP)}, a unified streaming framework that couples an Audio-Interaction expert with a motion expert through layer-wise joint attention. This allows motion generation to directly access the evolving interaction context. A chunk-wise causal mask prevents access to future context, while a vocal token encodes speaking state and the semantic emotion of the response. We construct \textbf{Embodied Interaction Dataset (EI-Data)} from co-speech and dyadic interaction data and train our model in two stages to learn state-adaptive and emotion-conditioned behavior. Combined with the frozen interaction expert, EIP supports continuous robot motion during proactive responses and interruption-aware turn-taking. Quantitative evaluations demonstrate improved motion quality over representative gesture-generation baselines. Compared with a representative cascaded system, EIP achieves higher interaction success and reduces warm-start latency by 64\%. Physical deployment and user studies further demonstrate improved motion naturalness and interaction comfort.

## What they do
A social robot must decide what to say, when to speak and how to move, and switch smoothly between listening, speaking and being interrupted. Existing systems cascade a turn-based or interaction agent with a separate motion generator. EIP merges them: a frozen full-duplex audio interaction model decides whether and how to respond, and a motion expert reads its hidden states to generate upper-body motion for an Astribot S1 on the same 400 ms clock.

## How it works
- **Two experts, joint attention.** The interaction expert is a pretrained Audio-Interaction model (Qwen2.5-3B), kept frozen. A 380M-parameter flow-matching motion expert, inspired by pi0, attends to it layer by layer and outputs 12-frame, 30 Hz chunks for 20 torso, head and arm joints.
- **Causality and emotion.** A chunk-wise mask hides future interaction context and other motion chunks. A vocal token encodes whether the robot is speaking and one of six response emotions, which also modulates motion features through FiLM.
- **Data and training.** EI-Data retargets co-speech (BEAT2, GUAVA, SHOW, with LLM-generated user turns) and dyadic (Seamless Interaction) human motion to the robot: about 60,000 episodes of 20 s. Stage I learns speaking, listening and idle motion; Stage II adds emotion conditioning.
- **Deployment.** Speech is synthesized phrase by phrase and motion only sees text whose audio has played. An MPC collision filter and a Ruckig trajectory generator produce 50 Hz commands.

## Results
- Motion quality in the S1 joint space: FGD 43.83 versus 74.51 for the best baseline (MIBURI retrained on EI-Data); final-layer broadcast (61.21) and a cascade (65.39) are worse.
- Over 20 test samples per scenario: average success 91.7% versus 76.7% for the cascaded variant and 18.3% for RoboGesture; emotion-aware motion 95% versus 55% for the cascade. Warm-start latency is 1.35 s versus 1.99 s and 3.78 s.
- Observers rate the final model 5.98/7 on average versus 3.11 for a vanilla model, and above RoboGesture on naturalness (6.33 vs 3.20). With the physical robot, 12 participants rate it highest on naturalness, comfort and willingness to re-engage.

## Limitations
EIP perceives the partner only through audio, so it ignores gaze, facial expressions and posture. Success is judged by human evaluators on 20 AI-generated speech samples per scenario, motion quality is measured on 20-episode test sets, and the main user studies have 12-16 participants.
