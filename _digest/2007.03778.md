abstract: When a toddler is presented a new toy, their instinctual behaviour is to pick it up and inspect it with their hand and eyes in tandem, clearly searching over its surface to properly understand what they are playing with. At any instance here, touch provides high fidelity localized information while vision provides complementary global context. However, in 3D shape reconstruction, the complementary fusion of visual and haptic modalities remains largely unexplored. In this paper, we study this problem and present an effective chart-based approach to multi-modal shape understanding which encourages a similar fusion vision and touch information. To do so, we introduce a dataset of simulated touch and vision signals from the interaction between a robotic hand and a large array of 3D objects. Our results show that (1) leveraging both vision and touch signals consistently improves single-modality baselines; (2) our approach outperforms alternative modality fusion methods and strongly benefits from the proposed chart-based structure; (3) the reconstruction quality increases with the number of grasps provided; and (4) the touch information not only enhances the reconstruction at the touch site but also extrapolates to its local neighborhood.

## What they do
Vision gives global shape but is blocked by the hand; touch gives exact local geometry but cannot extrapolate. This paper fuses the two for 3D shape reconstruction. A single RGB image and four touch readings from an Allegro hand with DIGIT-like sensors on each fingertip go in; a mesh of the whole object comes out. To train and test it, the authors build a simulated dataset of grasps on 1,732 bottles, knives, cellphones and rifles from 3D Warehouse.

## How it works
- **Charts.** The surface is a set of small independent mesh sheets: 95 vision charts arranged into a closed sphere, plus one touch chart per fingertip.
- **Touch charts.** A U-Net maps each 100x100 touch image to the depth of the impression on the gel, giving a local point cloud; a touch chart is then fitted to it.
- **Fusion.** A graph network deforms all charts three times, with image features pooled onto each vertex. Neighboring vision charts share boundary vertices, and each touch chart's center vertex is linked to all vision-chart boundaries. Successful touch charts are reset to their measured shape after every step, so vision charts fill in around them.
- **Data.** PyBullet places the hand on the object and closes the fingers; touch images are rendered by sphere tracing plus Phong shading, and Blender renders images with and without the hand.

## Results
- Test Chamfer distance: occluded vision 1.074, plus touch 0.991; unoccluded vision 0.861, plus touch 0.804; touch only 3.050.
- On validation, the full model (0.751) beats sphere-based fusion (0.813), no touch-chart copying (0.756) and no chart communication (0.788).
- More grasps help: with vision, 0.804 at 1 grasp to 0.654 at 5; touch only, 3.05 to 1.237.
- Touch also helps beyond the sensor: at the touch site, local error drops from 4.55e-5 to 1.51e-5 with unoccluded vision and stays lower out to five sensor widths.

## Limitations
Charts can overlap poorly, leaving noisy rather than smooth surfaces, and they are not forced to connect; stray charts appeared in 5% of 100 inspected test objects. Training needs full 3D ground truth and testing needs the full hand pose, which the authors call somewhat unrealistic outside simulation. All data is simulated.
