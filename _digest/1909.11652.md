However, many of the more complex behaviors are also notoriously difficult to control: Performing in-hand object manipulation, executing finger gaits to move objects, and exhibiting precise fine motor skills such as writing, all require finely balancing contact forces, breaking and reestablishing contacts repeatedly, and maintaining control of unactuated objects. ## What they do
PDDM (online planning with deep dynamics models) applies model-based RL to dexterous manipulation. It learns a neural-network model of the hand and objects and plans through it with MPC. The tasks are valve turning with a 9-DoF three-fingered hand, plus cube reorientation, handwriting and Baoding-ball rotation with a 24-DoF hand. The Baoding task also runs on a real Shadow Hand, trained only on real-world data.

## How it works
- **Dynamics model.** An ensemble of fully connected networks (2 hidden layers of 500) predicts the change in state. Each member starts from a different random initialization and trains on different batches, which gives an estimate of model uncertainty.
- **Planner.** At each step the planner samples N action sequences over a short horizon (7 steps) and scores them by the reward averaged over the ensemble. It updates the sampling mean with soft reward weighting (factor γ). The noise is filtered so actions stay smooth over time, which also shrinks the search space.
- **Loop.** The robot executes only the first action and then replans. Collected data is added to the dataset and the model is retrained. Because the model is separate from the reward, the reward or goal can be swapped at run time.

## Results
- Ablations on Baoding balls: model capacity and ensembles matter. PDDM's controller greatly outperforms CEM and random shooting, and medium γ values work best.
- On handwriting of arbitrary trajectories and on simulated Baoding balls, only PDDM succeeds. Baoding takes 100,000 data points (2.7 hours of data). With 8 reorientation goals, the model-free baselines get stuck in local optima.
- The simulated Baoding model can be reused without retraining for related tasks, such as moving one ball to a goal or rotating clockwise.
- On the real Shadow Hand (stereo-camera ball tracker, automated reset with a Franka arm), success is about 100% on 90° turns and about 54% on 180° turns, from 4 hours of data.

## Limitations
The paper leaves sparse-reward and long-horizon tasks to future work, along with adding vision and touch to the model. All rewards are hand-designed and dense. The real-world setup depends on an external ball tracker and a reset mechanism.
