abstract: 'While existing methods for reconstructing deformable object manipulation have made impressive progress, they typically represent a thin object with a single particle set that must serve two purposes at once: modeling bending, and resolving contact with the robot. To bridge this gap, we present HermiteTwin, a framework that reconstructs a physical digital twin of a thin deformable object from multiview video of a robot manipulating it. Our key idea is to represent deformation and contact separately, each at its own resolution. The object is simulated as a bicubic Hermite shell, a coarse grid of $C^1$ elements whose nodal positions and derivatives define a smooth surface with Kirchhoff--Love bending, while the gripper acts through dense samples in material space that are captured, clamped, and released, and whose attachment forces reach the shell through the Hermite basis. This decoupling keeps the physical state compact while contact stays fine-grained. We identify effective material and contact parameters with CMA-ES followed by adjoint-based refinement, and drive a reconstructed Gaussian appearance model with the simulated shell. In experiments on real cloth and foam manipulation, HermiteTwin outperforms the state-of-the-art spring--mass twin across reconstruction, resimulation, and future prediction.'

## What they do
Physical digital twins rebuild a deformable object's geometry, dynamics and appearance from video of a robot handling it, so the interaction can be resimulated, continued or rendered under new actions. For thin sheets, PhysTwin's single spring–mass particle set must model both bending and the gripper's grasp. HermiteTwin separates the two: deformation lives on a coarse smooth shell, contact on dense samples in material space.

## How it works
- **Shell.** The sheet is a 20×20 grid of bicubic Hermite elements whose nodes store positions and derivatives, giving a C1 surface with explicit Kirchhoff–Love bending, stepped implicitly with incremental potential contact against gripper and table.
- **Grasp.** About 21,000 material samples (2 mm apart on the cloth) are captured when a gripper pad sweeps over them, clamped to the gripper and later released. Their forces reach the shell through the Hermite basis, so contact is fine while the state stays at 5,292 DOFs. Clamp and release times come from fingertip tactile signals.
- **Identification.** Stiffness scales, attachment stiffness, table friction and damping are fitted to tracked object points over the whole rollout with CMA-ES, then refined with adjoint gradients. First-frame Gaussians move with the simulated shell.

## Results
- Ten Deform360 interactions (eight cloth, two foam): resimulation Chamfer error 42.05 mm² vs 61.89 for PhysTwin and 116.95 for GS-Dynamics; silhouette IoU 88.89% vs 85.22% and 83.24%.
- Predicting held-out cloth frames: 67.75 mm² vs 120.40 (PhysTwin) and 552.40 (GS-Dynamics), better on all five metrics.
- HermiteTwin reproduces a corner folded back over the cloth that both baselines leave nearly flat; giving PhysTwin the same grasp model does not recover the fold.

## Limitations
Each twin is fitted to one recorded interaction, so prediction is only tested on continuations of recorded motions; new actions (draping over a book, dropping into a box) are qualitative. It needs calibrated multiview capture with tracked points and targets sheets parameterized over a single rectangle. Appearance is transported rather than modeled, so wrinkle shading and newly exposed surfaces are not reproduced. Fitting all ten interactions takes about 6.5 h, against 5 h for PhysTwin.
