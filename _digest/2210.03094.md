abstract: Prompt-based learning has emerged as a successful paradigm in natural language processing, where a single general-purpose language model can be instructed to perform any task specified by input prompts. Yet task specification in robotics comes in various forms, such as imitating one-shot demonstrations, following language instructions, and reaching visual goals. They are often considered different tasks and tackled by specialized models. We show that a wide spectrum of robot manipulation tasks can be expressed with multimodal prompts, interleaving textual and visual tokens. Accordingly, we develop a new simulation benchmark that consists of thousands of procedurally-generated tabletop tasks with multimodal prompts, 600K+ expert trajectories for imitation learning, and a four-level evaluation protocol for systematic generalization. We design a transformer-based robot agent, VIMA, that processes these prompts and outputs motor actions autoregressively. VIMA features a recipe that achieves strong model scalability and data efficiency. It outperforms alternative designs in the hardest zero-shot generalization setting by up to $2.9\times$ task success rate given the same training data. With $10\times$ less training data, VIMA still performs $2.7\times$ better than the best competing variant. Code and video demos are available at https://vimalabs.github.io/

## What they do
Robot tasks are usually specified in one format per model: a language instruction, a goal image or a demonstration video. VIMA expresses all of them as one multimodal prompt, an interleaved sequence of text and images, and trains a single transformer to map prompt and interaction history to actions. They also build VIMA-Bench, a Ravens-based tabletop simulator with 17 task templates across six categories (simple manipulation, visual goals, novel concepts, one-shot video imitation, visual constraints, visual reasoning) and four generalization levels: new placements, new combinations, new objects, new tasks.

## How it works
- **Object tokens.** A fine-tuned Mask R-CNN parses every prompt image and observation into objects; each becomes a token from its bounding box and a ViT encoding of its crop. A pre-trained T5-Base encodes the interleaved prompt.
- **Cross-attention controller.** A causal transformer decoder alternates self-attention over the history with cross-attention to the prompt and outputs discretized end-effector poses for pick-and-place and wipe primitives.
- **Training.** Behavior cloning on 650K oracle trajectories (50K per task). Randomly injected false-positive detections make the policy robust to detector errors.
- **Baselines.** Gato-, Flamingo- and GPT-style variants get the same prompt encoder; all are trained at 2M-200M parameters.

## Results
- At 200M parameters VIMA reaches 81.5 / 81.5 / 78.7 / 48.6% success on levels L1-L4; the strongest baseline, VIMA-Gato, gets 58.0 / 53.1 / 46.0 / 16.8%.
- On novel tasks (L4) at 20M parameters VIMA scores 48.8% against 12.3% for VIMA-Gato, the 2.9x headline. Trained on 10% of the data it scores 46.0% on L4; VIMA-Gato trained on the full dataset gets 12.2%.
- Ablations: ground-truth boxes barely beat the Mask R-CNN pipeline; pixel-based tokenizers, or compressing objects into a fixed number of tokens, do worse; cross-attention beats a decoder-only variant mainly at small model sizes and on L4. T5 encoder size (30M-368M) makes no significant difference.

## Limitations
Simulation only, with high-level pick-and-place and wipe primitives rather than low-level control; physical realism was not a focus. VIMA inherits errors from the standalone object detector, which may struggle with occlusion or out-of-distribution object shapes. Success on novel tasks stays below 50% at every model size.
