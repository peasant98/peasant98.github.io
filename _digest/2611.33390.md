abstract: 'Many hard manipulation tasks come down to a spatial constraint that only a few motions satisfy, such as passing a part through a gap barely wider than itself or separating two interlocked parts. These motions depend on the exact geometry of each scene, so demonstrations and skills recorded elsewhere rarely transfer. We therefore ask: when manipulation hinges on spatial constraints, can robots reason out the right motions? ## What they do
Many hard manipulation tasks hinge on a spatial constraint: a gap barely wider than the part, two interlocked pieces, a rope that must not cross itself. RoboARC asks whether robots can reason out the few motions that satisfy such constraints. It packages 90 MuJoCo tasks (78 puzzles in 19 families plus 12 IKEA scenes) in six constraint types (interlocking, narrow passage, assembly, untangling, threading, shape control) on ALOHA 2, a Franka with a Robotiq gripper, or two Frankas, and evaluates coding agents and VLA policies that were never trained on them.

## How it works
- **Two regimes per task.** Privileged gives the full simulator state and unlimited resets; Standard gives only calibrated RGB-D cameras, proprioception and one live episode without resets. Both share scene, physics and grader.
- **Verified tasks.** Each task is a frozen scene with a physics-based grader and a reference solution that replays bit-identically; 46,662 stress episodes found no command that earns a success the task does not grant.
- **Open interfaces.** Coding agents connect through Harbor, VLA policies through a Gymnasium environment served as openpi policy servers.

## Results
- GPT-6 Astra solves 86% (Privileged) and 43% (Standard). The four proprietary agents reach 73-86% and 29-43%; the open-weight agents at most 31% and 7%.
- Four DROID-finetuned VLAs touch the task object in 31-61% of episodes but solve none of 216.
- On 15 puzzles GPT-6 Astra lost in Standard, giving back object poses adds 4 points, part geometry 13, and both together 36 (24% to 60%).
- Only 7% of failed runs pass more than half a task. On a real YAM arm playing a buzz-wire game, GPT-6 Astra covers 94% of the wire over its last four trials against 63% for Claude Opus 5.5.

## Limitations
Everything runs in simulation, with assisted grasps of rigid parts and cloth. Each task ships one reference solution, and the real-robot check covers a single task.
