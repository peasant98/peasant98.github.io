## What they do
This technical report has two parts. The first is a suite of simulated robotics tasks released in OpenAI Gym. A 7-DoF Fetch arm pushes, slides and picks and places objects, and a 24-DoF Shadow Dexterous Hand reaches with its fingertips and reorients a block, an egg or a pen. Every task is goal-conditioned and has a sparse binary reward. The second part is a list of open research problems, most of them about Hindsight Experience Replay (HER).

## How it works
- **Tasks.** The tasks run in MuJoCo at 25 Hz. Fetch actions are 4-D (gripper motion plus open/close), and a goal is a 3-D position reached within 5 cm. Hand actions are 20-D joint positions, and an object goal is a position plus a quaternion, met within 1 cm (5 cm for the pen) and 0.1 rad. Variants range from rotation about one axis to full position plus rotation.
- **Goal API.** A new `gym.GoalEnv` returns `observation`, `desired_goal` and `achieved_goal`. It also exposes the reward function, so the reward can be recomputed for a substituted goal, which HER needs.
- **Baselines.** DDPG with and without HER, each with sparse and dense rewards. Training uses 19 CPU cores and 5 seeds. One hyperparameter set, picked from 40 random samples on HandManipulateBlockRotateZ, is used for every task.

## Results
- All four configurations solve FetchReach. On the other Fetch tasks, DDPG+HER clearly beats the rest within 50 epochs (4.75 million timesteps).
- On the hand tasks (block, egg and pen get 200 epochs, 38 million timesteps), plain DDPG often fails to learn at all. HER learns partly successful policies on every task, but HandManipulatePen is not fully solved.
- With HER, sparse rewards work better than dense ones. The authors' explanation: a sparse return is easier for the critic to learn, and a dense reward pushes the policy toward one particular strategy.

## Limitations
Simulation only, with DDPG variants as the only baselines. The authors note that HER changes the distribution of replayed transitions in an unprincipled way, which could in theory break training in very stochastic environments, though they have not seen this in practice.
