abstract: Dynamic obstacle avoidance is essential for safely deploying humanoid robots in real-world environments, where rapidly approaching objects require immediate evasive responses. Recent advances in reflexive evasion have focused primarily on quadrupeds, which typically rely on large-scale whole-body maneuvers. In contrast, humanoids can exploit their rich body articulation to evade through small, localized body adjustments. By moving threatened body regions without repositioning the entire body, these responses may require less execution time and less surrounding free space. However, local adjustments can alter mass distribution or support conditions, making it challenging to achieve rapid evasion while maintaining whole-body stability. To this end, we propose BARE (Body-Aware Reflexive Evasion), a humanoid reflexive evasion framework guided by human motion priors that enables rapid local evasion while maintaining whole-body stability. A risk-guided motion adapter is designed to select reference motions from a retargeted human evasive motion library based on the threatened body region and to adjust their temporal phases based on collision urgency, allowing the robot to reach evasive postures before potential contact. Conditioned on the adapted reference and link-level collision risk, a reflexive evasion policy learns through reinforcement learning to coordinate local body adjustments with whole-body stabilization. We validate BARE in simulation and real-world experiments, demonstrating its ability to perform targeted local evasion while maintaining whole-body stability.

## What they do
A humanoid can dodge a fast incoming object by moving only the threatened body part, such as raising an arm or lifting a leg, instead of stepping away. Such local moves are quicker and need less free space, but they shift the mass distribution and can cost balance. BARE learns this kind of reflexive evasion on a 29-joint Unitree G1, guided by human evasion motions recovered from video.

## How it works
- **Motion library.** Human evasive motions are recovered from videos with GVHMR and retargeted to the G1 with GMR. Each is labelled as local (raise arm, lift leg) or whole-body displacement (sidestep, retreat), together with the body links it clears.
- **Link-level collision risk.** Each link is approximated by a sphere. Risk combines current clearance, predicted clearance at closest approach under constant velocity, and time to contact.
- **Risk-guided motion adapter.** Once peak risk crosses a threshold, a fixed rule picks a local motion that covers the high-risk links (otherwise a displacement maneuver), using the approach direction. A learned policy then scales the reference's phase rate so the key evasive pose is reached before contact.
- **Reflexive evasion policy.** Trained with PPO, it outputs masked joint residuals around the reference. Rewards cover clearance, reference tracking, locality (few joints moved, little base motion, low power) and stability.

## Results
- Simulation (mjlab, MuJoCo), avoidance success for short / medium / long arrival times (0.1-0.5 s, 0.5-1.5 s, 1.5-3 s): BARE 0.12 / 0.53 / 0.83, REBot (adapted to G1) 0.10 / 0.39 / 0.64, FootstepNet 0.08 / 0.31 / 0.61.
- In successful trials BARE moves its base 0.14-0.16 m at 0.33-0.42 kW peak power, lower than both baselines in every group (REBot moves 0.75-0.89 m).
- Ablations at long arrival times: random motion selection drops success to 0.11, a fixed phase rate to 0.42, and removing the locality reward to 0.72.
- Real G1, attacked with ball-on-rod thrusts and sweeps, kicks, disc throws and fist sweeps: 57% avoidance success.

## Limitations
Obstacle states come from OptiTrack motion capture, not onboard sensing. Success at the shortest arrival times stays low (0.12 in simulation). The authors suggest the drop to 57% on hardware may come from sensing and control latency, actuator response and ground-contact differences. Uneven terrain and constrained footholds are untested.
