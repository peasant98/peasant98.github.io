abstract: 'We present a challenging new benchmark and learning-environment for robot learning: RLBench. The benchmark features 100 completely unique, hand-designed tasks ranging in difficulty, from simple target reaching and door opening, to longer multi-stage tasks, such as opening an oven and placing a tray in it. We provide an array of both proprioceptive observations and visual observations, which include rgb, depth, and segmentation masks from an over-the-shoulder stereo camera and an eye-in-hand monocular camera. Uniquely, each task comes with an infinite supply of demos through the use of motion planners operating on a series of waypoints given during task creation time; enabling an exciting flurry of demonstration-based learning. RLBench has been designed with scalability in mind; new tasks, along with their motion-planned demos, can be easily created and then verified by a series of tools, allowing users to submit their own tasks to the RLBench task repository. This large-scale benchmark aims to accelerate progress in a number of vision-guided manipulation research areas, including: reinforcement learning, imitation learning, multi-task learning, geometric computer vision, and in particular, few-shot learning. With the benchmark''s breadth of tasks and demonstrations, we propose the first large-scale few-shot challenge in robotics. We hope that the scale and diversity of RLBench offers unparalleled research opportunities in the robot learning community and beyond.'

## What they do
Manipulation methods are usually tested on tasks each group builds for itself, or on control benchmarks such as OpenAI Gym and the DeepMind Control Suite, whose tasks the authors call toy problems. RLBench is a shared benchmark and learning environment for vision-guided manipulation, built on V-REP and PyRep: 100 unique tasks, from reaching and door opening to multi-stage tasks such as opening an oven and placing a tray in it.

## How it works
- **Scene.** Every task uses the same Franka Panda on a wooden table. Observations are RGB, depth and segmentation masks from an over-the-shoulder stereo camera and an eye-in-hand camera, plus joint angles, velocities, torques and end-effector pose. No object starts in the gripper, so tool tasks must first grasp the tool.
- **Tasks, variations, episodes.** Each task has one or more variations (e.g. a different target colour), each with text descriptions; unlimited episodes with new object positions can be drawn from each variation.
- **Interface.** A sparse +1 reward on completion only. Action spaces cover absolute or delta joint velocities, positions and torques, and end-effector velocities or poses.
- **Demos and task builder.** A task is a V-REP model file holding the scene and demo waypoints plus a short Python file for variations and success criteria. OMPL motion planning through the waypoints generates demos on demand, and a validator checks that planning rarely fails before a task is contributed.

## Results
The paper runs no experiments or baselines. What it defines:
- A few-shot challenge (v1.0): 10% of the 100 tasks are held out as meta-test; methods train on the rest and report 1-, 5- and 20-shot success on new episodes of the unseen tasks.
- Suggested uses beyond few-shot: RL with demonstrations, imitation learning, multi-task learning, sim-to-real (the Panda can be swapped for another arm with one line of code) and SLAM for manipulation.

## Limitations
Simulation only: the authors do not claim photorealistic rendering or generally realistic physics, though a domain-randomised rendering option is included. No baseline numbers are reported for any task.
