tldr: A sub-$50 gripper fingertip that fuses a dense piezoresistive array with magnets and magnetometers into a 12x32 map of normal and shear force. With it, visuo-tactile diffusion policies reach 91.7% success on test-tube prep, grain scooping and salt pouring, against 45% for vision alone.
abstract: Contact-rich manipulation benefits from tactile feedback that captures both detailed contact geometry and interaction forces. When scooping grains, contact patterns help infer spoon orientation, while resistance transmitted through the grasp guides motion. To provide these complementary sensing capabilities, we present PanoTac, a low-cost, compliant tactile fingertip designed for dense three-axis tactile sensing. Each fingertip provides a $12 \times 32 \times 3$ tactile representation, with one normal ($z$) and two shear ($x$, $y$) response components at each taxel. The sensor operates at 60 Hz and costs less than $50 per fingertip. Sensor characterization quantifies response repeatability and consistency across sensor instances and contact locations. We integrate PanoTac into a robotic system with a visuo-tactile pipeline. Across test-tube preparation, grain scooping, and salt pouring, the resulting policies outperform vision-only baselines by an average of 46.7 percentage points. Ablations show that removing either spatial contact information or shear feedback reduces overall performance across the task suite. Our project page is available at https://panotac.github.io.

## What they do
Piezoresistive skins give dense contact maps but no shear. Magnetic skins give shear but only at a few points. PanoTac stacks the two in one compliant fingertip for a parallel-jaw gripper, producing a dense three-axis tactile image at 60 Hz.

## How it works
- **Hardware.** A 12x32 piezoresistive array (2 mm pitch) sits on TPU tiles over two groups of neodymium magnets, with ten magnetometers at the base. Each group is a 2x2 checkerboard of alternating polarity, whose far field decays as r⁻⁵ instead of r⁻³, which cuts cross-talk between the two fingertips. Costs under $50, about 3 h of printing and 30 min of assembly.
- **Representation.** The normal channel is the contact map scaled by the magnetic z response. The two shear channels are magnetometer x/y readings interpolated onto the same grid, giving 12x32x3 per finger.
- **Learning.** Demonstrations come from VR teleoperation with tactile heat-map and audio feedback. A U-Net diffusion policy takes a wrist camera image, proprioception and both tactile fields through a small CNN.

## Results
- Franka with a Robotiq 2F-85 on three tasks: test-tube preparation (find a tube's orientation in hand and detect stopper release), grain scooping (infer spoon pose and resistance) and salt pouring (tell full from empty by weight).
- Overall success 91.7% vs 45.0% vision-only. Removing either the spatial contact map or the shear channels lowers success.

## Limitations
The magnetometers are sensitive to ferromagnetic objects and external fields. Force resolution is limited by elastomer compliance and magnetometer count. The fingertip has not yet been tried on a dexterous hand.
