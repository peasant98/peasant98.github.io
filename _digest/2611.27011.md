abstract: Vision-language-action (VLA) models have demonstrated strong performance in grasp-based manipulation, yet reliable recovery from local grasping failures remains challenging. Specifically, in pick-and-place tasks, policies may continue transport after a failed grasp instead of retrying, potentially reflecting overreliance on proprioceptive cues for stage progression and limited retry supervision. Existing recovery approaches typically rely on deployment-time guidance or demonstration augmentation. We introduce Force-guided Recovery (FoRe), a framework for autonomous recovery from local grasping failures. Using force feedback, FoRe partitions demonstrations into approach-grasp and transport-placement stages to train separate policies. To supplement limited retry supervision, FoRe then initializes a dedicated retry policy from the approach-grasp policy and refines it through local reinforcement learning (RL) from post-failure states. To avoid inappropriate stage progression, a controller combines force feedback with grasp-command status at inference to coordinate the stage policies, detect grasp failures, and invoke the retry policy. We also construct LIBERO-SUCTION, a force-augmented suction manipulation benchmark adapted from LIBERO, with complete demonstration trajectories for policy training. FoRe consistently improves task success rates for VLA-Adapter and $π_{0.5}$ on LIBERO-SUCTION, LIBERO, and real-world tasks. On LIBERO-SUCTION, average absolute gains are 18.4% and 6.5%, respectively. The corresponding real-world absolute gains are 25.0% and 21.7%. FoRe also remains effective with only one demonstration per task on both simulation benchmarks.

## What they do
VLA policies trained on successful demonstrations often keep transporting after a failed grasp: the arm's pose resembles the transport phase of the demos, and the data contain few retries. FoRe wraps an existing VLA backbone (VLA-Adapter or π0.5) with force feedback so the robot notices a missed or dropped object, grasps it again and resumes the task. The authors also build LIBERO-SUCTION, a 22-task suction-gripper version of LIBERO with force signals and 1,100 demonstrations.

## How it works
- **Stage split.** A force threshold plus the grasp command labels every demonstration step as approach-grasp or transport-placement, and a separate policy is fine-tuned on each (M1, M2).
- **Retry policy.** M3 starts as a copy of M1 and is refined with RL from post-failure states: the arm is set to an early transport pose while the object stays where it was. A lift test (force stays above the no-load baseline while lifting) gives a binary reward, and updates use group-relative advantages over rollouts from the same start.
- **Force-guided switching.** At run time the controller moves from M1 to M2 when force exceeds the threshold during an active grasp, and from M2 to M3 when force falls to the no-load baseline while the grasp command is still on.

## Results
- LIBERO-SUCTION: VLA-Adapter rises from 69.2% to 87.6% and π0.5 from 85.9% to 92.4%.
- LIBERO: 98.6% and 98.3% average, 1.0 and 0.4 points above CycleVLA on the same backbones.
- Real robot (DOBOT CR10AF with a vacuum gripper, 40 trials per task): average success goes from 26.7% to 51.7% (VLA-Adapter) and from 48.3% to 70.0% (π0.5). On long multi-object tasks VLA-Adapter goes from 20.0% to 42.5%.
- With one demonstration per task, gains are 17.7 to 24.1 points on the two simulation benchmarks. Without force in the switching rules, LIBERO-SUCTION success falls from 87.6% to 74.3%.

## Limitations
FoRe only recovers from failures after contact: if the approach-grasp policy never touches the target, the controller cannot advance stages, detect the failure or call the retry policy. The force threshold is chosen separately for each dataset by manual analysis of its force signals.
