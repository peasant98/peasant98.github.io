abstract: 'World Action Models (WAMs) jointly generate future video and robot actions through iterative denoising. Few-step distillation reduces the number of model evaluations, but its coarse updates can move the student away from the teacher''s trajectory. We propose Anchored Path Matching (APM), which supervises endpoint and short-transition predictions from states reached by the student. From each state, we continue the teacher to zero noise and use its endpoint and first intermediate state as targets. Modality-routed adapters learn to match both targets from the same starting state. When the two gradients conflict, anchor-priority updates preserve the endpoint gradient and remove the opposing component of the local gradient within each modality. One checkpoint supports 1-, 2-, and 4-step joint generation. The sampling budget can be chosen at deployment without retraining or teacher queries. On RoboTwin 2.0, we train only on Clean demonstrations and evaluate directly on the domain-randomized Random setting. At one step, APM improves closed-loop success over continued base distillation at matched inference budgets by 2.0 percentage points on Clean and 1.4 on Random. Video peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) rises from 30.169 to 32.317 dB, with gains in structural similarity (SSIM) and learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS). On two real-robot tasks, APM improves success over the one-step teacher and reduces control-cycle latency by $21.3\times$ relative to the eight-step teacher.Project page: https://jialonglong.github.io/Anchored-Path-Matching/.'

## What they do
World action models (WAMs) generate future video and robot actions together by iterative denoising, which is slow: the eight-step LingBot-VA teacher needs 4.3 s per control cycle on the real robot. Anchored Path Matching (APM) distills it into a single student checkpoint that runs at 1, 2 or 4 steps, with the step count chosen at deployment and no teacher needed at inference.

## How it works
- **Stage I.** LingBot-VA is distilled with AnyFlow into a finite-interval transition map that jumps directly from one noise level to a target level. Teacher targets come from eight integration steps.
- **Student-reached states.** Coarse steps drift off the teacher's path, so Stage II rolls out the student at 1, 2 or 4 steps and supervises a state it actually reaches. The teacher continues from that state to zero noise; its endpoint is the anchor target and its first intermediate state the local-transition target.
- **Anchor-priority correction.** The backbone is frozen and separate LoRA adapters for video and action are trained, keeping the base distillation loss. Where the local-transition gradient opposes the anchor gradient, that component is projected out within each modality.

## Results
- RoboTwin 2.0 (50 tasks, 2,500 episodes per condition, trained on Clean data only): APM averages 80.9%, 88.8% and 91.4% success at 1, 2 and 4 steps, against 79.2%, 88.6% and 89.8% for continued base distillation with matched parameters and training steps.
- Single-step video PSNR rises from 30.169 to 32.317 dB over continued distillation; Flash-WAM keeps better SSIM and LPIPS.
- Real robot (24 trials per task): one-step APM succeeds 62.5% on PenHolder and 75.0% on FoldTowel, against 29.2% and 25.0% for the one-step teacher and 83.3% and 70.8% for the eight-step teacher, at 203.1 vs 4323.6 ms per cycle.

## Limitations
Real-robot tests cover two tasks on one platform, with inference on an A800 GPU. At two steps the gain is 0.1 points, with Random success 0.6 points lower. Local-transition supervision without projection does not improve closed-loop success, and projection raises local-transition error.
