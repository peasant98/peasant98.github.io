## What they do
They train a multi-finger hand to rotate an object from any orientation to any goal orientation. The hand faces up, faces down over a table, or faces down with nothing below it. Everything runs on a simulated 24-DoF Shadow Hand in Isaac Gym. The objects are 2282 EGAD and 78 YCB meshes, with 5 random scalings of each. The policies never see the object's shape.

## How it works
- **Teacher.** PPO with 40K parallel environments trains an MLP or GRU policy on privileged state: joint, fingertip and object poses and velocities, plus the goal. It outputs relative joint-target changes at 60 Hz, with domain randomization and observation/action noise.
- **Students.** DAgger distills the teacher into policies that use only real-world-style inputs. One sees joint positions, object position and the rotation to the goal. The other sees a voxelized point cloud from two RGBD cameras plus a goal point cloud, encoded by a sparse 3D IMPALA network.
- **Palm down, in air.** Training this case directly gave 0% success. Episodes instead start from the end states of a separately trained lifting policy. For YCB objects, a gravity curriculum also steps gravity from +1 down to -9.81 m/s².

## Results
- Palm up, RNN teacher: 95.95% on EGAD and 80.40% on YCB. The reduced-state student reaches 91.96% and 81.04%. The palm-up policy learned on its own to toss the object with a spin and catch it at the goal orientation.
- Palm down over a table: 95.31% on EGAD and 81.59% on YCB.
- Palm down in mid-air: 82.27% on EGAD. The gravity curriculum lifts YCB from 52.81% to 74.74%.
- Vision students, trained one per object: over 80% on the non-symmetric toy and mug, and over 50% on symmetric objects.

## Limitations
All results are in simulation; real-robot transfer is argued from domain randomization and torque analysis, not tested. Each vision policy covers a single object, because GPU memory is the bottleneck. Long, small or thin objects cause 60% of palm-up YCB failures. The authors say they cannot fully rule out that the throwing strategy exploits the simulator.
