abstract: We show for the first time that a multilayer perceptron (MLP) can serve as the only scene representation in a real-time SLAM system for a handheld RGB-D camera. Our network is trained in live operation without prior data, building a dense, scene-specific implicit 3D model of occupancy and colour which is also immediately used for tracking. Achieving real-time SLAM via continual training of a neural network against a live image stream requires significant innovation. Our iMAP algorithm uses a keyframe structure and multi-processing computation flow, with dynamic information-guided pixel sampling for speed, with tracking at 10 Hz and global map updating at 2 Hz. The advantages of an implicit MLP over standard dense SLAM techniques include efficient geometry representation with automatic detail control and smooth, plausible filling-in of unobserved regions such as the back surfaces of objects.

## What they do
iMAP is a dense SLAM system for a handheld RGB-D camera. Its only scene representation is an MLP that starts from random weights and is trained live, with no prior data. A tracking process aligns each frame against depth and colour rendered from the network. In parallel, a mapping process keeps training the network on a small set of keyframes that acts as a replay buffer against forgetting. The whole system is written in PyTorch and runs on a single desktop CPU/GPU.

## How it works
- **Scene network.** A NeRF-style MLP (4 hidden layers of 256 units) with a learnable Gaussian Fourier positional embedding maps a 3D point to colour and volume density, without viewing direction. Volume rendering along rays gives depth, colour and depth variance.
- **Losses.** An L1 photometric loss plus a depth loss normalised by the rendered depth variance, minimised with Adam. Tracking optimises only the live pose against the frozen network. Mapping jointly optimises the network and the keyframe poses.
- **Keyframes.** A frame becomes a keyframe when less than 65% of its pixels are explained by a snapshot of the map. Each mapping step uses 5 frames: 3 keyframes sampled by loss, the last keyframe and the live frame.
- **Active sampling.** Only 200 pixels per image are rendered. Each image is split into an 8x8 grid, and samples are moved towards high-loss cells and high-loss keyframes.

## Results
- Tracking runs at 10 Hz and mapping at 2 Hz.
- Replica (8 scenes): average completion ratio of 79.1%, against 75.1% for TSDF fusion given the same poses (11 points higher in office-3). This uses 13 keyframes on average. Accuracy is worse: 4.43 cm against 3.45 cm.
- The map takes 1.04 MB, against 67.10 MB for a 256³ TSDF volume.
- TUM RGB-D tracking error is 2.0-5.8 cm, behind BAD-SLAM (1.1-1.73 cm) and ORB-SLAM2 (0.4-1.6 cm). On real data iMAP fills holes where the depth sensor fails, such as black, reflective and transparent surfaces.

## Limitations
Tracking is less accurate than BAD-SLAM and ORB-SLAM2, and the geometry is less accurate than TSDF fusion. A coarse map forms in seconds, but fine detail takes minutes of training. The authors name more structured, compositional scene representations as future work.
