abstract: 'Agricultural tasks such as harvesting and pruning require robots to reach through dense foliage, where contact is often unavoidable and vision is obstructed. Intermittent contact creates an imbalance between free-space and contact-rich observations, complicating the learning of contact-responsive behaviours. We present SPICE, a tactileconditioned diffusion policy for reaching targets in dense plant canopies using tactile sensing as the sole environmental feedback. SPICE exhibits three behaviours: moving through compliant foliage, manoeuvring around partially obstructing stems, and manipulating branches that block the target path. To the best of our knowledge, SPICE is the first tactile-conditioned diffusion policy for target reaching through physically cluttered plant canopies. Across over 180 real-robot trials, SPICE was evaluated against tactile-conditioned, image-conditioned, and reactive-control baselines. Spatial tactile information and explicit contact conditioning proved important, with SPICE achieving 95% branch-manoeuvring success versus 45% for the strongest tactile baseline. SPICE also achieved branch manipulation beyond the reactive controller and remained effective under visual occlusion, where the image-conditioned DP failed. Zero-shot transfer across 40 trials on unseen tomato and lime plants achieved 87.5% success rate. These results demonstrate the potential of tactile-conditioned generative policies for interaction in visually occluded plant environments.'

## What they do
Reaching fruit or a pruning site inside dense foliage means touching leaves and branches while the camera view is blocked. SPICE is a vision-free diffusion policy for an xArm6 that reaches a target using only tactile feedback from its gripper. One policy, trained on 240 teleoperated demonstrations without behaviour labels, learns three responses: pushing through compliant foliage, moving around a vertical branch, and pushing down a horizontal branch that blocks the path.

## How it works
- **Sensing.** Two Xela uSkin arrays of 4x4 three-axis taxels on the gripper front form one tactile tensor; a small CNN keeps the taxels' spatial layout.
- **Contact signal.** Contact appears in only about 30% of the demonstrations, so an explicit contact-intensity value, zero in free space, joins the conditioning.
- **Policy.** A Diffusion Policy U-Net, conditioned on the tactile embedding, contact signal and current and target joint configurations, predicts 16 joint positions, executes 8 and replans.
- **Data.** GELLO teleoperation on a mock canopy of balsa branches and artificial leaves, with the operator watching a tactile heat map.

## Results
- Branch manoeuvring: 19/20 for SPICE against 8/20 for the best alternative tactile representation; without the contact signal SPICE fails all 10 trials.
- Against the reactive RICE controller, both manoeuvre 30/30, but only SPICE meets the branch-manipulation criterion (20/20). RICE reaches more accurately (1.17 vs 2.78 cm target error).
- Against an image-conditioned Diffusion Policy under occlusion: 19/20 vs 13/20 for plain reaching, 18/20 vs 0/20 for manoeuvring.
- Zero-shot on unseen real plants: 20/20 lime and 15/20 tomato trials; the other five were safety stops.

## Limitations
Demonstrations come from a single operator without haptic feedback. Tactile sensing covers only the end-effector faces, which the authors link to the tomato safety stops, and branches that slid beyond it were sometimes released by hand.
