abstract: Mobile manipulators operating in homes and other complex environments must coordinate their base and arm over long horizons, navigating around obstacles, through narrow passages, and between constrained workspaces. Neural motion planners can provide fast closed-loop planning from partial observations, but scaling them to room-sized environments is difficult because generating high-quality training trajectories is itself a challenging motion-planning problem. To address this, we introduce MoMa-DemoSmith, a demonstration-generation pipeline that separates the problem into global path discovery for large complex scenes and local motion refinement for collision avoidance. This key separation enables the generation of millions of challenging whole-body trajectories across our procedurally generated scenes. We train MoMa-NMP on these demonstrations through behavior cloning (BC) followed by privileged on-policy distillation, achieving 87.0% success on our simulation benchmark and 77.5% across 80 trials in unseen real-world scenes. ## What they do
Moving a mobile manipulator across a room, from inside one shelf or drawer to another, takes long coordinated base-and-arm motion. Neural motion planners replan quickly from partial point clouds, but they need training trajectories that classical planners struggle to produce at room scale. MoMa-DemoSmith generates those trajectories, and MoMa-NMP learns to plan from them.

## How it works
- **Robot and policy.** A TidyBot++ holonomic base carries a Franka arm, a RealSense depth camera and a LiDAR. Given the current and goal end-effector poses relative to the base, the policy outputs five-step chunks of base x, y, yaw and arm-joint offsets.
- **DemoSmith.** Procedural scenes with one to seven tables, shelves, cabinets, drawers and pillars. cuRobo, with a 4 mm collision buffer and base velocity limits relaxed to 6x hardware, finds a global route. Geometric Fabrics then turns the route into higher-clearance action chunks. The dataset holds about 6.7 million trajectories.
- **Training.** Behavior cloning first, with PointNet++ on rendered depth-camera and LiDAR points that include occlusions. Then on-policy distillation: Fabrics, using privileged geometry, corrects the frozen BC policy's chunks to supervise the student.

## Results
- Simulation (9,937 held-out problems): 87.0% success, against 64.8% for BC alone. RRTConnect reaches 85.3%, but with oracle geometry and up to 60 s per plan. cuRobo reaches 46.9%.
- Training on raw cuRobo actions instead of Fabrics targets drops BC success to 47.0%.
- Real world (20 tasks in 10 unseen scenes, replanning at 12 Hz): 62/80 successes, against 23/80 for cuRobo and 17/80 for RRTConnect, which plan once from the initial point cloud.
- Training on rendered partial views instead of complete point clouds raises real success from 54.5% to 79.5% (44 trials each).

## Limitations
All 18 real-world failures were collisions. Many were with chairs, which the table-and-pillar training scenes do not represent, or with small obstacles hidden from both sensors.
