abstract: Pretrained antipodal grasping networks generalize well to canonical shapes but frequently fail zero-shot on novel objects with irregular mass distributions, thin rims, or unfamiliar handles. While episodic experience memory adapts a frozen base policy without retraining, naive retrieval suffers from visual appearance bias, negative transfer from overriding working base grasps, and degenerate closed-loop acquisition. We present VG-GP, a plug-and-play Visuo-Geometric Gaussian Process memory framework for closed-loop $SE(2)$ residual grasp transfer. First, we fuse color-invariant surface-normal DINOv2 features, canonical $SE(2)$ gripper-aligned 3D local heightmaps, and contact-geometry descriptors under a product kernel with Automatic Relevance Determination (ARD) lengthscales learned strictly on source objects. Second, we model the joint posterior over the base grasp and candidate $SE(2)$ alternatives, overriding the base policy only when the joint-covariance Lower Confidence Bound (LCB) clears a risk-sensitive threshold. Third, an online counterfactual curation gate using single-record necessity and sufficiency interventions quarantines source experiences causing negative transfer. Fourth, we close the acquisition loop via Bayesian Expected Improvement (EI) over equal-budget $SE(2)$ probes. Across 42 disjoint YCB objects in ManiSkill3 (20 source, 12 development, 10 held-out test), VG-GP wraps a pretrained GR-ConvNet base policy and raises physical lift rate across all 22 unseen objects from 38.6% (34/88) to 53.4% (47/88) with zero regressions (+13 rescues, 0 losses; object-clustered 95% CI [+6.8%, +22.7%], exact McNemar $p = 0.00024$). On the 10 held-out objects, EI active acquisition reaches 45.0% (18/40 vs. 32.5% base, 5/7 of oracle headroom), achieves 55.0% probe divergence over passive fallback, and monotonically reduces epistemic uncertainty.

## What they do
Pretrained grasp detectors often fail on their first try with unfamiliar objects, such as a hollow mug opening or an off-center hammer head, even when a workable grasp is among their own lower-ranked candidates. VG-GP keeps the detector frozen and adds an episodic memory of grasp outcomes on other objects, which decides when to override the detector's top grasp with one of its alternatives.

## How it works
- **Candidates.** GR-ConvNet proposes up to five distinct top-down (SE(2)) parallel-jaw grasps from an overhead RGB-D image; the highest-scoring one is the base grasp.
- **Features per candidate.** Frozen DINOv2 features computed on a surface-normal and height image instead of RGB (so object color does not dominate), an 8x12 local heightmap in the gripper frame, and 10 contact-geometry descriptors such as jaw clearance and center-gap occupancy.
- **Gated override.** A Gaussian process with a product kernel over the three feature types predicts success for all candidates jointly. It switches away from the base grasp only if the lower confidence bound on the improvement exceeds a threshold.
- **Memory curation and probing.** New source records are admitted only if leave-one-out and add-one tests on source objects show they do not hurt transfer. New probes are chosen by Expected Improvement.

## Results
All in ManiSkill3 simulation with a Franka Panda gripper, on 42 YCB objects split into 20 source, 12 development and 10 held-out objects.
- Across the 22 unseen objects (88 resets), lift success rises from 38.6% to 53.4%, with 13 rescues and 0 regressions (McNemar p = 0.00024). Naive memory baselines cause 11-14 regressions and end at 37.5% and 42.0%.
- Held-out objects: 45.0% vs 32.5% for the base detector, capturing 5 of the 7 rescues a top-5 oracle could make.
- Ablations: without the normal-image DINOv2 features held-out success drops to 37.5%; without the heightmap development success drops from 60.4% to 50.0%; without the confidence gate regressions return.
- Decision overhead is under 20 ms per scene on an RTX 4080.

## Limitations
Simulation only; tests on a real Franka Research 3 are left to future work. Grasps are limited to top-down SE(2), and exact GP inference scales cubically with memory size. Active acquisition adds little on held-out objects: Expected Improvement ends at 45.0%, the same as random probing.
