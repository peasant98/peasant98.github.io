abstract: 'In this work, we tackle the problem of learning universal robotic dexterous grasping from a point cloud observation under a table-top setting. The goal is to grasp and lift up objects in high-quality and diverse ways and generalize across hundreds of categories and even the unseen. Inspired by successful pipelines used in parallel gripper grasping, we split the task into two stages: 1) grasp proposal (pose) generation and 2) goal-conditioned grasp execution. For the first stage, we propose a novel probabilistic model of grasp pose conditioned on the point cloud observation that factorizes rotation from translation and articulation. Trained on our synthesized large-scale dexterous grasp dataset, this model enables us to sample diverse and high-quality dexterous grasp poses for the object point cloud.For the second stage, we propose to replace the motion planning used in parallel gripper grasping with a goal-conditioned grasp policy, due to the complexity involved in dexterous grasping execution. Note that it is very challenging to learn this highly generalizable grasp policy that only takes realistic inputs without oracle states. We thus propose several important innovations, including state canonicalization, object curriculum, and teacher-student distillation. Integrating the two stages, our final pipeline becomes the first to achieve universal generalization for dexterous grasping, demonstrating an average success rate of more than 60% on thousands of object instances, which significantly outperforms all baselines, meanwhile showing only a minimal generalization gap.'

## What they do
UniDexGrasp grasps and lifts arbitrary tabletop objects with a ShadowHand, using only a point cloud and proprioception, in Isaac Gym. Like parallel-gripper pipelines, it works in two stages. First it samples a grasp pose (hand rotation, translation, joint angles) for the observed object. Then a goal-conditioned policy reaches that grasp and lifts the object. The grasp data are 1.12 million synthesized grasps over 5519 objects from 133 categories.

## How it works
- **Grasp proposals.** The pose distribution is split into rotation and the rest. GraspIPDF, an implicit density over SO(3), samples a rotation. The point cloud is rotated into that frame and GraspGlow, a conditional normalizing flow, samples translation and joint angles. ContactNet predicts a target contact map, and the pose is optimized for 300 steps to match it without penetration.
- **Teacher policy.** PPO with oracle object state and a reward for reaching the proposed pose. States are expressed relative to the initial hand yaw (SO(2) canonicalization). Objects follow a curriculum: one object, then one category, three categories, and finally all categories.
- **Student policy.** DAgger distills the teacher into a policy that sees only the scene point cloud from five RGB-D cameras plus proprioception.

## Results
- Proposals reach a Q1 of 0.0423 on seen and 0.0322 on unseen categories, against 0.0269 and 0.0239 for GraspTTA. Their rotation spread is 127.6° against 4.9°. Without the contact optimization, Q1 falls to 0.0013 or lower.
- The state-based teacher succeeds on 74% of training objects, 71% of unseen instances and 66% of unseen categories. PPO, DAPG and ILAD reach 14%, 13% and 25% on training objects. Without the curriculum, the teacher reaches 31% on training objects.
- The vision student with predicted goals scores 66%, 59% and 58% on the same splits. Goals jittered to 1 cm penetration drop it to 37-47%.

## Limitations
Everything runs in simulation, with a floating hand that starts palm-down at a fixed height. The proposal stage uses ground-truth table and object segmentation. Only rigid objects are handled; articulated ones such as scissors are not.
