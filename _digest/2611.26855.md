abstract: 'Flow matching policies underpin the state-of-the-art in robot learning, with impressive results ranging from long-horizon planning to dexterous manipulation. However, they provide no measure of whether their predicted commands can be trusted, particularly in novel situations outside their training set. Epistemic uncertainty reflects uncertainty about the model''s weights and quantifies this ignorance, but calculating it at inference time from a flow matching policy is not trivial. Actions are produced by an ODE solver, requiring uncertainty to be propagated through the entire integration process. BayesDiff addresses this problem for diffusion models by propagating the mean and variance of the state through the denoising chain under a last-layer Laplace posterior approximation, but it severely underestimates the epistemic uncertainty. In this paper we: i) prove that, for flow matching and diffusion ODEs, BayesDiff''s total variance decays at rate $O(1/T)$ as the number of Euler steps $T$ used by the solver increases; ii) introduce Coherent Moment Propagation (CoMP), two recursions inspired by the extended and unscented Kalman filters that fix this problem by carrying the cross-covariance between state and weights; and iii) widen the last-layer posterior with observation embeddings the policy already computes, improving uncertainty estimates without retraining for CoMP and several test-time estimators alike. We demonstrate these benefits on three manipulation benchmarks with applications in failure detection and demonstration selection for fine-tuning, where CoMP corrects BayesDiff''s variance collapse while matching or outperforming several uncertainty estimators.'

## What they do
Flow-matching policies such as π0.5, SmolVLA and Diffusion Policy give no signal of whether an action chunk can be trusted. BayesDiff propagates the mean and variance of the sample through the solver under a last-layer Laplace posterior, but the authors prove its total variance is bounded by O(1/T) in the number of Euler steps T: refining the solver drives the reported uncertainty to zero. CoMP fixes this and runs post hoc on a single pre-trained policy.

## How it works
- **Cross-covariance.** BayesDiff marginalizes the weights at every step, treating them as independent of the state they generate. CoMP draws the weight deviation once and carries the state mean, full covariance and state-weight cross-covariance through the linearized Euler step, giving a variance bound that does not depend on T.
- **Two variants.** CoMP-EKF uses autodiff state Jacobians; CoMP-UKF pushes 2D+1 sigma points through the Euler map.
- **Augmented head.** The last-layer posterior is widened with the image, language and proprioception embeddings the policy already computes, with zero output weights so actions are unchanged and nothing is retrained.

## Results
- Robomimic calibration against a 6000-draw posterior ensemble: BayesDiff recovers 6-23% of the reference variance; both CoMP variants recover it to within 0.2%.
- LIBERO-90 failure detection with π0.5: CoMP-UKF has the highest timestep-wise accuracy (0.643), accuracy (0.794) and lowest normalized detection time (0.474), with a posterior of ≲1% of an ensemble's parameters.
- LIBERO-10 demonstration selection with SmolVLA: CoMP-UKF gains 3.5 pp over random selection, more than a two-member ensemble, using a 75 MB posterior instead of a second 1.2 GB policy. BayesDiff's gain drops from 2.7 to -2.9 pp between 10 and 50 steps; CoMP-UKF stays at 3.3-3.7 pp.

## Limitations
All experiments are in simulation. CoMP needs a linear last layer, and its covariance storage grows quadratically with the action-chunk dimension. It is slow: CoMP-UKF takes 1.85 s per decision and 10.8 GB of peak memory on π0.5, and 3.38 s on SmolVLA, three times VFD's time; CoMP-EKF costs more still. Code is promised after publication.
