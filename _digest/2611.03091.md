## What they do
KlaskTron is an open-hardware robot (under $2,500) for the two-player tabletop game Klask, with an Isaac Lab twin that already transfers PPO policies zero-shot. This paper adds an imitation-learning layer on top: a three-tier demonstration dataset and baselines that are meant to fail in interpretable ways under contact-rich, adversarial dynamics.

## How it works
- **Platform (from prior work).** Two CoreXY gantries move magnetic pegs under the board at 50 Hz, and an overhead camera with an EKF gives a 12D state (both pegs and the ball). Policies output 2D velocities. A learned actuator network, identified on the robot within ±0.2 m/s, replaces the PD controller in simulation.
- **Dataset.** Tier 1 is 80,591 timesteps (27 min) of human play recorded on the robot. Tier 2 mirrors it across the board's symmetry axes (322k). Tier 3 is 5M timesteps of PPO self-play from an expert that itself runs on hardware, used at 80k/250k/1M/5M.
- **Baselines.** An MLP BC policy, GAIL, and PPO with random or BC initialization under sparse or dense reward.

## Results
- BC plateaus: from 80k to 5M steps it stays about 0.015 goal differential below the expert, while held-out R² rises from 0.44 to 0.67. Trained on human data, its pegs drift to the walls.
- GAIL's discriminator ranks peg-ball proximity perfectly (Spearman ρ = -1.0), but its logit spans only 0.00-0.09 and the policy turns passive.
- Under sparse reward, BC initialization cuts peg-in-goal terminations from 34% to 13%; under dense reward it has no detectable effect.
- On hardware, BC-5M shifts less from its simulated behavior than the PPO expert (normalized W1 0.05 vs 0.15) but engages the ball 31% less often.

## Limitations
State input only, with no image dataset, and the game's magnetic obstacles are left out. Human action labels are finite-differenced positions, so velocity inputs must be zeroed to avoid leakage. Only one BC policy has been run on hardware. GAIL and BC+RL command up to ±1.0 m/s, five times the actuator model's range, and real peg speed is about half the simulated value.
