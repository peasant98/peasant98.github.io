abstract: Humanoid motion generation must accommodate evolving task requirements, where localized adjustments should satisfy new geometric constraints without disrupting desired motion details. However, language-based control lacks geometric precision, while joint-level constraints require explicit spatial targets. Local geometric constraints are also underdetermined, complicating target satisfaction and source preservation. We present AGLR (Attribute-Guided Latent Refinement), a unified framework for fine-grained humanoid motion generation and editing through source-anchored refinement. A compact geometric interface supports composable, time-varying control of limb extents, torso orientation, and root trajectories. Given a generated or encoded base latent, an Attribute Residual Adapter predicts a single-pass correction, with dual-level supervision enforcing target matching and selective preservation across attributes and motion coordinates. On HumanML3D-G1, AGLR achieves the highest controllable-generation and preservation-constrained editing success rates among evaluated methods. Editing take only 9.1 ms for a cached 500-frame motion, without additional sampling or test-time optimization. Zero-shot evaluation on BONES-SEED-G1 further demonstrates transfer without fine-tuning.

## What they do
Text-to-motion models give humanoids reference motions, but many tasks need only a local change: raise one arm while walking, lean the torso, bend the path. Language is imprecise for that, and joint-level targets demand more detail than the task supplies. AGLR edits a generated or existing G1 motion through 21 per-frame geometric attributes and changes only the selected ones. A tracking policy then executes the edited reference on the robot.

## How it works
- **Robot-native prior.** HumanML3D motions are retargeted to the Unitree G1 and tracked in simulation by a privileged policy. A VAE and a text-conditioned latent flow model are trained on these rollouts, and the flow model is distilled to four steps with DMD2.
- **Geometric attributes.** How far each arm and leg extends front, out and up (normalized by limb length), torso tilt and heading relative to the pelvis, and root displacement, height and orientation. A mask selects channels, and targets can vary over time.
- **Attribute Residual Adapter.** A transformer adds one correction to the base latent. Losses pull selected attributes to their targets, keep unselected attributes and unrelated joint coordinates at their source values, and penalize joint-limit violations.

## Results
- Controllable generation on HumanML3D-G1: 80.34% attribute success rate, versus 73.66% for ProjFlow, in 90 ms instead of 13.6 s.
- Editing: 76.81% and 77.28% success under attribute- and joint-drift limits, versus 63.80% and 65.64% for TRACE. It takes 13.15 ms end-to-end, about 40,000× faster than RG-DNO.
- With both preservation losses dropped, generation success rises to 85.55% but editing success falls to 7.79%.
- Zero-shot on BONES-SEED-G1 it again has the best generation and editing success. On a real G1, a MimicLite tracking policy executes limb, torso, time-varying and root-trajectory edits.

## Limitations
Training and testing use clips of at most 500 frames (about 10 s); longer motions are untested, and because the adapter processes the whole sequence at once it cannot edit a stream. Real-robot results are qualitative frame sequences from three base motions, with no tracking metrics. RG-DNO reaches lower target error (0.1008 vs 0.1431), though with far larger drift.
