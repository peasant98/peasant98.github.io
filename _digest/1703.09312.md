abstract: To reduce data collection time for deep learning of robust robotic grasp plans, we explore training from a synthetic dataset of 6.7 million point clouds, grasps, and analytic grasp metrics generated from thousands of 3D models from Dex-Net 1.0 in randomized poses on a table. We use the resulting dataset, Dex-Net 2.0, to train a Grasp Quality Convolutional Neural Network (GQ-CNN) model that rapidly predicts the probability of success of grasps from depth images, where grasps are specified as the planar position, angle, and depth of a gripper relative to an RGB-D sensor. Experiments with over 1,000 trials on an ABB YuMi comparing grasp planning methods on singulated objects suggest that a GQ-CNN trained with only synthetic data from Dex-Net 2.0 can be used to plan grasps in 0.8sec with a success rate of 93% on eight known objects with adversarial geometry and is 3x faster than registering point clouds to a precomputed dataset of objects and indexing grasps. The Dex-Net 2.0 grasp planner also has the highest success rate on a dataset of 10 novel rigid objects and achieves 99% precision (one false positive out of 69 grasps classified as robust) on a dataset of 40 novel household objects, some of which are articulated or deformable. Code, datasets, videos, and supplementary material are available at http://berkeleyautomation.github.io/dex-net .

## What they do
Learning grasps from human labels or physical trials takes tedious labeling or months of robot time. Dex-Net 2.0 instead renders 6.7 million synthetic depth images of 1,500 3D models, labels parallel-jaw grasps with a robust analytic metric, and trains a Grasp Quality CNN (GQ-CNN) that predicts grasp success directly from a depth image.

## How it works
- **Dataset.** Meshes from Dex-Net 1.0 (3DNet and KIT) are placed in stable poses with random planar pose, camera pose and friction. Up to 100 antipodal grasps per object are scored with robust epsilon quality under pose and friction uncertainty. A grasp is labeled positive if it clears a threshold and is collision-free (about 21% of the data).
- **GQ-CNN.** Input is a depth image centered on the grasp and rotated to its axis, plus the gripper's depth. Depth noise (multiplicative Gamma plus Gaussian Process) is resampled during training.
- **Planning.** Sample antipodal candidates from depth-image gradients, score each with the GQ-CNN, and execute the most robust grasp that is reachable and does not hit the table.

## Results
- Known objects (8 adversarial 3D-printed parts, 80 trials): the model finetuned on synthetic images of these parts reaches 93% success and 94% precision in 0.8 s. Point-cloud registration reaches 95% but takes 2.6 s. Pretraining on a 150-model subset instead of the full dataset gives 85%.
- Novel objects (10 household items, 50 trials): 80% success and 100% precision (29 grasps rated robust, no false positives), versus 52% for registration and 60% for an image-based heuristic.
- 40 novel objects, some articulated or deformable, with cross-entropy-method planning: 94% success and 99% precision (68 of 69), at 2.5 s per plan.
## Limitations
The method only handles singulated rigid objects on a table, seen from one overhead depth view, with planar grasps. The order-fulfillment demo needed a separate pushing policy to break up piles. Failures came from the depth sensor missing thin parts and from grasps in narrow openings that collide with the object but were rated robust.
