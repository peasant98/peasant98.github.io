abstract: Many rehabilitation robots execute prescribed motions using reactive tracking controllers that do not explicitly incorporate the user's instantaneous human-ground interaction. This paper presents Ground Reaction Force-Aware Model Predictive Control (GRF-MPC), a framework for upright physical assistance. By integrating live GRF and center of pressure (CoP) measurements directly into a human centroidal dynamics model, GRF-MPC formulates a real-time quadratic program (QP) that optimizes assistive forces and moments over a receding horizon. We deployed GRF-MPC on the Robotic Upright Stand Trainer (RobUST) in an underactuated 4-cable configuration and evaluated it against a fully actuated 8-cable Cartesian impedance baseline in nearly 300 trials across six healthy participants. Across tasks spanning impulsive perturbations, lunge trajectories, and voluntary limits of stability, GRF-MPC reduced peak planar center-of-mass (CoM) error by 19.6% during perturbations, lowered trajectory tracking error by 24.1%, required 28.7% less net mechanical work, and permitted a 45.9% greater normalized voluntary excursion area. The underlying QP solves in under 1 ms, comfortably enabling 100 Hz replanning and demonstrating that incorporating live human-ground interaction into predictive control yields stronger, more transparent physical assistance with half the actuators.

## What they do
Robots that support a person standing or moving upright usually track a reference with impedance control: they react to position errors but ignore the forces the person is putting into the ground. GRF-MPC measures those ground reaction forces (GRF) and the center of pressure with force plates and puts them into a centroidal-dynamics model of the person, so a model-predictive controller can plan assistance ahead. It runs on RobUST, a frame-mounted cable-driven trainer pulling on a chest belt, with only 4 cables.

## How it works
- **Human model.** The person is one rigid body: center of mass (CoM) from a Vive tracker on the sacrum, inertia from a cylinder sized by mass, height and shoulder width. Measured GRF and CoP enter as an external force, held constant over the horizon.
- **Convex MPC.** Linearizing at the current state gives a single QP over cable tensions with hard 10-200 N limits. Horizon 0.5 s, replanned at 100 Hz; the QP solves in under 1 ms.
- **Low level.** A 1 kHz PID loop tracks the commanded tensions from load cells.
- **Baselines.** An 8-cable Cartesian impedance controller (8 cables are needed for force closure) and a transparent mode with 10 N per cable.

## Results
Six healthy participants, nearly 300 trials:
- 150 N backward pulse while standing: 19.6% lower peak CoM error than impedance, which gave no significant reduction over transparent mode. Settling time was 48.0% shorter than in transparent mode and not significantly different from impedance.
- Lunge with a 20 cm CoM drop: 24.1% lower tracking error than impedance with 28.7% less mechanical work.
- Voluntary leaning: 45.9% more excursion area than impedance allowed.

## Limitations
Only healthy young adults were tested. The method needs floor force plates, the rigid-body inertia is coarse, and holding GRF constant over the horizon hurts prediction when contact forces change quickly.
