tldr: A five-fingered hand drives each finger's three joints with one motor through a split-stage gear–spring train, so the fingers wrap around objects on contact without tactile sensing. abstract: Anthropomorphic robotic hands offer the potential to manipulate objects designed for human hands in environments adapted to human use. However, developing a robotic hand that replicates the human hand in terms of morphology, degrees of freedom, force output, durability, and sensing capabilities remains a formidable challenge, as these requirements inherently involve trade-offs. Here, we propose a robotic hand mechanism designed to address several of these trade-offs by enabling contact-adaptive finger motion, slender fingers, a wide grasping range, and sustained gripping force. We also introduce a motion glove based on quaternion swing-twist decomposition to extract human manipulation intentions and translate them into robotic hand commands, along with a forearm-mounted interface designed to enhance the operator's sense of embodiment. Experimental demonstrations show that a robotic hand with limited active degrees of freedom and no tactile sensing can perform complex tasks, including smartphone pickup and charging, in-hand manipulation, object unboxing and repackaging, and cutting and tying cords, through the combination of the proposed mechanism and effective bimanual coordination.

## What they do
Human-like robot hands trade degrees of freedom against motor size, force, durability and finger thickness. This 794 g hand uses one actuator per finger and a gear-and-spring transmission that lets each finger's three joints conform to the object mechanically. To test the hand apart from arm control and perception, an operator wears two hands on forearm mounts and drives the fingers with motion gloves.

## How it works
- **Split-stage gear–spring finger.** Two spur-gear stages pass motor torque to the MCP, PIP and DIP joints. Torsion springs keep PIP and DIP extended until contact blocks a link, then the next joint flexes to wrap the object. Gear ratios stay constant over the range of motion, and the fingers are fully enclosed.
- **Closing-ejection condition.** A simplified static model gives gear-ratio and length conditions under which a fingertip pinch does not flex the outer joints; with the chosen gears, 35.9% (8.1 mm) of the distal phalanx is a stable pinch region.
- **Joint sensing.** The motor encoder plus two angle sensors in the proximal phalanx recover all three joint angles.
- **Motion glove.** Six orientation sensors; swing-twist decomposition reduces each fingertip's rotation relative to the back of the hand to one flexion angle per finger.

## Results
- One finger: 277.1 mm/s mean fingertip speed and 12.16 N mean fingertip force over 60 s at stall (18.31 N ideal), dropping 4.88% over that time.
- Extra distal rotation starts with contact 65 mm from the MCP joint, 10.5 mm inside the conservative model's 75.5 mm boundary.
- Teleoperated demos: scooping a phone off a table and plugging in its charger, screwing in a light bulb by rotating it in the fingers, cutting rope with scissors, tying bricks, unboxing and cleaning a wine glass, moving twelve 2 L bottles in a row, and working normally after being buried in sand.

## Limitations
A human operator supplies arm motion, bimanual coordination and perception, so the demos do not predict autonomous performance; learning from demonstration is only proposed. The demos report no success rates or trial counts. The hand has no tactile sensing and no thumb abduction, and the forearm mount has no wrist joint and puts the hands' weight on the operator.
