abstract: 'Predicting future scene or object states does not necessarily reveal how robot-object interactions will advance or hinder a task. This challenge is particularly pronounced in dynamic robotic manipulation, where objects continue moving during perception and execution. Effective action generation therefore requires not only modeling object dynamics but also anticipating how future interactions will shape task progress. To this end, we propose PhysVAM, a Physics-oriented Future Value Action Model that represents anticipated interactions through their effects on future task progress. This value-based formulation provides task-directed foresight without reconstructing future visual states. Conditioned on current observations and historical object and robot motion, PhysVAM jointly learns action generation and multi-horizon future-value prediction. The value branch learns horizon-specific latent representations through future task-value prediction from successful and failed trajectories. These future-value representations are integrated into the action policy through gated cross-attention, guiding action generation toward interactions that facilitate task success. Extensive simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate superior task success, with absolute success-rate improvements of 27.0 and 27.5 percentage points over the strongest baselines in simulation and real-world tasks, respectively. Project page: https://metarobo.github.io/physvam.github.io/.'

## What they do
When objects keep moving while the robot perceives and acts, a grasp that looks good now can still miss. Instead of predicting future images, PhysVAM predicts future *task progress*: how much an upcoming interaction will advance or set back the task. Those predictions condition the action generator.

## How it works
- **Inputs.** Language, proprioception and three camera views, plus four steps of 3D object position with velocities from finite differences, and the robot's recent actions.
- **Two coupled DiTs.** A 1B-parameter ActionDiT, initialized from the Wan2.2 video DiT as in Fast-WAM, outputs 32-step action chunks. A smaller ValueDiT has one learned query per horizon (4, 8, 16, 32 steps) and predicts the task value at each.
- **Value targets.** Each episode gets a value curve in [-1, 1] whose sign is the final outcome. Failed trajectories train only the value branch, successes train both.
- **Guidance.** Value-query features are injected into three ActionDiT layers through gated cross-attention.

## Results
- Simulation (ten object scenes, objects moving at 0.2-0.4 m/s or, in five scenes, at 0.4 m/s and faster; 20 trials per scene over three seeds): 58.83% average success and 8.23 s completion time, against 31.83% for PUMA and 17.33% for DynamicVLA. Adding the value branch to PUMA raises it to 41.50%.
- Ablations: no value branch 50.50%, scalar value conditioning 40.83%, current-value instead of future-value targets 46.50%, no object-motion history 17.17%.
- Real AgileX PiPER arm, 20 trials per object: 72.5% on the conveyor belt, 65.0% on the turntable and 55.0% on rolling objects, 64.17% overall against 36.67% for PUMA.

## Limitations
The policy learns only from offline trajectories, and on the real robot object motion comes from FoundationPose tracking, which needs a generated object mesh and color-based segmentation. Real-world tests cover five objects on one arm. The authors list online improvement, more kinds of object motion, multi-object interactions and longer tasks as future work.
