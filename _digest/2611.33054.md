abstract: Progress in embodied foundation models is largely measured by task success, while the physical safety of their decisions remains underexamined. Safety evaluations centered on rule following and binary judgments do not adequately assess whether embodied models understand environmental hazards and make safe decisions. We introduce EmbodiSafety, comprising 1,092 snapshot tasks and 540 trajectory tasks across 844 visual scenarios to assess safety over short and long horizons. Six response modes cover direct execution, refusal, clarification, constrained execution, autonomous recovery, and human escalation. Distinguishing recovery from human intervention makes the boundary of safe autonomy an explicit evaluation target. Our protocol assesses mode correctness, risk identification, and actionable handling, with complete decision accuracy requiring all applicable checks to pass. Claude-Opus-5.5 achieves the highest complete decision accuracy among reference models, reaching 56.1% on snapshots and 61.5% on trajectories. We further develop a data construction pipeline and curate 40k examples for safety alignment. Supervised training on this corpus raises the complete decision accuracy of Qwen3.5-2B and Qwen3.5-4B.

## What they do
Embodied VLMs that act as a robot's "brain" are mostly scored on task success, not on whether their decisions are safe. EmbodiSafety asks a model, at one decision point, to choose among six responses: proceed, refuse, clarify, constrain execution, recover autonomously or escalate to a human. It must also name the risk and give a next action. The benchmark has 1,092 single-image (Snapshot) and 540 frame-sequence (Trajectory) tasks over 844 scenes in homes, workplaces, industrial, clinical and public settings.

## How it works
- **Generated scenes.** GPT-5.6 expands scripted choices of embodiment, viewpoint, environment and hazard into scenario seeds. Seedance2.5 renders them as videos, because such hazards cannot be staged safely.
- **Review.** GPT-5.6 checks frames against each seed. Five experts and GPT-6-astra then audit the labels, revising 71 examples and removing 65. Blind relabeling gives Fleiss' kappa of 0.84 (Snapshot) and 0.81 (Trajectory).
- **Scoring.** L1 checks the mode, L2 the risk mechanism and L3 the next action, clarifying question or constraints. A GPT-5 judge agrees with human consensus at kappa 0.873 and 0.860.
- **Training data.** 40k separate examples are used to LoRA-tune Qwen3.5-2B and 4B.

## Results
- Claude-Opus-5.5 leads with L3 of 56.1% (Snapshot) and 61.5% (Trajectory). The best open-source model on Trajectory, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, reaches 26.9%; MiMo-Embodied-7B drops from 35.8% to 7.8% between tracks.
- Closed-source models are over-conservative: they restrict 26.3-75.4% of safe tasks.
- Qwen3.5-35B-A3B chooses recovery in 84.9% of cases needing a human; Claude hands 22.2% of recoverable trajectories to a human.
- Fine-tuning lifts Qwen3.5-4B L3 by 14.9 (Snapshot) and 14.3 (Trajectory) points, but premature handover rises from 7.1% to 55.6%.

## Limitations
All visual evidence is generated, and models answer offline: there is no simulated or real-robot, closed-loop evaluation. Both adapted models restrict 71.9% of benign Trajectory tasks.
