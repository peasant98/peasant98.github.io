abstract: 'Dexterous manipulation involves not only moving objects, but also arranging the hand around them to maintain useful contacts and enable subsequent actions. Yet many learning-based systems specify goals primarily through object poses or trajectories, leaving hand-object configurations to emerge implicitly. We introduce DexHOT, which formulates dexterous manipulation as hand-object configuration transfer: moving between feasible configurations that jointly specify hand articulation and object pose relative to the wrist. DexHOT learns a reusable relation-conditioned transfer primitive from synthetic feasible configuration pairs, without human reference motions, and composes long-horizon behaviors through closed-loop tracking of configuration subgoals. On DexHOTBench with 120 objects, a single policy achieves 80.5% transfer success on seen objects and 69.4% on unseen objects; on a real dexterous hand, it achieves 61.5% success over 200 real-robot transfers and 90% task success across six demonstration-guided tasks. These results show that explicit hand-object goals provide an expressive and composable interface for dexterous manipulation. More details and videos are available at https://dex-hot.github.io/.'

## What they do
Most learned dexterous policies are given an object pose as the goal, so how the hand ends up holding the object is left to chance. DexHOT makes the goal a hand-object configuration: the hand's joint angles plus the object's pose relative to the wrist. One policy learns to move between such configurations with the palm facing down, so the hand must keep the object from falling while it regrasps.

## How it works
- **Configuration pools.** BODex grasp synthesis on 40 objects from 40 categories, filtered in Isaac Gym, gives 825,473 stable configurations. Two configurations are compared by the mean distance between 28 hand keypoints expressed in the object frame.
- **Local-goal training.** A recurrent policy for a 22-DoF Sharpa hand is trained only on nearby targets (20-40 mm apart), with population-based RL (EPO, 64 latent-coded blocks) across 524,288 environments with domain randomization.
- **Chaining.** At test time a greedy planner, or a single retargeted human demonstration, supplies subgoals at 1 Hz while the frozen policy runs at 30 Hz. On hardware, FoundationPose tracks the object and a separate controller moves the Franka arm.

## Results
- Simulation (DexHOTBench, 120 objects): 80.5% success on seen objects, 74.4% on unseen instances and 64.4% on unseen categories, averaged over close and far transfers.
- Replacing EPO with a single PPO agent drops close-transfer success to 2.5%; sampling targets from the whole pool lowers the average from 0.805 to 0.682.
- Real robot: 61.5% of 200 transfers succeed on ten objects (63% seen, 60% unseen), ranging from 95% on a tennis ball down to 20% on a screwdriver or hammer.
- Six demonstration-guided tasks: 27/30 trials, against 2/30 for an adapted SimToolReal and 0/30 for replaying the kinematic retargeting.

## Limitations
Elongated tools wedge between fingers or swing during alignment. Other failures: slips on low-friction objects that feedback cannot catch, configurations that are only marginally stable on hardware, stalled reorientation, and FoundationPose losing track. The greedy planner does not guarantee that each step is dynamically feasible. Each task was tested five times from one fixed layout.
