abstract: For perceptive humanoid locomotion, the relevance of terrain geometry depends on the robot's body state and how both evolve under its actions. Existing work has advanced terrain-aware sensory fusion and cross-modal prediction, improving how visual and proprioceptive information is combined and related. However, feature aggregation alone does not specify which cross-modal relationships should be learned, while prediction between simultaneous observations does not explicitly constrain their action-dependent evolution. We present Spatiotemporal Training with Explicit Prediction (STEP), a one-stage framework that jointly supervises cross-modal relationships and action-dependent state evolution within a shared control representation. Multiple learned queries aggregate depth and proprioceptive tokens into a compact policy state. Bidirectional masked prediction uses each visible modality to predict latent features of the omitted modality, explicitly supervising terrain-body relationships. An action-conditioned predictor maps the current policy state and executed action to the next fused state, with an exponential-moving-average target defined directly in the policy representation space. Simulation comparisons show overall improvements in terrain traversal over evaluated fusion alternatives and ablations, while depth-dropout and delay experiments demonstrate the benefits of both predictive objectives under degraded visual input. Deployed on a 23-DoF Noetix E1 humanoid with onboard sensing, STEP succeeds in 98 of 100 hardware trials across stairs, a 40 cm platform, a 70 cm gap, stepping stones, and backward stair traversal without rearward terrain visibility.

## What they do
STEP trains a depth-based humanoid locomotion policy in a single stage: the PPO policy and its multimodal state representation learn together. Two training-only prediction losses tell the encoder which body-terrain relationships to keep. The policy runs on a 23-DoF Noetix E1 humanoid with an onboard depth camera.

## How it works
- **Multi-query fusion.** A five-frame proprioceptive history (5 tokens) and a 16x24 depth image (96 tokens from a CNN) form one shared context. Four learned queries read it through two cross-attention blocks and are pooled into a 64-dimensional policy state.
- **Cross-modal prediction.** With depth masked, the state must predict an EMA teacher's depth features; with proprioception masked, its proprioceptive features.
- **Next-state prediction.** A small predictor maps the current state and the executed action to the teacher's next fused state. SIGReg prevents latent collapse, and prediction heads and teachers are dropped at deployment.
- Training runs in Isaac Lab with PPO, a privileged critic, an AMP motion prior, a separate velocity estimator and a Soft-MoE actor.

## Results
- Simulation (gaps, stairs up and down, stepping stones at two difficulty levels): STEP has an overall mean of 95.17% across success and traversal rates. That compares with 91.02% without the prediction losses, 69.98% with one query and 86.17% for a proprioception-queried cross-attention baseline. On the hardest stepping stones, success is 74.9% versus 57.2% without prediction and 43.5% for the baseline.
- With a 20 ms depth delay, STEP reaches 88.6% on stepping stones; removing the next-state loss drops this to 54.1%, removing cross-modal prediction to 0%.
- Hardware: 98 of 100 trials. It goes 20/20 on 20 cm stairs, the 40 cm platform, stepping stones and backward 15 cm stairs without a rearward view, and 18/20 on the 70 cm gap.

## Limitations
If depth stops updating altogether, every variant, STEP included, fails on gaps and stepping stones. Prediction looks only one step ahead, and the policy keeps no memory of terrain it has already seen. Hardware tests use 20 trials per task, and both failures were on the gap.
