abstract: 'Embodied navigation requires agents to achieve persistent spatial awareness and long-horizon execution stability in open-world environments. However, existing navigation architectures struggle to sustain both capabilities, remaining effective only over a handful of recent frames. We present Compass 1, the first embodied navigation foundation model to unify language understanding and video generation while enabling memory backtracking through nearly 2mins of visual history. Without altering the underlying architecture, we introduce History Representation, which integrates sparse visual keyframes with an explicit History Representation Map to preserve long-term spatiotemporal context. To effectively leverage this history, we further introduce History Utilization, a structured VQA supervision protocol comprising three categories: task-stage grounding, exploration consistency, and spatiotemporal target retrieval, enabling map-grounded reasoning and stable persistent long-horizon navigation. To fuel this system, we establish a complete annotation pipeline for monocular RGB videos and open-source a massive dataset featuring over 500 hours of high-quality real-world interactions and over 2,000 hours of annotated web videos. Trained mainly on the distribution of 1m height camera, Compass 1 generalizes seamlessly across diverse embodiments and scenarios, ranging from indoor environments to pedestrian-dense streets, spanning wheeled robot at 0.35m, quadruped dog at 0.5m, humanoid at 0.85m, and aerial drone at 1.5m. Compass 1 achieves a dominant overall success rate of 79.4% across open-world evaluations, compared to 17.8% for the strongest baseline. It demonstrates exceptional zero-shot execution exceeding 90% on foundational tasks, a 250% boost in exploration, and an unparalleled leap from 0% to 58% in challenging persistent history backtracking.'

## What they do
Navigation models trained in simulation often fail on real robots: they stop early, turn the wrong way, get stuck in left-right deadlocks and barely use their history. Compass 1 trains on real monocular RGB video and adds a long memory, sparse keyframes plus a drawn map of its past path, without changing the backbone architecture.

## How it works
- **Backbone.** Cosmos3-Edge-4B with an autoregressive language head for reasoning and a diffusion head that generates future video and 12 actions (dx, dy, dyaw).
- **History.** Keyframes are taken at fixed distance and angle thresholds. The History Representation Map draws them, with indices and heading arrows, as an extra input image.
- **VQA supervision.** Three question types: which task stage is running and whether to stop; which directions are already explored; and which keyframe shows the target and in what direction it lies. Without these questions the model ignores the map.
- **Data.** Over 500 h of self-collected camera video plus over 2,000 h of YouTube video, labeled semi-automatically with actions from π3 and MoGe-2.

## Results
- On a Unitree Go2 with its camera at 0.5 m (training is mostly at 1.0 m), 8 tasks × 4 scenes × 10 trials: 79.4% overall success. Four baselines, each scored at the better of its released and its fine-tuned weights, reach 17.8%, 11.6%, 6.3% and 0.3%.
- History backtracking: 78% vs 4%, with 98% on basic and 58% on persistent backtracking. Exploration: 84%. Instruction following: 78%, but only 50% on multi-stage routes.
- In crowded streets the best baseline drops by nearly 65%; Compass 1 drops only slightly.
- Inference runs at about 28 Hz at the start and 17 Hz at 63 keyframes.

## Limitations
Multi-stage routes fail after turning errors accumulate or when targets blend into glass reflections or harsh lighting. Persistent backtracking can lose its heading when obstacles block the way back. Camera jitter lowers success on the humanoid and the drone. Inference runs on a remote GPU, and velocity control relies on odometry.
