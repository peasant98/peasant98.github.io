abstract: 'Time is a fundamental dimension of robotic intelligence: a robot may fail despite physically correct actions if it acts outside a valid time window, responds too slowly to an event, or fails to meet required throughput. Yet existing robotic benchmarks primarily emphasize spatial correctness and logical task completion, largely overlooking the temporal constraints of real-world applications. We present RoboClock, a unified benchmark for time-aware robotic manipulation featuring 60 tasks across five application domains. Mirroring these failure modes, RoboClock structures tasks around three types of temporal constraints: Temporal Window, Event-Reactive, and Throughput. Furthermore, RoboClock incorporates a Latency-Aligned evaluation protocol that continuously advances environment time during model inference and control-interface delays, paired with time-sensitive metrics. Evaluating over 30 methods, we find that the best-performing approach is the trained VLA, yet it achieves only 8.3% temporal success even on Easy-level tasks, with persistent challenges in generalization and transfer. These findings reveal fundamental limitations in current robotic systems and highlight the need to jointly optimize physical competence, temporal reasoning, and execution latency.'

## What they do
Most manipulation benchmarks pause the simulator while the policy computes and only ask whether the task eventually gets done. RoboClock makes timing part of success: 60 bimanual tasks in Isaac Sim across laboratory, healthcare, household, industrial and sports/game scenes, each with a time requirement, plus an evaluation mode in which slow inference costs environment time.

## How it works
- **Three constraint types.** Temporal Window (act inside a window, e.g. close a faucet while the water is in a target band), Event-Reactive (respond to a randomized cue, e.g. press an emergency stop within 3 s of a fault warning) and Throughput (keep up a minimum completion rate). Three difficulty levels tighten the main constraint.
- **Latency-Aligned protocol.** The simulator saves its state, waits for the model, then replays the previous commands for the measured inference time plus a fixed control delay before the new action chunk takes effect.
- **Metrics.** Progress score, physical success (PSR), temporal success with inference paused (TSR) and with latency counted (LTSR). A scripted oracle reaches 99.2% joint physical and temporal success, and human teleoperators reach 96% TSR.

## Results
- Released VLA and world-action-model checkpoints reach at most 2.3% PSR. Fine-tuned on oracle demos from 45 tasks, π0.5 gets 10.3% TSR and 8.3% LTSR; OpenWAM-α falls from 10.0% TSR to 3.8% LTSR. On the 15 held-out tasks only X-VLA gets a nonzero TSR (2.0%).
- Zero-shot VLM agents reach 19.0% TSR (GPT-6 Astra) and 18.2% (Gemini 4 Argon), but only 3.0% and 8.2% LTSR; no open-weight VLM exceeds 1.2% LTSR.
- Assuming faster decoding lifts GPT-6 Astra's LTSR from 5.7% (35 tokens/s) to 18.0% (3K tokens/s).
- A real-arm pilot (six VLMs, six tasks, 360 episodes) ranks models in the same order as simulation (Spearman 0.85 on LTSR).

## Limitations
Simulated processes are simplified and some time scales are compressed. Transfer to physical robots is untested apart from the descriptive pilot. LTSR depends on the inference hardware and API serving speed.
