## What they do
CoTacWAM is a world-action model for contact-rich manipulation with a wrist camera and two tactile fingertip pads. Its local encoder is trained so that wrist images and touch answer the same questions (is the pad touching the object, and how hard) while each keeps its own detail. The resulting joint vector conditions the policy, is predicted into the future during training, and guides corrections after a detected failure.

## How it works
- **Shared contact semantics.** Tactile features (dense force fields and tactile images) and wrist features (frozen Wan2.2 VAE plus a trainable readout) feed one shared head that predicts contact and one of four strength levels. Alignment and tactile-to-wrist distillation tie the modalities together; preservation losses keep force and visual detail; modality and single-pad dropout train for missing inputs.
- **Predictive action learning.** Video, interaction and action experts are trained with flow matching; an attention mask keeps future sensory tokens away from the action tokens.
- **Failure-conditioned recovery.** A separate GRU detector on tactile force/torque and motion history confirms failure; a snapshot of that failure plus an adjustment instruction conditions the action expert, trained on corrective demonstrations.
- **Platform.** Agilex Piper arm with two Xense G1-WS tactile pads, head and wrist cameras; 20 trials per method and task.

## Results
- 71.3% mean success over wiping, insertion, tofu grasping and pouring, best on every task; baselines (π0.5, Fast-WAM, N0-VTLA, N0-TWAM, FTP-1) reach 27.5-35.0%.
- Retries raise insertion from 50% to 80% and wiping from 80% to 100%.
- Without wrist vision: 70% on wiping and 75% on tofu, where all three tactile baselines score 0%. Without touch: 60% and 55%.
- The shared head reads contact with 99.91% accuracy from touch and 92.65% from wrist vision (constant prior: 81.37%). Dropping future-interaction supervision cuts tofu success from 85% to 45%; dropping history cuts wiping from 80% to 30%.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. All results come from one arm and one tactile sensor, with task-specific policies and 20 trials per condition; retries were tested only on insertion and wiping, and missing inputs only on wiping and tofu. Recovery needs reviewed corrective demonstrations and a detector trained on task-specific failure annotations.
