## What they do
Segmentation has no web-scale data source, so the authors build three linked pieces: a promptable segmentation task (return a valid mask for any point, box, mask or text prompt, even an ambiguous one), a model for it (SAM), and a model-in-the-loop data engine that produced SA-1B, 1.1B masks on 11M images. New tasks are then solved zero-shot by prompting.

## How it works
- **Model.** An MAE-pretrained ViT-H encodes the image once. Prompts are embedded (CLIP for text) and a lightweight two-way-attention decoder turns them into masks in about 50 ms on a CPU in a browser.
- **Ambiguity.** Each prompt yields 3 masks (whole, part, subpart) ranked by a predicted IoU; training backpropagates only the lowest loss among them.
- **Data engine.** Assisted-manual (4.3M masks; time per mask fell from 34 to 14 s), semi-automatic (5.9M more), then fully automatic from a 32x32 point grid, keeping confident, stable masks. SA-1B holds only automatic masks, and SAM trains on them.

## Results
- In a 500-image sample, 94% of automatic masks have over 90% IoU with professionally corrected versions.
- From one point, SAM beats RITM's mIoU on 16 of 23 unseen datasets, by up to about 47 IoU, and on all 23 when an oracle picks among its 3 masks. Human raters score its masks 7-9 out of 10, consistently above RITM.
- Zero-shot: BSDS500 edges at ODS .768 (HED, trained on BSDS500: .788); LVIS proposals at AR@1000 59.3 vs 63.0 for ViTDet-H; box-prompted instance segmentation at 46.5 / 44.7 mask AP on COCO / LVIS vs 51.0 / 46.6, though annotators rate SAM's masks higher.

## Limitations
SAM misses fine structures, sometimes hallucinates small disconnected components, and draws less crisp boundaries than "zoom-in" methods; dedicated interactive segmenters do better with many points. The full model is not real-time with the heavy encoder, text-to-mask is not robust, and it is unclear how to prompt for semantic or panoptic segmentation.
