abstract: This paper presents a robotic pick-and-place system that is capable of grasping and recognizing both known and novel objects in cluttered environments. The key new feature of the system is that it handles a wide range of object categories without needing any task-specific training data for novel objects. To achieve this, it first uses a category-agnostic affordance prediction algorithm to select and execute among four different grasping primitive behaviors. It then recognizes picked objects with a cross-domain image classification framework that matches observed images to product images. Since product images are readily available for a wide range of objects (e.g., from the web), the system works out-of-the-box for novel objects without requiring any additional training data. Exhaustive experimental results demonstrate that our multi-affordance grasping achieves high success rates for a wide variety of objects in clutter, and our recognition algorithm achieves high accuracy for both known and novel grasped objects. The approach was part of the MIT-Princeton Team system that took 1st place in the stowing task at the 2017 Amazon Robotics Challenge. All code, datasets, and pre-trained models are available online at http://arc.cs.princeton.edu

## What they do
A pick-and-place system for objects it has never seen, given only their product images. It reverses the usual recognize-then-grasp order: it picks an object without knowing what it is, lifts it away from the clutter, then recognizes it. The system was part of the MIT-Princeton entry that won the stowing task of the 2017 Amazon Robotics Challenge (ARC).

## How it works
- **Four primitives.** A gripper with parallel jaws and a retractable suction cup can suction down, suction from the side, grasp down, or flush-grasp an object lying against the bin wall.
- **Dense affordances.** One FCN scores every pixel of each RGB-D view for suction. A second scores top-down grasps on a heightmap, rotated to cover 16 gripper angles. Both are built on ResNet-101 and trained on 1837 hand-labeled images. A planner executes the highest-scoring proposal.
- **Recognition by matching.** A two-stream network learns to map camera images of the held object onto the ImageNet features of a frozen product-image stream, so novel objects are identified by nearest neighbor. A first stage decides whether the object is known or novel and routes it to the network trained for that case.

## Results
- Top-1 suction precision is 92.4% (Dex-Net 69.3%, heuristic baseline 35.2%); top-1 grasp precision is 96.7% (Dex-Net 80.4%, baseline 92.5%).
- On a 1-vs-20 recognition benchmark, the two-stage system scores 88.6% on mixed objects (93.6% known, 77.5% novel), vs 74.2% for a Siamese network.
- ARC 2017 stow: 58.3% suction and 75% grasp pick success and 100% recognition, with all 20 objects stowed. At the competition, parallel-jaw grasps came from the heuristic baseline because the grasp network was not yet stable.

## Limitations
It only picks objects that a single primitive can reach directly; it does not push or clear other objects first. Grasps run open loop, so small affordance errors lead to failed or marginal grasps. Picking a specific target object needs state tracking, which loses track when several objects move at once. In the finals, a camera disconnection also forced a hard reset.
