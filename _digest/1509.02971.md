abstract: 'We adapt the ideas underlying the success of Deep Q-Learning to the continuous action domain. We present an actor-critic, model-free algorithm based on the deterministic policy gradient that can operate over continuous action spaces. Using the same learning algorithm, network architecture and hyper-parameters, our algorithm robustly solves more than 20 simulated physics tasks, including classic problems such as cartpole swing-up, dexterous manipulation, legged locomotion and car driving. ## What they do
DQN needs a max over discrete actions; discretizing a 7-DoF arm with three levels per joint already gives 2187 actions. DDPG builds instead on the deterministic policy gradient (DPG): an actor network maps states to continuous actions and a critic learns Q(s, a). The paper adds the tricks that stabilized DQN and runs one algorithm and set of hyperparameters on more than 20 simulated MuJoCo tasks and the Torcs racing game, from low-dimensional states and from raw pixels.

## How it works
- **Actor-critic.** The critic is trained on Bellman targets as in Q-learning. The actor is updated with the deterministic policy gradient, the chain rule applied to the critic's estimate of return.
- **Replay and soft targets.** Transitions go into a 10^6-sample replay buffer. Slowly tracking copies of both actor and critic (tau = 0.001) compute the targets, which keeps critic training from diverging.
- **Batch normalization** on the state input and hidden layers, so the same hyperparameters work across observations with different units and scales.
- **Exploration** adds temporally correlated Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise to the actor's output. From pixels, each action is repeated for 3 simulator steps and the three 64x64 frames are stacked to expose velocities.

## Results
- Scores are normalized so a random policy gets 0 and an iLQG planner with full access to the dynamics gets 1, averaged over 5 replicas. From states DDPG averages 0.946 on pendulum, 0.903 on cheetah and 0.655 on gripper. On hardCheetah it averages 1.311 from states and 1.204 from pixels, beating the planner.
- Pixel policies are close to state-based ones on some tasks (fixedReacherDouble 0.872 vs 0.924) and far behind on others (hopper 0.112 vs 0.676).
- Without target networks learning is poor: DPG with replay and batch normalization averages -0.008 on cheetah.
- Nearly all tasks are solved within 2.5 million steps, 20 times fewer than DQN needs on Atari.

## Limitations
Like most model-free RL, DDPG needs many training episodes, and nonlinear function approximators remove any convergence guarantee. Q estimates are accurate on simple tasks but worse on harder ones. On Torcs some replicas learned to complete a lap while others failed. All experiments are in simulation.
