abstract: Online reinforcement learning offers an effective approach to precise laboratory manipulation, yet costly real-world demonstrations and limited access to dense process rewards hinder its practical application. We present TwinBridge, a digital twin-driven framework that supplies policy initialization and dense visual rewards for real-world online learning. We construct digital twins by combining 3D Gaussian splatting with physics simulation. Here, simulator states provide task events and geometric progress under physical prerequisites and ordering constraints, yielding rewards for policy pretraining with simulated demonstrations and reinforcement learning. We distill this supervision into a visual reward model. During real-world interaction, its predictions are combined with execution history to compute dense rewards for human-in-the-loop policy refinement. Across five real-world laboratory tasks, TwinBridge achieves a final mean autonomous success rate of 98% with at most 25 minutes of real-world interaction per task. Experiments show that dense rewards improve interaction efficiency over sparse rewards, and simulation-based policy initialization further accelerates learning.

## What they do
Real-world RL can learn precise lab manipulation, but it needs demonstrations of every stage and rewards that show intermediate progress. TwinBridge gets both from a digital twin of the bench: a policy pretrained in simulation, and a visual reward model trained on simulator labels, which together drive human-in-the-loop RL on the real robot.

## How it works
- **Twin.** Isaac Sim physics with 3D Gaussian splatting for rendering. Rigid-body poses move the Gaussians, so the rendered images follow the robot-object interaction.
- **Physics-grounded rewards.** Each task has ordered events with prerequisites (moving a tube only counts after a secure grasp). A ledger credits each event once, plus any new record in geometric progress, and potential-based shaping turns this credit into a dense reward.
- **Visual distillation.** A GRU on frozen Octo features predicts events and progress from images. It is trained on simulator-labeled sequences plus a small set of real sequences annotated with events.
- **Real-world RL.** HIL-ConRFT actor-critic, initialized from the simulation policy, with operator interventions. Hardware: a JAKA Minicobo with a parallel gripper, and a LinkerHand L7 holding a pipette.

## Results
- Final autonomous success is 100% on four tasks and 90% on tube loading (98% mean). Without simulation initialization it is 92%; sparse-reward HIL-ConRFT reaches 44%, HG-DAgger 76% and HIL-SERL 4%.
- Simulation initialization reaches 80% rolling success sooner: about 3.9 vs 9.2 min on tube pickup and 7.6 vs 12.9 min on lid removal.
- On held-out real trajectories the reward model scores 99.30% event F1 and 0.889 Spearman correlation between predicted progress and time.
- In simulation, dense rewards reach 100%, 90% and 90% rolling success on pickup, lid removal and loading; sparse rewards reach at most 50%.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. Each task needs its own twin, hand-specified events and prerequisites, and some annotated real sequences. Real-world learning relies on a human operator, and the initial gripper position is randomized only within ±20 mm horizontally.
