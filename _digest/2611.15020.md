## What they do
A simulation benchmark is only useful if its scores say something about real robots. This report tests Genie Sim Benchmark, which has 78 simulated manipulation tasks covering instruction following, spatial reasoning, robustness and general manipulation. Simulated tasks are paired with real ones, and the task-level comparison is backed by robot and object system identification.

## How it works
- **Paired evaluation.** Each simulated layout mirrors a real test layout, with the same criteria and trial counts. Task-specific pi0.5 policies fine-tuned on real-robot data run in both. People score the real runs and the simulator scores its own, as a stage-averaged Score. The 22 pairs cover 16 tasks on G1 Omnipicker, G2 Swiftpicker, G2 Wuji and Arx X5, from target selection and sorting to bimanual handover, stacking and cloth folding.
- **Robot identification.** For both G2 arms, friction sweeps, multi-frequency sines and a coupled Fourier trajectory fit link inertias, armature, friction and torque bias, under physical-feasibility constraints.
- **Object identification.** Five assets (kettle, gum box, sponge, shuttlecock, pen) are fit to one recorded real interaction each, then replayed from recorded commands in MuJoCo, MuJoCo-Warp and PhysX.

## Results
- Across the 22 pairs, real and simulated Scores correlate at Pearson r = 0.9686 (MAE 0.0405, RMSE 0.0520), with gaps from -0.110 to +0.080.
- On 14 G2/Arx pairs, identification raised r from 0.9518 to 0.9669 and cut MAE by 20%. Eight gaps shrank, one grew (Arx size recognition) and five were unchanged.
- On a 32.7 s teleoperation recording left out of identification, per-joint position RMSE was 0.006-0.035 rad and torque RMSE 0.74-4.0 N m.
- Fitting cut kettle error from about 39-41 mm to 25-26 mm in all three engines, while the gum box barely changed. Frozen MuJoCo parameters failed in PhysX (the sponge was not lifted, the shuttlecock and pen rolled away). Native PhysX contact settings then let all three complete their replays.

## Limitations
The numbers are descriptive, with no confidence intervals, and the G1 scores come from earlier Genie Sim 3.0 experiments without identification. Each object study is one recorded episode, and its "held-out" windows are later parts of the same recording. The authors state that the identification work does not establish a causal reduction in task-score gaps.
