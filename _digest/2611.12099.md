## What they do
Vision-based traversability models usually predict each frame on its own, so the drivable region can flicker from frame to frame and leave the planner with no path. SAM-VTP fine-tunes SAM2's video memory for prompt-free traversability segmentation, feeds in terrain geometry measured from depth, and trains on continuous per-pixel targets between 0 and 1 instead of binary labels.

## How it works
- **Video memory.** Each frame attends over the features and masks of the 7 most recent frames. The decoder emits a single mask token, and a residual head refines it at full resolution so thin structures such as kerbs survive.
- **Measured geometry.** Slope, roughness, height, log range and validity are computed from depth, encoded and added to the coarsest backbone features.
- **Continuous labels, built offline.** A hand-set table maps each semantic class to a prior (engineered surfaces 1.0 down to blocked 0.0). Slope and roughness then scale it down, and pixels more than 0.55 m above the local terrain are set to 0. Neither robot driving nor per-pixel annotation is needed.
- **Training.** On TartanGround, a synthetic dataset of ground-robot RGB-D sequences, with a fuzzy Jaccard loss plus BCE, focal and temporal-consistency terms.

## Results
- On held-out TartanGround trajectories, fuzzy Jaccard rises from 0.908 to 0.924 and binary IoU from 0.854 to 0.886 over the frame-wise SAM-TP baseline (seed spread below 0.001). Flicker drops by 31%.
- In an ablation at a shorter training budget, geometry alone adds 0.025 fuzzy Jaccard and memory alone 0.019. Training on binary targets instead costs 0.181.
- On a JetAcker robot with a RealSense D455f, the planner found no feasible path on 9.9% of cycles against 32.4% for the baseline on a paved course, and 3.0% against 20.6% on rough ground.
- It runs at 21.4 FPS on the robot's Jetson Orin Nano.

## Limitations
All segmentation accuracy is measured in simulation against the authors' own targets; the field trials measure planner behaviour, not mask quality. Building targets needs per-pixel semantic labels, which real footage does not have, and the model needs depth (withholding it costs 0.061 fuzzy Jaccard). In the field the baseline saw RGB only, so those trials do not separate memory from depth.
