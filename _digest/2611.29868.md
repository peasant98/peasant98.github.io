abstract: 'Reproducing and modifying a meter-scale humanoid remains challenging for research and teaching groups with limited fabrication resources and engineering support. This paper presents Arcadian, an open-source humanoid ecosystem that offers accessible and customizable humanoid hardware and software with community support. Arcadian aims to strike a balance among cost-effectiveness, structural durability, and full customizability, lowering the barrier to entry for research and education. Specifically, this meter-scale humanoid adopts metal-embedded 3D printing to achieve a high strength-to-cost ratio, eliminating the need for computer numerical control (CNC) machining while retaining design flexibility. Extensive simulation and hardware tests validate the design: a reinforcement-learning (RL) locomotion policy trained in simulation transfers to the hardware, and a virtual reality (VR) teleoperation interface drives both arms for simple manipulation tasks. ## What they do
Meter-scale humanoids are hard for labs and classrooms to reproduce: commercial ones are closed or import-restricted, and open-source designs are either small or depend on CNC-machined parts. Arcadian is an open-source 1.11 m, 20 kg, 19-DoF humanoid that costs about $6,800 and needs no CNC parts, custom actuators or custom PCBs. CAD, bill of materials, assembly guide and code are released, with a Discord server and a forum that feed fixes back into the design.

## How it works
- **Metal-embedded shells.** 3.175 mm waterjet-cut 6061 aluminum inserts run along the load paths of the 3D-printed leg shells. The print pauses, the insert is placed on locating bosses, and printing resumes to encapsulate it.
- **Transmission.** Off-the-shelf Damiao quasi-direct-drive actuators. The knee and ankle-pitch motors sit on the thigh and drive their joints through printed double-helical PA6-CF gears (2.5:1 and 3:1) and waterjet-cut parallel linkages; the knee ranges from 180° to 30°.
- **Software.** A ROS 2 stack on a Jetson Orin NX that also runs in MuJoCo by swapping the drivers. Walking is a PPO policy trained in Isaac Lab; arm teleoperation uses a Meta Quest 3S and QP-based differential IK.

## Results
- FEA under a 196.2 N standing load: the metal-embedded shin shell is 8.3x stiffer than plain PLA (317.5 to 2641.6 N/mm), and peak PLA stress drops 45.8% (6.03 to 3.27 MPa).
- In MuJoCo, the robot carried a box 2.3 m from a table to a rack in all 60 trials (0.64-2.14 kg boxes, varied headings and offsets), with RMS tracking errors of 0.05 m/s forward and 0.06 rad/s yaw.
- On hardware, the RL policy walked a round trip with a 180° turn in a mocap arena following gamepad commands, and VR teleoperation pushed a box with both arms.

## Limitations
Hardware results are preliminary: the authors say the walking "is not yet perfect", and no hardware tracking errors are reported. Deployment takes base velocity and height from Vicon motion capture. The arms have no wrists or hands yet, loco-manipulation was shown only in simulation, and teleoperation only for simple box pushing.
