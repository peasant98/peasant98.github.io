abstract: Bimanual dexterous manipulation relies on multimodal inputs that capture visual context and non-visual physical interaction cues for precise and coordinated control. To address the challenge of effectively integrating these modalities for policy learning, we propose HiMoDex, a two-stage framework centered on hierarchical multimodal world-state representations. In Stage 1, we pretrain a hierarchical representation network on the collected dataset of 1.19 million synchronized multimodal time steps. The network integrates visual context with tactile, force-torque, and audio cues into unified world-state embeddings. In Stage 2, we freeze the pretrained network and use its embeddings to condition a shared world-action transformer. The transformer jointly learns action generation and action-conditioned future-state prediction. Experiments across five real-world tasks show that HiMoDex achieves an average success rate of 78.7\%, representing an absolute improvement of 32.0\% over the strongest evaluated baseline. Extensive ablations validate the benefits of hierarchical representation learning and two-stage training. Failure analysis helps explain performance differences across variants. Code, data, and demos will be released at https://anonymous.4open.science/w/research-website-BAB4/.

## What they do
Vision alone says little about contact in bimanual dexterous manipulation. The authors build a two-arm robot with BrainCo Revo2 hands, a front and two wrist cameras, fingertip tactile sensing, two end-effector force-torque sensors and two hand-mounted microphones. HiMoDex first learns a world-state representation from all these signals, then freezes it and trains a policy on top.

## How it works
- **Stage 1 encoder.** A frozen V-JEPA 2.1 encodes each camera. Tactile, force-torque and audio histories each pass through their own Intra-Inter Sensor Transformer, and a learned softmax gate fuses them. A world-state encoder merges the visual and interaction embeddings. It is pretrained on 287,718 windows of 16 steps (about 0.5 s).
- **Pretraining losses.** Consistency with an EMA target while non-visual modalities are randomly dropped. Reconstruction of masked tactile and force signals and prediction of their change. A triplet loss that separates task stages, labeled by an LLM-assisted pipeline.
- **Stage 2 policy.** A shared world-action transformer uses the frozen embeddings, proprioception and a UMT5 instruction embedding. It generates 36-D action chunks by flow matching and, during training only, predicts future world-state embeddings.

## Results
- Over 30 trials per task, HiMoDex with all modalities averages 78.7%. The full-modality Qwen3Pi and JEPA-WAM baselines trail by 32.7 and 32.0 points.
- Insert Plug reaches 76.7% and Scrub Pan 83.3%; the best baselines reach 13.3% and 16.7%.
- Adding touch, force and audio lifts HiMoDex from 56.0% to 78.7%, while the same inputs gain Qwen3Pi 4.0 points and JEPA-WAM 6.7.
- In ablations (10 trials per task), removing either pretraining loss drops the average from 74% to 44%. Training end to end in a single stage gives 52%.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. No method, HiMoDex included, completes the full Tighten Screw task: the average counts only grasping the screwdriver and bringing it near the screw (43.3%). Audio does not help every task, since the variant without it does better on Wipe and Tighten. All tests use one robot platform and 770 task demonstrations.
