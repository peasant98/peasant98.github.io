abstract: 'We introduce Ego4D, a massive-scale egocentric video dataset and benchmark suite. It offers 3,670 hours of daily-life activity video spanning hundreds of scenarios (household, outdoor, workplace, leisure, etc.) captured by 931 unique camera wearers from 74 worldwide locations and 9 different countries. The approach to collection is designed to uphold rigorous privacy and ethics standards with consenting participants and robust de-identification procedures where relevant. Ego4D dramatically expands the volume of diverse egocentric video footage publicly available to the research community. Portions of the video are accompanied by audio, 3D meshes of the environment, eye gaze, stereo, and/or synchronized videos from multiple egocentric cameras at the same event. Furthermore, we present a host of new benchmark challenges centered around understanding the first-person visual experience in the past (querying an episodic memory), present (analyzing hand-object manipulation, audio-visual conversation, and social interactions), and future (forecasting activities). ## What they do
Most video datasets are short, curated, third-person clips. Ego4D collects long, unscripted, head-mounted video of daily life: 3,670 hours from 931 participants in 74 cities and 9 countries, gathered by 14 teams. On top of it the paper defines five benchmarks for first-person perception and runs baselines on each.

## How it works
- **Collection.** Participants wore one of seven head-mounted cameras for 1 to 10 hours at a time; a typical raw clip lasts 8 minutes. Parts of the data add audio, Matterport3D scans, gaze, stereo or synchronized multi-camera views. Wearers gave informed consent; faces in public footage are blurred.
- **Narrations.** Every video gets two independent timestamped narrations: 3.85M sentences, 1,772 verbs and 4,336 nouns, used to build taxonomies and select clips per benchmark.
- **Benchmarks.** Episodic Memory (visual, language and moment queries over past video), Hands and Objects (object state changes), Audio-Visual Diarization, Social Interactions (looking at me, talking to me) and Forecasting (locomotion, hands, next interaction, long-term actions).

## Results
- Annotation took over 250,000 annotator hours.
- Episodic Memory: visual queries reach 42.9% success but 0.13 tAP; language queries 5.80% top-1 recall at tIoU 0.3; moment queries 5.96% mAP.
- Hands and Objects: single-frame state-change object detection reaches only 8-14% AP.
- Social: 78.07 mAP for looking-at-me, 55.06 for talking-to-me. Short-term interaction anticipation: 2.45% Top-5 mAP on test.

## Limitations
74 locations is far from global coverage, wearers live mostly in urban or college-town areas, COVID-19 skewed footage toward stay-at-home activities, and battery life favors the more active parts of the day. Narrations come from annotators at two sites in Africa, so their wording reflects local usage. Each benchmark annotates only 48 to 1,000 of the 3,670 hours.
