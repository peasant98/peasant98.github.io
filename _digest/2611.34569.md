abstract: 'Humanoid robots on deformable elastic terrains face a surface whose position and motion are coupled in space and time: deflection and rebound persist between contacts, and load at one foothold alters the support at others, so training must capture how each contact changes the support that follows. We present RIPPLE (Robust Interaction via Proprioceptive Policies on Large-deformation Elastic terrain), a simulation-to-real learning framework for humanoid control on such terrains. RIPPLE integrates ElasticMembrane, a pretensioned mass-spring mesh that models spatially coupled deflection, energy storage, and rebound, together with deformable contact into MJLab for GPU-parallel policy learning. Staged training then transfers rigid-ground skills to balance and jumping on these terrains using only proprioception, without vision or force sensing. Deployed zero-shot, the policies enable a MagicBot Z1 humanoid to balance, jump consecutively, and enter a trampoline from a rigid platform. In simulation, ElasticMembrane training holds standing success of at least 98.7% (mean over three seeds) on ElasticMembrane, IndependentSprings, and BouncyPlate under 0.3 m/s perturbations, whereas rigid-ground or contact-compliance training transfers poorly to the first two. A fixed policy tolerates stiffness from 0.35 to 2.8 times the training value, beds up to 7 m wide, and seven further simulated support scenarios, so transfer needs no calibration to the trampoline''s stiffness. We plan to release the code soon. The interactive browser demo, videos, and scene configurations are available on the project page: https://fly-pigTH.github.io/wave_rider/'

## What they do
A trampoline bed sags under each step, keeps moving between footfalls, and shifts load from one foot to the other. RIPPLE trains humanoid policies for this kind of elastic ground in simulation and deploys them zero-shot on a MagicBot Z1 standing on a 1.5 m trampoline. The policies use only proprioception, with no cameras, no foot force sensing and no measurement of the bed.

## How it works
- **ElasticMembrane.** The bed is a grid of point masses (11x11 nodes at 10 cm spacing on a 1 m bed) joined by pretensioned axial and diagonal springs built from MuJoCo spatial tendons, with a collision-only flex mesh as the contact surface. It runs inside MJLab and MuJoCo-Warp for GPU-parallel training.
- **Staged training.** PPO with 4x1024 environments turns a flat-ground velocity-tracking policy into an ElasticMembrane velocity-tracking policy, then fine-tunes that into a jumping policy. No reward term is specific to elastic terrain, and the bed parameters stay fixed rather than randomized.
- **Policy.** An MLP reads four frames of proprioception (angular velocity, gravity direction, commands, joint states, previous action) and outputs targets for 23 joints at 50 Hz, run onboard through ONNX and RKNN.

## Results
- Hardware: the ElasticMembrane policy logged 657.5 s of control over three sessions, with pushes and velocity and yaw commands, without a fall. The rigid-ground policy and all six terrain-model baselines failed to stand. It crossed from a rigid platform onto the bed in 2/2 tries, settling in 2.3 and 3.3 s.
- The jump policy made 15 consecutive jumps (flight of at least 150 ms, about 22 cm foot clearance). Its median positive work per cycle was 39% lower than when jumping on rigid ground (59 vs. 96 J).
- Simulation, 0.3 m/s pushes: 98.7% standing success on IndependentSprings, against 88.7% for a policy trained on that model. A baseline that models compliance only at the contact reached 8.0% on ElasticMembrane.
- One fixed policy stood in at least 16/20 trials from 0.35x to 2.8x the training stiffness and 85-100% of trials on beds 1-7 m wide.

## Limitations
The membrane was tuned only qualitatively against the trampoline's observed deflection, and quantitative validation of load-deflection and rebound is future work. One robot and one physical terrain were tested; the other seven scenarios are simulated. Transfer varies across training seeds, and the specific contribution of spatial coupling is not yet isolated. Yaw tracking reaches a median 60% of the commanded rotation.
