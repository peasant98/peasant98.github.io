abstract: LocoMuJoCo is an open-source benchmark for imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL) on whole-body control. The library couples MuJoCo (CPU) with MJX and the MjWarp backend (GPU) under a single API, exposes 9 humanoid, 3 (musculo-)skeletal, and 4 quadruped embodiments, and ships a curated native motion-capture dataset for every humanoid, to which it can also retarget AMASS and LAFAN1. ## What they do
Humanoid imitation and RL research is spread across single-robot codebases, so changing robots means rebuilding data pipelines, retargeting, simulators and metrics. LocoMuJoCo puts all of these behind one API: 9 humanoids (Unitree H1/H1v2/G1, Atlas, Talos, Apollo, Booster T1, ToddlerBot, Fourier GR1T2), 3 SMPL-derived skeletal and muscle models, and 4 quadrupeds, with mocap data and a companion repository, loco-mujoco-real, for deploying policies on hardware.

## How it works
- **Backends.** Environment code is written once and runs on CPU MuJoCo, MJX or MjWarp. Observations, rewards, control modes, domain randomization and terrain are stateless plug-ins, so the whole step is JIT-compilable.
- **Retargeting.** An SMPL body shape is fitted to each robot once using 15 mimic sites; human clips (native dataset, LAFAN1, AMASS) are then tracked with soft MuJoCo constraints that respect collisions and joint limits. Robot-to-robot transfer goes back through SMPL.
- **Algorithms and metrics.** JAX implementations of PPO, GAIL, AMP, DeepMimic-style tracking, SuperMimic, SAC, TD3 and others, plus Euclidean, DTW and Fréchet trajectory metrics.

## Results
- On a Unitree G1 with full self-collision, MjWarp reaches 4.9×10⁵ steps/s on one RTX 4090, 20-54× more than MJX, and runs 1.25-1.9× faster than mjlab at the environment level.
- On Unitree H1 over 13 clips, PPO reaches a return of about 1530 vs. 1260 for SAC and 1080 for TD3. PPO + DeepMimic beats GAIL by about 2× in return and 5-10× in joint-position error.
- One SuperMimic policy per robot on all 40 LAFAN1 clips (about 50 min on one GPU) completes 99.7% of frames on the X2 Ultra and 99.1% on the G1 in simulation.
- A SuperMimic policy trained on all of BONES-SEED runs a 43 s chain of its dance clips on the real X2 Ultra at 50 Hz using onboard sensing only.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. Sim-to-real tooling covers two robots, and the hardware run is shown as a figure without metrics. Algorithm comparisons use one humanoid and 13 clips, and the LAFAN1 evaluation turns randomization off, so it measures tracking rather than robustness.
