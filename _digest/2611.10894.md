abstract: 'Pre-training large parametric "Vision-Language-Action" (VLA) models on large demonstration datasets has recently yielded promising results for general-purpose manipulation. However, these models still struggle under even small domain shifts relative to the training data, such as changes in camera viewpoint, background, object identity, or robot morphology. Motivated by the success of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in language models, we pursue retrieval to boost VLA performance. Specifically, we seek to leverage retrieval from domain-mismatched, "in-the-wild" demonstration datasets like DROID to guide a VLA''s behavior in a new target domain. To achieve this, we introduce RAGu, which overcomes robotics-specific challenges for RAG: (1) It incorporates simple geometric calibration to transform source demonstrations into the target setup and reduce cross-domain differences. (2) It uses a coarse-to-fine retrieval strategy that selects, from a large pool of pre-warped candidates, the trajectory most compatible with the base VLA. (3) It guides the VLA''s flow-matching inference procedure rather than seeking to retrain the base model or expand its limited context window. We evaluate RAGu across real-world, simulation, sim-to-real transfer, and cross-embodiment humanoid manipulation. We find it improves real-world VLA success rates from 20% to 80% and outperforms the closest-related baselines in sim. Project website: raguvla.github.io'

## What they do
Vision-language-action models degrade under small shifts in viewpoint, background, objects or embodiment. RAGu brings retrieval-augmented generation to VLAs without retraining: it pulls demonstrations of the same task category from a large, mismatched dataset (DROID or MetaWorld), warps them into the target scene, and uses the best one to steer the frozen policy's action denoising.

## How it works
- **Filter and warp.** Demonstrations are filtered by task keywords such as "pick" and "place". Grounding DINO boxes locate the task-relevant objects in the target scene, and each source trajectory's waypoints are shifted onto them. Warping runs offline.
- **Coarse-to-fine retrieval.** Dynamic time warping against one reference trajectory prunes the pool to the top N. At inference, the candidate whose cost gradient has the smallest norm is selected, i.e. the one that requires the least steering away from what the VLA already intends.
- **Guidance.** The selected Cartesian trajectory defines an energy added to the flow-matching velocity. Forward kinematics maps actions to end-effector paths, so no inverse kinematics or joint mapping is needed across embodiments.

## Results
- Real Franka, 5 tasks with 10 rollouts each: pi0.5 20%, Omniguide 40%, RICL 0%, RAGu 80%. On Wipe Table RAGu reaches 100% against 30% for Omniguide.
- RoboLab simulation, 3 tasks: 25.3% for RAGu against 5.3% for pi0.5 and 12% for VLS. Over nine tasks, mean success goes from 23.6% unguided to 28.4% with one retrieved trajectory and 36.4% with a pool of 100.
- Sim-to-real from MetaWorld Sawyer demonstrations: 60% on the Cabinet to Shelf layout, where pi0.5 and Omniguide get 0%.
- Franka demonstrations guiding GR00T-N1.5 on a GR-1 humanoid in RoboCasa: +13.6 points on average, +12.0 for the fine-tuned model.

## Limitations
The source database must contain demonstrations of roughly the target task category. The method is not invariant to camera placement and occlusion, since correspondence failures corrupt the warp; in RoboLab 34.9% of warping attempts fail. Guidance can only reshape motion within the VLA's prior.
