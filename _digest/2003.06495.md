abstract: Optimizing lower-body exoskeleton walking gaits for user comfort requires understanding users' preferences over a high-dimensional gait parameter space. However, existing preference-based learning methods have only explored low-dimensional domains due to computational limitations. To learn user preferences in high dimensions, this work presents LineCoSpar, a human-in-the-loop preference-based framework that enables optimization over many parameters by iteratively exploring one-dimensional subspaces. Additionally, this work identifies gait attributes that characterize broader preferences across users. In simulations and human trials, we empirically verify that LineCoSpar is a sample-efficient approach for high-dimensional preference optimization. Our analysis of the experimental data reveals a correspondence between human preferences and objective measures of dynamicity, while also highlighting differences in the utility functions underlying individual users' gait preferences. This result has implications for exoskeleton gait synthesis, an active field with applications to clinical use and patient rehabilitation.

## What they do
Exoskeleton gaits should be comfortable, but no numeric metric is known to track comfort, so the authors learn from which of two gaits a user prefers. Earlier preference-learning methods handled at most two gait parameters. LineCoSpar scales this to many parameters and is run on the Atalante lower-body exoskeleton over six gait parameters with six able-bodied subjects. The collected preferences are then analyzed to find what makes a gait preferred.

## How it works
- **Preference model.** A Gaussian process posterior over gait utilities is fit to pairwise preferences with a Laplace approximation, using a sigmoid link function that the authors found works better than the Gaussian one of prior work.
- **One line at a time.** Each iteration draws a random line through the current best gait and updates the posterior only over points on that line plus previously tested gaits. The cost is constant in the number of dimensions, where CoSpar's grows exponentially.
- **Thompson sampling.** A utility function is sampled from the posterior and its maximizer is executed; the user compares it with the previous gait or reports no preference.
- **Coactive feedback.** The user may also suggest a change along one parameter, which is stored as a preferred gait.

## Results
- Simulation: on the Hartmann3 benchmark LineCoSpar reaches higher objective values faster than CoSpar and stays robust under noisy preferences. On random 6-dimensional polynomials it samples high-valued actions within about 20 iterations with coactive feedback.
- Human trials: after 30 trials, the predicted best gait was checked against four random gaits. It won all four comparisons for four of six subjects, three for one subject and one for the other.
- A Linear Inverted Pendulum cost and its dynamic-stability term capture the preferences of five of six subjects; a static-stability cost fully predicts those of the remaining subject.

## Limitations
All subjects were able-bodied; subjects with paraplegia likely have different preferences and are left to future work. The method does not account for preferences changing over time. Gaits come from a pre-computed library, and LineCoSpar is not compared against other optimizers because no other preference-based Gaussian process method is tractable in high dimensions.
