However, to be widely applicable across a range of robotic learning scenarios, environments, and tasks, such policies need to handle diverse sensors and action spaces, accommodate a variety of commonly used robotic platforms, and finetune readily and efficiently to new domains. In this work, we aim to lay the groundwork for developing open-source, widely applicable, generalist policies for robotic manipulation. ## What they do
Octo is a generalist robot policy pretrained on 800k demonstrations from 25 Open X-Embodiment datasets, released fully open source: checkpoints, finetuning and pretraining code, and data loaders. It takes a language instruction or a goal image, controls several robots out of the box, and adapts to new cameras, sensors, action spaces or robots by finetuning on about 100 demonstrations.

## How it works
- **Tokenizers.** Language goes through a frozen t5-base encoder; images go through a shallow CNN and are split into 16x16 patches. Missing inputs are masked out, so datasets with different cameras and labels train one model.
- **Backbone and head.** A ViT-style transformer (27M Small, 93M Base) with block-wise causal attention over two frames of history. Learned readout tokens read the sequence without influencing it, and a small diffusion head turns them into a chunk of future actions.
- **Finetuning.** A new input or action space only needs a new encoder or head. The full model is finetuned for 50k steps with one shared recipe, about 5 hours on a 24 GB A5000.

## Results
- **Zero-shot** on WidowX, UR5 and the RT-1 robot: on average 29% higher success than RT-1-X, and similar to the 55B-parameter RT-2-X on WidowX and the RT-1 robot. On WidowX, goal images give 25% higher success than language.
- **Finetuning** with ~100 demos: 72% average over six setups, against 20% for a from-scratch ResNet+Transformer and 15% for VC-1. The setups include force-torque input, joint position control, an unseen ViperX arm and a bimanual ALOHA.
- **Ablations** (WidowX, Octo-Small at 83%): the 11-dataset RT-X mix drops to 60%, Bridge data alone to 43%, a ResNet-50 encoder to 70%, an MSE head to 35% and discretized actions to 18%. Zero-shot success grows from 10M to 93M parameters.

## Limitations
Octo handles wrist cameras poorly: finetuning was often stronger with the third-person camera alone. Language conditioning trails goal images. The authors attribute both to the data, where 27% of episodes have wrist images and 56% have language. Zero-shot success falls to 40% in new environments and 5% on unseen skills. Training uses only optimal demonstrations, and only single- and dual-arm manipulators were tested.
