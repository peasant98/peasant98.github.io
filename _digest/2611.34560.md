tldr: M2AdaptVLA gives a VLA a test-time-training visual memory, a context-gated history of wrist forces and torques, and online adapter updates driven by force-prediction error. Across eight real bimanual tasks it reaches 82.71% mean success, against 51.25% for the best baseline.
abstract: Effective robotic manipulation calls for multimodal memory and rapid adaptation to shifts in contact dynamics. However, many existing policies, which retain either visual or wrench history, may miss key cues from the other modality. Moreover, even with wrench memory, fixed policies can struggle when contact conditions shift at deployment. To this end, we introduce M$^{2}$AdaptVLA, a vision-language-action model that integrates visual and wrench memories with predictive test-time adaptation. For visual memory, the model uses test-time training (TTT) to encode observation history in fast weights. Each entry in the bounded TTT bank combines the corresponding frame's features with a readout from these weights, preserving cues that predate the oldest retained frame. For wrench memory, a context gate controls how much the action expert draws on wrench history as it jointly denoises future actions and wrenches. For predictive test-time adaptation, the auxiliary wrench prediction loss also provides online self-supervision. Using measured wrenches as targets, we update low-rank policy adapters by backpropagating only through the final denoising step. Real-world experiments show that the TTT bank outperforms visual memory baselines on progress tracking, scene recall, and demonstration following. On contact-sensitive tasks, the context gate yields higher success rates than direct injection or gated cross-attention. Predictive test-time adaptation improves robustness to shifts in contact dynamics. On eight tasks requiring visual and wrench memory, M$^{2}$AdaptVLA achieves a mean success rate of 82.71%, a 31.46% absolute gain over the strongest baseline. The project page is available at https://tlf-tlf.github.io/M2AdaptVLAPage/.

## What they do
Some manipulation steps depend on what happened earlier: which drawer was open at the start, which passcode digit comes next, whether a key actually went down. M2AdaptVLA is a vision-language-action model built on WALL-X (Qwen3.5 backbone) that keeps both a visual memory and a history of wrist forces and torques. When contact conditions change at deployment, it keeps adapting itself online.

## How it works
- **TTT bank.** Each frame's visual features update the fast weights of a small test-time-training MLP. A bank entry stores the frame's features plus a gated readout from those weights, so a cue can survive after its frame leaves the five-entry FIFO.
- **Context gate.** A causal TCN encodes the last ten six-axis wrench samples from each wrist. At each joint-attention layer, a gate token reads the instruction, current views, visual memory and proprioception and outputs one scalar that sets how much the wrench history feeds the action expert.
- **Joint denoising.** The flow-matching expert predicts a 32-step action chunk together with the wrenches it expects to measure.
- **Test-time adaptation.** Once measured wrenches arrive, the prediction error is backpropagated through only the final denoising step to update LoRA adapters in the first four layers. One update takes 159.5 ms on an RTX 5090.

## Results
- Eight real tasks on two bimanual platforms (5,953 demonstrations): 82.71% mean success, against 51.25% for π-Mem, the best of five baselines that also include π0.5, MemoryVLA, ForceVLA and TA-VLA. On Open Safe it reaches 66.7%, against 26.7% for MemoryVLA.
- On Open Safe the TTT bank completes 69.7% of keypresses, against at most 35.0% for four other memory designs. It reopens the right drawer in 90% of trials (next best 75%).
- The context gate reaches 79.6% mean stage success on Open Safe, against 69.4% for feeding wrenches directly to the action expert and 48.5% for gated cross-attention.
- With the whiteboard raised or flipped, test-time adaptation raises wiping success from 85.0% to 97.5%. Adapting all 24 layers instead gives 87.5%.

## Limitations
The authors name one limitation: keeping both memories raises inference latency. Each task is scored over 20-30 trials. The contact-shift tests cover only two whiteboard setups and one raised power strip.
