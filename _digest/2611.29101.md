abstract: 'Human videos promise a scalable way to reduce expensive robot data collection, but the embodiment gap limits the resulting policies to coarse behaviors, and they fail at precise tasks such as insertion. Existing methods co-train or finetune a single policy on both data sources, treating each source as equally useful in every state: the policy either absorbs the embodiment error at precision-critical steps or spends scarce robot data relearning free-space motion. We observe that the value of each source depends on the state: human data is useful where precision requirements are low, while robot data is disproportionately valuable near precision-critical states. We therefore propose \textbf{DUO Policy} (Dual-Actor Policy), which decouples a manipulation skill into a hand policy, pretrained on human videos for global free-space motion, and a robot policy, trained on sparse in-hand data for local precise corrections, with a gate learned from human interventions switching between them. This design is robust to moderate embodiment error in human data and generalizes to scene layouts beyond the coverage of the robot data. On tasks such as K-cup coffee making, desk cleanup, and cluttered candy sorting, DUO Policy outperforms the baseline with the highest average success by 12.9 percentage points over four simulated tasks and by 51.7 points over three real-world tasks, and matches robot-only training with 2.75$\times$ less data-collection time. ## What they do
Policies trained on human video cover the workspace cheaply but inherit a pose error from retargeting the hand, which breaks precise steps like insertion. DUO Policy splits a skill between two actors: a hand policy trained on human video does the free-space motion, and a robot policy trained on sparse in-hand robot data does the precision-critical steps. A learned gate decides which one is in control.

## How it works
- **Hand policy.** A diffusion policy on a fused point cloud from calibrated RGB-D cameras, predicting absolute world-frame gripper poses. WiLoR hand poses are retargeted to gripper poses; the human (or, on the robot, the arm) is removed from the point cloud and a gripper rendered in its place.
- **Robot policy.** A separate diffusion policy that sees only the wrist camera and predicts motions relative to the gripper frame, so a correction learned at one object placement carries over to others.
- **Gate from interventions.** The hand policy is deployed and an operator takes over with a SpaceMouse when it is about to fail. Takeover moments label precision-critical states; the gate is trained on them from the in-hand view.

## Results
- Real world (Franka): desk cleanup 17/20 vs 9/20 (DP3) and 0/20 (Masquerade-3D); candy sorting 72/80 vs 28/80 and 47/80; K-Cup coffee 14/20 vs 2/20 for both.
- Candy sorting reaches 90% with 40 minutes of data collection; DP3 needs 110 minutes for 82.5%, 2.75x more.
- Simulation (four MimicGen tasks): 63.8% average vs 50.9% for the best baseline. Without human pretraining success drops by 67.3 points; a single co-trained policy loses 14.7.
- The real-world gate predicts held-out intervention labels with 94-98% accuracy.

## Limitations
The hand policy needs at least one calibrated RGB-D camera for both human data and deployment. Robot data is collected by intervening on one specific hand policy, so retraining or replacing it may require new robot data.
