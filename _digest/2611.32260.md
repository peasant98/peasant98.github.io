abstract: Training humanoid vision-language-action (VLA) models relies heavily on costly real-robot teleoperation. Recent efforts convert egocentric human demonstrations into humanoid training data. However, most methods still require wearable motion trackers and leave important hand-object interaction cues implicit. Many also mix in real-robot teleoperation data for VLA post-training. These dependencies limit scalable data collection, while implicit interaction cues make precise contact harder to learn. To overcome these limitations, we propose EgoLoAF, the first paradigm to derive all training data for humanoid loco-manipulation directly from egocentric demonstrations without wearable motion trackers. We post-train the VLA on the converted data alone, without real-robot teleoperation demonstrations, and use explicit hand-object interaction cues to guide action generation. Specifically, to obtain humanoid action targets, we estimate torso motion from recorded camera poses and reconstruct both hand trajectories from video. From the same recording, we use each hand's current pose relative to the object to represent hand-object affordances. During post-training, we inject these affordances into the action expert through cross-attention while keeping the vision-language backbone frozen. Additionally, we release 128,400 egocentric demonstration episodes spanning 1,000 hours and 1,284 tasks, together with converted humanoid action trajectories and rendered robot-view observations. Across four real-world tasks, EgoLoAF nearly achieves teleoperation performance (73.1 vs. 74.4 in mean Task Progress Score), while achieving a mean 4.03 times improvement in data-collection efficiency.

## What they do
Humanoid VLA policies usually need real-robot teleoperation data. EgoLoAF builds all the training data for humanoid loco-manipulation from egocentric human recordings made with a chest-mounted iPhone, with no wearable motion trackers and no teleoperation. It also gives the policy explicit hand-object affordances: where each hand is relative to the object right now. The authors release 128,400 episodes (1,000 hours, 1,284 tasks) with converted actions and rendered robot views.

## How it works
- **Action targets.** The phone's recorded camera poses, through a calibrated camera-to-torso transform, give a virtual torso trajectory; HaWoR reconstructs both hands. Future torso and hand poses in the current torso frame form 30-step action chunks.
- **Rendered observations.** Human hands and forearms are erased, inpainted, and replaced by rendered G1 arms and O6 hands, with occlusions preserved.
- **Affordances.** Monocular depth, scaled to match the reconstructed hands, feeds an object pose estimator. Object poses and object-relative hand poses become tokens injected into the π0.5 action expert by cross-attention, with the VLM frozen.
- **Deployment.** On a Unitree G1, the same affordances come from forward kinematics and RGB-D object pose; a frozen whole-body controller tracks the predicted torso and hand targets.

## Results
- Four real tasks, 150 human demonstrations each: 73.1 mean task progress against 74.4 for the teleoperation-trained policy; 65.8 vs. 66.0 on the long-horizon cabinet task.
- Data collection took 4.03x fewer person-hours on average, and 8.00x fewer on the cabinet task.
- Affordances raise human-data policies from 53.2 to 73.1, and teleoperation-data policies from 74.4 to 86.3.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. Evaluation covers four tasks, each with its own policy. With affordances added to both, the teleoperation policy still scores higher (86.3 vs. 73.1). Deployment relies on online object pose estimation and an extra arm correction for torso-tracking errors, without which scores drop on every task.
