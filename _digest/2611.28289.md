abstract: Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) have shown strong potential for learning complex robotic behaviors from demonstrations. However, learning safe behavior directly from data requires extensive coverage of safety-critical situations, which is often impractical in real-world settings. Test-time safety filters provide an alternative by correcting unsafe actions during deployment, but these interventions can drive the robot into states poorly covered by the training data, thereby degrading task performance. We introduce InteR-VLA, an intervention-robust framework for VLAs that leverages diverse task-completing demonstrations to broaden task-relevant state coverage and maintain competence under safety interventions. By better aligning the training distribution with safety-filtered deployment under the same data budget, our approach mitigates intervention-induced distribution shift while preserving task performance. Real-world experiments across in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings show that InteR-VLA maintains safety, increases Safe Success Rate up to 90% from 0% for nominal policies, and consistently outperforms nominal-data training on both evaluated VLAs.

## What they do
Control-barrier-function safety filters can stop a vision-language-action policy (VLA) from hitting obstacles at test time, but each correction moves the robot off the paths it was trained on, and the policy then fails the task. InteR-VLA keeps the filter for safety and changes the training data instead: under a fixed budget, demonstrations show many different ways of completing the task, so the states the filter pushes the robot into are covered.

## How it works
- **Safety filter.** GroundingDINO finds the obstacle in the RGB image, depth turns it into a point cloud fit with an ellipsoid, and a CBF quadratic program minimally changes each VLA action so the end-effector ellipsoid never intersects it.
- **Diverse demonstrations.** Obstacle-free demos along curved, left-offset and right-offset paths; no policy rollouts or filter-specific tuning needed.
- **Multimodal avoidance demonstrations.** When hazards are known in advance, demos go around a path-blocking obstacle above, left and right.

## Results
- Real Franka Panda, two tasks (sponge into basket, close a drawer), fine-tuned π0.5 and GR00T N1.5, 50 base plus 150 extra demos, 20 rollouts each.
- In distribution, safe success rate is 0.65-0.90 with diverse or multimodal demos vs 0.05-0.40 for nominal data with the filter; without the filter the nominal policy collides in every episode.
- With unseen obstacle sizes, types and locations, both strategies beat nominal data by at least 0.20, reaching up to 0.90.
- More avoidance modes at the same budget raise safe success; with two obstacles or an unseen object, safe success is at least 0.55 vs 0 and 0.05.

## Limitations
Only two tabletop tasks with one robot. The demonstrations alone do not give safety: without the filter, safety drops to 0.35-0.80. Performance falls as the extra demonstration budget shrinks.
