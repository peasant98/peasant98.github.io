abstract: Studying electrosurgical tasks on a research robot requires control of both instrument motion and energy activation, along with a record of when each action occurs. This paper presents an open-source module for da Vinci X/Xi instruments on a UR5/UR5e robotic arm. Five motors transmit torque through timing belts to spring-loaded drive discs. An electrosurgical interposer accepts software commands, gives the surgeon's foot pedals priority, and records the source of each activation request. In bench testing, the interposer recorded all 12 activation and deactivation events from six pedal presses. Ex vivo demonstrations included monopolar and bipolar coagulation of splenic tissue and monopolar cutting during gallbladder dissection. Optical tracking of the Fenestrated Bipolar Forceps showed that calibration brought pitch and yaw gains from 0.904 and 1.14 to 1.00 and 1.02, respectively. The module enables researchers to record instrument motion, video, and the timing and source of electrosurgical commands during laparoscopic tasks.

## What they do
Research platforms built on commercial arms can drive da Vinci EndoWrist instruments, but existing adapters handle only standard graspers, needle drivers and scissors. None of them can trigger electrosurgical energy from software or log it. This module, part of the open-source ARTS platform, drives da Vinci X/Xi instruments, including the SureForm 45 stapler, on a UR5/UR5e. An interposer board sits between the foot pedals and the generator. It can fire energy from software, gives the pedals priority, and labels every activation as coming from a pedal or from software, so it can serve as an action label for learning from demonstration.

## How it works
- **Driver.** Five DYNAMIXEL XC330 servos (72% smaller in bounding-box volume than the MX-28T they replace) turn the instrument discs through timing belts. Spring plungers push the adapter discs into place as they rotate. Snap-fit clips hold the instrument.
- **Kinematics and homing.** Each instrument type has a calibrated coupling matrix. A current-based routine homes joints by finding their mechanical limits.
- **Energy interface.** An ESP32 board with six relays runs a 10 ms control loop over micro-ROS. Software commands must be renewed every 100 ms and expire after 500 ms. Pressing a pedal cancels software control, and an unpowered board passes the pedals straight through.

## Results
- Software timeout measured 503.1 ± 3.8 ms over 8 trials. A pedal took over 11 ms after software released, and all 12 pedal events were logged in order with the correct source.
- Instrument exchange took 2.41 ± 0.34 s over 20 trials, against 3.39 ± 0.51 s reported for DASH Xi. The module weighs 0.740 kg, or 1.270 kg with the stapler.
- Recalibration moved forceps pitch and yaw gains from 0.904 and 1.14 to 1.00 and 1.02. Homing took 34.9 s with a joint-angle SD of 0.24-0.36°.
- Ex vivo, the module performed suction and monopolar coagulation and bipolar coagulation on spleen, and monopolar cutting during gallbladder dissection.

## Limitations
Stapler firing is not yet tested. The instrument type has to be selected by hand. The bench tests used no instrument and did not time RF output, so they establish interface behavior rather than delivered energy. The board does not cover a hung controller or a welded relay, and hysteresis from the cable drive remains.
