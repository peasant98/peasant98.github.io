abstract: Touch is a crucial sensing modality that provides rich information about object properties and interactions with the physical environment. Humans and robots both benefit from using touch to perceive and interact with the surrounding environment (Johansson and Flanagan, 2009; Li et al., 2020; Calandra et al., 2017). However, no existing systems provide rich, multi-modal digital touch-sensing capabilities through a hemispherical compliant embodiment. Here, we describe several conceptual and technological innovations to improve the digitization of touch. These advances are embodied in an artificial finger-shaped sensor with advanced sensing capabilities. Significantly, this fingertip contains high-resolution sensors (~8.3 million taxels) that respond to omnidirectional touch, capture multi-modal signals, and use on-device artificial intelligence to process the data in real time. Evaluations show that the artificial fingertip can resolve spatial features as small as 7 um, sense normal and shear forces with a resolution of 1.01 mN and 1.27 mN, respectively, perceive vibrations up to 10 kHz, sense heat, and even sense odor. Furthermore, it embeds an on-device AI neural network accelerator that acts as a peripheral nervous system on a robot and mimics the reflex arc found in humans. These results demonstrate the possibility of digitizing touch with superhuman performance. The implications are profound, and we anticipate potential applications in robotics (industrial, medical, agricultural, and consumer-level), virtual reality and telepresence, prosthetics, and e-commerce. Toward digitizing touch at scale, we open-source a modular platform to facilitate future research on the nature of touch.

## What they do
Vision-based tactile sensors like Digit and GelSight mostly capture contact geometry, at camera rates of 30-60 Hz. Digit 360, from FAIR at Meta, is a fingertip-shaped sensor that keeps a high-resolution camera view of a hemispherical gel, adds non-visual channels (vibration, pressure, heat, smell) and puts a neural network accelerator inside the finger for reflex-like control. The design is open-source.

## How it works
- **Optics.** A custom solid-immersion hyper-fisheye lens images the whole dome onto a 240 fps CMOS sensor with 1.1 µm pixels. The gel is a solid volume rather than a shell around an internal light pipe, which removes glints and hotspots.
- **Elastomer.** A 6 µm silver film is grown chemically onto the gel, thinner than painted or airbrushed coatings. The surface scatters light at a 20-25° half-width instead of Lambertian, trading background uniformity against indentation contrast.
- **Other modalities.** Microphones, MEMS pressure, IMU, temperature and gas sensors sit on a stack of modular boards.
- **Edge AI.** A GAP9 accelerator (9-core RISC-V) runs models in the fingertip and can send actions straight to the robot hand.

## Results
- Force: a ResNet-50 regressor predicts normal and shear force with 1.01 mN and 1.27 mN median error at the tip (1.41 and 1.64 mN at the side), without markers. A specular finish beats a Lambertian one (1.17 vs 1.77 mN mean normal-force error).
- Spatial resolution: features as small as 7 µm are resolved at the tip.
- Multimodal: gas sensing identifies six household materials with 91% accuracy. With four sensors on an Allegro hand (~620k samples), IMU data dominates action classification, audio and vision dominate material classification, and all modalities together reach 85.8% average accuracy.
- Reflex arc: 1.2 ms event-to-action on-device, vs 2.5 ms through a host and over 6 ms for Digit.

## Limitations
Apart from the reflex demo, the evaluation is sensor characterization and classification of three actions and three materials; no manipulation task uses the sensor. Resolution drops away from the tip. The authors note that 240 Hz may still miss highly dynamic motion and that on-device gains may not be significant today. The paper's latency numbers differ between sections (6 to 1.2 ms, 4 to 1 ms, 3.1 to 0.7 ms).
