abstract: 'Recovering accurate visible-surface depth from a single RGB-D image and an object mask is essential for robotic grasping and manipulation of transparent objects. Specular reflections cause missing depth, while refraction corrupts nonzero returns with background depth, making raw measurements unreliable as surface anchors. Under a fixed sampling budget, refining an initial single-view prediction within a single search interval either misses large refraction offsets or sacrifices fine local resolution. To resolve this single-view trade-off, MIR-Depth constructs Coordinate-Aware Multi-Range Hypotheses: a 3D-aggregated global inverse-depth volume and depth-axis-filtered pixel-wise local volumes around a prior-guided initial depth. Selective Recurrent Refinement then introduces out-of-bounds lookup masking and validity-masked softmax fusion to iteratively update inverse depth from these volumes using a multi-scale ConvGRU. Finally, Spatially Gated Correction applies a learned per-pixel spatial gate to the full-resolution residual, preserving accurate initial geometry without imposing raw sensor readings as hard constraints. Experiments on TransCG, TODD, TRansPose, ClearPose, and HouseCat6D show that MIR-Depth achieves lower error than ReMake, DFNet, BP-Net, and PSD. Real-robot evaluations confirm accurate surface geometry for 6-DoF grasp pose prediction. Code and pretrained models will be publicly available.'

## What they do
Depth cameras fail on glass and clear plastic: specular reflections leave holes, and refraction returns the depth of the background behind the object. MIR-Depth takes one RGB-D frame and an object mask and recovers the visible surface. It starts from a prior-guided initial depth, as in ReMake, and refines it iteratively, using raw sensor readings as soft evidence rather than as anchors.

## How it works
- **Initial depth.** Transformer and CNN encoders combine RGB, raw depth, the mask and a frozen Depth Anything V2 relative-depth prior; a decoder predicts metric depth.
- **Multi-range hypotheses.** One global inverse-depth volume shared by all pixels (aggregated with a 3D hourglass) covers large refraction offsets; two narrow per-pixel volumes around the initial estimate (±40% and ±10%) keep fine detail. Candidates are scored by image features and agreement with raw depth.
- **Recurrent refinement.** A multi-scale ConvGRU updates inverse depth for 8 steps; out-of-range lookups are masked, and a softmax over the valid ranges weights them.
- **Gated correction.** A per-pixel gate sets how much of the final residual to apply, keeping regions the initial depth already got right.

## Results
- TransCG (23,524 test frames): 6.83 mm MAE vs 8.04 mm for ReMake and 12.57 mm for DFNet; the opaque-scene methods BP-Net and PSD are near 38 mm.
- On corrupted nonzero sensor returns it brings 89.74% of >20 mm outliers back within 20 mm (ReMake: 88.52%).
- Zero-shot on TODD, TRansPose, ClearPose and HouseCat6D it has the lowest MAE of the five methods; on ClearPose 122 mm vs 309 mm for ReMake.
- On an AgileX PIPER arm with a wrist RealSense D435i and SAM masks, its depth yields cleaner GraspNet grasp poses on bottles, containers and clutter. This test is qualitative; no grasp success rates are reported.

## Limitations
A single frame cannot reveal unobserved geometry, and a fully transparent object on a uniform background may show almost no boundary. The spatial gate slightly worsens TransCG numbers (6.81 mm MAE without it), though it gives more continuous surfaces on real captures. Extending to RGB-D streams captured during arm motion is left to future work.
