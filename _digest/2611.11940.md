abstract: Underwater leader-follower formation control requires continuous relative-pose estimation despite degraded sensing conditions and limited onboard computation. This paper presents a reliability-adaptive multimodal perception framework for six-degree-of-freedom relative-pose tracking between two moving underwater robots. The framework integrates complementary visual, acoustic-ranging, and hydrodynamic measurements while accounting for their distinct physical characteristics and time-varying reliability. It adaptively weights modality contributions and models temporal dependencies with linear computational complexity, enabling efficient pose estimation on embedded hardware. Extensive experiments were conducted using a custom-built AUV platform, with 648 pool trials spanning two leader vehicle types, three illumination conditions, and nine trajectory patterns. Evaluation yields an average translational error of 3.8 cm and an average orientation error of 2.4°. On an onboard NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, the fusion network achieves an inference latency of 17.62 ms, providing a 2.69-fold speedup over the evaluated Transformer-based fusion baseline. Closed-loop experiments further demonstrate stable formation maintenance with a moving leader under onboard computational constraints. A multimodal dataset containing measurements from all 648 trials will be made publicly available.

## What they do
For two underwater vehicles to fly in formation, the follower must keep estimating where the leader is, without GPS and with cameras that fail in dark or murky water. The follower here combines three cheap sensors that work at different ranges and conditions: a camera, ultrasonic range sensors, and a pressure array that detects the leader's propeller wake. A network fuses them and leans on whichever is reliable at the moment.

## How it works
- **One encoder per modality.** YOLO segments the leader, and an information-bottleneck encoder compresses the result; an acoustic encoder adds the sensor geometry as a physics prior; a CNN-BiLSTM wake encoder reads pressure sequences, regularized with a KL term.
- **Reliability-weighted fusion.** A small weight generator scores each modality's confidence, and Mamba state-space blocks fuse the features over time in linear time.
- **Closed loop.** The 5 Hz pose estimate feeds a sliding-mode controller on a Jetson AGX Orin aboard a 1 m OpenAUV.

## Results
- Dataset: 648 pool trials (2 leader vehicles, 3 lighting levels, 9 trajectories) with motion-capture ground truth. Average errors are 3.8 cm in position and 2.4° in orientation; yaw is the worst axis at 5.7°.
- In extremely dim light, translation error rises 15.7% and orientation error 26.5%. Removing the wake branch costs the most accuracy (yaw error 5.6° to 6.6°).
- Errors are 20-32% lower than with plain feature concatenation. Accuracy is similar to Transformer fusion, but the method runs 2.69x faster on the Orin (17.62 vs 47.48 ms).
- Holding 2.10 m behind a moving leader, the follower's position RMSE is 12.2 cm, 9.9 cm and 2.6 cm along the three axes.

## Limitations
Tests ran only in a 4 m x 2 m pool; currents, turbidity and larger separations need open-water trials. While the training data was recorded, the follower hovered in place. Closed-loop results are reported for one representative 30 s segment.
