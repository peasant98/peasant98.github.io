abstract: 'World Action Models (WAMs) improve robotic manipulation by jointly modeling visual dynamics and robot actions. However, most existing WAMs model dense short-horizon visual dynamics, capturing local interactions but providing limited long-range task context. Extending prediction horizons increases uncertainty and computation while entangling task-level evolution with fine-grained local dynamics, obscuring their dependencies and distinct roles in action generation. We propose HiLo-WAM, which decouples long-horizon task evolution from local visual dynamics while jointly modeling their interaction with robot actions. A sparse long-horizon visual stream captures historical task context and future evolution, while a dense local dynamics visual stream models short-horizon robot-object interactions. The two streams operate at different temporal scales within a unified dual-DiT architecture and guide actions through structured cross-stream attention. Progressive inference further reduces unnecessary computation through stream-wise partial denoising, joint denoising, and dynamic skipping. Experiments demonstrate strong long-horizon manipulation performance: HiLo-WAM achieves a 93.70% average success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 and a 17.43 progress score on RoboDojo long-horizon tasks, with robust in-domain and OOD performance on the Fourier GR-3 robot. Ablations confirm the complementary contributions of long-horizon task evolution and local interaction dynamics, while temporal-scale analyses highlight the importance of balancing temporal coverage and resolution. Progressive inference further achieves a 2.93x speedup while improving performance.'

## What they do
World action models (WAMs) predict future video and robot actions together, but usually over a short window, which says little about how a long task is progressing. HiLo-WAM splits the video prediction into two streams at different time scales: a sparse one for task history and coarse future, and a dense one for local robot-object interaction. It is tested on RoboTwin 2.0, long-horizon RoboDojo tasks, and three bimanual block tasks on a Fourier GR-3 humanoid.

## How it works
- **Dual DiT.** A Video DiT initialized from Wan2.2-5B and an Action DiT exchange information through joint attention at every layer. All three streams are trained with flow matching, each at its own sampled noise level.
- **Two time scales.** The long-horizon stream holds 8 past and 8 future keyframes spaced 4x wider than the dense local stream, which predicts short-horizon frames; actions come in 32-step chunks.
- **Structured attention.** Local and action tokens see the history and the nearest future keyframe, but not the distant future, so control does not lean on uncertain long-range predictions.
- **Progressive inference.** Visual streams stop after 5 of 10 denoising steps and are cached while actions finish; steps are skipped when consecutive predictions barely change.

## Results
- RoboTwin 2.0 (50 bimanual tasks): 93.70% average success, vs. 92.80% for AHA-WAM and 91.85% for Fast-WAM, without embodied pretraining. Removing the long-horizon stream drops it to 90.58%.
- RoboDojo long-horizon tasks: progress score 17.43 vs. 9.14 for Fast-WAM.
- Real GR-3 (50 h of teleoperated data, 10 trials per task): in-domain progress 6.9/10 and 33% success vs. 5.9 and 23% for π0.5. Under lighting, background and clutter changes: 6.0 and 30%, while Fast-WAM drops to 0% success.
- Progressive inference cuts latency from 1114 ms to 380 ms (2.93x) with no loss in success.

## Limitations
The model is bound by the video backbone's semantic understanding; tasks that need explicit subtask decomposition, progress estimation or higher-level reasoning may stay hard. Real-world success is about one in three trials, from 10 trials per task.
