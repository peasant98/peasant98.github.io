## What they do
Handheld Universal Manipulation Interfaces (UMIs) collect demonstrations without a robot, but existing ones need external base stations, use rigid fingers that give no force signal, or use parallel jaws that limit grip. This paper builds on the YUBI interface: no base stations, battery-powered, and soft angled fingers that match the robot's gripper and deform visibly under load as a visual proxy for force. With it the authors collect 99.7 hours (over 51,000 episodes) of single-object pick and place over 53 objects and test what the data does for a π0.5 policy.

## How it works
- **Hardware.** Soft TPU fingertips, 1.5x longer than YUBI's, open up to 90°. A 450 g aluminum robot gripper shares their dimensions, fingertips and camera placement, so demonstrations map one-to-one. Meta Quest 3S controllers give the gripper poses; the headset, worn around the neck, carries a top camera. A full set costs about 3,000 USD and runs about 19 hours per charge.
- **Frame-free state and actions.** The state is the left gripper's pose in the right gripper's frame, both apertures and the gravity direction in each gripper frame; actions are 30-step chunks relative to the current pose. Nothing depends on the tracking frame, so no calibration to the robot is needed.
- **Training.** π0.5 is mid-trained on 92 hours over 49 objects from 5 operators, then fine-tuned on one held-out object at a time with 1.5-2.4 hours of data.

## Results
50 trials per policy and object.
- A top camera, present in only 48 of the 92 hours and dropped out half the time in training, raises mean success on 5 seen objects from 47% to 60%.
- On 4 held-out objects on the R1 Pro, mid-training raises success after fine-tuning from 35% to 57% (45% to 74% on box, porridge and soup). Both fail on a wire that needs a precise grasp (4% and 6%). Without fine-tuning, the mid-trained policy succeeds in 7% of trials.
- On the R1 Lite, which cannot reach 83% of the mid-training episodes and is fine-tuned on teleoperation data, mid-training still raises success from 80% to 89%.

## Limitations
The handhelds are cabled to a battery and laptop, and tracking degrades when the grippers leave the headset cameras' view. Evaluation covers pick and place in one environment on a few objects, so per-object numbers are noisy. Policies struggle with precise grasps and often grip too loosely, letting a bottle slip; the state representation was chosen from preliminary tests, not a full ablation.
