## What they do
World-action models (WAMs) generate future video and actions together, but execute every action chunk without checking it, and their demonstrations rarely contain collisions, slips or drops. WAtcher uses the policy's own video model as a forward model: before each chunk runs, it imagines the outcome, and a small separately trained head, Safe-Guard, decides whether to execute it or retreat.

## How it works
- **Compact WAM.** A 1.0B policy: a 0.8B video expert distilled from Wan2.2-TI2V-5B and a 0.2B flow-matching action expert, linked by bidirectional joint attention. Fixing the action tokens to a candidate chunk lets the video expert imagine its low-resolution outcome in two denoising steps.
- **Safe-Guard.** A 1.7M-parameter Transformer fuses CNN tokens of the current and imagined views, the robot state and one token per command. It scores each command and the whole chunk; above a threshold, a bounded recovery controller retreats (in simulation, toward the start of the last accepted chunk). The check adds 32 ms to the policy's 92 ms.
- **Hazard data.** An automatic pipeline perturbs expert chunks in RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO and on a real UR7e, executes them and labels each window from recorded events (unintended contacts, losses of support, motion errors, semantic dangers such as a knife near a hand). An LLM agent reviews ambiguous windows and a human verifies real-robot labels: 25,434 windows, 7,392 risky.

## Results
- Risk detection: 92.1% balanced accuracy on a 1,352-window blind set (fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B 89.4%, ContactGuard-style monitor 87.9%) and 94.2% on a 300-question benchmark, where ten zero-shot multimodal LLMs reach at most 73.0%.
- Closed loop on RoboTwin (400 paired trials): hazardous trials drop from 27.0% to 10.8% (ContactGuard-style 15.3% at the same rejection rate). On four unseen tasks: 29.8% to 17.3%.
- Task success: 90.4% on RoboTwin 2.0 and 82.4% on ManiSkill3 with 10 demos per task (pi0.5 81.1%).
- UR7e, five tasks, 200 trials: 74.0% with Safe-Guard (75.5% without) against 59.5% for pi0.5 and 48.5% for Fast-WAM. In staged hazards, Safe-Guard cuts hazards from 43 to 6 of 60 runs, with 3 false alarms in 60 obstacle-free runs.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. On the real robot Safe-Guard had to be adapted with 258 human-verified windows, the hazards were staged, and success dipped slightly (75.5% to 74.0%). It only judges hazards within about one chunk (roughly 1 s), and on unseen tasks it leaves 17.3% of trials hazardous.
