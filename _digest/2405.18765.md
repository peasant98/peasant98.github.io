abstract: The current electroencephalogram (EEG) based deep learning models are typically designed for specific datasets and applications in brain-computer interaction (BCI), limiting the scale of the models and thus diminishing their perceptual capabilities and generalizability. Recently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have achieved unprecedented success in text processing, prompting us to explore the capabilities of Large EEG Models (LEMs). We hope that LEMs can break through the limitations of different task types of EEG datasets, and obtain universal perceptual capabilities of EEG signals through unsupervised pre-training. Then the models can be fine-tuned for different downstream tasks. However, compared to text data, the volume of EEG datasets is generally small and the format varies widely. For example, there can be mismatched numbers of electrodes, unequal length data samples, varied task designs, and low signal-to-noise ratio. To overcome these challenges, we propose a unified foundation model for EEG called Large Brain Model (LaBraM). LaBraM enables cross-dataset learning by segmenting the EEG signals into EEG channel patches. Vector-quantized neural spectrum prediction is used to train a semantically rich neural tokenizer that encodes continuous raw EEG channel patches into compact neural codes. We then pre-train neural Transformers by predicting the original neural codes for the masked EEG channel patches. The LaBraMs were pre-trained on about 2,500 hours of various types of EEG signals from around 20 datasets and validated on multiple different types of downstream tasks. Experiments on abnormal detection, event type classification, emotion recognition, and gait prediction show that our LaBraM outperforms all compared SOTA methods in their respective fields. Our code is available at https://github.com/935963004/LaBraM.

## What they do
EEG deep learning models are usually built for one dataset and task, because recordings differ in electrode count, length and task design, and labeled sets are small. LaBraM (Large Brain Model) is a single Transformer pre-trained without labels on 2,534.78 hours of EEG from about 20 public and self-collected datasets, then fine-tuned for each downstream task.

## How it works
- **Channel patches.** Each EEG channel is cut into 1-second patches (200 samples at 200 Hz). A temporal convolutional encoder embeds each patch, and learnable temporal and spatial (electrode) embeddings are added, so one model accepts any channel set and length, up to 256 patches.
- **Neural tokenizer.** A vector-quantized tokenizer maps patches to a codebook of 8192 codes. It is trained to regress the Fourier amplitude and phase of each patch, since directly reconstructing raw EEG did not converge.
- **Masked EEG modeling.** Half of the patches are masked and the Transformer predicts their tokens; the inverted (symmetric) mask is also used, reusing the same tokens.
- **Three sizes.** Base (5.8M parameters), Large (46M) and Huge (369M).

## Results
- TUAB abnormal detection: AUROC 0.9022 (Base) to 0.9162 (Huge), against 0.8815 for the best baseline, BIOT.
- TUEV event classification: balanced accuracy 0.6409 (Base) to 0.6616 (Huge), against 0.5281 for BIOT.
- SEED-V emotion recognition: accuracy 0.4095 to 0.4102, against 0.3802 for BIOT. MoBI gait prediction: the Base model is competitive with the best baseline (R2 0.2876 vs 0.2911), and Large and Huge exceed it (R2 up to 0.3145).
- Training from scratch without pre-training causes a steep performance drop. On TUAB the Base model with 500 hours of pre-training data already exceeds its 2,500-hour result, while the Huge model keeps improving as data grows.

## Limitations
The data and model are still far smaller than those of large vision and language models. LaBraM needs full fine-tuning for each downstream task, which is costly in computation and memory; linear probing is much worse (0.3461 balanced accuracy on TUEV). It is trained on EEG only, with no other modalities. The tokenizer reconstructs phase less well than amplitude.
