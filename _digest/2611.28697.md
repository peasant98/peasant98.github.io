abstract: 'Tactile feedback benefits contact-rich manipulation, yet deploying tactile sensors introduces additional hardware and maintenance requirements. Tactile experience can still provide effective supervision for policies that operate without tactile sensors at deployment. Tactile imagination methods such as TacImag remove this hardware dependency by predicting tactile observations, but retain a reconstruction stage whose errors can propagate into action generation. We present TacitTouch, a distillation framework that transfers tactile-guided behavior directly into a vision-only policy. The core shift is from "learn to imagine what touch would feel like" to "learn to behave as if you could feel touch": the student directly learns tactile-guided action patterns without reconstructing tactile observations. A frozen visuotactile teacher supervises the student''s denoising predictions and intermediate action representations at the same noisy action and flow time. Training also uses demonstrated-action supervision. Deployment retains only the visual policy, with no tactile encoder, expert, or generator. Across four simulated UniVTAC tasks and four real-world tasks, TacitTouch achieves absolute improvements of 16.0% and 22.5% in mean success rate over vision-only control, respectively. The absolute gaps to the teacher using measured tactile input are only 2.0% and 3.75%, respectively. In robustness tests, it also outperforms TacImag, with absolute gains of up to 20.0% in mean success rate. Project page: https://pier-lab.github.io/tacittouch/'

## What they do
Tactile sensors help contact-rich manipulation but add hardware to deploy and maintain. TacImag avoids sensors at deployment by predicting touch from vision, but keeps a tactile generator in the action path, so its prediction errors carry into actions. TacitTouch instead trains a vision-only π0.5 student to act like a teacher that does feel touch, without ever reconstructing tactile images.

## How it works
- **Teacher and student.** The teacher is a frozen, task-trained SimpleTouch policy: π0.5 plus a T3 tactile encoder and a tactile expert fed GelSight images. The student has the same architecture without the tactile modules and starts from pretrained π0.5 weights.
- **Distillation.** At a noisy action and flow time taken from the student's own ten-step denoising trajectory, the student matches the teacher's denoising velocity and the hidden states of all 18 action-expert layers.
- **Demonstrations.** A separate flow-matching loss on demonstrated actions. Training is offline on 50 demonstrations per task, with no environment rollouts.
- **Deployment.** Only the visual backbone and action expert run: 3.35B parameters and 0.337 s per call, 25.8% faster than TacImag.

## Results
- Simulation (four UniVTAC tasks): 77.5% mean success, against 61.5% for vision-only π0.5, 73.8% for TacImag, 65.5% for HapticVLA and 79.5% for the teacher with measured touch.
- Real robot (Franka FR3, wrist camera, 20 trials per task): 72.5% mean, against 50.0% vision-only, 55.0% TacImag and 76.25% for the teacher. Chip Moving reaches 100%, against 55% vision-only.
- Robustness: with camera images at half brightness, the real-world mean is 66.25% against 46.25% for TacImag; under simulated camera yaw shifts it is 58.5% against 43.3%.
- Ablations: without feature distillation the simulated mean falls to 65.0% (Insert Tube 92% to 55%). With matched distillation-only objectives, a vision-only teacher gives 66.0% and the visuotactile teacher 74.5%.

## Limitations
Transfer is uneven. On Pull-out Key, Insert Hole and Grasp Classify, touch helps the teacher a lot (Insert Hole 25% to 85%), but the student stays near vision-only (25% on Insert Hole). Insert USB stays hard on the real robot, at 25% even for the teacher. Training still needs demonstrations recorded with tactile sensors.
