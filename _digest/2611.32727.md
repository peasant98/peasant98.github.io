abstract: Reconstructing a scene for physics simulation requires recovering its spatial layout and the support relationships that govern its behavior. We present Support2Sim, a Real2Sim system that reconstructs simulation-ready scenes from a single RGB image. Our design objective is to maximize source fidelity, support realization, and stability within a practical time budget for scene generation. Support2Sim infers a support graph, which a contact-guided pose initializer uses to propose poses for objects with multiple supports. The initializer generates and ranks candidates by geometric fit and source-image agreement. A coarse-to-fine refiner allocates a fixed search budget across supported objects and evaluates pose updates with physics rollouts. After an accepted update, it searches from the new pose at a finer translation and rotation scale. We benchmark our method on 50 scenes spanning four datasets. Compared with the strongest baseline, Support2Sim improves source-mask IoU after 10 seconds by 21.4 pp, realizes 87.1% versus 73.2% of intended supports, and reduces mean object drift from 79.6 to 45.3 mm. See our project page at https://support2sim.github.io for more details.

## What they do
Building a physics scene from one photo often leaves gaps or overlaps where objects should touch, so the scene shifts once gravity is on. Support2Sim reconstructs rigid-body scenes from a single RGB image while keeping agreement with the image, the support contacts it infers (what rests on what) and stability, within a fixed time budget.

## How it works
- **Evidence and support graph.** A VLM lists the objects and infers an acyclic support graph; SAM 3, Depth Anything 3, SAM 3D Objects and FoundationPose supply masks, depth, meshes and initial poses.
- **Graph-guided seating.** Objects are seated in support order on a gravity-aligned ground plane: the smallest lateral shift that keeps the center of mass over the support, then lowered to a 0.1 mm gap.
- **Contact-guided initializer.** For objects on two supports, such as a spoon across two dishes, matched contact-point pairs give up to 6,000 candidate poses; the feasible one with the best rendered source-mask IoU wins.
- **Coarse-to-fine refiner.** Within 60 s it tests pose updates for the worst support groups in 2 s PyBullet rollouts and halves the search range after each accepted update.

## Results
- On 50 scenes from four datasets, mean IoU goes from 52.6% at release to 51.7% after 10 s; SAM3D + FoundationPose drops from 70.2% to 20.9%.
- It realizes 325/373 intended supports versus 273/373 for SimFoundry and cuts mean drift from 79.6 to 45.3 mm, taking 6.0-18.8 min per scene.
- In the ablation on 16 contact-rich scenes, graph-guided seating adds the most: 44 supports and 27.6 pp IoU.
- Franka BC + DART policies in four reconstructed YCB-Video scenes average 91.0% success in simulation; plain BC reaches at most 3.1%.

## Limitations
Results depend on the VLM's support graph and on SAM 3D and FoundationPose. Only rigid objects are handled, policies were tested only in simulation, and in the HyperSim dining room drift is 761.0 mm versus 26.9 mm for SimFoundry.
