abstract: Online surface reconstruction from LiDAR scans requires observation models that respect the sensor's physical measurement geometry. Unlike unordered point sets, LiDAR returns carry anisotropic range noise along known rays, which also implicitly identify free space. We introduce the Occupancy Planar Patch (OPP), an incremental bounded planar patch representation that evaluates surface likelihoods and free space directly in range-space, allowing sensor noise, plane uncertainty, and ray clearance to be explicitly modelled during reconstruction. A single ray-to-patch association weight jointly updates plane geometry, refines surface boundaries, and blends camera imagery. The method is evaluated using six sequences from the Oxford Spires and Multi-Campus datasets. OPP achieves the highest precision and lowest reconstruction error among evaluated methods, while producing stored maps among the most compact. By conservatively admitting surfaces only where geometric evidence is certain, OPP prioritises structural precision over unconstrained completeness, which is well suited for architectural modelling. Geometric reconstruction operates in real time, requiring 81 ms per frame on a representative sequence, with texture projection adding 134 ms.

## What they do
Building facades and interiors are mostly flat, and architectural modelling wants walls, window reveals and other elements as separate surfaces rather than one mesh. OPP reconstructs a scene online from LiDAR scans, camera images and given sensor poses as a set of bounded, textured planar patches. All inference happens in range-space, along each LiDAR ray, where the sensor noise acts and where the space in front of each return is known to be empty.

## How it works
- **Representation.** Each patch is an infinite supporting plane plus a continuous 2D occupancy field made of compactly supported radial basis kernels. The patch boundary is the field's 0.5 contour, and a texture atlas stores colour.
- **Soft ray assignment.** For each ray, every candidate patch predicts a 1D Gaussian range that combines range noise, pose noise, plane uncertainty and surface roughness. A Dirichlet-process mixture, with a geometric-certainty prior in place of point counts, gives each patch a responsibility; a new patch is created only when no existing one explains the return.
- **Updates.** The responsibility weights an EKF update of the plane and an online GGAP-RBF update of the boundary. Rays passing through a patch to a farther surface carve free space, and overlapping coplanar patches suppress each other.
- **Texture and memory.** Images are blended onto patches with A-buffer visibility; disk-backed paging keeps resident memory bounded as the map grows.

## Results
- Six sequences from the Oxford Spires and Multi-Campus datasets, scored against a survey-grade tripod scan and compared with Puma, PINGS, ImMesh and VDBFusion, all given ground-truth poses. OPP has the lowest mean accuracy error and the highest 5 cm precision on all six (Keble03: 0.030 m and 86.2%, versus 0.052 m and 76.7% for VDBFusion).
- Stored maps are among the smallest: 30.2 MiB on Keble03 versus 30.6 to 1,744.7 MiB for the baselines.
- On an Apple M2 laptop, geometry takes a median 62-81 ms per frame on Oxford Spires (about 30k points), within the 10 Hz sensor rate; texturing adds 98-134 ms.
- Replacing the mixture with hard nearest-neighbour assignment grows the Keble03 map from 30.2 to 110.9 MiB and raises the error to 0.033 m.

## Limitations
OPP adds surfaces only where the evidence is certain, so completeness and recall suffer, most at long range and on foliage: 5 cm recall on kth_day_10 is 28.2% versus 60.5% for ImMesh. It needs poses from elsewhere, patch edges are not yet joined into closed geometry, and texture quality is not evaluated quantitatively.
