## What they do
Human hand motion can guide dexterous robots, but one camera leaves depth and scale ambiguous, and lab capture rigs don't reach everyday scenes. WinDEX records with CyberCap, a low-cost head-worn fisheye stereo camera with an IMU. From that footage it recovers metric 3D hand poses in a gravity-aligned world frame, which then drive RL policies (Hand2Policy) and live arm–hand teleoperation (Hand2Teleop).

## How it works
- **Annotation.** MANO hands are initialized from WiLoR and stereo depth, then fit to human-reviewed 2D keypoints in both fisheye views. Temporal refinement switches between smooth, occlusion and rapid-motion modes. Fits with more than 15 px reprojection error are dropped, and cuVSLAM camera poses place the hands in the world frame.
- **WinDEX-Net.** An end-to-end fisheye stereo network (VGGT-Omega and DINO features, ray and calibration conditioning, hand crops, odometry-conditioned temporal context) localizes and reconstructs both hands. Its predictions seed new annotations, which grew the labels from 20 h to a 1,000 h corpus from more than 200 operators.
- **Distillation.** WinDEX-Window (116M parameters, 43.61 FPS on an RTX 4090) is for offline use. WinDEX-Stream (8.92M parameters, 171 FPS, causal) is for live control.
- **Robot interfaces.** Wrist motion drives the arm through inverse kinematics, and finger articulation is retargeted to the hand.

## Results
- Against optical motion capture, WinDEX-Window has 15.70 mm wrist error and 16.68 mm MPJPE. The best fine-tuned baselines reach 27.45 mm (UmeTrack) and 23.48 mm (HaWoR).
- In the world frame with cuVSLAM, joint error (W-MPJPE) is 37.29 mm vs 97.65 mm for fine-tuned HaWoR. In-the-wild reprojection error is 14.5 px vs 22.42–26.72 px for fine-tuned baselines.
- RL on Inspire and Wuji hands averages 61% simulated grasp–lift–place success, vs 8% with WiLoR and 17% with HaWoR references. Policies transfer to a real Wuji hand on all five objects.
- Hand2Teleop drives a 7-joint arm and a 20-joint Wuji hand at 30 Hz, tracking the operator only with the headset.

## Limitations
During long occlusions the last confident pose is held, so articulation the cameras can't see is not recovered. All training data comes from one capture device. Real-robot policy runs use privileged object and arm poses from an external camera and FoundationPose. Teleoperation is shown as task examples without success rates. WinDEX-Stream's wrist error (42.7 mm) is well above WinDEX-Window's 27.1 mm on the same recordings.
