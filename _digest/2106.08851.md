abstract: Vision-based tactile sensors have the potential to provide important contact geometry to localize the objective with visual occlusion. However, it is challenging to measure high-resolution 3D contact geometry for a compact robot finger, to simultaneously meet optical and mechanical constraints. In this work, we present the GelSight Wedge sensor, which is optimized to have a compact shape for robot fingers, while achieving high-resolution 3D reconstruction. We evaluate the 3D reconstruction under different lighting configurations, and extend the method from 3 lights to 1 or 2 lights. We demonstrate the flexibility of the design by shrinking the sensor to the size of a human finger for fine manipulation tasks. We also show the effectiveness and potential of the reconstructed 3D geometry for pose tracking in the 3D space.

## What they do
Earlier GelSight sensors measure high-resolution 3D contact geometry but are too bulky for robot fingers, and the slimmer GelSlim is not optically designed for photometric stereo. GelSight Wedge is a compact finger that meets both the optical and the mechanical constraints. The paper compares 3D reconstruction with 3, 2 and 1 lights, shrinks the sensor to human-finger size, and uses the reconstructed point cloud for pose tracking.

## How it works
- **Optics.** Lensless LED arrays sit on three sides of a 4.5 mm acrylic block; the tip has no light so it can pick up small objects. Gray filters cut internal reflections (a filter passing 1/4 of the light cuts them to 1/16), diffusers light the boundary, and a curved gel evens out illumination. A $16 Raspberry Pi camera (120° FOV) views the gel through a mirror tilted 30°.
- **Color to gradient.** A small MLP (5-32-32-32-2) maps each pixel's color and position to surface gradients. It is trained on 32 presses of a 2.4 mm-radius calibration ball, and a fast Poisson solver integrates the gradients into depth.
- **Missing lights.** When one axis has no light, a U-Net trained on 10k synthetic random surfaces infers the missing horizontal gradient from the vertical one, with no extra real data.
- **Pose tracking.** ICP on the reconstructed point cloud tracks a cube by its corner at about 10 Hz, given the initial pose.

## Results
- Reconstructions of a ball array, a Lego block, a rubber tyre, a screw cap and an M2 screw; the authors report more fidelity than GelSlim.
- Gradient angle error (x/y): RGB 2.25°/2.19°, RG 2.33°/2.29°. With two opposing lights (RB) the U-Net cuts the x error from 5.71° to 2.66°; with one light, from 5.60° to 2.90°.
- Marker gradients are interpolated in 10 ms at 200×150 with nearest-neighbour; linear and cubic take 60 and 70 ms.

## Limitations
Markers leave small bumps unless their gradients are interpolated, and gel deformation and shadows distort the shape of sharp objects. A single light still produces artifacts. Pose tracking needs the initial pose, and its error is not analysed. The tip thickness is set by the LED size.
