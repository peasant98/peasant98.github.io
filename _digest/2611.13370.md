## What they do
A humanoid dancing to live music has to commit each chunk of motion before it hears the music that chunk goes with, and it cannot revise motion it has already sent to the robot. AHEADance generates reference motion directly in Unitree G1 coordinates, 0.533 s at a time, from streaming audio. A fixed whole-body tracker (SONIC) executes it.

## How it works
- **ListenAhead.** A frozen MRT-2 music model forecasts 14 hidden states reaching about 0.5 s past the audio heard so far. The generator attends to them with a timing term for when each state is expected and a reliability weight for how far to trust it.
- **Commit-aligned codec.** Each 16-frame chunk is a root trajectory, discrete codes and continuous corrections, sampled in that order by diffusion and decoded once from the pose and velocity of the last committed chunk.
- **Commit Forcing.** Training reconstructs and commits the 8 preceding chunks with the model itself, so both the latent history and the decoded boundary pose come from model output, as at inference.
- **Data.** 61.7 h of dance from six public datasets retargeted to the G1. Standing segments in the data let the robot stop when the music stops, with no rule.

## Results
- On 42 songs unseen in training, AHEADance has the highest sync accuracy (28.6%) and song-matching score (MMR 0.187, against at most 0.046) of the compared generators, and the lowest kinetic FID (18.8 against at least 23.7).
- Removing the forecast channel lowers sync accuracy from 24.2% to 17.4%. Commit Forcing cuts low-activity windows from 83.5% to 10.3% (real dance 9.7%).
- A chunk is committed 0.13 s after its audio cutoff. SONIC tracks the references in simulation without a reset in 99.5% of 369 trials.
- Physical G1, 12 runs: the robot came to near-standing after each of 20 music stops and danced after each of 20 starts, with no fall and a median joint tracking error of 5.5 degrees.

## Limitations
Kinetic diversity stays below real dance (2.22 against 6.22). Forecasts do not anticipate abrupt changes such as a sudden stop, so the robot reacts afterwards. The robot moves about 1.2 s behind the music the audience hears. The generator gets no measured robot state and runs off-board on a workstation; music scores are computed on the references, not the executed motion.
