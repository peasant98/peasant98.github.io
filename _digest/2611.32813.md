abstract: 'Long-horizon manipulation requires robots to translate task intent into physical interactions as the scene changes. Vision-language models (VLMs) offer semantic and spatial reasoning, while visuomotor policies provide fine-grained control. Yet ambiguous goals and dependencies between actions make their integration challenging. We introduce Atomic Policy, a framework that composes learned manipulation skills through grounded steering and execution feedback. A VLM estimates the symbolic state and goal, and a PDDL solver constructs a skill plan. An agentic harness steers each skill through language, visual pointing, or both, and verifies its outcome to guide phase transitions and replanning. A common phase-level interface supports specialized policies and generalist action models. Experiments in simulation and on a real robot show how Atomic Policy improves task following and supports recovery in complex, long-horizon manipulation. ## What they do
Atomic Policy splits long-horizon manipulation into short learned skills ("atomic phases") and lets a VLM-driven harness decide which skill to run, on what target, and when it is done. The same interface drives per-skill diffusion policies and generalist models (π0.5, MolmoBot). It is tested on three simulated suites and three real Franka tasks whose goals are ambiguous or given only visually.

## How it works
- **Planning.** Given a hand-written PDDL domain, a VLM estimates the current predicates and the goal from the image, and a breadth-first solver returns the skill sequence.
- **Steering.** Each phase gets a templated language prompt ("grasp the blue cube and lift it"), a 2D image point from MolmoPoint-8B, or both. On the real robot, a wrist-camera point is kept up to date by projecting a 3D target through the arm's forward kinematics.
- **Checking.** A VLM checker periodically compares the scene with a phase checklist and returns done, not_done or replan; a phase timeout also triggers replanning from the current observation.

## Results
- Simulation, with CLIP diffusion skills: 78.7% success on Push 5 Blocks and 64.0% on the drawer task, against 8.0% and 40.0% for the best end-to-end baselines. Without replanning the drawer task drops to 17.8%, without points to 4.4%.
- MolmoSpaces pick-and-place: 48.5% vs 43.5% for fine-tuned MolmoBot.
- Real Crossword with unseen words: end-to-end MolmoBot and π0.5 score 0; their Atomic versions score 72.5 and 60.0, Atomic + DP 90.0. Baseline failures are mostly wrong letters or order (36 of 39); Atomic failures are mostly grasp or place errors (25 of 31).

## Limitations
Phases, PDDL domains and checker prompts are written by hand per task family, and demonstrations need phase and point labels. VLM calls add latency: on in-distribution Stacking, successful Atomic + π0.5 trials take 128.6 s against 61.6 s for fine-tuned π0.5. Replanning cannot fix imprecise skills (Atomic + DP succeeds in 20% of in-distribution Stacking trials), and on planar pushing direct gpt-6-astra control does better (98.0% vs 86.3%).
