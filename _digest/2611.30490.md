abstract: 'Egocentric human manipulation data offer a scalable resource for training dexterous robot policies. However, how effectively robots can learn and benefit from these data remains unclear due to the embodiment gap between humans and robots. We present EgoStudy, a systematic, controlled study of egocentric data for robot learning. Holding the robot dataset fixed, we vary the scale and diversity of egocentric data, data processing strategies, and co-training configurations. We investigate three questions: (1) Which data processing and pretraining choices most effectively exploit egocentric data? (2) Which components of robot action prediction benefit most from scaling these data? (3) How do scaling effects differ between vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs)? Our experiments yield three main findings. First, data diversity, alignment between trajectories, and detailed language annotations are key factors in effective transfer. Second, scaling egocentric data yields larger gains in predicting end-effector translation than in predicting fine-grained finger movements. Third, WAMs perform better when egocentric data are limited, whereas VLAs exhibit greater gains as the amount of egocentric data increases. Together, these findings provide practical guidance on processing and using egocentric data to train VLAs and WAMs, while clarifying the benefits and limitations of scaling these data.'

## What they do
Earlier studies of learning from egocentric human video differ in robot data, architectures and metrics, so it is unclear what transfers. EgoStudy fixes the robot data and the training budget and varies only the egocentric data: its scale, curation, captions, source mix and temporal sampling. A vision-language-action model (VLA) and a world-action model (WAM) are compared under the same protocol.

## How it works
- **Shared action space.** Human and robot hands map to one 108-D target: per hand, a wrist pose and five fingertip poses (3D position plus 6D rotation), predicted as 32-step chunks.
- **Models.** The VLA pairs a Qwen3-VL-4B backbone with a GR00T-style action expert. The WAM is Fast-WAM on a Wan2.1-I2V-14B video backbone and predicts future video and actions jointly.
- **Data.** 75% of pretraining samples are egocentric (self-collected plus EgoDex, HOI4D, HOT3D and VITRA). A real bimanual XHand robot supplies 20% and RoboCasa-GR1 simulation 5%, fixed across runs.
- **Evaluation.** Wrist translation, wrist rotation and fingertip error on held-out robot episodes over 3 seeds, plus closed-loop success in RoboCasa-GR1 after posttraining (240 trials).

## Results
- From 1% to 100% of the egocentric pool, wrist translation error falls 20.2% (VLA) and 18.8% (WAM) on XHand, and 14.6% and 9.6% on RoboCasa-GR1. The VLA gains more; the WAM has lower error.
- RoboCasa-GR1 success rises from 38.3% to 43.8% for the VLA and from 39.6% to 45.4% for the WAM.
- Curated, semantically complete clips beat unsplit recordings and detailed captions beat two-word ones. EgoDex-only pretraining gives 2.8-5.7x the error of a mixed-source pool.
- Wrist rotation and fingertip errors show no consistent improvement with more human data. With the smaller Wan2.2-5B backbone, the WAM struggles to turn more egocentric data into lower error.

## Limitations
Closed-loop tests are simulation only, with rollouts at three data fractions; the real XHand robot is scored only by offline action error. The gains concentrate in wrist translation, not the finger and wrist-rotation motions dexterous manipulation needs; the authors hypothesize that label errors from video and human-robot hand differences limit transfer.
