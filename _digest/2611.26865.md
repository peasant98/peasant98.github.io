abstract: 'Imitation learning for surgical robots is limited by the cost of collecting demonstrations. Meanwhile, many robot-assisted procedures are recorded as stereo video, yet policies cannot learn from them directly; clinical video carries no action labels. We present FlowScope, which pretrains a surgical policy network on unlabeled clinical video by forecasting scene flow, the metric 3D motion of scene points, with labels computed by off-the-shelf stereo depth and optical flow models. We pretrain the trunk of an Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) on a dataset of 1,149 clinical procedures (1.35M clips) and discard the flow head afterward, so the deployed policy is a standard ACT with no added inference cost. On a real surgical robotic system, FlowScope raises needle pickup success from 54% to 90% and first-handover success on needle regrasp from 4% to 52% (full task: 2% to 14%) over training from scratch (n=50 trials each), and retains its gains with a generative flow-matching action head. Ablations indicate that metric 3D motion is the strongest pretraining signal among those tested; kinematics, 2D tracks, depth alone, and scale-free 3D flow, each paired with kinematics, yield smaller or no gains. Representation analyses suggest that scene-flow pretraining encodes metric needle depth more accurately, that kinematics pretraining increases reliance on the robot state, and that needle-depth probes follow the ordering of real-robot success where offline validation error does not.'

## What they do
Surgical demonstrations are scarce and expensive, but robot-assisted surgeries are routinely recorded as stereo endoscope video, without action labels. FlowScope uses that video to pretrain a policy: the trunk of an Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) learns to predict where scene points are in metric 3D and how they move over the next 1.7 s. The flow head is then discarded, so the deployed policy is a standard ACT with no extra inference cost.

## How it works
- **Pseudo-labels.** From 1,149 clinical procedures (1.35M clips with a still endoscope), Fast-FoundationStereo gives metric depth and SEA-RAFT gives optical flow; tracks are kept only where left-right disparity and forward-backward flow agree within 1 px.
- **Pretraining.** A frozen surgical DINOv2 tokenizes the left image; point queries read the features at their pixels, and an L1 head predicts each point's 3D position and displacement over 10 keyframes. The robot-state input is zeroed, so no kinematics or actions are used.
- **Fine-tuning.** The trunk is trained on teleoperated demonstrations (898 pickup, 2,255 regrasp) with ACT's head or a flow-matching action head.

## Results
- Real robot, 50 trials per policy: needle pickup within three attempts rises from 54% (ACT from scratch) to 90%; Diffusion Policy reaches 62% and fine-tuned π0.5 42%. On regrasp, first-handover success rises from 4% to 52% and full-task completion from 2% to 14%.
- Pretraining on the surgeons' recorded kinematics barely helps (60% pickup); with the flow-matching head it is worse than no pretraining.
- With half the demonstrations, FlowScope reaches 52% pickup, matching ACT from scratch on all of them (54%).
- Ablation (n=20): metric 3D flow alone gives 100% pickup, scale-free flow plus kinematics 35%. After kinematics pretraining, image tokens give 24.5% of their attention to the robot-state token.

## Limitations
One surgical platform and suture pads only, with no ex vivo or in vivo tissue. Labels inherit stereo and optical-flow errors, full regrasp success stays at most 14%, and the clinical video cannot be released. Other video-pretraining systems such as SurgVIL are not compared.
