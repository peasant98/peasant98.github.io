abstract: Scaling robot manipulation learning remains challenging due to the high cost of collecting real-world interaction data. Real-to-Sim-to-Real (Real2Sim2Real) offers a promising route by enabling policy learning in simulation grounded in real-world observations, yet scaling this paradigm across diverse tasks and physical interactions remains difficult. We introduce Real2Worlds2Real, a self-improving agentic framework that integrates scalable simulation-world construction with policy development. Given limited visual observations and task descriptions, its tool-orchestration harness constructs faithful digital twins and expands them into diverse, physically executable digital cousins. The agent adaptively selects tools and revisits construction decisions, using visual and execution feedback to refine geometry and physical behavior. Building on these worlds, the agent orchestrates policy development by coordinating data generation, policy learning, and refinement based on task requirements and simulation rollout feedback. Across both stages, the agent distills validated procedures into reusable skills that guide subsequent tasks. Experiments demonstrate improved world fidelity and successful real-world policy transfer, with performance gains from increasing world diversity and interaction-data volume. Skill reuse further improves world-building efficiency and long-horizon task success.

## What they do
Collecting real robot data is costly, and Real2Sim2Real pipelines usually build each simulation with a fixed sequence of steps. Real2Worlds2Real hands both world building and policy development to a coding agent (GPT-6 Astra) inside a tool-orchestration harness. From RGB images or videos and a task description, it builds a digital twin of the scene, expands it into many varied "digital cousins", and develops a policy in those worlds for a real robot. Procedures that worked are saved as reusable skills for later tasks.

## How it works
- **World building.** The agent writes Blender scripts to model task objects, including articulated parts and deformables, and reconstructs the background as meshes, Gaussian splats or both. It compares renders with the real views and runs simulated interactions, revising geometry or physics when a check fails. Simulation runs in PhysX or Newton.
- **Digital cousins.** Variations in object geometry, appearance and material, plus layout, background and lighting, each checked for stability and manipulation feasibility.
- **Physics calibration.** Given real interaction recordings, the agent designs simulation trials and fits parameters such as mass and friction to the observed motion.
- **Policy development.** The agent chooses among code-as-policy with grasp optimization and cuRobo planning, RL with agent-written rewards, MPC, and imitation learning on simulated demonstrations, refined with DAgger or a residual RL decorator.

## Results
- Real robot (Dexmate with Sharpa hands, 30 trials per task): refining an ACT policy trained on 60 real demos inside the twins gives 100, 100, 93.3, 70 and 93.3% on peg insertion, cup stacking, gear insertion, wiping and cap tightening. Refining with real-world interactions gives 86.7, 96.7, 86.7, 60 and 93.3%.
- Against agentic baselines, nut tightening reaches 73.3% versus 63.3% (ASPIRE) and 53.3% (ENPIRE).
- In simulation, with 1,000 demos fixed, going from 10 to 1,000 training worlds raises Stack Cups success on unseen scenes from 17% to 71%.
- Skill reuse halves the tokens to build a held-out lemon-cutting world (28.1M to 13.9M) and lifts LIBERO-Pro Long success from 52.9% to 82.2% with 136 skills.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. Its real-robot comparison of simulation worlds uses digital twins only, the data and world scaling studies are on a single simulated task, and no code release is mentioned.
