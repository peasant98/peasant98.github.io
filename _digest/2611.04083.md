abstract: As robot learning moves beyond the tabletop, many tasks require mobility, adjustable reach, active perception, and bimanual dexterity. Commercial platforms combining these capabilities are costly, while affordable open-source alternatives cover only a subset and require software integration. We present MABEL, an open-source anthropomorphic mobile bimanual robot with 56 degrees of freedom (DOF) and a US$8.1k parts cost. It combines two 7-DOF arms with 17-DOF hands, a pitching torso, a lift, a 3-DOF neck, and a holonomic base. Back-drivable arms and torso support compliant interaction, with fingertip reach from floor level to 2.23 m. The head and torso roughly triple modeled camera coverage relative to a torso-fixed camera. Apple Vision Pro head and hand tracking enables whole-body teleoperation without handheld controllers, with 137 ms mean glass-to-glass stereo video latency over the robot's Wi-Fi hotspot. We demonstrate an end-to-end imitation learning pipeline, from teleoperated data collection to policy deployment, using a shared command interface and whole-body controller.

## What they do
Commercial robots that combine mobility, floor-to-overhead reach, active gaze and dexterous hands cost US$69k-120k. MABEL packs all of these into a fully open design with a US$8,056 parts cost. CAD, firmware, ROS 2 stack, controller, retargeter, learning pipeline and a MuJoCo and Isaac Lab twin are all released. The authors built a second unit from the released files in about two weeks.

## How it works
- **Hardware.** Two back-drivable 7-DOF arms adapted from OpenArm carry 17-DOF ORCA hands (swappable for grippers). A quasi-direct-drive torso pitches from -60° to 15°, a desk lift adds 0.635 m of travel, a 3-DOF neck carries a stereo head camera, and the base has three swerve modules.
- **Teleoperation.** Vision Pro head and hand tracking alone sets the whole body: leaning sets torso pitch, raising or lowering the head sets lift height, walking moves the base. Arm IK scales for reach, and finger retargeting preserves pinch apertures.
- **Shared control.** Teleoperation, simulation and policies use one controller: collision-avoiding joint velocities, low-gain impedance with gravity and friction compensation, and base speed limits that tighten as the lift rises.

## Results
- Median palm repeatability is 1.65 mm and path error 3.7-6.7 mm RMS. Gravity feedforward cuts palm error from 194 mm to 45 mm under compliant gains.
- Wheel odometry differs from visual-inertial tracking by 13.5-17.7 mm RMS.
- Glass-to-glass latency is 137 ms on the robot's hotspot, 150 ms on LAN and about 260 ms through a cloud relay. The camera accounts for 101-108 ms.
- An ACT policy trained on 117 episodes (38 min) grasps an object and places it on a plate in 8 of 10 trials.

## Limitations
The lift is slow, and OpenArm's link proportions differ from a human's. The policy demos are mostly upper-body, so they validate the pipeline but not mobile behavior. Long-horizon autonomy and onboard mapping are future work.
