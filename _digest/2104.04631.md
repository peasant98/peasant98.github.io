## What they do
Joint hand and object pose estimation needs real data of hands handling objects, but gloves, magnetic sensors or mocap markers can change how hands move and look. DexYCB records 10 subjects picking up 20 YCB-Video objects from a table without markers, and labels the 3D hand (MANO) and 6D object poses. The paper benchmarks existing methods on 2D detection, 6D object pose and 3D hand pose, and adds a robot task: generating handover grasps that do not pinch the person's hand.

## How it works
- **Capture.** 8 calibrated, synchronized RealSense D415 cameras record color and depth at 640x480 and 30 fps. In each 3 s trial the subject picks up a target object placed among 2-4 others and holds it up: 1,000 sequences, 582K frames.
- **Annotation.** Mechanical Turk workers track 21 hand joints, plus 2 landmarks of their choosing on each object, in every view.
- **Pose solving.** Per frame, MANO and object poses are optimized against a depth term (signed distance of the merged point cloud to the meshes), keypoint reprojection and a hand-pose prior, initialized from the previous frame.
- **Splits.** Default, unseen subjects (S1), unseen views (S2) and unseen grasped objects (S3).

## Results
- Cross-dataset hand pose: tested on HO-3D, a DexYCB-trained model has 1.4-1.9x the error of an HO-3D-trained one (31.76 vs 18.05 mm root-relative); tested on DexYCB, an HO-3D-trained model has 3.4-3.7x the error (48.30 vs 12.97 mm).
- 6D object pose: grasping hurts, PoseCNN AR drops from 56.53 on static objects to 41.65 on grasped ones. On unseen subjects, DeepIM RGB-D (57.54) and CosyPose (57.43) lead.
- 3D hand pose from RGB: 52.26 mm absolute error but 6.83 mm after Procrustes alignment; unseen views are hardest (80.63 mm).
- Handover: better object pose gives better grasps, and CosyPose beats DeepIM RGB-D on grasping despite near-equal AR.

## Limitations
Lab capture on a black background: a DexYCB-trained hand detector makes false positives, misses and bad keypoints on COCO images. One task only (pick up and hold), with subjects always facing the same way. The handover baseline misses hands occluded by the object, and it is scored offline against pre-generated parallel-jaw grasps.
