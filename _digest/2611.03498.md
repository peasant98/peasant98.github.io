Existing approaches typically address either crowd avoidance or semantic path planning in isolation. We propose a reinforcement learning (RL) navigation policy that jointly reasons about semantic terrain and dynamic obstacles from a single semantically annotated bird's-eye-view (BEV) map, without relying on explicit human-state estimation. Our policy outperforms established crowd-navigation and semantic-aware baselines and transfers zero-shot to a legged robot, demonstrating socially compliant navigation in highly dynamic real-world scenarios.

## What they do
City robots must avoid pedestrians and also follow norms such as staying on sidewalks. Crowd-navigation methods usually need explicit pedestrian positions and velocities, while semantic planners treat the scene as static. SNAP trains a single RL navigation policy on a robot-centric, semantically annotated bird's-eye-view (BEV) map that holds both terrain classes and nearby moving obstacles, with no pedestrian state estimation. The policy is deployed zero-shot on an ANYmal-D quadruped.

## How it works
- **Training worlds.** Procedurally generated terrain of varying risk, static obstacles and pedestrians driven by a randomized Social Force Model. Pedestrians walk norm-respecting paths, sometimes in groups, and sometimes slow down for the robot; a curriculum grows the crowd.
- **Policy.** PPO with an asymmetric actor-critic: the actor sees proprioception, the goal and a 5 x 5 m BEV map (CNN encoder), and only the critic gets pedestrian states. It outputs body-frame velocity commands for a frozen learned locomotion policy.
- **Rewards.** Goal and progress terms; penalties for being near pedestrians, for high-cost terrain under the feet and for failures; smoothness regularizers.
- **Deployment.** 10 Hz on ANYmal-D, localized in a pre-defined semantic map with Open3D SLAM; LiDAR-detected pedestrians are projected into the BEV map.

## Results
Success rates over 500 simulated episodes per setting:
- Against SARL (5 pedestrians): 93.2% vs 37.4% (original) and 59.2% (retrained) at 0.5 m/s, and 58.6% vs 19.0% and 38.4% at 1.3 m/s.
- Against DRL-VO with static obstacles: 91.8% vs 56.6% and 61.7% with 10 pedestrians; 81.8% vs 43.0% and 50.4% with 20.
- Against ViPlanner on semantic terrain: 96.0% vs 80.4% without pedestrians, 82.6% vs 58.0% with 16. A front-only 90° BEV variant gets 89.2% and 76.2%.
- On ANYmal-D, the robot stays in the safe region, passes between crossing pedestrians and reaches the goal without collision; no real-world success rates are reported.

## Limitations
The 5 x 5 m view leaves little time to react to fast pedestrians: with 13 of them, success falls from 84.4% at 0.5 m/s to 45.2% at 1.3 m/s. Deployment uses a pre-defined semantic map instead of onboard semantic perception.
