abstract: 'World action models (WAMs) adapt pretrained video generators for robot control by jointly predicting future camera frames and actions, transferring physical priors learned from video into manipulation policies. However, generating future frames before every action makes test-time inference too slow for closed-loop control, while most existing policies remain restricted to short observation histories. Building on Fast-WAM, which showed that video prediction shapes policy representations during training so that future frame synthesis can be skipped at deployment, we present G0.7, a generalist world action model scaled along two axes: extending the context window from a single observation to up to five minutes of history, and scaling pretraining to a large-scale corpus of robot teleoperation and human egocentric (ego) video. Across public simulation suites and real-robot deployments, direct action decoding (Fast mode) matches imagine-then-act (IDM) inference from the same checkpoint while running in 60 ms per 16-step action chunk on a single RTX 5090 (7.6x faster than IDM) and supporting pause-free real-time chunking. Performance scales consistently with pretraining data, with human ego video driving the largest gains in out-of-distribution generalization: G0.7 achieves a 70.8 mean score across five simulation benchmark suites, 48% and 54% zero-shot pick-and-place success on Galaxea R1 Pro and R1 Lite, 96.7% in-distribution and 93.3% out-of-distribution success on video-conditioned in-context learning tasks, and 86.9% mean task progress across seven real-world post-training tasks including multi-minute box folding, drink preparation, and product bagging.'

## What they do
World action models (WAMs) build robot policies on pretrained video generators, but most render future frames before every action and see only the latest frame or two. G0.7, Galaxea's new generalist policy, scales the authors' Fast-WAM recipe: video prediction serves only as a training objective, the context grows to five minutes of camera history, and pretraining adds human egocentric video to robot teleoperation.

## How it works
- **Dual-expert WAM.** A language expert conditions a vision/action expert over video latents, proprioception and 16-step action chunks, trained with joint video-action flow matching.
- **Long context.** Up to five minutes of real frames sit in a sliding-window KV cache. A human demonstration video placed in the cache specifies a task by example.
- **Fast mode.** Random attention masking in training lets one checkpoint either imagine future frames first (IDM) or decode actions directly (Fast), which fits pause-free real-time chunking.
- **Data.** Galaxea R1 Pro and R1 Lite fleet data plus DROID, RoboMIND and BridgeData; human hands in ego video become another embodiment (wrist pose plus five fingertips).

## Results
- Fast mode matches IDM on all four evaluation suites at 60 ms per chunk on one RTX 5090, 7.6x faster.
- Five simulation suites: mean 70.8 versus 43.9 for OpenWAM; 86.2% on memory-heavy RMBench, where single-frame baselines stay below 12%.
- Zero-shot pick-and-place: 12/25 on R1 Pro and 27/50 on R1 Lite; without ego video, 6/50 on R1 Lite.
- With in-context mid-training, the shell game and sorting by example reach 96.7% in-distribution and 93.3% out-of-distribution success. Seven real post-training tasks average 86.9% progress versus 64.3% for G0.5; box folding succeeds 90% of the time.

## Limitations
The report has no limitations section, does not state the pretraining corpus size and gives no project page or code. Zero-shot pick-and-place still fails about half the time, and product bagging succeeds in 63% of trials.
