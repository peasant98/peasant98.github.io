abstract: 'Learning reusable latent actions from action-free videos offers an effective route to improving Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. However, reconstruction-oriented latent actions do not necessarily benefit policy learning: as they may encode task-irrelevant static appearance with scene identity, or entangle slowly varying task progress with fine-grained execution dynamics in limited discrete code. We propose FreshVLA, a VLA framework built on a frequency-guided, dynamics-sensitive learning paradigm for acquiring temporally hierarchical continuous latent actions. FreshVLA uses a high-pass prior to guide visual transition encoding toward dynamical regions, thereby reducing reliance on static appearance cues. It further organizes latent actions into coarse-grained task progress and fine-grained execution dynamics through cross-timescale sampling and frequency-domain regularization, enhancing their capacity to represent long-horizon behaviors. During policy training, we freeze the pretrained latent action encoder and transfer its representations to robot control through joint flow-matching of latent and robot actions. FreshVLA achieves 98.2% average success on LIBERO and 98.0% on LIBERO-Long, exceeding GR00T-N1.6 on the latter by 3.6%. It also reaches an average sequence length of 4.52 on CALVIN and strong overall success rate of 80.4% in the zero-shot LIBERO-PLUS evaluation. Furthermore, FreshVLA exhibits greater robustness to visual perturbations and a more distinct division of roles between two branches in modeling low- and high-frequency motion, respectively. In 35-step action regression, it achieves a 51.3-percentage-point higher relative error reduction than its hierarchical discrete counterpart. Real-world experiments demonstrate that FreshVLA improves the average success rate over $π_{0.5}$ by 7.5%. The FreshVLA project page is available at https://freshvla.github.io/.'

## What they do
Latent action models learn action-like codes from video, and these codes can supervise VLA training without action labels. The authors argue that codes trained by reconstruction pick up static appearance and mix slow task progress with fast execution details. FreshVLA learns continuous latent actions at two timescales that focus on regions that change. It then uses them as an extra denoising target when training a 2B-parameter VLA.

## How it works
- **High-pass prior.** An attention adapter pools frozen DINOv3 patch tokens into frame features. A KL loss (PAWKL) pulls its attention toward patches with high temporal-frequency change, found with an FFT along time.
- **Two timescales.** Feature differences between frames are encoded at a substage scale (6 intervals per 36-frame window, for task progress) and an execution scale (the residual, for local dynamics). A DCT loss ties the substage branch to low temporal frequencies. A causal transformer predicts frame features forward and backward from the latents. Pretraining uses 227,894 episodes from DROID, RT-1, Bridge, RoboCOIN, RoboMIND2, CALVIN and LIBERO.
- **Joint flow matching.** The latent action encoder is frozen. A Qwen3.5-2B backbone and a DiT action expert denoise latent actions and 7-D robot actions together, and only the robot actions are executed. This adds under 2 ms per policy call.

## Results
- LIBERO: 98.2% average and 98.0% on LIBERO-Long, against 97.0% and 94.4% for GR00T-N1.6. CALVIN: average length 4.52, against 4.26 for π0.5.
- Zero-shot LIBERO-PLUS: 80.4% average, against 77.3% for GR00T N1.7. FreshVLA is best in six of the seven perturbation types.
- Without the latent-action stream, scores drop to 96.0% on LIBERO (93.0% on LIBERO-Long) and 75.3% on LIBERO-PLUS.
- On a real Flexiv arm, with 30 demonstrations for each of four tasks, FreshVLA averages 84.2%, against 76.7% for π0.5 and 72.5% for ACoT-VLA.

## Limitations
FreshVLA trails π0.5 on LIBERO-Goal (96.3% vs 98.0%). The authors attribute this to latent actions that are not conditioned on language goals. Pretraining uses only fixed third-person views, because ego-motion from wrist cameras confounds the latents. Camera changes remain the hardest perturbation (60.0% on LIBERO-PLUS). Real-world generalization to new objects, backgrounds and lighting is shown only qualitatively.
