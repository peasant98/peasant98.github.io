## What they do
An overview of control barrier functions (CBFs), the safety counterpart of control Lyapunov functions (CLFs). Safety means a set C = {x : h(x) ≥ 0} is forward invariant: a system that starts in C never leaves it. The paper reviews the main theory and summarizes four robot applications.

## How it works
- **CBF condition.** Some input must make ḣ ≥ −α(h) at every state, where α is an extended class-K∞ function. Any Lipschitz controller that meets it keeps C invariant and also drives the system back into C after noise. For compact sets the condition is necessary and sufficient.
- **Safety filter.** The condition is affine in u, so a quadratic program (QP) finds the smallest change to a desired input. Adding a relaxed CLF constraint lets one QP give up stability to stay safe.
- **Input limits and higher relative degree.** CBFs that respect input limits can be built from a nominal evasive maneuver or found with sums-of-squares programs. Exponential CBFs handle constraints such as positions, with gains set by pole placement.

## Results
- DURUS biped (simulation): exponential CBFs on the swing-foot position land each step on a stepping stone.
- Khepera robots: lane keeping and adaptive cruise control hold together, and the barrier values stay non-negative.
- Segway: a CBF from Hamilton-Jacobi reachability runs onboard in 0.4 ms on average. Only with the filter on does the Segway stay within its π/12 tilt limit under a command that exceeds it, and stay upright after a kick.
- Robotarium: six robots cover an area for longer than their simulated battery life, avoiding two obstacle robots and recharging.

## Limitations
The applications summarize earlier papers, and CBFs are not compared with other safety methods. Finding a valid CBF under input limits is hard: the trajectory-based construction needs numerical gradients that get expensive in high dimensions, and the sums-of-squares search must be iterated. On the Segway, simple angle barriers missed some physical limits, so the safe set was computed offline on a grid. The stepping-stone results are simulation only.
