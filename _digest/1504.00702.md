In this paper, we aim to answer the following question: does training the perception and control systems jointly end-to-end provide better performance than training each component separately? To this end, we develop a method that can be used to learn policies that map raw image observations directly to torques at the robot''s motors. ## What they do
Robot policy search usually sits on top of a hand-built vision pipeline that is trained separately and never adapted to the task. This paper asks whether training perception and control jointly works better. Its policies map monocular RGB images and joint state directly to 7-DoF torques on a PR2 at 20 Hz, for hanging a coat hanger, inserting a block into a shape-sorting cube, fitting a toy hammer's claw under a nail, and screwing a cap onto a bottle.

## How it works
- **Guided policy search.** A trajectory-centric RL method fits time-varying linear-Gaussian controllers for a few target positions, using full state (the robot's other arm holds the target object) and locally fitted dynamics. The CNN is trained by supervised learning to match them, and a BADMM formulation alternates the two until they agree. Only the local controllers run on the robot during training.
- **Spatial softmax.** Three convolutional layers without pooling, then a softmax over each of 32 response maps and an expected-position step that yields 2D feature points. These join the robot configuration in fully connected layers that output torques; about 92,000 parameters.
- **Pretraining.** The vision layers first regress the target's 3D pose from about 1000 images, and the controllers get 15 iterations alone before end-to-end training.

## Results
- End-to-end success (training / new positions / visual distractors): coat hanger 100/100/100%, shape cube 96.3/91.7/87.5%, hammer 91.1/86.7/78.3%, bottle cap 88.9/83.3/62.5%. Frozen pose-estimation features reach 27.5-88.9%; feeding the predicted pose to the controller scores 0% on the cube and 8.9-18.3% on the hammer.
- Spatial softmax localizes the target with 1.30 cm error, versus 2.59-4.75 cm for standard CNN heads.
- Each policy needs 156-288 trials and 3-4 hours of training, about 15 minutes of it on the robot.

## Limitations
Full state must be known during training, which here means placing targets at known positions; freely moving objects would need motion capture. Policies tolerate distractors set apart from the target but fail under drastic backdrop changes or when clutter touches or occludes the object. The feature points assume each feature appears exactly once per image.
