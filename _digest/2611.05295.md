abstract: 'Regularizing online reinforcement learning (RL) toward a pretrained base policy can improve sample efficiency and learning stability, but regularization that is too restrictive limits how much the policy can improve. Base policies -- typically diffusion or flow-matching models trained by imitation learning on large, diverse datasets -- capture many behaviors that are reasonable but suboptimal. Existing regularizers using such base policies are too restrictive in one of two ways: behavioral cloning regularizers pull the actor toward the full base-policy distribution, penalizing it for discarding suboptimal modes, while hard support constraints forbid improvements that lie just outside the base policy''s support. We propose Geometric Support Behavior Regularization (GSBR), which penalizes actions by their distance from the base policy''s support. Actions within the support incur no penalty, so the actor can concentrate on high-value modes; outside the support, where value estimates are less reliable, the penalty grows smoothly with distance while still permitting improvement when the value gained outweighs the penalty. GSBR estimates this distance from a finite set of base-policy samples, so it requires only sample access to the base policy. Across 19 tasks on four robotic benchmarks, GSBR raises the average success rate from 33% for the base policy to 95%, achieving the highest average success rate and throughput among all compared methods, with about 23% higher normalized throughput than the next-best RL method. Project website and open-source code at https://charlesxu0124.github.io/gsbr/ .'

## What they do
Online RL on top of a frozen diffusion or flow-matching policy is usually regularized toward it. Behavior-cloning penalties stop the actor from dropping poor modes; hard support constraints forbid anything just outside the base policy's actions. GSBR charges nothing inside the base policy's support and a penalty that grows with distance outside it, using only samples from the base policy.

## How it works
- **Penalty.** Zero inside the support and the squared distance to the nearest supported action outside it, so the actor leaves the prior only when the critic's value gain outweighs the cost.
- **Support from samples.** Draw N action chunks from the base policy, set a radius to a quantile q of their nearest-neighbor distances, and take the union of balls of that radius around the samples. The approximation error vanishes as N grows.
- **Actor-critic.** A Gaussian chunk actor conditioned on the state and the reference actions, trained against 10 critics on a 50/50 mix of offline and online data.

## Results
- 19 simulated tasks (RoboMimic, OGBench cube-triple, OCBench, and LIBERO-90 with a π0.5 base policy from pixels): average success rises from 33% for the base policy to 95%, against 92% for the next best (RLT). Normalized throughput is 0.95 against 0.77.
- OGBench cube-triple, where the base policy never succeeds: 99% average success; on task 5, 95% against 58% for the best baseline.
- Ablations on cube-triple: 8 reference samples suffice, and a zero radius drops normalized throughput from 1.00 to 0.66.

## Limitations
Simulation only; the authors name real-robot validation as the next step. Sampling the base policy at test time adds compute and latency, and the quantile q is an extra hyperparameter, swept per task together with the penalty weight.
