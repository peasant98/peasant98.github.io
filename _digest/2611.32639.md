abstract: 'Online reinforcement learning (RL) enables vision-language-action policies to improve through environmental interaction. However, in long-horizon manipulation, sparse success/failure feedback provides little information about where policies encounter bottlenecks. Moreover, repeated full-task rollouts also leave these bottlenecks weakly analyzed and insufficiently practiced. To this end, we introduce ReFocus, a progress-guided multi-round RL framework that alternates between Global and Local RL to identify task-specific bottlenecks and allocate additional practice to them. Specifically, during Global RL, we decompose each task into a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) of subtasks. Then a DAG-based Progress Reward is leveraged for both policy training and bottleneck diagnosis. Based on the identified bottlenecks, ReFocus selects Adaptive Practice Windows, defining task segments from an entry stage to a target stage. It allocates additional interaction to these segments during Local RL while updating the entire policy. After that, it evaluates the policy to perform the full task for the next round of RL, forming an iteration between Global and Local RL. Experiments on LIBERO-Long and RoboCasa365 show that ReFocus achieves success rates of 97.8% and 51.3%, respectively, compared with 90.6% and 39.3% for the $π_{0.5}$ baseline. Furthermore, we evaluate a real-world adaptation on three long-horizon tasks. On BreadBaker, ReFocus achieves 95% success, compared with 30% for the $π_{0.5}$. ## What they do
When a VLA policy is fine-tuned with RL on a long-horizon task, it usually gets only a success-or-failure signal at the end. A rollout that fails at the last stage then scores the same as one that fails at the first, and the stage that blocks success gets little practice. ReFocus fine-tunes a π0.5 policy by alternating full-task RL with targeted practice on the stage where rollouts keep stalling.

## How it works
- **Progress reward.** Each task is a DAG of checkable subtasks grouped into stages. Every stage gets an equal share of a fixed reward budget, split among its subtasks, so a stage with more subtasks does not get more weight. A subtask is credited once, after its prerequisites are done.
- **Bottleneck diagnosis.** An LLM Scheduler reads progress records and frame montages from validation rollouts and finds the earliest stage where progress stalls. It then picks a practice window (entry stage, target stage, step budget) and finer milestone rewards inside that window.
- **Local RL.** Rollouts restart from saved simulator states at the window entry. Prefix rollouts (from the task start) and suffix rollouts (to the task end) are mixed in to tie the practiced segment to the rest of the task. A history of past windows and outcomes decides whether to keep, revise or skip each window over three rounds. Both phases use PPO.
- **Real robot.** Mid-episode resets are impossible on hardware. There, the progress reward and window-based upsampling of recorded segments are added to RECAP instead.

## Results
- LIBERO-Long: 97.8%, versus 90.6% for SFT π0.5 and 96.2% for StructRL, the best baseline. RoboCasa365 (16 composite tasks): 51.3%, versus 39.3% and 45.8%. On the longest RoboCasa tasks (1,400-2,900 steps): 46.4% vs 39.4% for StructRL.
- LIBERO-Long ablation, starting from sparse-reward PPO at 92.6%: progress reward alone reaches 96.8%, practice windows alone 93.4%, and both together 97.8%.
- On a Unitree G1-D humanoid with 50 demos per task, ReFocus scores 100% on StackBowl, 100% on PickFruit and 95% on BreadBaker. RECAP scores 90/85/70% and SFT 80/75/30%.
- Scheduler choice on RoboCasa365: GPT-6 Astra gives 51.3% at $1.28 per task. The open-weight Qwen3.8-27B gives 48.8% at $0.05.

## Limitations
Local RL depends on restoring intermediate simulator states. The real-robot version is therefore a different, RECAP-based method that only reweights recorded data. In simulation, progress depends on task-specific simulator checks for every subtask. The Scheduler is a frontier LLM that runs between training rounds. Real-world tests cover three tasks with 20 trials per run.
