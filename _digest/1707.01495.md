abstract: 'Dealing with sparse rewards is one of the biggest challenges in Reinforcement Learning (RL). We present a novel technique called Hindsight Experience Replay which allows sample-efficient learning from rewards which are sparse and binary and therefore avoid the need for complicated reward engineering. It can be combined with an arbitrary off-policy RL algorithm and may be seen as a form of implicit curriculum. We demonstrate our approach on the task of manipulating objects with a robotic arm. In particular, we run experiments on three different tasks: pushing, sliding, and pick-and-place, in each case using only binary rewards indicating whether or not the task is completed. Our ablation studies show that Hindsight Experience Replay is a crucial ingredient which makes training possible in these challenging environments. We show that our policies trained on a physics simulation can be deployed on a physical robot and successfully complete the task.'

## What they do
Robot RL usually needs a carefully shaped reward. Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) learns from a binary success signal instead. A failed attempt to reach goal g still shows how to reach the state the agent actually ended up in, so HER also stores the episode with that state as the goal. It works with any off-policy algorithm.

## How it works
- **Goal-conditioned learning.** Policy and Q-function take a goal as input (as in UVFA). Here the goal is a 3D object position, and the reward is -1 at every step until the object is within a fixed tolerance of it.
- **Hindsight replay.** Each transition goes into the replay buffer with the original goal and again with substitute goals, with the reward recomputed. The goal does not change the dynamics, so this relabeled data is valid off-policy. Strategies: the episode's final state, or k states reached later in the same episode (future).
- **Setup.** DDPG controls a simulated 7-DoF Fetch arm in MuJoCo with a 4-D action (gripper displacement plus finger opening) on pushing, sliding and pick-and-place.
- **Real robot.** A CNN trained on domain-randomized renders estimates the box position from the head camera. The policy runs in a simulator initialized from that estimate, and its joint angles are sent to the real arm.

## Results
- Bit flipping: DQN alone solves only up to 13 bits; with HER it solves up to 50.
- Plain DDPG solves none of the three tasks and count-based exploration helps only on sliding. DDPG+HER solves all three almost perfectly.
- Shaped distance rewards fail on every task, with or without HER. Future replay with k = 4 or 8 works best and is the only strategy that nearly solves sliding; k above 8 hurts.
- On the physical robot, pick-and-place first succeeds 2/5. After retraining with 1 cm observation noise it succeeds 5/5.

## Limitations
HER needs an off-policy learner and a known map from states to achieved goals. Pick-and-place only trained once half the episodes started from a recorded grasped state, and pushing needed an extra reward term against MuJoCo object penetration. Gripper rotation is fixed, and the real-robot test is only 5 trials.
