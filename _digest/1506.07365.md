E2C consists of a deep generative model, belonging to the family of variational autoencoders, that learns to generate image trajectories from a latent space in which the dynamics is constrained to be locally linear. ## What they do
Locally optimal controllers like iLQG need a known model or a hand-designed low-dimensional state. Embed to Control (E2C) learns that state from images. Instead of fitting a latent space and then linearizing whatever dynamics result, it builds a locally linear transition into a VAE, so the latent space is shaped for linear-quadratic control. Training is unsupervised, on random (image, action, next image) tuples.

## How it works
- **Encoder/decoder.** A VAE maps each image to a diagonal Gaussian latent and back. Tasks where velocity matters stack two frames.
- **Locally linear transition.** A network predicts A, B and an offset o from the latent, and the next latent is A z + B u + o. A is a rank-one perturbation of the identity.
- **Loss.** The VAE bound on the current and next images, plus a KL term that pulls the predicted next latent toward the encoding of the real next image. This keeps multi-step predictions on valid encodings.
- **Control.** The cost is quadratic distance to the encoded goal image. iLQR or AICO replans at every step with horizon T=10.

## Results
- Planar navigation (40x40 images, six obstacles): E2C reaches the goal from every start with a real trajectory cost of 25.1 (true model: 20.24). AE, VAE and VAE-with-slowness baselines succeed 0% of the time.
- Pendulum swing-up (48x48 images): E2C succeeds 90% of the time with cost 15.4 (true model: 9.8). The non-linear-latent variant reaches 63.33%; Global E2C and E2C without the latent KL term reach 0%.
- Cart-pole (80x80) and three-link arm (128x128), 8-D latent: 93% and 90% success, against 63% and 40% for non-linear E2C. Example trajectory costs are 11.13 and 85.12, versus 7.28 and 60.74 for AICO on the true dynamics.

## Limitations
All four tasks are simulated, with rendered images. Control needs a goal image, and planar obstacles are encoded in advance with an added cost term. Render discretization makes small velocities hard to predict: uncontrolled cart-pole predictions drift right. Costs stay above planning on the true model.
