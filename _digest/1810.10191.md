abstract: Contact-rich manipulation tasks in unstructured environments often require both haptic and visual feedback. However, it is non-trivial to manually design a robot controller that combines modalities with very different characteristics. While deep reinforcement learning has shown success in learning control policies for high-dimensional inputs, these algorithms are generally intractable to deploy on real robots due to sample complexity. We use self-supervision to learn a compact and multimodal representation of our sensory inputs, which can then be used to improve the sample efficiency of our policy learning. We evaluate our method on a peg insertion task, generalizing over different geometry, configurations, and clearances, while being robust to external perturbations. Results for simulated and real robot experiments are presented.

## What they do
Peg insertion needs vision to find the hole and touch to align and insert, but deep RL on raw images and forces needs too many samples for a real robot. The authors first learn a compact representation of vision, force and proprioception with self-supervision, then freeze it and train a small policy on top with RL.

## How it works
- **Encoders.** A 6-layer CNN encodes 128x128 RGB images from a fixed camera, causal convolutions encode the last 32 readings of a wrist force-torque sensor, and an MLP encodes end-effector position and velocity. A fusion MLP outputs a 128-d representation.
- **Self-supervised objectives.** Given the next action, predict the optical flow it will cause and whether the end-effector will make contact in the next control cycle; also classify whether the visual and haptic streams are time-aligned. Labels come from robot kinematics and simple force heuristics; the training set is 100k states, collected in 90-120 minutes.
- **Policy and control.** TRPO trains a 2-layer MLP that outputs 3D end-effector displacements at 20 Hz, with a staged reward; freezing the representation leaves 3% of the parameters to learn. An impedance and operational-space controller turns the displacements into compliant torque commands at 200 Hz on a KUKA iiwa.

## Results
- Simulation (square peg): the full model completes nearly 80% of insertions; without vision, without haptics, or with proprioception only, completion stays under 5%. The no-haptics model gets the peg into the hole about half the time but rarely finishes.
- Real robot: each policy trains for 300 episodes (about 5 hours) on round, triangular or semicircular pegs with about 2 mm clearance, and succeeds at a level similar to simulation.
- Transfer: the triangular-peg policy succeeds over 60% of the time on unseen hexagonal and square pegs. Training a new policy on the frozen triangular-peg representation raises this by 19% and 30%.
- The policy recovers from camera occlusion and pushes to the arm (shown qualitatively).

## Limitations
Actions are 3D position displacements; full 6-DoF control of position and orientation is left to future work. Experiments cover only peg insertion, use a hand-designed staged reward, and train a separate representation and policy for each peg shape.
