abstract: Model-based reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms can attain excellent sample efficiency, but often lag behind the best model-free algorithms in terms of asymptotic performance. This is especially true with high-capacity parametric function approximators, such as deep networks. In this paper, we study how to bridge this gap, by employing uncertainty-aware dynamics models. We propose a new algorithm called probabilistic ensembles with trajectory sampling (PETS) that combines uncertainty-aware deep network dynamics models with sampling-based uncertainty propagation. Our comparison to state-of-the-art model-based and model-free deep RL algorithms shows that our approach matches the asymptotic performance of model-free algorithms on several challenging benchmark tasks, while requiring significantly fewer samples (e.g., 8 and 125 times fewer samples than Soft Actor Critic and Proximal Policy Optimization respectively on the half-cheetah task).

## What they do
Model-based RL with neural-network dynamics models is sample-efficient but usually converges to worse policies than model-free RL, partly because the networks overfit the little data available early on. PETS (probabilistic ensembles with trajectory sampling) keeps model uncertainty in both learning and planning. It learns only a dynamics model, no policy, and controls with MPC.

## How it works
- **Model.** An ensemble of 5 bootstrapped networks, each outputting a Gaussian over the next state change. Each network's variance captures aleatoric noise; disagreement between networks captures epistemic uncertainty from lack of data. Output variance is clipped to the range seen in training.
- **Propagation.** 20 particles are rolled out through the ensemble. In TS∞ each particle keeps one bootstrap for the whole horizon; in TS1 it resamples one every step.
- **Planning.** CEM searches for the action sequence with the highest expected reward over the particles; the first action is applied and the search repeats at the next step.
- **Tasks.** Four MuJoCo tasks: cartpole swing-up, reaching and pushing with a simulated 7-DOF PR2 arm, and half-cheetah.

## Results
- PETS reaches PPO's asymptotic performance in under 100 trials on all four tasks; on half-cheetah it needs 8x fewer samples than SAC and 125x fewer than PPO.
- It substantially exceeds the asymptotic performance of the deterministic model-based method of Nagabandi et al. A GP baseline with moment matching does slightly better on cartpole but was infeasible to run on the higher-dimensional tasks.
- Ablations: probabilistic ensembles are best on every task except cartpole, ahead of single probabilistic networks and deterministic ensembles; a single deterministic network is worst. The propagation method matters less, though moment matching is unreliable on the higher-dimensional half-cheetah.

## Limitations
Tested only on four simulated tasks. Running CEM-MPC at every step is expensive at test time, and the authors' attempts to learn a policy by backpropagating through the uncertainty-aware models did not work. Epistemic uncertainty is separated out but not yet used for exploration.
