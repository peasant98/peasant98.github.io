abstract: 'Recent advances in robot foundation models have highlighted the potential of in-context learning (ICL) for adapting robot behavior from demonstrations without further fine-tuning. Previous work has used retrieval-based post-training to equip pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models with the capability for ICL by teaching them to condition action predictions on retrieved visually-relevant demonstration chunks. However, the effectiveness of retrieval hinges on selecting context relevant to the current execution stage, which visual similarity alone may not capture. We introduce VICTR, a framework enabling in-context learning in VLAs via progress-informed context retrieval built around an In-Context Value Function Estimator (IC-VFE). IC-VFE conditions on a reference demonstration video, a language instruction and the current observation to estimate task-relative progress. By conditioning progress estimation on a reference demonstration, IC-VFE can align the current state with a demonstrated execution even for tasks unseen during training. VICTR uses the estimated progress to select demonstration chunks at corresponding stages of execution and supplies them to a $π_{0.5}$ policy post-trained for in-context action generation. On unseen out-of-domain real-world manipulation tasks, VICTR raises the success rate from 6.2% for a fine-tuned $π_{0.5}$ baseline to 23.8% and more than doubles the task progress rate, from 28.9% to 55.5%. ## What they do
Retrieval-based in-context learning puts a chunk of a stored demonstration into a VLA's context. The VLA can then adapt to a new task from a few demos without fine-tuning. Earlier work (RICL) retrieves the most visually similar chunk, but similar-looking frames can come from different stages of a task. VICTR retrieves by estimated task progress instead.

## How it works
- **IC-VFE.** A progress estimator built from Gemma 3 4B's frozen vision encoder and a Gemma 3 270M decoder. It reads the current observation, the instruction and a full reference demo compressed to 16 tokens per view. It outputs progress in [0,1] as a distribution over 201 bins. Training labels are pseudo-labels from the Robo-Dopamine reward model.
- **Retrieval.** Each task has a pool of 10 demos split into one-second chunks, each scored by IC-VFE; the shortest demo is the reference. At every step the chunk with the closest progress value is retrieved.
- **IC-VLA.** π0.5 post-trained so that the retrieved chunk's images, state and FAST action tokens precede the current query under block-causal attention.
- **Robot.** Bimanual YOR robot with two AgileX Piper arms, a head camera and two wrist cameras; 28 tasks, 7.5 h of data.

## Results
- On 8 unseen tasks (10 rollouts each), success goes from 6.2% (fine-tuned π0.5) and 15.0% (visual retrieval) to 23.8%, and progress from 28.9% and 42.1% to 55.5%. Adding visual similarity to the retrieval signal does not help (21.2%).
- On 8 seen tasks, all retrieval methods reach 87.5-90% progress versus 82.2% for π0.5.
- Against human-annotated progress on unseen tasks, IC-VFE scores Pearson r 0.65 vs 0.57 for RECAP; without the reference demo it drops to 0.49.
- On 30 unseen LIBERO-100 tasks: 45.9% vs 37.8% (π0.5) and 41.2% (RICL).

## Limitations
Success on unseen tasks stays low. All data and tests come from one physical environment. Squeezing a full demo into the estimator's context costs spatial detail.
