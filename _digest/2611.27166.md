abstract: Long-term memory enables embodied agents to reuse past observations across tasks. However, existing cross-episode vision-and-language navigation (VLN) benchmarks often leave it unclear whether successful navigation relies on historical memory or current exploration. We introduce Mem-X, a benchmark designed to evaluate memory-dependent cross-episode VLN. Mem-X organizes episodes into sessions in which subsequent instructions refer to objects or routes encountered in earlier episodes. Its three levels evaluate historical-object recall, in-memory localization and episodic indexing, and off-memory relocalization and historical-route reproduction. For the latter two levels, agents receive episode-local poses but no transformations between episode frames, requiring them to establish spatial correspondence with historical memory. Mem-X comprises 2,071 episodes across 710 sessions in 34 scenes, including 1,204 memory-dependent episodes. We also provide MemBridge, a modular baseline that combines semantic scene memory, cross-episode localization, and coarse-to-fine retrieval to support existing VLN agents. Experiments with state-of-the-art VLN methods demonstrate the benefits of MemBridge while revealing substantial room for improvement on memory-dependent navigation. The benchmark and baseline code are available at https://github.com/NJU-R-L-Group-Embodied-Lab/Mem-X.

## What they do
Existing cross-episode VLN benchmarks let agents keep memory between episodes, but the later tasks can usually be solved by exploring anyway. Mem-X is a Habitat benchmark in which later instructions refer to objects or routes seen in earlier episodes, so completing them requires memory. The authors also release MemBridge, a modular memory baseline that plugs into existing VLN agents.

## How it works
- **Three levels.** Level 1: return to an object seen earlier, in a shared coordinate frame. Level 2: revisit a target from an earlier episode using only episode-local poses, with no transforms between frames, so the agent must localize itself against memory. Level 3: start outside previously observed space, relocalize, then reproduce a specified part of a historical route.
- **Data and metrics.** Collectors drove the agent through HM3DSem scenes to record paired instructions and trajectories: 2,071 episodes in 710 sessions across 34 scenes, 1,204 of them memory-dependent. Besides success rate and SPL, new metrics check memory use: recall rate, localization success (0.25 m / 10°), constrained success (memory anchors visited in order) and route similarity (ST-nDTW).
- **MemBridge.** It builds a semantic scene graph per episode (DINOv3 keyframes, SAM3 segmentation, depth). It localizes across episodes by retrieving historical keyframes, matching with SuperPoint and LightGlue, and fitting an SE(2) transform with RANSAC, exploring frontiers when no unique match is found. A VLM (Qwen-3.5-27B) parses the instruction and picks the target from the top scene-graph candidates.

## Results
- Four agents were tested (CMA, ETPNav, JanusVLN, Uni-LaViRA). Reaching the goal often skips the memory step: at Level 3, JanusVLN reaches 28.03% success but only 5.73% constrained success.
- With MemBridge, Level 3 constrained success rises from 5.73% to 28.66% (JanusVLN) and from 7.64% to 26.75% (Uni-LaViRA). ST-nDTW rises from 13.80% to 33.14% and from 13.15% to 28.90%.
- At Level 2 these two agents localize in 99.2% and 98.1% of tasks, yet constrained success stays at 36.56% and 42.23%. Alignment alone is not enough.
- Without frontier exploration, Uni-LaViRA+MemBridge's Level 3 constrained success drops from 26.75% to 11.46%.

## Limitations
Simulation only, with single-floor scenes and ground-truth odometry from the simulator, which the authors note differs from real settings. Memory recall is measured coarsely, as coming within 1 m of the target. Even with MemBridge, Level 3 constrained success stays below 30%.
