abstract: 'We present a new task that predicts future locations of people observed in first-person videos. Consider a first-person video stream continuously recorded by a wearable camera. Given a short clip of a person that is extracted from the complete stream, we aim to predict that person''s location in future frames. To facilitate this future person localization ability, we make the following three key observations: a) First-person videos typically involve significant ego-motion which greatly affects the location of the target person in future frames; b) Scales of the target person act as a salient cue to estimate a perspective effect in first-person videos; c) First-person videos often capture people up-close, making it easier to leverage target poses (e.g., where they look) for predicting their future locations. We incorporate these three observations into a prediction framework with a multi-stream convolution-deconvolution architecture. Experimental results reveal our method to be effective on our new dataset as well as on a public social interaction dataset.'

## What they do
The paper introduces a new task, future person localization: from a short wearable-camera clip of a person, predict where they will appear in the next frames, as a step toward assistive navigation in crowds. It uses three cues specific to first-person video: the wearer's ego-motion, the target's scale (a cue to perspective) and the target's pose.

## How it works
- **Inputs.** From OpenPose keypoints: location (hip midpoint), scale (neck-to-hip distance) and a 36-D normalized pose. Ego-motion is a 6-D rotation and translation per frame from an unsupervised estimator.
- **Network.** Three streams of 1D temporal convolutions are concatenated and decoded by 1D deconvolutions into future location-scale offsets. Trained with an MSE loss.
- **Setup.** One second in, one second out at 10 fps. The metric is final displacement error (FDE) in pixels on 1280x960 frames.
- **Data.** The new First-Person Locomotion dataset: about 4.5 h of chest-camera walking video, more than 5,000 people, about 50,000 samples.

## Results
- Average FDE is 77.26 px, against 107.15 for constant velocity, 98.38 for nearest neighbor and 118.10 for a modified Social LSTM. People walking toward the camera are hardest: 109.03 here, 166-179 for the baselines.
- 73% of predictions are within 100 px (about 10 degrees). Assuming a 60 cm hip-to-neck length, the average error is about 60 cm.
- Ablation: locations alone give 88.16. Each cue helps, and all three together give 77.26.
- On the First-Person Social Interaction dataset it gets 125.42, against 162.02-213.59 for the baselines.

## Limitations
All methods fail when the wearer moves suddenly and unexpectedly. The authors suggest also predicting the wearer's own motion. The ego-motion estimator broke down when wearers turned their heads often, so the second dataset used optical flow instead, and there ego-motion barely helped (127.40 to 127.28 px). Predictions are 2D image locations, at most two seconds ahead.
