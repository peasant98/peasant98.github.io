abstract: World Action Models (WAMs) use the predictive knowledge of pretrained video diffusion models for robot control through future video prediction. However, iterative video denoising is costly, and inaccurate visual predictions can compromise action prediction. Our key idea is to make this predictive knowledge available through visual representations computed from the current observation, enabling action prediction without generating future videos. We introduce DreamFree, a WAM that repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model as a predictive visual encoder through representation distillation. The visual encoder learns to predict a frozen video diffusion model's representation using only the current observation. An action module is then trained to predict actions by extracting action-relevant information from the visual encoder. Across real-world and simulated tasks, DreamFree achieves higher success rates than state-of-the-art VLAs and WAMs, while performing real-time closed-loop control at 11Hz with a 14B video diffusion backbone. Experiments show that DreamFree can translate stronger video priors and additional video data into better robot control and enable learning new robot tasks from video demonstrations, including videos from a different embodiment, without action annotation for the new tasks.

## What they do
World Action Models (WAMs) get robot control out of pretrained video diffusion models, but usually by generating future video at test time, which is slow and fails when the predicted video is wrong. DreamFree keeps the video model's foresight but skips generation: a visual encoder learns to predict, from the current frame alone, the features a frozen video model computes over the current and future frames. An action module then reads actions off those features.

## How it works
- **Predictive visual encoder.** Initialized from a Wan2.2 video backbone (5B or 14B). An attention mask keeps current-frame tokens from attending to future tokens, so their features depend only on the current observation and instruction.
- **Future representation distillation.** A small projector maps current-frame features onto the frozen teacher's representation of the whole noised clip, trained with a cosine loss plus the original flow-matching video loss. No action labels are needed, so any demonstration video can be used.
- **Action module.** A Perceiver-style readout compresses encoder tokens into 64 tokens; a 1B DiT predicts 32-step action chunks with flow matching. At test time the encoder runs once, with no video denoising.

## Results
- Real DROID Franka, 10 tasks with new objects or arrangements, 5 rollouts each: 64% success and 83% task progress, vs 56% / 71.8% for DreamZero and 16% / 33.5% for π0.5. DreamZero fails every trial of towel covering, hat hanging and bolts in bowl.
- 11 Hz closed-loop control with the 14B backbone: 17.8x faster than DreamZero, 1.1x the latency of π0.5.
- RoboCasa-Kitchen (24 tasks): 71.7% with the 14B backbone, 13.2 pp above 5B, and 74.4% with extra action-free videos. LIBERO average 98.6%.
- Learning new tasks from videos without actions: composite RoboCasa tasks rise from 0% to 21.7%; videos from a dual-arm Franka lift ALOHA-AgileX success from 6.7% to 30%.

## Limitations
Pump pressing succeeded once in 5 trials despite contact every time. Video-only fine-tuning cannot add a motor skill the action module lacks. The pipeline has three training stages, DROID training took about a week on 16 H200s, and extra video data was not tested on DROID. The bimanual YAM results are qualitative only.
