abstract: Learning-based robotics research driven by data demands a new approach to robot hardware design-one that serves as both a platform for policy execution and a tool for embodied data collection to train policies. We introduce ToddlerBot, a low-cost, open-source humanoid robot platform designed for scalable policy learning and research in robotics and AI. ToddlerBot enables seamless acquisition of high-quality simulation and real-world data. The plug-and-play zero-point calibration and transferable motor system identification ensure a high-fidelity digital twin, enabling zero-shot policy transfer from simulation to the real world. A user-friendly teleoperation interface facilitates streamlined real-world data collection for learning motor skills from human demonstrations. Utilizing its data collection ability and anthropomorphic design, ToddlerBot is an ideal platform to perform whole-body loco-manipulation. Additionally, ToddlerBot's compact size (0.56m, 3.4kg) ensures safe operation in real-world environments. Reproducibility is achieved with an entirely 3D-printed, open-source design and commercially available components, keeping the total cost under 6,000 USD. Comprehensive documentation allows assembly and maintenance with basic technical expertise, as validated by a successful independent replication of the system. We demonstrate ToddlerBot's capabilities through arm span, payload, endurance tests, loco-manipulation tasks, and a collaborative long-horizon scenario where two robots tidy a toy session together. By advancing ML-compatibility, capability, and reproducibility, ToddlerBot provides a robust platform for scalable learning and dynamic policy execution in robotics research.

## What they do
ToddlerBot is an open-source miniature humanoid (0.56 m, 3.4 kg) designed as a research platform that both runs policies and collects the data to train them, in simulation and in the real world. It is entirely 3D-printed around off-the-shelf Dynamixel motors and a Jetson Orin NX, for a total cost under 6,000 USD, 90% of which is motors and compute.

## How it works
- **Hardware.** 30 active DoFs: 7 per arm, 6 per leg, 2 in the neck and 2 in the waist, packed in with spur gears, coupled bevel gears and parallel linkages. Two swappable end effectors (parallel-jaw gripper, compliant palm), two fisheye cameras, an IMU, a speaker and microphones.
- **Digital twin.** 3D-printed calibration devices set the motor zero points within a minute. A motor test bed and chirp-signal tracking data fit an actuation model per motor type; the parameters carry over to other motors of the same model.
- **Sim data.** Keyframe motions are tuned in a MuJoCo GUI tool and run open-loop; walking is a PPO policy rewarded for imitating a ZMP reference.
- **Real data.** A second upper body serves as leader arms, with a handheld gaming computer for walking, squatting, neck and waist. A two-layer PD controller keeps the lower body balanced. Diffusion policies are trained on the result.

## Results
- Hugs objects up to 27 x 24 x 31 cm, lifts 1484 g (40% of its weight), and steps in place for up to 19 minutes without falling.
- Push-ups, pull-ups and RL walking transfer zero-shot; the fitted actuation model tracks with a 1.3° average error.
- With 60 demonstrations (collected in 20 minutes), diffusion policies succeed in 90% of 20 trials on bimanual and 75% on full-body manipulation, running onboard at about 100 ms latency.
- A student with no hardware experience built a second robot in three days; the manipulation policy reached the same 90% on it without new sysID.

## Limitations
Off-the-shelf motors cap speed, torque and communication rate, which limits agile motions. The actuation model ignores motor temperature and is less accurate near the performance limit; heating caused more falls over time in the endurance test. The small scale restricts interaction with human-sized objects, and 3D-printed parts break more easily on impact than metal ones (up to 7 falls before breaking).
