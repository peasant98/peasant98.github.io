abstract: 'Hierarchical pipelines for humanoid loco-manipulation typically pair a task-agnostic whole-body controller (WBC) with a downstream task policy. Such WBCs are often trained through manual reward shaping, which yields visibly machine-like motions; more consequentially, they are trained without the manipulated object in the scene. Object dynamics are instead approximated through randomized end-effector force disturbances, a proxy that fails to reproduce the distributed multi-contact wrench a real payload applies across the arms and torso, with the gap becoming critical near the platform''s payload limit. In this work, we train the low-level (LL) controller itself as an object-aware motion prior: a single policy trained via motion imitation on retargeted motion-capture (MoCap) references that span the core motion primitives of the downstream task, with the manipulated object physically present in the scene. A reference-free high-level (HL) policy is then trained on top, with its action channel anchored to the empirical envelope of the low-level training data so that it generalizes the priors to a long-horizon multi-stage task while structurally preventing queries that would push the low level off-distribution. We instantiate the framework on a Unitree G1 for bimanual box pick-and-place: the same deployed policy generalizes across object masses and dimensions, initial and goal poses, and reactively recovers from intentional drops mid-carry. ## What they do
Humanoid loco-manipulation stacks usually put a task policy on top of a generic whole-body controller. That controller is trained without the object, with random end-effector forces standing in for the payload. HLO-AMP instead trains the low-level controller to imitate retargeted human motion capture of box handling, with the box physically in the simulation. A high-level RL policy then steers it through a full bimanual pick-and-place on a Unitree G1.

## How it works
- **Motion library.** One person's MoCap of carrying, picking up and placing a box, retargeted to the G1 and re-solved for 10 box variants (30 clips), plus a walking clip.
- **Low-level prior.** A PPO motion-imitation policy tracks the robot and box references but does not observe the box. It takes a compact 12-D command: base height, roll/pitch, planar velocity and both hand positions. Noise on this command during training lets the high-level policy steer it later.
- **High-level policy.** It is reference-free and observes proprioception, the six box face centers and the goal. Its commands are clipped to the range seen in the low-level training data. A five-phase reward scheduler (walk, lift, carry, place, idle) is used only during training.
- **Deployment.** Zero-shot sim-to-real, with the box pose from external motion capture.

## Results
- Hardware: every condition succeeded in 3/3 trials, including recovery after the box was pushed out of the grasp, a 60 cm cube larger than any training box and multi-box sequences. The exceptions were 7 kg (2/3) and 8 kg (0/3, thermal shutdown or a preemptive stop).
- Simulation, 20 s episodes: 80.7% success vs 50.3% for a whole-body controller trained without the object and 77.1% (with a 78.0% drop rate vs 26.8%) for one trained with a force surrogate. An end-to-end policy reaches 3.6%.
- Removing any one component (observation noise, the box in low-level training, targeted resets or command clipping) drops success to 3-16%.

## Limitations
Box pose comes from motion capture, so on-board perception is untested. Behaviors are limited to the four primitive families. Each new task needs new demonstrations, a new phase graph and new rewards. Naturalness is measured only by a proxy index, not a user study.
