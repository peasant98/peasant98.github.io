abstract: Language-guided UAV navigation has largely focused on long-horizon route planning, leaving short-range, object-relative flight maneuvers underexplored. To address this gap, we formulate Flying-on-a-Word (Flow), a task requiring a UAV to convert concise natural-language instruction, egocentric visual observation, and current state dynamics into continuous flight actions. Flow encompasses both primitive motion commands and complex object-interactive behaviors, assessing motion intent comprehension and spatial context grounding. To enable policy learning and closed-loop evaluation, we introduce UAV-Flow, a comprehensive dataset comprising 30,692 expert-piloted real-world trajectories across eight distinct motion categories, alongside 10,109 simulation trajectories in the UAV-Flow-Sim environment equipped with task-specific completion criteria. Furthermore, we develop FlowVLA, which undergoes imitation pretraining on mixed real-sim demonstrations and online post-training via Tail-Aware GRPO (TGRPO). By leveraging group reward distributions, TGRPO effectively reinforces high-reward action sequences. Evaluated on 273 held-out UAV-Flow-Sim episodes, pretrained FlowVLA achieves a mean success rate of 75.58%, outperforming the strongest baseline by 6.72 percentage points, which TGRPO post-training further elevates to 93.16% with superior sample efficiency. Physical UAV experiments demonstrate robust execution of object-relative commands in both indoor and outdoor real-world scenes. Together, Flow establishes a novel benchmark and learning framework for language-conditioned flight maneuvers.

## What they do
Language-guided drone research has mostly targeted long-horizon route planning in simulation. UAV-Flow++ targets short maneuvers relative to what the drone sees, such as "Approach the right side of the sign" or "Circle clockwise around the maple tree at a radius of 2 meters". It extends the authors' UAVFlow conference paper with a real-world demonstration dataset, a simulation suite with automatic task scoring, and FlowVLA, a policy trained by imitation and then RL.

## How it works
- **Real data.** Certified pilots, each with over 800 flight hours, flew DJI Mavic 3T RTK drones over three university campuses (5.02 km²), steering only from the onboard view. Video and flight logs are synchronized at 5 Hz into image, 6-DoF state and action, and labeled with short commands: 30,692 trajectories in eight motion categories, most under 20 m.
- **Simulation.** UAV-Flow-Sim (UnrealCV, one campus scene) adds 10,109 trajectories and scores each closed-loop episode on both the maneuver and the final state.
- **FlowVLA.** Qwen3-VL-2B with LoRA takes the first and current images, the state as text and the instruction, and predicts ten position-yaw steps (2 s) as 256-bin discrete actions.
- **TGRPO.** RL in simulation on four tasks, with GRPO advantages reweighted toward rewards that few rollouts in a group reach. On hardware, a ground station runs inference and skips waypoints to make up for latency.

## Results
- On 273 held-out simulated episodes, imitation-only FlowVLA succeeds 75.58% of the time versus 68.86% for π0-UAV, the best baseline. GRPO raises it to 92.06% and TGRPO to 93.16%; TGRPO passes 90% after about 40 updates, GRPO after about 80.
- Adding the real flights to simulated training data raises simulated success from 73.14% to 75.58%. A FAST action head drops it to 64.10%.
- Real flights show passing, approaching and orbiting outdoors (π0-UAV) and indoors (FlowVLA).

## Limitations
All numbers come from simulation, in the same campus scene as the simulated training data; real flights are shown only as examples. Inference runs on a ground station over a wireless link, which adds latency. TGRPO slightly lowers success on tasks left out of RL.
