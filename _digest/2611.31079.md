abstract: A generalist manipulation policy should perform new tasks, operate in unfamiliar scenes, and transfer skills across robot embodiments. We present Wrist Video Policy (WVP), a closed-loop manipulation framework that learns how manipulation unfolds through video prediction and recovers executable motion through geometric estimation. Our key idea is to represent robot motion through wrist-camera trajectories, which determine the corresponding end-effector poses through a fixed hand-eye transformation. We pair a video generator that predicts future wrist views with a pose estimator adapted from a pretrained 3D model to recover the corresponding camera trajectories. Model stitching maps the generator's predicted latents directly into the estimator's intermediate feature space, bypassing RGB decoding. This decomposition allows task knowledge to expand through broad human and robot video data while reusing geometric motion estimation across tasks and embodiments. The resulting pose estimator transfers across Franka Panda, PiPER-X, and Galaxea R1 Pro without further fine-tuning. Across tabletop and laundry-room settings, WVP generalizes to tasks ranging from precise grasping and inserting to handling deformable objects, achieving a success rate of over 50% in zero-shot out-of-distribution evaluations. Our project page is at https://wrist-video-policy.github.io.

## What they do
Video models can predict how a manipulation task should unfold, but turning those predictions into robot commands usually needs action-labeled robot data. Wrist Video Policy (WVP) instead treats the action as the motion of the wrist camera. The camera is rigidly attached to the gripper, so a predicted camera trajectory plus each robot's hand-eye calibration gives end-effector targets for any arm.

## How it works
- **Pose estimator.** VGGT is fine-tuned to predict metric wrist-camera poses relative to the first frame, plus gripper opening from a small added head. Training data are wrist-camera videos from AgiBot, DROID, BEHAVIOR-1K, InternData-A1 and RH20T (693,009 episodes).
- **Model stitching.** Instead of decoding predicted video to RGB, a 1x1 convolutional adapter maps the generator's VAE latents into VGGT's fifth block. The stitched model is distilled from the frozen RGB expert, so it needs no action labels.
- **Closed loop.** A video generator (VPP2, VERA or Cosmos3) predicts future wrist views from the instruction. The estimator returns camera poses and gripper states, inverse kinematics converts them to joint targets, and the robot executes 4-8 steps before replanning.

## Results
- 12 real tasks, 10 trials each, one estimator with no fine-tuning: 72.5% on Galaxea R1 Pro (laundry room; there the video model was fine-tuned on RGB recordings only), 52.5% on Franka and 50.0% on PiPER-X.
- On four Franka tasks it beats VERA's own inverse-dynamics head on every task, outperforms π0.5-DROID and is comparable to Cosmos3-Nano-DROID. With the Cosmos3 video backbone it also beats that model's action head.
- Stitching gives 40% vs 0% for the RGB expert on decoded frames on "put the vegetable on the bread", even though offline pose error is higher (113 vs 65 mm translation RMSE).
- Fine-tuning cuts VGGT's translation error on 1,000 held-out Galaxea episodes from 191 mm to 65 mm.

## Limitations
Video generation takes 39-53 s per call (pose estimation under 0.2 s), so the robot waits between segments. Executing longer segments means fewer calls but lower success: 60% at 4 steps vs 10% at 8 on one task. Pose estimation struggles when a held object fills the wrist view, and many failures come from inaccurate video predictions. Screw driving, complex bimanual coordination and large-rotation tasks are not tested.
