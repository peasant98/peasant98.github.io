abstract: We present the Screw Attention Policy, a geometry-centric manipulation framework that supplies task geometry explicitly and learns only execution. The policy operates on object poses, relative SE(3) transforms, and joint screw axes provided by perception and robot models. The policy requires only 30K learned parameters. Trained entirely in simulation on a single robot arm, the policy generalises to new scene layouts, transfers zero-shot across robot embodiments, and deploys directly on a real robot, without adaptation or fine-tuning. It achieves 96% success on LIBERO, 85.8% under layout changes, 80.2% across five unseen robot arms, and 21 of 24 trials on a real robot. ## What they do
Image-based manipulation policies must infer scene geometry, object relations and robot kinematics from pixels. This technical report instead supplies that geometry from off-the-shelf perception and the robot model, and learns only the execution. The Screw Attention Policy has about 30K trainable parameters, is trained on one simulated arm, and runs on other arms and on a real robot without fine-tuning.

## How it works
- **Scene analyser.** CNOS and FoundationPose estimate object poses and boxes from wrist and external cameras; a language model splits the instruction into steps (action, target, destination). The policy never sees images.
- **Screw Attention.** One Transformer token per object, articulated part (with its joint screw) and the end effector. Tokens exchange screw-valued features through supplied, not learned, relative transforms, so the layer is equivariant to changes of reference frame. The robot appears only as the end-effector token; no joint states reach the policy.
- **Primitives and output.** A small estimator picks the primitive (reach, grasp, carry, place, pull, push, press) and grasp and release orientations. The policy outputs ACT-style chunks of hand-frame end-effector twists for an operational-space controller. Behaviour cloning on LIBERO's 2,000 demonstrations takes about two hours on one L4 GPU.

## Results
- LIBERO: 96.0% mean (97.0% with true poses), against 96.9% for π0.5 and 97.1% for OpenVLA-OFT. LIBERO-Plus: 78.2% under robot-initialization and 85.8% under layout perturbations.
- Five unseen arms, zero-shot: 80.2% mean (71-87% per arm) against 33.0% for SkelWAM; π0.5 and OpenVLA-OFT score at most 1% on any arm.
- Given the same pose inputs, an MLP and Transformers stay below 30% under the LIBERO-PRO position swap; the policy reaches 74.7%. It also recovers 76% of missed grasps and 56% of dropped objects without recovery training.
- Real Panda: 21 of 24 trials on layouts unlike simulation, at 9 ms per step on a laptop CPU.

## Limitations
The real-robot test is a preliminary feasibility study on one task ("put the bowl on the plate"); the author defers a broader evaluation to a full paper. The policy depends on the perception stack, which uses object meshes for pose estimation; estimated instead of true poses drop the LIBERO Goal suite from 96.2% to 91.0%.
