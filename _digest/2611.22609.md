abstract: Successful manipulation requires the manipulated objects to progress toward a desired task outcome. Generative policies can produce plausible robot motions without ensuring this progress, allowing successive action chunks to stall or undo earlier changes to the scene. We introduce a task-centric inference-time refinement framework for flow matching policies that evaluates candidate action chunks through parallel physics-based rollouts and searches for motions satisfying sufficient conditions for task convergence. Task-level performance funnels bound temporary regressions while requiring net progress over each chunk. We establish sufficient conditions under which repeated funnel satisfaction yields convergence throughout execution, and use interval-wise Lipschitz bounds to verify continuous-time funnel satisfaction from sampled rollouts. To accommodate contact-rich hybrid dynamics, we refine the flow's source seeds using derivative-free Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES), constrained to radial quantile bands of the source distribution to help retain the learned motion prior. This formulation supports both Euclidean and pose-valued Riemannian Flow Matching (RMFM) policies. On PushT and three-cube stacking, refinement raises success from 31.3% to 88.5% and from 77.0% to 86.0%. On PushT, whose coverage score responds continuously to the motion of the block, a learned funnel lowers the average maximum loss of achieved progress by 19% relative to constraining the endpoint of each chunk alone, at a similar success rate. With the radial band, 11.3% of the executed motion windows are out of distribution, a rate within the range of held-out demonstrations, compared with 34.1% for an unconstrained search.

## What they do
Diffusion and flow-matching policies produce plausible action chunks, but successive chunks can stall or undo earlier progress. This paper adds an inference-time refinement step: simulate candidate chunks in parallel, track a task-error potential V along each rollout, and execute a chunk that meets conditions under which repeated execution drives the task error to the goal.

## How it works
- **Performance funnels.** An envelope on V over the chunk that tolerates temporary increases but requires a lower value at the end. If every executed chunk stays in its funnel, with uniformly positive terminal contraction and a bounded transient allowance, task error provably converges. Funnels are fixed, calibrated, or predicted from the observation by a small network fitted to demonstrations.
- **Verification.** Interval-wise Lipschitz bounds check the funnel between simulation samples, using rates estimated from the rollout.
- **Seed search.** The trained flow stays fixed; CMA-ES searches its source seed, projected into a radial band of the Gaussian so decoded chunks stay close to the demonstrations. Works for Euclidean and pose-valued Riemannian flow matching.

## Results
- 400 paired initial conditions, all in simulation. Constraining only the chunk endpoint gives most of the gain: 31.3% to 86.8% on PushT, 77.0% to 86.0% on stacking. The calibrated funnel reaches 88.5% on PushT.
- Requiring V to fall throughout the chunk reaches only 54.5-60.5% on PushT, where good pushes often first move the T away.
- Learned funnels retain progress: on PushT the mean drawdown of the worst 10% of episodes drops from 62.8 to 52.3 points vs endpoint-only, at on-par success; on stacking from 46.1 to 40.5.
- On PushT, banded CMA-ES reaches 86.5% vs 70.0% for L-BFGS and 82.5% for best-of-N. Removing the band gives 96.0% but raises out-of-distribution motion windows from 11.3% to 34.1%.

## Limitations
Simulation only. The method needs the full scene state, a simulator that predicts execution, and a hand-designed V. Guarantees cover only chunks that pass their funnel, and verification uses estimated rather than certified rates. Planning takes seconds per chunk while the scene must stay still.
