## What they do
The CoRL 2026 RoboEval challenge scores eight bimanual Franka tasks in MuJoCo with the RoboEval Diagnostic Score (RDS), which combines success and stage progress with safety, efficiency and coordination measured against human demonstrations. This solo entry trains one policy for all eight tasks: a generic RL loop that optimizes the score directly, plus generated data for behaviors the demonstrations never show.

## How it works
- **Policy as its own critic.** pi0.5 gets a value query token predicting success, stages, completion and the behavior terms still to come, and a Q head that picks the best of N sampled action chunks.
- **RL on the exact score.** The reward is the RDS linearized around the current policy; advantages drive AWR sampling and RECAP-style advantage conditioning, with classifier-free guidance at inference.
- **Tuning while collecting.** Each of the 2000 episodes per cycle draws about 20 inference parameters (flow steps, temperature, CFG scale, best-of-N, speed) by Thompson sampling on its score.
- **Data beyond the demos.** Scripted policies with privileged simulator state generate full-reward demonstrations, and failed rollouts are continued from branch points into recovery episodes.

## Results
- The generic loop alone, with only the organisers' demonstrations and its own rollouts, reached 0.85 RDS in about two days on one GPU.
- The submitted policy scores 0.990 RDS on 500 held-out episodes per task and 0.987 RDS with 99.25% success on the official seeds; five of eight tasks score exactly 1.000.
- Replayed as a policy, the organisers' demonstrations score 0.875 RDS on average; only 1 of 141 StackTwoBlocks and 2 of 99 PackBox demonstrations reach every stage.
- Checkpoint averaging and per-task parameter search added about +0.01 RDS (0.981 to 0.990).

## Limitations
Simulation only. Getting past the demonstrations' ceiling took privileged scripted demonstrations (around 200k generated), more than 240k rollouts and about 600 GPU-hours, which the author calls not data-efficient. Scripts only work where the simulator is trustworthy and the task is easy to script, and after the switch to cleaner scripted data the policy visibly lost some regrasps and retries. A 50-seed run varies by about 0.005 RDS.
