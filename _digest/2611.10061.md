abstract: Robot datasets are costly to collect and are usually tied to a specific embodiment, making it difficult to reuse demonstrations across robots with different geometry, appearance, and kinematics. We introduce UniLatent, a cross-embodiment transfer framework that reduces the embodiment-induced observation gap in a shared latent space. UniLatent renders paired source-target robot observations in simulation and aligns a source visual encoder to a frozen target encoder. A policy trained on encoded source demonstrations can then run directly on target observations, with no pixel-level image translation. Across three simulation tasks and two target robot arms, UniLatent reaches up to 93% zero-shot success, exceeding the strongest pixel-translation baseline by over 30 points on average. In real-world xArm-to-Franka transfer, fine-tuning with only 10 target demonstrations per task yields 27/30 successful rollouts. Code, videos and data are available at https://selina2023.github.io/unilatent/.

## What they do
Demonstrations collected on one robot arm transfer poorly to another, because a third-person camera sees a different arm, gripper and occlusion pattern even when the motion is the same. Prior work edits the images (masking, inpainting, diffusion) to look like the target robot. UniLatent closes the gap in feature space instead: it aligns a source-robot image encoder to the encoder the target robot will use, then trains the policy on those features.

## How it works
- **Paired rendering.** In IsaacLab/RoboVerse, both robots are placed at the same tool-center-point pose by inverse kinematics and rendered from the same camera in the same randomized scene. About 100k generic pairs, plus an optional 10k with the task's objects.
- **Alignment.** Both encoders start from DINOv2-base. The target encoder stays frozen; only the source encoder is trained, with an MSE loss, to output the features of the paired target image.
- **Latent policy.** A flow-matching policy is trained on source demonstrations encoded by the aligned source encoder and predicts chunks of Cartesian end-effector poses with a binary gripper command.
- **Deployment.** Target images go through the frozen target encoder. No image is translated at test time.

## Results
- Simulation, Franka to xArm7 and Sawyer, zero-shot, 100 rollouts per setting: direct transfer gets 0-5% despite 80-97% on the Franka. UniLatent averages 76.0% over six task-robot settings against 44.5% for RoVi-Aug*, a pixel-translation baseline built on the same aligned encoder.
- The largest gain is on Stack Cup into Bowl: 15% to 65% on xArm and 5% to 51% on Sawyer.
- Real xArm to Franka, fine-tuned on 10 Franka demonstrations per task: 27/30 rollouts succeed, against 23/30 with RoVi-Aug pretraining and 11/30 with target-only training.
- Sampling manipulation-relevant poses for the pairs matters: 93%/81% on xArm/Sawyer against 60%/32% with unconstrained sampling.

## Limitations
Only 7-DoF single arms with parallel-jaw grippers and a shared end-effector action interface are covered. Source and target share the task, scene and camera; use with external datasets such as Open X-Embodiment is not shown. The real-robot result is few-shot, not zero-shot, on a single physical setup, and robustness to real clutter and lighting is untested.
