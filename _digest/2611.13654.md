tldr: EgoHMP predicts a person's full-body motion in a scanned 3D scene from their head-camera video and head motion, without eye tracking. On GIMO it cuts mean trajectory error from 575 mm to 481 mm and pose error from 141.2 mm to 114.9 mm compared with the best gaze-based baseline.
abstract: The complex interactive dependencies between human behavior and 3D scenes make accurately predicting human intent a major challenge for human motion prediction (HMP). While recent studies use human gaze to infer intent, gaze signals suffer from ambiguity, drift, and a reliance on eye-tracking hardware. Research in cognitive science indicates that egocentric views inherently encode potential interactions, while head motion serves as a crucial precursor in motion planning. Motivated by this, we propose EgoHMP, a novel framework that uses egocentric views and head motions as robust carriers of interaction intent. By translating this intent into an envisioning of future interactions, EgoHMP achieves precise HMP in 3D scenes. EgoHMP first employs a vision-motion alignment encoder to align the visual and motion feature spaces. Based on these aligned features, an egocentric intent predictor with a modality-aware modulation mechanism adaptively suppresses invalid interaction cues to predict an accurate interaction probability map. Furthermore, to enhance the stability and realism of the generated motions, we propose a contact-aware motion decoder. Specifically, inspired by the human cognitive process of planning trajectories prior to action execution, we first predict a global trajectory to serve as a self-prompt, effectively mitigating the optimization conflict in diffusion models. Subsequently, we introduce a multi-stage denoiser, which relieves motion artifacts by incorporating human contact modeling through a multi-stage optimization strategy. Finally, a GCN-based motion decoder is employed to synthesize physically plausible and semantically consistent motions. Extensive experiments demonstrate that EgoHMP achieves state-of-the-art performance.

## What they do
Predicting how a person will move through a room depends on what they intend to interact with. Earlier scene-aware predictors infer that intent from eye gaze, which drifts, is ambiguous and needs eye-tracking hardware. EgoHMP replaces gaze with the egocentric video and head motion from a head-worn camera. From 6 observed frames and a point cloud of the scene, it predicts the next 10 frames of SMPL-X body motion (GIMO is sampled at 2 fps).

## How it works
- **Vision-motion alignment.** DINOv2 features of the egocentric frames and an encoding of the head trajectory are pre-trained to align, with a contrastive loss plus a KL term.
- **Intent predictor.** Learnable per-modality scaling tokens let training down-weight the less reliable cue, such as video during erratic head turns. Scene points encoded by PointNet++ query the fused features to give a per-point interaction probability map. A pseudo-label built from the future contact point and head position supervises the map.
- **Trajectory first, then pose.** A transformer predicts the global root trajectory toward the most likely interaction point. A diffusion model then generates body poses conditioned on that trajectory and on the local scene around it.
- **Contact refinement.** The denoiser also predicts which body vertices touch the scene and uses this to correct the poses. A graph network refines the final joints.

## Results
- GIMO, compared with the gaze-based GAP3DS: trajectory error 481 vs 575 mm, pose error (MPJPE) 114.9 vs 141.2 mm, interaction-point error (top-1) 161.3 vs 210.5 mm, scene penetration 0.81% vs 0.95%.
- GTA-IM: trajectory error 522 vs 610 mm, pose error 129.6 vs 155.0 mm.
- Ego-Exo4D: pose error 128.6 mm, while egocentric baselines given the ground-truth trajectory reach 144.7 mm at best. Without the scene input, EgoHMP (213.5 mm) trails EgoAllo (207.4 mm).
- Swapping the egocentric video for gaze raises GIMO pose error to 145.9 mm. Dropping half the scene points raises it to 145.4 mm.

## Limitations
The authors note that intent inference fails when video and head motion both mislead. They also note that the fixed-length output truncates longer actions or pads short ones with stationary poses. The method needs a 3D scan of the scene. It is evaluated only offline on recorded datasets, with no robot in the loop.
