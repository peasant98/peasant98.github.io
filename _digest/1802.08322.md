## What they do
Exoskeletons such as EksoGT, ReWalk and HAL need crutches for balance; REX is hands-free but walks statically at about 0.05 m/s. This paper brings bipedal-robot control (virtual constraints, hybrid zero dynamics) to ATALANTE, a 12-actuator exoskeleton from Wandercraft, so paraplegic users walk dynamically without crutches. Patients tested a single-gait controller (PHZD); a newer one combining trajectory optimization and supervised learning (G-HZD) is tested in simulation.

## How it works
- **Model.** User and exoskeleton form one rigid 18-DOF floating-base model walking flat-footed, with 0.7 s steps, a 27 cm stance width and constraints on ZMP, friction, foot impact velocity and torso lean.
- **PHZD.** Virtual constraints on forward hip velocity and joint angles are driven to zero by input-output linearization. Direct collocation picks their parameters so that one periodic gait is stable and invariant through foot impact.
- **G-HZD.** Periodic gaits over a range of hip velocities, plus three-step transition gaits converging to a target gait, are regressed into a function of hip velocity and time. It re-plans joint targets right after a disturbance; feedback linearization with PD gains tracks them.

## Results
- Three complete paraplegics (lesions T6-T12) walked 8.9, 10.56 and 9.5 m at 0.11-0.15 m/s with no outside assistance.
- In Gazebo, the learned controller recovers from hip pushes of 750 N forward and 650 N backward; a fixed-gait controller fails above 300 N and 100 N. It recovers by changing step length.
- It tracks commanded speeds up to 0.15 m/s with a small steady-state error, withstands a constant ±75 N torso force for 10 s, and 100 Nm knee torques (modeled spasticity) raise knee tracking error from 0.0130 to at most 0.0180 rad.

## Limitations
The learned controller has not run on hardware. Patient walking was slow, straight-line only, with a gait chosen and tuned per patient; failed trials came from foot scuffing or loss of lateral balance. The tests are not a clinical evaluation.
