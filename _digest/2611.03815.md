abstract: 'AIRoA-0 (A-0) is a bimanual manipulation system learned from 16,151 hours of handheld demonstrations, with no robot data collected at any stage. The demonstrations are recorded with YUBI, our open-source handheld gripper, which lets people record manipulation data anywhere with no robot present. From a single instruction, A-0 sorts more than a dozen small parts into their numbered cells and changes the mop pads and brush of a robot vacuum; among other tasks, it unzips a pencil case and folds a towel. It performs these tasks using only a camera on each wrist, with no head or scene camera, at a site where none of its training data was recorded. Across the ten tasks of our evaluation, it averages a success rate of 78%, against 27% for the strongest of four other vision-language-action models post-trained on the same task demonstrations. We collect no teleoperation data and use no reinforcement learning or on-robot corrections, and the robot appears only at test time. The system learns which regions of each wrist view matter for the task, predicts the stage the task has reached, and decodes actions as a continuous stream. A-0 is embodiment-agnostic: it runs on robots that mount the YUBI gripper, such as a bimanual Franka, TX-G2, OpenArm and UR. We are releasing the dataset, to our knowledge the largest open dataset of handheld-gripper demonstrations with task and subtask instructions, together with the pre-trained weights and the post-training recipe. Videos of A-0 on every task are on the project page, https://a-0.airoa.io. Anyone can build the gripper, record a task of their own and post-train the model on it in under four hours on a single node of eight H200 GPUs.'

## What they do
AIRoA-0 (A-0) learns bimanual manipulation from 16,151 hours of demonstrations recorded with YUBI, an open-source handheld gripper. It uses no teleoperation, RL, simulation or on-robot corrections. The policy sees only two wrist cameras and is tested at a site where none of its data was recorded. The dataset, pre-trained weights and post-training recipe are released.

## How it works
- **Anchored actions.** A π0.5 backbone predicts gripper poses relative to the pose at observation time, so handheld and robot trajectories share coordinates and one set of weights drives different arms.
- **Pre-training.** Demonstrations carry task and subtask instructions (10.65M subtask segments). A visual budget gate learns, without annotation, which wrist-image tokens to keep, and an auxiliary loss predicts the current subtask.
- **Post-training.** A stage head tracks progress through long tasks (EMA-smoothed at run time) and a memory attends over the last ten proprioceptive states. One recipe serves every task, in under four hours on eight H200s.
- **Non-uniform streaming decoding.** Two buffered action chunks at noise levels (0.1, 1) keep motion continuous between chunks; a query takes about 60 ms on an H200.

## Results
- Ten tasks on a bimanual Franka (15 trials each, partial credit): A-0 averages 78% against 27% (π0.5), 23% (XR-1), 16% (GR00T N1.7) and 14% (MolmoAct2), each baseline with a per-task hyperparameter search. A-0 is best on every task, e.g. 73% on the robot vacuum, where no baseline exceeds 14%.
- When a person pushes the bins aside mid-placement, the arm moves to the correct bin in one continuous motion.
- Post-training loss follows a power law in pre-training hours (R² = 0.98); each doubling lowers it by about 2.4%.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. Each task still needs handheld post-training data: 3-6 h for six tasks, up to 25.8 h for earbuds, the weakest task at 30%. Only robots carrying the YUBI gripper and wrist cameras can run the policy, and runs on TX-G2, OpenArm and UR are shown without success rates.
