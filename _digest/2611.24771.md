abstract: This paper presents a hierarchical admittance-impedance controller that combines an inner Cartesian impedance controller, an outer 6D admittance supervisor, and workspace-aware mobile-base assistance for guided load transfer. The method is motivated by assistive tasks such as airport baggage handling, where a robot should support a payload while a human worker guides its motion through small physical forces. Following an interaction-role interpretation of admittance and impedance, the torque-controlled Franka Research 3 manipulator behaves as a Cartesian impedance at the robot-object interface, providing compliant load support around a commanded equilibrium pose. At the human-facing interaction level, the outer admittance supervisor converts measured human wrench input into smooth Cartesian reference motion. The workspace-aware assistance layer shares the admittance-generated translational motion between the manipulator and a velocity-controlled base when larger motion is required, while front-side load alignment keeps the carried object near the robot's frontal region for safer and more intuitive guidance. The system was implemented in ROS and validated through a suitcase transfer experiment, showing that the robot can support the load while allowing the human to guide the transfer with relatively little effort.

## What they do
A mobile manipulator that helps a person move a heavy object, motivated by airport baggage handling. The robot carries the payload while the person steers it with small pushes and pulls, so the human decides where the load goes without carrying its weight. Following Hogan's view of interaction, the robot is an admittance toward the person and an impedance toward the object.

## How it works
- **Inner Cartesian impedance controller.** A torque-controlled Franka Research 3 arm holds the load compliantly around a moving equilibrium pose.
- **Outer 6D admittance supervisor.** The measured human wrench is bias-corrected, low-pass filtered and deadbanded, then drives a virtual mass-damper (with zero or weak stiffness). The resulting twist moves the arm's equilibrium.
- **Workspace-aware base assistance.** When the arm nears its workspace limits, part of the horizontal motion goes to the velocity-controlled base. A yaw command turns the base to keep the load in front of the robot.
- **Task-state logic.** Approach and grasp, lift (admittance locked, stiffer impedance), guided carry, place and release, then reset.

## Results
One suitcase transfer (3.3 kg) onto a table, with metrics taken over the 28 s guided-carry stage:
- The human guidance force averaged 7.43 N, about 21% of the 35.40 N estimated supported load. It peaked at 20.22 N during a combined forward-and-upward correction.
- The suitcase travelled about 2.44 m.
- Arm extension stayed between 0.570 and 0.645 m (mean 0.612 m). The absolute load angle averaged 10.31° (max 19.54°).

## Limitations
The evaluation is a single trial with a light payload and no comparison against another controller. Admittance and impedance gains, workspace thresholds and base limits are tuned by hand, and performance depends on them. The gripper is guided to the suitcase by hand rather than finding and grasping it on its own.
