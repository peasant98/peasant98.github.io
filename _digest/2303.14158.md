## What they do
BundleSDF tracks an unknown rigid object through a monocular RGBD video and reconstructs its shape and texture at the same time. The only input besides the video is a 2D object mask in the first frame: no CAD model, no category prior, and no model of whatever moves the object (hand, body or robot arm). Processing is causal, and the tracker runs at about 10 Hz.

## How it works
- **Coarse pose.** A video segmentation network masks the object in each frame. Transformer-based feature matching plus RANSAC on depth gives a pose relative to the previous frame.
- **Memory pool and online pose graph.** Keyframes enter the pool only if they add a new viewpoint (rotation distance ignoring in-plane rotation). Each new frame is optimized jointly with up to 10 memory frames using correspondence, point-to-plane and SDF losses, solved by Gauss-Newton in CUDA.
- **Neural Object Field.** In a parallel thread, a hash-encoded neural SDF with an appearance network trains on all memory frames while refining their poses (about 6.7 s per round). The refined poses and the SDF go back to the tracker to limit drift.
- **Hybrid SDF.** Rays from background-mask pixels or missing depth are treated as uncertain free space with a small positive SDF target, so noisy masks and hand occlusions do not bias the shape.

## Results
- HO3D (hand-held objects): 92.62% ADD and 0.57 cm chamfer distance, against 66.01% and 52.05 cm for BundleTrack and 33.36% and 30.84 cm for DROID-SLAM.
- YCBInEOAT (robot-arm manipulation): best ADD-S (93.77%) and chamfer distance (1.16 cm); ADD of 86.95% is on par with BundleTrack's 87.26%.
- BEHAVE (full-body interaction, single far-range view): 67.52% ADD versus 45.03% for BundleTrack.
- Ablations on HO3D: without the memory, ADD falls to 56.96%; without the Neural Object Field, to 76.69%.

## Limitations
Tracking fails when several challenges co-occur (severe occlusion, segmentation errors, little texture or geometry), and symmetric shapes can give a wrong pose when the object reappears. Depth is required, which rules out objects like transparent ones, and the object must be rigid. Parts never seen in the video are not reconstructed.
