abstract: Human demonstrations provide a rich source of bimanual dexterous skills, yet transferring them to robot hands remains challenging. Existing methods primarily optimize object trajectory tracking, often overlooking how human hands interact with the object. Even with accurate tracking, robots may contact incorrect object parts or slide their fingers, resulting in unstable manipulation. We present DexDuet, a framework that learns contact-preserving bimanual dexterity from a single human demonstration. DexDuet employs two interaction rewards to preserve human hand-object configurations and contacts. To enable coordinated manipulation, we factorize the bimanual policy into two weight-sharing per-hand policies that condition on each other's states. We further propose assisted reference-state initialization (A-RSI), an adaptive curriculum that adjusts object assistance and training starts across demonstration segments to overcome difficult manipulation phases. Across four benchmarks spanning three simulators and five robot embodiments, DexDuet improves object-tracking ADD-AUC by 11 to 41 points over the corresponding baselines while consistently achieving better contact preservation. Notably, among 122 paired runs with comparable object-tracking accuracy, DexDuet preserves the human's contacts better in 117 cases. Code and videos are available at https://rcl-robotics.github.io/Dexduet.io/

## What they do
Methods that transfer a human bimanual demonstration to robot hands mostly reward tracking the object's trajectory. A robot can then move the object correctly while grasping the wrong part or letting its fingers slide. DexDuet trains an RL policy in simulation that also preserves the human's contacts: whenever a human hand holds a part, the matching robot hand should hold the same part without its contact points drifting.

## How it works
- **Per-hand policies.** Two policies share one network. Each sees its own hand, the object and its partner's state, and PPO clips each hand's probability ratio separately, with one value head per hand.
- **Interaction rewards.** A hand graph reward matches palm and finger directions and fingertip gaps to the human hand, relative to the object. A hand-object contact reward pulls each region (palm, thumb, fingers) toward where the human region touches the part.
- **A-RSI curriculum.** A virtual controller assists the object, with a separate assistance level for each demonstration segment that drops when episodes from that segment succeed. Episodes start from states the policy has already reached, more often in failing segments.

## Results
- With Inspire hands on the DexMachina suite, ADD-AUC rises from 52.5 to 88.7 and Contact-AUC from 33.6 to 79.6. On the V2D humanoid challenge, ADD-AUC goes from 39.8 (CHORD, PPO) to 77.4.
- Among run pairs whose ADD-AUC differs by at most 3 points, DexDuet has the higher Contact-AUC in 117 of 122. Eleven raters picked the higher-Contact-AUC run as more human-like in 251 of 264 judgments.
- Ablations: a unified policy drops ADD-AUC from 89.7 to 74.1; removing both interaction rewards drops Contact-AUC from 80.5 to 49.0.
- Real robots (G1 with Inspire hands; two Franka arms with Allegro hands) replay the simulated rollouts open-loop. No success rates are reported.

## Limitations
One policy per demonstration, about a day on one GPU, with no generalization to new objects or motions. The policy reads the object state from the simulator and needs accurate hand and object motion in the demonstration. Closed-loop real-robot deployment is left to future work.
