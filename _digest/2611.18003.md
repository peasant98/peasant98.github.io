## What they do
Humanoids mount their leg joint axes differently (oblique hips, inclined pitch axes, different orderings). RL locomotion policies output native joint angles, and action scales, bounds, gains and penalties are all set per native joint, so a recipe tuned on one robot does not carry over. The authors let the policy act and observe in a common body-aligned joint space (hip yaw-roll-pitch, knee, ankle roll-pitch), leaving each robot's transmission and low-level control unchanged.

## How it works
- **Virtual joints from link frames.** Virtual hip and ankle angles come from the relative orientation of the bounding links (pelvis-thigh, shank-foot), calibrated to zero at the nominal pose and derived from the URDF.
- **Mapping to native targets.** Virtual targets become native ones by orientation matching; a coordinate Jacobian maps joint velocities and converts shared virtual-space gains into native PD gains.
- **Post-hoc insertion.** The policy trains on a copy of the robot whose leg axes are replaced by the canonical ones, and the map is applied only at deployment. Applying it inside the training loop was unstable.
- One recipe, adapted from Unitree's G1 velocity-tracking setup, is trained in Isaac Lab for the G1, PND Adam and a custom humanoid, and tested in MuJoCo.

## Results
- On Adam and the custom robot it had the lowest velocity error (0.085 and 0.029 m/s) and lateral drift; on the G1, whose hip is nearly body-aligned, all interfaces tracked similarly.
- Mean toe-out on Adam was 0.5°, against 4.8° for native joints and 14.2° for a task-space interface.
- Training was unstable for at most 0.1% of iterations, against 15.3% for the in-loop variant on Adam.
- G1 hardware, 6 straight-walking rollouts per interface: yaw rate 0.89 ± 0.54°/s against 5.31 ± 1.90°/s for native joints. Every proposed rollout reached the end of the walkway (13.8 ± 1.1 m); native rollouts drifted off after 5.1 ± 1.7 m. The map costs about 0.2 ms per step on a Jetson Orin NX.

## Limitations
Legs-only velocity tracking, three seeds per configuration, and hardware tests on one robot, with drift measured as IMU yaw rate because base position was not logged. The authors note that explaining the hardware gap would need further hardware ablations.
