## What they do
A robot leading someone, for example to an airport terminal, must hold its route while slowing, waiting or resuming as the follower lags, stops or drops out of view. Predicting the whole trajectory end to end lets follower behavior distort the route geometry, especially at turns. LeadVLA keeps the given route fixed and learns only how to execute it. The authors also build LeadInfra, an Unreal Engine data generator, and LeadBench, a closed-loop benchmark.

## How it works
- **Path, Pace, Residual.** A Qwen3-VL-2B backbone reads 12 front and rear images, an instruction naming the follower and the requested distance and side, and nearby route waypoints. It predicts a scalar Pace and a 24-step Residual. A deterministic composer advances along the route's arc length at the rate-limited Pace, so the route never bends, and then adds the Residual.
- **Follower semantics.** Six auxiliary heads (target box, lateral, distance, visibility, motion, formation) shape the shared features; only lateral and formation feed the Residual directly.
- **Referent-aware co-training.** Paired episodes differ only in which person the instruction names, and some switch the follower mid-run.
- **Data.** About 4M samples over roughly 1K routes, labeled by a scripted expert that reacts only after observable effects such as growing separation.

## Results
- LeadBench: direct trajectory prediction reaches 0.174 success, Path + Pace 0.354, and the full model 0.396.
- Single-person, strongest interference: 0.50 success versus 0.08 for both a Fiore-style modular controller and a direct-trajectory VLA with the same backbone. Multi-person at that level: 0.04 versus 0.75 for human operators.
- Co-training raises success from 0.112 to 0.396, and the share of behavior changes attributed to the right person from 60.0% to 86.7%.
- On a real Unitree G1 in unseen places (20 trials each): 16/20, 12/20, 10/20 and 4/20 across Core-S, Core-M, Easy-S and Easy-M, with route fidelity of 0.80-0.95.

## Limitations
Real-world Easy failures often involve hitting the obstacle, since the controller does no replanning. Left/right formation transfers poorly. Similar-looking target and distractor can be confused. The robot adapts only by moving, not by gesturing or speaking.
