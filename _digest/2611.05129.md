## What they do
Web maps give walking directions for almost any city, but point-goal navigation policies depend on accurate GNSS, and vision-language navigation models are brittle even on single instructions. WayTrace splits long-range urban navigation into two problems: deciding which atomic manoeuvre applies now ("cross the road to the left"), and executing it from the camera image. Language connects the two. The system runs on a Unitree B2W wheeled-legged robot.

## How it works
- **Orchestrator.** Every 2 s, an off-the-shelf VLM on a remote server reads the web-map directions, a 50 m satellite map and camera images of the last 15 m, then issues the next command. GNSS only selects the current route step.
- **Trace predictor.** RynnBrain-9B, fine-tuned with LoRA, takes the command, five past frames and the walked path, and predicts a goal point and a ten-point path in the image. It runs onboard on a Jetson AGX Thor in about 1.5 s.
- **Training data.** 1.19M samples from street-level image sequences: 288k from reconstructed walks labelled by a VLM, and 863k intent-conditioned traces that pair one image with several commands.
- **Execution.** Stereo depth lifts the trace to 3D, and an RL point-goal policy follows a carrot 4.5 m ahead at 5 Hz while avoiding obstacles.

## Results
- Ten simulated Zurich missions (4.68 km in total) with perfect GNSS: 83.3% success, against at most 20% for released point-goal and vision-language baselines.
- With 7 m RMS GNSS drift, success falls to 63.3%, against 23.3% and 10.0% for CityWalker and VAMOS on the same RL policy. Crosswalk and road violations stay below 6% and 1%.
- Open-loop trace prediction scores 83.2 (NaviTrace score) on hand-labelled traces, against at most 57.0 for baselines.
- Real world: three city-centre missions of 248-426 m with no interventions, and a 1.37 km mission with two operator overrides of the current command.

## Limitations
Most failures come from the orchestrator, which issues turns or crosswalk entries too early or too late (5.1% critical errors per decision). About 3 s of latency rules out reacting to red lights or trams. The simulation has no traffic or pedestrians, and the real-world missions are qualitative demonstrations without baselines.
