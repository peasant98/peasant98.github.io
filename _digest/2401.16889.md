abstract: 'This paper presents a comprehensive study on using deep reinforcement learning (RL) to create dynamic locomotion controllers for bipedal robots. Going beyond focusing on a single locomotion skill, we develop a general control solution that can be used for a range of dynamic bipedal skills, from periodic walking and running to aperiodic jumping and standing. Our RL-based controller incorporates a novel dual-history architecture, utilizing both a long-term and short-term input/output (I/O) history of the robot. This control architecture, when trained through the proposed end-to-end RL approach, consistently outperforms other methods across a diverse range of skills in both simulation and the real world. The study also delves into the adaptivity and robustness introduced by the proposed RL system in developing locomotion controllers. We demonstrate that the proposed architecture can adapt to both time-invariant dynamics shifts and time-variant changes, such as contact events, by effectively using the robot''s I/O history. Additionally, we identify task randomization as another key source of robustness, fostering better task generalization and compliance to disturbances. The resulting control policies can be successfully deployed on Cassie, a torque-controlled human-sized bipedal robot. This work pushes the limits of agility for bipedal robots through extensive real-world experiments. We demonstrate a diverse range of locomotion skills, including: robust standing, versatile walking, fast running with a demonstration of a 400-meter dash, and a diverse set of jumping skills, such as standing long jumps and high jumps.'

## What they do
A single RL framework that produces walking, running and jumping controllers for Cassie, a human-sized torque-controlled biped. Each policy covers a range of commands for its skill and can also stand. The paper credits the policies' adaptivity to the robot's input/output history and their robustness to training on many tasks.

## How it works
- **Dual-history policy.** A 1D CNN encodes 2 s (66 steps) of observations and actions. A base MLP gets that embedding plus the last 4 steps directly, a preview of the reference motion and the command, and outputs motor position targets at 33 Hz. Trained end to end with PPO in MuJoCo, with no teacher-student stage.
- **Reference motions:** a trajectory-optimized gait library for walking, retargeted human mocap for running, a hand-made animation for jumping.
- **Three stages:** one fixed task, then randomized commands, then dynamics randomization (friction, link masses, spring stiffness, PD gains, sensor noise, up to 25 ms delay).

## Results
- In simulation the dual-history policy learns better than residual, state-only, long-only, short-only and RMA/A-RMA variants on all three skills. On hardware it walks in place with the least drift.
- The encoder's latent tracks foot impact forces, take-off and landing, and shifts when dynamics change while tracking error barely moves.
- Task randomization adds robustness: pushed while standing, the versatile walking policy steps to recover, while standing-only policies fall.
- Hardware: walking command errors of 0.10 m/s, 0.10 m/s and 0.06 m (height), with the same policy still working 325 and 492 days later. Running covered 400 m in 2 min 34 s, 100 m in 27.06 s (peak estimated 4.2 m/s) and slopes up to 10°. Two jumping policies did 19 distinct jumps.

## Limitations
Each skill is a separate policy, and adding more skills to one policy risks catastrophic forgetting; after large jumps the robot sometimes oscillates while standing. Jumping onto platforms while turning was too hard to train. The velocity estimator underestimates speed above 3 m/s, and there is no vision.
