## What they do
Habitat 2.0 is a simulation stack for training mobile manipulators on household rearrangement. It has three parts: ReplicaCAD, an artist-built interactive recreation of the Replica 'FRL-apartment' (92 objects, 105 furniture layouts, an articulated fridge and drawers); the H2.0 simulator, which gives up breadth (no deformables, fluids, audio or touch) for speed; and the Home Assistant Benchmark (Tidy House, Prepare Groceries, Set Table), where a Fetch robot gets only the start and goal 3D position of each object.

## How it works
- **Lazy physics.** Distant objects are not updated, the base moves kinematically on a navmesh, and Bullet physics and Magnum rendering are integrated in C++.
- **Interleaved physics and rendering.** The policy acts on the observation from one step earlier, so the CPU computes the next state while the GPU renders the current view.
- **Simplify and reuse.** Compressed textures (4-6x less GPU memory), cached assets for fast resets, and convex collision meshes.
- **Baselines.** Grasping is abstracted: an object within 15 cm snaps into the gripper. One flat RL policy (MonolithicRL) is compared with a STRIPS task planner chaining RL skills (TP+SRL) or RRTConnect motion planning (TP+SPA).

## Results
- Speed: 1,191 steps/s in one process (iGibson reports 100), 8,186 on one GPU and 25,734 on 8 GPUs, each step rendering RGB-D and simulating 1/30 s.
- Pick: MonolithicRL reaches 86.3% on unseen layouts versus 72.7% for SPA and 80.0% for SPA with ground-truth geometry, but drops to 52.7% on unseen receptacles (SPA 60.3%).
- A blind policy using only proprioception still picks 78% of the time.
- On the benchmark tasks MonolithicRL "performs abysmally"; the hierarchical methods lose success as the number of interactions grows, and TP+SRL beats TP+SPA on Prepare Groceries, where the cluttered fridge is hard to perceive.

## Limitations
Simulation only, with abstracted grasping. Chained skills suffer hand-off problems, such as navigating to a spot where the next manipulation fails. Opening unknown containers with SPA is left out of scope, so Set Table has no SPA baseline. ReplicaCAD models apartments from one country (USA), and full training is bottlenecked by environment synchronization and asset reloading on reset.
