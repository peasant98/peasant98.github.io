abstract: 'In this work, we introduce general purpose touch representations for the increasingly accessible class of vision-based tactile sensors. Such sensors have led to many recent advances in robot manipulation as they markedly complement vision, yet solutions today often rely on task and sensor specific handcrafted perception models. Collecting real data at scale with task centric ground truth labels, like contact forces and slip, is a challenge further compounded by sensors of various form factor differing in aspects like lighting and gel markings. To tackle this we turn to self-supervised learning (SSL) that has demonstrated remarkable performance in computer vision. ## What they do
Vision-based tactile sensors such as DIGIT and GelSight usually get a custom model trained on labeled data for each task and sensor, and labels like force and slip need expensive lab instrumentation. Sparsh instead pre-trains general touch encoders with self-supervised learning on 460k+ unlabeled tactile images from DIGIT, GelSight 2017 and GelSight Mini. A companion benchmark, TacBench, measures how useful the frozen features are on six tasks.

## How it works
- **Data.** About 661k images from YCB-Slide, Touch-and-Go, ObjectFolder and a new Touch-Slide dataset; 70% (462.7k) are used for pre-training.
- **Pre-training.** ViT-B encoders trained with MAE, DINO, DINOv2, I-JEPA and V-JEPA objectives. The input is two frames 5 samples apart stacked along channels (about 80 ms at 60 FPS), or a 4-frame clip for V-JEPA, with background subtraction to suppress lighting and gel differences between sensors.
- **Evaluation.** The encoder is frozen and a small attentive probe (cross-attention plus a 2-layer MLP) is trained per task on 1-100% of the labels. The E2E baseline trains the same architecture from scratch. Tasks: force estimation, slip detection, SE(2) pose change, grasp stability, textile recognition and a bead-maze diffusion policy.

## Results
- Averaged over TacBench, Sparsh beats E2E by 95.1% with 33-50% of the labels. The DINO and I-JEPA variants are the strongest overall.
- With a third of the labels: DIGIT force RMSE 44.0 mN (DINO) vs 61.4 mN for E2E; slip F1 0.828 (V-JEPA) vs 0.238; pose-change accuracy 0.834 (DINO) vs 0.245.
- Bead maze on the real robot: Sparsh policies travel 9.4-10.8 cm before failure vs 6.7 cm for E2E.

## Limitations
The pre-training data mostly contains discrete contacts with little shear-rich interaction, and the length of the tactile history is not ablated. No bead-maze policy completes the full maze on the real robot; compounding errors make the bead fall out of the fingers.
