abstract: 'Vision-language-action models (VLAs) are capable low-level controllers, but they tie where objects go to the layouts they were trained on: when objects are moved or obstacles block the way, they carry them to the wrong place or into collisions. Fine-tuning on new layouts needs robot data for each one and erodes skills the model already has. We introduce Cairn, which keeps the VLA frozen and steers it at test time along learned 3D trajectories of the object in its gripper. A small prior reads point clouds of the scene and the held object, along with its pose when grasped and the instruction, and samples SE(3) trajectories up to its release that lead it around obstacles and turn it to fit through gaps. The prior and the VLA form the two levels of a hierarchical planner in which feedback runs both ways: the VLA''s intended motion chooses and reshapes the trajectories, and the chosen one guides the VLA''s denoising. Its predicted actions, mapped to the held object''s poses through the grasp, are pulled toward the next waypoint, while the VLA''s weights stay untouched. Across 1,941 held-out LIBERO scenes with displaced objects and obstacles, Cairn raises the success rate of a frozen π0.5 from 24.5% to 95.1%, above that of a π0.5 fine-tuned on our training layouts, without hurting standard LIBERO. On the third-party LIBERO-PRO benchmark, it raises success from 27.3% to 38.0%, and from 54.4% to 81.1% on the episodes it steers. On a real Franka, with a prior trained purely in simulation, it guides the frozen DROID π0.5 around an obstacle and to a placement at the limit of the arm''s reach, where it otherwise fails. All code, models and data will be made public.'

## What they do
Vision-language-action models (VLAs) such as π0.5 grasp and place objects well, but they carry them where their training layouts put the goal: when the receptacle moves or an obstacle blocks the way, they miss it or collide. Cairn adds a small 3D planner for the route of the held object and uses it to steer the frozen VLA at test time, without changing its weights.

## How it works
- **Route prior.** A 4.35M-parameter rectified-flow model built on 3DFA, with a Point Transformer V3 scene encoder and its own branch for the held object's point cloud. From the scene and object clouds, the grasp pose and the instruction, it samples SE(3) waypoint routes from grasp to release, so the goal is inferred along with the route.
- **Generated data.** LIBERO's 4,605 human demos are re-anchored to new layouts, bent around obstacles from six families, checked with MPLib and executed in simulation: 16,049 demos plus 79,470 alternative routes, without robot actions.
- **Two-way coupling.** An unsteered VLA sample pulls and reweights 16 sampled routes; routes that pass within 1.5 cm of the scene are rejected and the medoid becomes the plan. At each denoising step, the object pose implied by the action chunk is pulled toward the next waypoint with a damped Gauss-Newton step, leaving the pace to the VLA.

## Results
- LIBERO-Shift (1,941 held-out scenes): frozen π0.5 goes from 24.5% to 95.1%, and from 13.4% to 88.8% where the object must turn. OmniGuide with an attractor at the true goal reaches 50.9%.
- Fine-tuning π0.5 on the same layouts reaches 91.3% but drops standard LIBERO from 96.5% to 70.5%; Cairn keeps 97.0%.
- LIBERO-PRO position shift: 27.3% to 38.0% overall, 54.4% to 81.1% on the episodes it steers.
- Real Franka with π0.5-DROID and a prior trained only in simulation: bowl behind a box 0/10 to 9/10, cabinet top 0/10 to 8/10, free path unchanged at 10/10.

## Limitations
Only rigid single-object pick-and-place: steering starts after the grasp, so wrong grasps stay failures, and 13 of the 40 LIBERO-PRO tasks (two objects, drawers, the stove) run unsteered. The route covers the held object, not the arm; most remaining LIBERO-Shift failures are things like the arm touching an obstacle while the object follows the route. The real-robot test is three cube tasks with 10 trials each.
