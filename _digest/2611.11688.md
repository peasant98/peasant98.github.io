abstract: 'Failure monitors for vision-language-action (VLA) policies are trained offline and then frozen. After deployment, lighting, objects and cameras drift, and the policy itself is updated or moved to new tasks. On recorded real-robot runs, after a switch of task or policy version, a frozen monitor fit on the old task ranks failures little better than chance. We present Streaming Failure Forecasting (SFF), a monitor that keeps learning from the one label every episode provides: whether it succeeded. SFF keeps the offline monitor''s network fixed and refits only its last linear layer after each episode, at 2.18 ms per policy call on a Jetson CPU core. ## What they do
Failure monitors for VLA policies are trained offline and then frozen, but after deployment the lighting, objects, camera, task and policy version change without notice. Streaming Failure Forecasting (SFF) keeps the monitor learning from the one label each episode provides, whether it succeeded, and is tested with all settings locked before each test result was opened.

## How it works
- **Fixed features.** A small two-hidden-layer network is trained on nominal warm-up episodes to predict failure at every policy call from the VLA's internal activations (projected to 128 dimensions) plus elapsed time. Its hidden layers then stay fixed.
- **Online readout.** After each episode, only the last linear layer is refit in closed form by discounted least squares (one 130x130 solve), anchored to the offline head so it starts out ranking exactly like the offline monitor. No episodes are stored.
- **Success-only threshold.** The alarm threshold follows adaptive conformal inference but moves only after successful episodes, the only ones where an alarm can be false.
- **Evaluation.** SmolVLA on LIBERO suites under injected drifts (light, object colour, camera, wear), and 434 recorded ArmnetBench runs of SmolVLA, pi0.5, pi0 and MolmoAct 2 on real SO-101 arms, replayed across unannounced task or policy switches.

## Results
- Simulation: within-task AUROC beats the frozen monitor on 4 of 5 test suites (+0.021 to +0.050; LIBERO-10: 0.736 against 0.707, p=0.026) and beats detect-and-reset on all five (+0.024 to +0.082).
- Real runs: SFF ranks failures above the frozen monitor for every VLA. Pooled, it leads detect-and-reset by +0.113 and a full reset at the true switch by +0.044 (exploratory).
- False-alarm rate on successes is 0.082-0.089 against a 0.1 target, but 0.19-0.37 under compound drift.
- Cost: median 2.18 ms per policy call on one Jetson Orin Nano CPU core, with 1.0 MB of state.

## Limitations
The real-robot evidence replays recorded runs; SFF has not run live. Simulation uses one policy family, gains are modest (0.02-0.05), and there is no clear difference with the authors' own fine-tuned policy. No monitor wins on every suite: an unanchored warm-up-start readout leads on three. When nothing changes, SFF ranks slightly below the frozen monitor. It assumes a correct outcome label after every episode.
