abstract: 'Large language models (LLMs) exhibit a wide range of promising capabilities -- from step-by-step planning to commonsense reasoning -- that may provide utility for robots, but remain prone to confidently hallucinated predictions. In this work, we present KnowNo, which is a framework for measuring and aligning the uncertainty of LLM-based planners such that they know when they don''t know and ask for help when needed. KnowNo builds on the theory of conformal prediction to provide statistical guarantees on task completion while minimizing human help in complex multi-step planning settings. Experiments across a variety of simulated and real robot setups that involve tasks with different modes of ambiguity (e.g., from spatial to numeric uncertainties, from human preferences to Winograd schemas) show that KnowNo performs favorably over modern baselines (which may involve ensembles or extensive prompt tuning) in terms of improving efficiency and autonomy, while providing formal assurances. KnowNo can be used with LLMs out of the box without model-finetuning, and suggests a promising lightweight approach to modeling uncertainty that can complement and scale with the growing capabilities of foundation models. ## What they do
LLM planners can be confidently wrong, and instructions are often ambiguous: "place the bowl in the microwave" when there is a plastic and a metal bowl. KnowNo asks for help only when needed. With human help, the robot succeeds at a user-chosen rate 1-ε while keeping the amount of help small. No fine-tuning.

## How it works
- **Multiple-choice scoring.** The LLM writes four different next steps, plus "an option not listed here". The next-token probabilities of the labels A-E are the scores, which avoids the length bias of scoring whole sentences.
- **Conformal prediction (CP).** 400 labeled calibration scenarios set a score threshold. Options above it form the prediction set. One option: the robot acts. More: a human picks one.
- **Multi-step.** Calibration runs over whole sequences, each scored by its least-confident step. Per-step sets with the same threshold keep the guarantee, even though actions change later inputs.

## Results
- Simulated tabletop rearrangement (ε = 0.15) with attribute, numeric and spatial ambiguity: KnowNo strays least from the target success rate. In the more ambiguous settings it needs up to 24% less help than Simple Set (the same scores thresholded without CP).
- UR5 food sorting by preference, 50 trials, plan success matched at 0.76: help on 0.58 of steps vs. 0.72. Task success is 0.74 vs. 0.72, and 0.38 with no help.
- Kitchen mobile manipulator: help 0.67 vs. 0.81. Task success is 0.76 because low-level actions succeed only about 86% of the time.
- Bimanual KUKA arms: 9 of 10 trials succeed.

## Limitations
The guarantee assumes the text input describes the scene correctly, low-level actions succeed and the human answers truthfully. Labeling the multi-step calibration set took about 4 hours. GPT-3.5 favored options D and E, so it needed more help (0.86).
