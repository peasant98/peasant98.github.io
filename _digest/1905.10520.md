## What they do
To pick up an unknown object seen from a single depth view, a robot has to choose a full 6-DOF gripper pose, and it needs many candidates because some will be unreachable or in collision. 6-DOF GraspNet generates a diverse set of parallel-jaw grasps directly from the object's partial point cloud, then scores and improves them with a second network. Both are trained only on simulated grasps and run on a real robot with no extra steps.

## How it works
- **Training data.** FleX simulates a free-floating gripper on 206 ShapeNet objects (boxes, cylinders, bowls, bottles, mugs): 7.07M simulated grasps, 2.1M of them successful.
- **Grasp sampler.** A PointNet++ conditional VAE with a 2-D latent space, trained on successful grasps only. At test time, sampling z from N(0, I) gives different grasps for the same point cloud.
- **Grasp evaluator.** A PointNet classifier sees the object point cloud merged with a point cloud of the gripper at the grasp pose and predicts success. Hard negatives are perturbed positives that collide with or miss the object.
- **Refinement.** The gradient of the success score with respect to grasp translation and Euler angles moves each grasp, at most 1 cm per step; grasps below a threshold are discarded.

## Results
- Real robot (Franka Panda, wrist-mounted RealSense D415, 17 objects, 51 trials): 88% success versus 47% for GPD. On mugs it is 86% versus 6%, because GPD generates no grasps on the rim.
- In simulation, refinement raises both success and coverage, plateauing after 10 iterations, and the learned sampler beats a surface-normal sampler on both.
- A forward pass takes 0.04 s and each refinement iteration 0.3 s for 200 samples on a Titan XP.

## Limitations
Grasps are generated for one segmented object; reachability and clutter are handled afterwards by a motion planner filtering the samples. The simulator uses a free-floating gripper without gravity and fixed friction and density. Some real failures came from grasps with a finger tangent to an object face, where small execution errors turn the grasp into a push.
