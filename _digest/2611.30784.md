abstract: Reinforcement learning policies are often trained with static domain randomization for real-world deployment. However, real-world environments often exhibit unmodeled dynamic variations, such as payload changes or friction shifts, that lie outside the randomization distribution, leading to performance degradation upon deployment. We propose a framework that moves beyond static domain randomization by adapting a learned dynamics model to the deployed target conditions, with a concurrent teacher-student paradigm applied to both the policy and dynamics model. This framework integrates the dynamics model into an on-policy reinforcement learning pipeline, letting the performance of the policy align with the empirical target dynamics. Experimental validations on bipedal locomotion tasks demonstrate competitive adaptability under extreme physical variations, including a payload reaching 80% of self-weight and an oil-lubricated low-friction surface.

## What they do
Domain randomization handles a fixed range of physics. Conditions outside it, like a heavy payload or a slippery floor, break a narrowly randomized policy, while a very wide range makes the policy conservative. This framework takes a policy trained with nominal randomization and adapts it to one specific target condition. A learned dynamics model is fitted to a short batch of real-robot data, and the policy is then fine-tuned with PPO entirely inside that model, with no further real-world interaction.

## How it works
- **Joint pre-training.** In IsaacSim, a privileged teacher policy, a proprioceptive student policy, and the privileged and proprioceptive branches of a dynamics model train together in one stage.
- **Dynamics model.** A 6-layer causal Transformer over a 16-step state-action history, conditioned through FiLM on an embedding of the physical parameters (inferred from trajectories at deployment), with an ensemble of MLP heads whose spread estimates uncertainty. It is trained with a multi-step autoregressive loss.
- **Real-world adaptation.** About 30 min of data from 20 behavior policies (checkpoints and noisy variants) adapts the proprioceptive branch. The privileged branch supplies reward signals such as base velocity, and an ensemble-uncertainty penalty keeps PPO out of poorly modeled regions.

## Results
- Tested on a TRON 1 point-foot biped (about 19 kg) with a 15 kg payload (about 80% of its weight) and on oil-lubricated ground. Both lie outside the nominal randomization range.
- Real robot, five trials per condition: the nominal policy fails every trial; the adapted policy succeeds 80% with the payload and 100% on oil.
- IsaacSim-to-MuJoCo, payload: 97.3% success, with 45.4% and 25.0% lower velocity tracking error than RMA and system identification. Low friction: 98.7% success, where system identification fails completely.
- Behavior diversity matters: growing the data-collection pool from 1 to 320 policies raises the fine-tuned policy's normalized return from 0.15 to 0.90.

## Limitations
Each adaptation targets one condition and needs a new real-world data batch, collected by running pre-trained policies on the hardware. Real-robot results are five trials per condition. Imagined rollouts must stay short: 1000-step rollouts drop low-friction success from 93.6% to 12.6%. The authors name safe data collection as future work.
