abstract: Quasi-Direct Drive (QDD) actuators are widely used in legged robots due to their low reflected inertia, high backdrivability, and high torque density, commonly employing planetary or cycloidal gearboxes. Single-stage planetary gearboxes offer high efficiency but limited torque density; multi-stage designs improve torque density at the cost of efficiency; and cycloidal gearboxes provide high stiffness but suffer from torque ripple. Compound Planetary Gearboxes (CPG) offer a compromise, but conventional designs result in increased actuator width, mass, and reduced torsional stiffness in 3D-printed actuators. This paper proposes the BiPath Compound Planetary Gearbox (BiPathCPG), a novel architecture that introduces an idle second-stage sun gear and load-sharing second-stage planets within the otherwise unused space of a conventional CPG to simultaneously improve stiffness, reduce actuator width, and reduce mass. An optimization framework computes BiPathCPG parameters for a given motor and target gear ratio. The architecture is validated through a 3D-printed proof-of-concept actuator design, benchmarked against a conventional CPG using custom-built test rigs. The BiPathCPG achieves a torsional stiffness of 1394 Nm/rad, a 1.70x improvement over the conventional CPG, with a 32% reduction in backlash, 25 g lower mass, and approximately 6.4 mm reduction in axial width, while achieving a peak stall torque of 36.49 Nm.

## What they do
Legged-robot actuators often use quasi-direct-drive motors with planetary gearboxes. Compound planetary gearboxes reach higher ratios than single-stage ones at similar efficiency. However, the small second-stage planet carries a large ring force, so it and the ring must be wide. 3D-printed versions also end up less stiff than cycloidal drives. BiPathCPG uses the unused space in the second stage for a second load path.

## How it works
- **Idle gears.** Idle second-stage planets, as many as the compound planets and the same size as their small gears, sit between them. An idle sun on its own bearing meshes with all of the second-stage planets.
- **Load sharing.** The idle path makes the force balance indeterminate, so they introduce a force-sharing factor. A frictionless FEA gives about 0.57 and they adopt 0.6. The primary planet-ring mesh then carries 62.5% of a conventional gearbox's force, so stage-2 gears shrink from 16.4 mm to 10.2 mm wide.
- **Design optimizer.** The COMPAct actuator-design framework is extended with the extra meshing and clearance constraints. It sweeps motors and ratios from 4:1 to 20:1, and a MAD M6C12 motor at 14:1 was built in PLA alongside a conventional baseline.

## Results
- Stiffness is 1394 ± 167 vs 822 ± 10 N·m/rad (1.70x), above the 1.18x their mesh model predicts. Backlash is 0.213° vs 0.311°.
- Measured width is 78.9 vs 85.3 mm and mass is 795 vs 820 g. Peak stall torque is 36.49 vs 36.22 N·m.
- Efficiency is 87.3% vs 90.6%, and backdrive torque is 0.59 vs 0.52 N·m.
- Both ran 12 h at about 8 N·m peak without failure. Under cycling with peaks of about 28 N·m, the stage-1 sun stripped after 45 min (BiPathCPG) and 37 min (baseline).

## Limitations
The extra meshes cost about 3 points of efficiency, make it slightly harder to backdrive and complicate assembly. Both prototypes are PLA, and the shared first-stage sun failed within an hour under heavy cyclic load. Cycloidal drives remain more axially compact at comparable ratios. The actuator is tested only on bench rigs, not in a robot.
