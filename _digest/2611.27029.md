abstract: 'Motor decoding from electroencephalography (EEG) supports non-invasive brain-computer interfaces, yet existing methods either rely on costly cue-based trials and coarse categorical labels or use pretraining objectives that do not directly capture how movement unfolds, limiting their ability to learn transferable motor representations. To this end, we propose the Mind World Model (MWM), a framework that learns motor EEG representations from scalable naturalistic EEG and egocentric video without action annotations. MWM treats EEG observed up to the current frame as the latent action of a world model: a mind model encodes the EEG, and a world model uses it to predict the representation of a future frame, yielding a movement-grounded pretraining objective. We further introduce the Lateralization-Aware Transformer (LAT), which uses frame averaging to separate shared bilateral from side-specific lateral features, encoding hemispheric correspondence to reduce the burden of learning left-right relationships from data. MWM pretraining produces two models: a mind model for downstream motor decoding and a world model that can be adapted to prediction conditioned on real actions, opening a scalable and flexible path to collect egocentric data for embodied AI. Across 9 classification and 7 regression tasks, the mind model improves task-averaged balanced accuracy by 5.9% and Pearson correlation by 16.2% relative to the strongest baseline in each task family. Compared with an inverse-dynamics latent-action baseline, the world model achieves lower prediction error and benefits 3.3 times more from matched real actions within recordings and 4.7 times more on held-out subjects.'

## What they do
EEG motor decoders usually train on cue-based lab trials with coarse labels such as "left hand", and EEG foundation models pretrain on EEG-only objectives. MWM instead learns from 63.28 hours of synchronized EEG and egocentric video (40 EgoBrain participants) without action labels: the EEG observed up to the current frame serves as the latent action of a world model that must predict the next frame.

## How it works
- **World model.** A frozen DINOv2 encodes the current frame and the frame 0.2 s later. A two-layer Transformer predictor, modulated by the EEG action through AdaLN, adds a predicted change to the current patch features and is trained with an L1 loss against the future features. The EEG window ends at the current frame, so it cannot contain the target.
- **Lateralization-Aware Transformer (LAT).** A learned gate splits EEG tokens into bilateral and lateral parts, and a mirror swaps only the lateral part between homologous electrodes (e.g. C3 and C4). Frame averaging over the original and mirrored passes gives an invariant bilateral readout and an equivariant lateral one.
- **Training.** Videos are flipped horizontally with the matching EEG mirror to offset dominant-hand bias. The loss adds masked EEG reconstruction, EEG-frame contrastive alignment and future prediction.

## Results
- Linear probes on participant-disjoint splits: MWM is best on 15 of 16 tasks; REVE stays ahead on grip and load force (0.460 vs 0.433 Pearson).
- Ablations: removing alignment lowers average transfer by 17.6%, removing prediction by 2.6% and removing frame averaging by 2.3%.
- Swapping homologous raw EEG channels flips the lateral features' correlation with relative hand use from 0.096 to -0.091, while the bilateral total-speed correlation barely moves (0.463 to 0.451).
- With IMU as a proxy for real actions, the world model has the lowest prediction error, and shuffling the IMU inputs hurts it 3.3x (within recordings) and 4.7x (held-out subjects) more than an inverse-dynamics latent-action model.

## Limitations
The world model is judged only by prediction error in DINOv2 feature space, with IMU standing in for actions; end-to-end use such as robot planning is left to future work. Pretraining uses a single dataset, and several gains are small (FineMI 0.200 vs 0.199 balanced accuracy).
