## What they do
GPU-parallel tactile simulators usually derive touch from raw penetration depth, so normal force saturates at the solver's depth limit and tangential force is undefined when contacts stick. Tactile benchmarks also mostly use two-finger grippers. ViTacLab tackles both: a simulator that stays physically consistent at scale, a benchmark that spans grippers, dexterous hands and bimanual arms, and a policy that accepts any number and type of tactile sensors.

## How it works
- **ViTacSim.** Built on PhysX (Isaac). Each sensor outputs a sparse contact-force lattice, a dense force array and a tactile image. Normal force is corrected by local stiffness alignment against the solver's force-to-depth ratio; tangential force is rebuilt separately for sliding and sticking regions and clipped to the Coulomb friction cone. A final projection keeps the summed field equal to the solver's wrench.
- **ViTacBench.** 15 tasks (peg insertion, gear meshing, nut threading, in-hand manipulation, blind grasping, bottle opening, handover and others) graded from vision-dominant to tactile-dominant; in Level 3 the wrist camera is masked.
- **ViTacPolicy.** Each tactile input is tagged with an embedding of its sensor's pose and fused by a Transformer, so sensor count and placement can vary; the policy also sees RGB and proprioception. Force-array and tactile-image encoders are pretrained as a two-stream VQ-VAE with a shared codebook.

## Results
- Force RMSE versus a penetration-only model: normal 2.31 to 0.42 N (Hertzian reference), tangential 1.85 to 0.53 N (real-sensor sliding). Throughput reaches 2,482.9 tactile FPS at 256 environments, with 1.9% more GPU memory than TacSL.
- In simulation the full policy is best on 4 of 6 reported tasks (grasp 90% vs 29% for Diffusion Policy), but every method gets 3% or less on Blind Grasp.
- Real gripper (peg, nut, gear; 50 trials each): average success 61.3% with sim-pretrained touch vs 48.7% without pretraining. No single task is significant (Fisher p = 0.126 to 0.537); pooled, the odds ratio is 1.82 (p = 0.018).

## Limitations
No deformable objects, adhesion, wear or large elastomer deformation, and contacts must stay within the tactile patches. Validation covers only component-level force error, not dense-field or tactile-image fidelity. Dexterous and bimanual real deployments are qualitative only.
