abstract: Robotic manipulation policies can improve action prediction by anticipating how objects move and how their spatial relationships change. World Action Models (WAMs) learn to predict future visual observations, but their training objectives typically leave the underlying 3D geometric transitions implicit. To alleviate this problem, we introduce Foresight Forcing ($\mathcal{F}^{2}$), a WAM that learns a spatial transition representation from changes in a pretrained 3D feature space. During training, a frozen 3D foundation model extracts patch-wise feature differences from aligned current and future observations that provide dense supervision for geometry-aware feature transitions. We distill these targets into spatial transition queries, which are progressively refined from the current observation, language instruction, and proprioceptive state, and use them alongside current visual features to generate actions. At deployment, the policy predicts the queries without future observations or the 3D teacher. Experiments on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and RoboTwin2.0 demonstrate strong manipulation performance and improved robustness, including 95.01% average success on RoboTwin2.0 Full. On six real-world manipulation tasks, $\mathcal{F}^{2}$ achieves a mean task score of 85.8%.

## What they do
World action models (WAMs) learn to act while predicting future images, but the 3D changes that matter for control, such as an object's pose relative to a target, stay implicit in the pixels. Foresight Forcing (F²) builds on ImageWAM and adds latent queries that learn to predict how a frozen 3D foundation model's features will change over the next action chunk. The action expert reads these queries when generating actions.

## How it works
- **Target.** Each current image is warped onto the future frame (16 steps ahead) with optical flow. Both images go through a frozen VGGT, and the patch-wise feature difference is the target.
- **Queries.** There is one learnable query per visual token, inside a FLUX.2 [klein] 4B backbone. After each double block, a shared cross-attention updater refines them from current-image, instruction and proprioception tokens; future tokens are masked out.
- **Training.** The projected queries are aligned with the target in direction and scale, alongside ImageWAM's image and action flow-matching losses.
- **Deployment.** Neither future frames nor VGGT are needed. Inference takes 195 ms per 16-action chunk against ImageWAM's 187 ms.

## Results
- RoboTwin 2.0 Full: 95.01% average, 1.70 (clean) and 1.56 (randomized) points above ImageWAM. LIBERO: 98.5% against ImageWAM's 98.4%.
- LIBERO-Plus: 84.5% against ImageWAM's 81.6%, with the largest gains under robot-state (+8.7) and camera (+5.4) perturbations. Feature-difference targets beat no distillation (82.3%) and current- or future-feature targets (83.2%, 81.2%).
- Real world: six bimanual tasks with 15 trials each give an 85.8% mean score against 83.5% for π0.5 and 70.2% for ImageWAM, without robot pretraining.

## Limitations
The target is a change in teacher features, not a measurement of 3D motion. Robot-state perturbations remain hard, with π0.5 and OpenWAM-α ahead there. On RoboTwin Clean-to-Random, ImageWAM given the same extra augmentation reaches 76.00% against F²'s 77.45%, and success drops from 87.84% on clean scenes to 67.06% on randomized ones. The real-robot tests use the six training tasks only; the authors leave generalization to future work.
