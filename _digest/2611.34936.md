abstract: 'Synthesizing diverse dexterous manipulation policies is challenging: existing simulation-based approaches rely on human demonstrations which are difficult to scale, or use generic reward functions, which provide limited control over the hand-object interactions. We propose DexLink, which addresses these limitations by synthesizing diverse dexterous behaviors from sparse, reusable hand-object interaction templates. Given key task waypoints, DexLink retargets static interaction templates to target objects using an optimization that preserves spatial hand-object relationships. It then links the retargeted poses into an approximate reference trajectory and uses residual reinforcement learning to refine this reference into a closed-loop policy in simulation. Experiments spanning single-hand, bimanual, articulated, and dynamic tasks demonstrate that DexLink provides fine-grained control over task timing, grasp types, and high-level execution. The modular interaction templates transfer reliably across object scales, instances, and categories; without object-specific human demonstrations, the framework also matches the performance of dense reference-tracking methods. Finally, zero-shot deployment of policies trained purely in simulation shows promise for the simulation-based framework.'

## What they do
Hand-written RL rewards give little control over how a dexterous hand touches the object, and dense human demonstrations are costly and tied to one object. DexLink takes a sparse task description instead: a sequence of object waypoints, with a human hand-object interaction template (from OakInk or ARCTIC) attached to the waypoints where the grasp matters. From this it trains a closed-loop policy for an XHand.

## How it works
- **Object-aware retargeting.** For each template, an optimization finds a robot wrist pose and finger configuration that keep the human hand's finger directions, palm orientation and relation to the object surface. It works on a different target object, and for hands not yet in contact, as in handovers.
- **Chaining.** Retargeted poses are interpolated along the object waypoints, in the object frame, into an approximate reference for wrist and fingers.
- **Residual RL.** A PPO policy in Genesis predicts bounded corrections to the reference, rewarded for object tracking, keeping the specified interaction and smooth motion. Episodes reset at random points along the reference.

## Results
- Retargeting 287 OakInk interactions: about 60% satisfy force closure at a 1 cm contact threshold (80% at 2 cm).
- Five bottle and mug templates at four object scales give perfect lift success in 13 of 20 conditions, failing mostly at the extreme scales. Templates moved to another instance or category (e.g. apple to camera) lift with 100% success.
- Ablations: without the reference nothing is learned. The full method reaches 92% on a mug grasp change, 100% on notebook opening, 86.7% on stacking three blocks and 97.3% on a bimanual banana handover.
- Real Franka + XHand with point-cloud policies: grasp-and-lift succeeded 7/10 (mug), 9/10 (bottle), 6/10 (cup) and 8/10 (block); cup and block were not seen in training.

## Limitations
Tasks must be specified explicitly as waypoints and templates. Real-world tests cover grasp-and-lift only, objects are rigid, and some task variants vary strongly across RL seeds.
