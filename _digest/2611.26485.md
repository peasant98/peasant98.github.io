abstract: 'Surgical teleoperation deprives surgeons of touch, forcing visual inference of tissue forces; the commercial platforms now restoring force feedback remain closed, preventing open research on haptic shared control. This paper provides a system contribution: it presents an open-source, ROS 2-native framework that integrates the compact, three-degree-of-freedom (3-DOF) Haply Inverse3 haptic device with the da~Vinci Research Kit (dVRK). The framework includes a novel mapping strategy that combines 3-DOF translational device input with separately tracked VerseGrip orientation into 6-DOF patient-side manipulator commands. Two virtual-fixture-inspired guidance modes serve as reference implementations to evaluate the framework in a within-subjects user study on a teleoperated hot-wire benchmark task in simulation, and the architecture is further demonstrated on physical dVRK hardware. Relative to unassisted manual control, linear guidance significantly reduced both the number of wire contacts and the task completion time, whereas the progressive-stiffness guidance produced consistent but statistically non-significant trends in the same direction. Subjective ratings of workload, agency, and satisfaction showed favorable descriptive trends for the guidance modes. The results demonstrate the feasibility of haptic shared control with a compact device coupled to the dVRK and provide an openly available, reproducible platform for future research. They further suggest that simpler linear feedback may be more robust for inexperienced operators than more complex profiles.'

## What they do
Commercial surgical consoles with force feedback are closed, and dVRK haptics research mostly uses sigma.7 or PHANToM masters. The authors release a ROS 2-native framework that connects the compact 3-DOF Haply Inverse3 and its VerseGrip orientation-tracking stylus to the dVRK patient-side manipulator (PSM). They validate it with two simple virtual-fixture guidance modes on a hot-wire task.

## How it works
- **Driver.** A ROS 2 package wraps Haply's WebSocket service, publishes the device position, orientation and buttons, and accepts force commands or PID position targets. Per-axis force saturation and a 0.5 s watchdog zero the output if commands stop arriving.
- **Mapping.** A bridge combines the 3-DOF stylus position with VerseGrip roll-pitch-yaw into relative 6-DOF commands. The experiments use a joint-space mapping (stylus yaw to instrument roll, roll and pitch to the wrist), with stylus buttons for clutching and the gripper.
- **Guidance.** Within 15 mm of the wire, Mode 1 pulls the operator back with a linear spring (80 N/m) plus damping. Mode 2 is softer near the wire (40 N/m) and stiffer farther out (120 N/m), with the same maximum force.

## Results
- 20 participants, within-subjects and counterbalanced, used the full software stack with a kinematic PSM model and a virtual wire on a 2D monitor.
- Linear guidance reduced wire contacts from 31.85 to 20.90 (p=.005) and completion time from 96.3 s to 75.0 s (p=.003). Progressive guidance trended the same way (24.35 contacts, 84.3 s) but was not significant.
- Ratings of workload, agency and satisfaction favored guidance, but none survived Bonferroni correction.
- On a real dVRK the loop ran at 1 kHz along a wire trajectory with no instability or watchdog interruptions.

## Limitations
The user study ran in simulation. The physical dVRK run is a preliminary demonstration, with no quantitative validation or latency measurement. The hot-wire task tests path following, not surgical skill, and the monocular 2D display may inflate the benefit of guidance.
