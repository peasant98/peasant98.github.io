abstract: Many embodied world models predict future states in latent spaces optimized for RGB reconstruction, which do not explicitly prioritize object-level semantics and geometry relevant to motion generation. We present MotionRep, a visual representation world model that jointly generates future DINO representations and 3D object flow, coupling semantic and geometric information with object-centric dynamics. To make high-dimensional DINO features amenable to generative modeling, we introduce DINO-VAE, which combines temporal and channel compression with latent regularization to produce compact latents while preserving semantic and geometric information. Conditioned on an initial observation, object query points, and a language instruction, a unified diffusion transformer predicts future visual representations and 3D object flow through joint attention. MotionRep employs asymmetric modality-specific noise schedules to keep the visual stream ahead of 3D object flow during joint denoising, allowing progressively refined future visual representations to guide flow generation. Experiments on simulated and real-world manipulation benchmarks demonstrate that MotionRep outperforms pixel-based baselines in 3D object flow accuracy and generalization to unseen scenes, while requiring only three denoising steps for inference. These results support predictive visual representations as an effective future-state space for object-centric dynamics modeling.

## What they do
3D object flow, the future 3D trajectories of points on a manipulated object, is a useful action representation for manipulation. Models that predict it alongside future video usually work in pixel-VAE latents built for RGB reconstruction, which spend capacity on texture. MotionRep instead predicts the future in DINOv3 feature space. Given one image, 64 query points on the target object and a language instruction, it generates 48 future frames of DINO features and object flow together.

## How it works
- **DINO-VAE.** Compresses DINOv3-L features 4x in time and 8x in channels (to 128 dimensions) with a KL-regularized ViT autoencoder, so diffusion can model them.
- **Joint diffusion transformer.** Built on Cosmos3-Nano (6.97B trainable parameters). DINO latents and flow tokens, which carry 3D position encodings, share joint attention.
- **Asymmetric noise schedules.** Separate schedules keep the visual stream less noisy than the flow, so predicted future features guide the trajectories. The same model can also predict flow from given future features, or features from given flow.
- Trained on 967,377 clips from 11 simulated, internet and in-house sources; real-world flow labels come from SAM 2 plus the Track4World tracker.

## Results
- WorldFlowBench mean trajectory error: 0.0447 m in simulation (WorldFlow, the best baseline: 0.0510 m), 0.0657 m versus 0.0810 m on real internet video and 0.0567 m versus 0.0600 m on in-house real video.
- Predicts 48 frames in 2.21 s with 3 denoising steps, 6x faster than WorldFlow and 83x faster than MolmoMotion.
- Removing future DINO prediction raises error on RoboTwin from 0.0315 m to 0.0517 m.
- A linear probe on DINO-VAE latents scores 79.17% on UCF101, versus 14.51% and 16.94% for CogVideoX and Wan2.2 video-VAE latents.

## Limitations
Real-world flow labels come from 3D trackers and contain tracking noise. Evaluation compares predicted trajectories with recorded clips; no robot executes the predicted flow. Longer-horizon tasks are left to future work.
