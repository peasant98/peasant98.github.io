abstract: 'We present a progressive imitation framework that learns terrain-appropriate humanoid parkour skills and subsequently adapts their hand--knee support. Human demonstrations provide traversal behaviors, but their contact patterns need not suit a robot''s proportions or the available support surfaces. Our framework changes the role of demonstrations across three stages: privileged imitation produces physically executable robot references; terrain-indexed adversarial motion priors guide skill acquisition; and geometric support guidance with a reduced motion-prior weight enables contact adaptation. Candidate-based precontact attraction and actual-contact evaluation guide support without prescribing a contact sequence. The deployed policy selects behaviors from onboard depth, proprioception, and velocity commands without an external skill label. On the Noetix E1, the policy achieves 96.34\% hybrid success in simulation and transfers without real-world policy fine-tuning, completing 29 of 30 individual obstacle trials and four of five hybrid courses. These results support acquiring skills from demonstrations before relaxing their influence to accommodate robot-specific support configurations.'

## What they do
Human parkour clips show how to get over an obstacle, but a robot with different proportions may need its hands and knees elsewhere. One perceptive humanoid policy is trained in three stages: make human clips executable, learn terrain-appropriate skills under motion priors, then relax the priors and reward support that suits the robot. It picks skills from onboard depth and proprioception, with no skill label.

## How it works
- **Reference preparation.** Privileged mimic training turns eight retargeted human clips into executable robot rollouts with torques, contacts and height scans.
- **Terrain-indexed motion priors.** An AMP discriminator with a shared trunk and four heads (flat, and Low, Mid and High frames of about 0.4, 0.6 and 1.0 m) scores the policy against the current terrain's references. The actor sees a 24x24 depth image, proprioception and velocity commands.
- **Support adaptation.** Flat, reachable patches on obstacle surfaces become hand and knee candidates; rewards pull limbs toward them and score the support achieved, while the AMP weight is halved.

## Results
- Simulation (Isaac Lab, 2,048 episodes per condition): 96.3% success on a Low-Mid-High course vs 79.7% for a multi-skill distillation baseline; a single AMP prior and plain velocity tracking never clear High. At heights 1-3 cm outside training: 93.5% vs 65.3%.
- On a 1 m tall, 1 m x 1 m platform: 93.5% success, vs 46.2% without candidate rewards and 58.9% without relaxing the prior.
- Zero-shot on the 23-DoF Noetix E1: 10/10 Low, 9/10 Mid, 10/10 High, 4/5 courses, 13/15 at out-of-range heights.

## Limitations
On hardware, the failed Mid trial lost arm support, and the failed course had an abrupt heading change followed by the Mid skill on a Low platform; the authors name support maintenance and skill transitions as open problems. Stage ablations also drop training iterations, so they do not isolate each stage's objective. Candidates are near-flat patches; irregular surfaces are future work.
