abstract: Reasoning about people's plans and goals based on their actions is fundamental to human social interaction and essential for cooperative, embodied artificial intelligence. Formalizing this capacity in naturalistic, embodied settings has been a longstanding challenge. Here, we present GenEA, a novel agent representation capable of reasoning over multi-step, long-horizon embodied plans. At the heart of GenEA lies a multilevel state-space that couples the coarse biomechanics of an articulated body and its interactions with nearby objects by extending the task-and-motion planning framework from robotics with simulation-based intuitive physics. By embedding this state-space in a generative model, GenEA yields a joint probability distribution over goals and embodied plans. Across several experiments spanning the qualitatively distinct domains of object manipulation and navigation, featuring diverse tasks and multiple behavioral measures, we show that GenEA, through simple transformations of this joint distribution, recapitulates how humans reason about embodied plans. Crucially, GenEA consistently outperforms strong alternative models, including plausible heuristics, standard task-and-motion planning, and a state-of-the-art vision-language model. GenEA thereby offers a new perspective on the architecture of human reasoning and social cognition, suggesting that embodied artificial intelligence systems seeking to seamlessly interact with humans should adopt GenEA-like generative models of action planning.

## What they do
People read goals and plans off the bodily actions of others. Existing computational accounts treat the agent as a point in a 2D grid world. GenEA (Generative Embodied Agent) is an agent model with an articulated body that manipulates blocks and walks or jumps across platforms. It is scored on how well it predicts human judgments about such scenes, not on robot task success.

## How it works
- **Three levels.** A symbolic level holds action primitives (Pick, Cache, Fix; Step, Jump, Turn) and an automaton over them. A kinematics level turns each into joint trajectories with noisy endpoints, using KOMO for manipulation and Crocoddyl with the lower body of a Talos humanoid for navigation. A dynamics level, absent from standard TAMP, runs a physics engine (PhysX or MuJoCo) so objects fall or topple under gravity.
- **Distribution over plans.** A breadth-first planner returns the plans that reach a final configuration. Each plan gets a Boltzmann probability from its cost, the kinetic energy of its trajectories.
- **Queries.** Goal inference, action inference, one hand versus two, and task difficulty are all read out of this joint distribution over goals and plans, with a parameter-free choice rule.

## Results
- Goal and action inference on block structures (60 participants): r = .88 with human ratings, significantly above TAMP without the dynamics level, geometric heuristics and a vision-language model.
- One hand or two (22 trials, 60 participants): r = .93.
- Difficulty of obstacle courses (22 trials, 60 participants): r = .91. Humans and GenEA both rate wooden platforms harder than stone ones; TAMP shows no such sensitivity.
- A GenEA-derived evidence rate also correlates with human response times (r = -.54 and r = -.59).

## Limitations
GenEA is not image-computable: it is handed the object configurations, and the authors name perception as the extension needed for robotics use. Action primitives, their rules and the noise parameters are predefined by hand. The authors frame it as a computational-level account, not the algorithm the mind runs. Stimuli are small block scenes and platform courses.
