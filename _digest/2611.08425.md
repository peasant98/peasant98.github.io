abstract: Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) enable general-purpose embodied intelligence, but their performance is brittle under distribution shift, where unanticipated failures can damage equipment or endanger people. Deploying VLAs in real-world systems therefore requires a runtime monitor that is timely, calibrated, and informative while being efficient enough to run on the edge. Existing methods fail to fulfill these requirements. We present VLA-Tug, a lightweight probe over the latent layers of a VLA backbone that improves early failure detection and bounds the missed-detection and false-alarm rates, with minimal inference overhead. We show that supervising on the pre-failure segment of a trajectory increases early failure detection at matched false-positive rates by up to 30% over the strongest learned baseline. Under shift to unseen tasks, the bounds on missed-detection and false-alarm rates remain valid in up to 86% of runs, versus at most 21% for baselines. Unlike existing methods, VLA-Tug predicts the failure mode to enable corrective actions, raising top-3 accuracy on unseen tasks by up to 44% over a majority baseline. VLA-Tug enables real-time monitoring where the well-timed and informative probe supports lightweight steering that improves task success. We release a dataset of 3,180 annotated manipulation episodes to support future work on latent failure detection in VLAs at https://vla-tug.github.io.

## What they do
VLA-Tug is a runtime failure monitor for vision-language-action policies. A small probe reads the VLA's own backbone embeddings at every policy step and outputs a failure score and a predicted failure mode. A calibrated threshold on the score raises the alarm, ideally before the failure happens, so an expert model can step in. They test it on π0.5 and GR00T-N1.7 over 228 simulated tasks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Occ, LIBERO-X) and 8 real tasks on a Kinova Gen3 arm.

## How it works
- **Probe.** Per step, the mean backbone tokens of the agent camera, wrist camera and prompt are concatenated into a 6144-d vector. A 256-unit LSTM feeds three MLP heads: failure score, a 10-way failure mode, and an auxiliary CLIP-aligned description of the current action.
- **Pre-onset supervision.** Failed rollouts are labeled positive only before the failure time and cut off there. Gemini labels failure time, mode and a timeline from each rollout video, for about one cent per failed episode.
- **Calibration.** Learn-then-Test picks a threshold that jointly guarantees false-positive rate ≤ α and early true-positive rate ≥ β. This bounds the chance that an alarm is a real failure, and the method abstains when the targets can't be met.

## Results
- Early-detection area on unseen tasks: best in four of six settings. Against SAFE-LSTM it scores -1% on new-task, +14% on new-signature and +30% on new-skill splits. On the real robot the lead is small (25.8 vs 25.1), and it does not lead on LIBERO-10 with GR00T.
- Calibration on LIBERO-X: both targets held in every certified run on seen tasks. On unseen tasks the false-alarm target held in 40-86% of runs, versus at most 21% for max-conformal.
- Failure mode: top-1 balanced accuracy up to 149% (seen) and 79% (unseen) above a majority baseline on LIBERO-X.
- Trains in 6.8 s and adds 0.12 ms per step on a GPU.

## Limitations
The guarantees assume exchangeable episodes and hold only empirically on unseen tasks. The real-robot set is too small (12-14 calibration successes per run) to certify the calibration targets. The steering demos use simple heuristics and are not evaluated.
