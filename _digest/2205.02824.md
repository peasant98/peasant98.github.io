## What they do
A single neural network controller for the MIT Mini Cheetah, trained with RL in simulation and deployed zero-shot, tracks commanded forward, lateral and yaw velocity up to high speeds. Sampling fast commands uniformly from the start makes training fail, so they add an automatic curriculum over commands and reuse teacher-student online system identification from prior work.

## How it works
- **Policy.** Inputs are joint angles and velocities, the IMU gravity vector, the previous action and the velocity command; outputs are joint position targets for a PD controller at 50 Hz. PPO in Isaac Gym: 400M steps from 4000 parallel agents, under three hours on one RTX 3090.
- **Grid Adaptive curriculum.** Forward and yaw commands are sampled jointly from a 0.5 m/s by 0.5 rad/s grid that starts at [-1, 1] for both. When a command's tracking rewards pass a threshold, its grid neighbors are added. Unlike the Box Adaptive variant, which samples the two independently, it avoids infeasible combinations of fast running and turning.
- **Online system identification.** A teacher sees the randomized friction, restitution, payload, center of mass and motor strength through an encoder. A student module regresses the same latent from 15 steps of history and reuses the teacher's policy body.

## Results
- Indoors (motion capture, 6.0 m/s command): 3.81 m/s average across seeds, 3.9 m/s top sustained speed, versus the previous 3.7 m/s record from model-predictive control on this robot. Froude number 5.1. Without system identification: 2.49 m/s.
- Outdoors: a 10 m grass dash in 2.94 s (3.4 m/s). Yaw rate reaches 5.7 rad/s, 90% of the 6.28 rad/s model-based record, and the robot stays stable spinning on ice.
- Without a curriculum the policy fails to learn; Grid Adaptive covers a larger command area than Box Adaptive at all error thresholds. The student nearly matches the teacher, and both beat domain randomization.
- An MPC baseline did not recover from slipping on a gravel incline or tripping over a barrier; the learned policy did.

## Limitations
A large sim-to-real gap at top speed remains (5.46 m/s in simulation vs 3.81 m/s real). Outdoor tests are qualitative, without motion capture. The policy only controls planar body velocity and has no vision, so it cannot plan for stairs or pitfalls. It was trained only on flat ground; adding terrain roughness in training lowers high-speed performance.
