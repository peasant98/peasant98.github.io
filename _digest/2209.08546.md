abstract: Recently, Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) has shown promising performances on reconstructing 3D scenes and synthesizing novel views from a sparse set of 2D images. Albeit effective, the performance of NeRF is highly influenced by the quality of training samples. With limited posed images from the scene, NeRF fails to generalize well to novel views and may collapse to trivial solutions in unobserved regions. This makes NeRF impractical under resource-constrained scenarios. In this paper, we present a novel learning framework, ActiveNeRF, aiming to model a 3D scene with a constrained input budget. Specifically, we first incorporate uncertainty estimation into a NeRF model, which ensures robustness under few observations and provides an interpretation of how NeRF understands the scene. On this basis, we propose to supplement the existing training set with newly captured samples based on an active learning scheme. By evaluating the reduction of uncertainty given new inputs, we select the samples that bring the most information gain. In this way, the quality of novel view synthesis can be improved with minimal additional resources. Extensive experiments validate the performance of our model on both realistic and synthetic scenes, especially with scarcer training data. Code will be released at https://github.com/LeapLabTHU/ActiveNeRF.

## What they do
NeRF trained on few images, or on images that miss part of the scene, generalizes poorly and can collapse to zero density in the unseen regions. ActiveNeRF works under a fixed capture budget: it adds an uncertainty estimate to NeRF and uses it to decide which new views are worth capturing.

## How it works
- **Uncertain radiance.** An extra MLP branch with a softplus predicts a variance for each point's color. Because points are independent and volume rendering is a weighted sum, each ray's color is also Gaussian. Training minimizes its negative log-likelihood plus a density-sparsity term that prevents blurry surfaces. Only the fine network gets this branch.
- **Acquisition.** For a candidate view, Bayesian updating gives the posterior variance of every sampled point without knowing the new image's colors. The summed drop from prior to posterior variance scores the view, and the top-k candidates are added.
- **Using new views.** Either keep training on them (continuous learning) or plug their colors into the posterior mean without training (Bayesian estimation). Candidates can be scored at reduced resolution to save time.

## Results
- Fixed training sets: with all images it is on par with NeRF (30.45 vs 31.01 PSNR on synthetic scenes, LPIPS 0.072 vs 0.081). With 5 images it reaches 23.23 vs 21.14 PSNR.
- Active setting with 20 total views: continuous learning gets 26.24 PSNR on synthetic scenes vs 24.25 random and 26.00 furthest-view sampling, and 23.12 vs 20.65 and 22.41 on LLFF. Training takes 2.2 h vs 2.0 h.
- Bayesian estimation takes 30 min but drops to 25.67 PSNR, below furthest-view sampling.
- Scoring candidates at 1/5 resolution keeps 26.12 PSNR; at 1/20 it falls to 24.67.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. Its active-learning experiments choose among held-out dataset images rather than capturing new ones. With all training images, the uncertainty model scores slightly below NeRF in PSNR on both datasets. With 10 total views, the training-free Bayesian variant falls below random selection on synthetic scenes.
