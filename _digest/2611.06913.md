## What they do
cuRoboV2-Fast is a single-GPU planner for collision-free, torque-limited arm motions that cuts both planning latency and execution time. The payload is part of the dynamics, so a heavy object can change the path itself, not just slow it down.

## How it works
- **B-spline trajectory optimization.** Each trajectory sample depends on four controls, so GPU threads evaluate samples independently and each control gathers gradients only from its local support. This matches cuRoboV1's success with 25 instead of 125 initial iterations.
- **TimeOpt.** With the path fixed, a second cubic B-spline over squared path speed is optimized under sampled velocity, acceleration, jerk and torque limits. Local support gives banded Newton systems, solved by a primal-dual predictor-corrector method in 0.28 ms on average.
- **Faster robot computations.** Shared gradient accumulation for kinematics, per-link bounding boxes that skip sphere-pair collision tests, and differentiable inverse dynamics whose payload can change at runtime without recompiling.
- **Parallel GPU RRT** seeds the optimizer when a straight line fails, in 0.305 ms on average.

## Results
- With a 3 kg payload, planning takes 4.43 ms on an RTX 5090 (14.7x faster than cuRoboV2) and 25.99 ms on a Jetson AGX Orin, faster than cuRoboV1 + TOPPRA on the RTX 5090. Success matches cuRoboV2 and beats the TOPPRA baselines by 14.5-15.7 points at the heaviest payload.
- TimeOpt shortens mean motion duration by 15.7% beyond uniform timestep refinement.
- On a real YAM arm doing table clearing and bin picking from stereo cameras, mean cycle time is 5.93 s, 34.6% shorter than cuRoboV2; planning takes 1.0% of it.

## Limitations
TimeOpt converges to a local optimum, with no guarantee of time-optimality. With planning down to 1% of the cycle, the authors name reliable grasping and contact-rich interaction as the remaining bottleneck.
