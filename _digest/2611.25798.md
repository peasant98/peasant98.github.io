abstract: 'We propose a simple stair traversal mechanism that augments a four-wheel wheelchair with an auxiliary telescopic chassis actuator. The proposed mechanism provides the following key contributions during stair ascent: (A) enhanced safety by ensuring that either the front or rear wheels remain stationary at all times; (B) primary reliance of the required force and mechanical work on the telescopic chassis rather than the wheels; (C) insensitivity of the required force and mechanical work to stair inclination; and (D) reduced risk of wheel slip on steeper stairs. ## What they do
Stair-climbing wheelchairs exist but are mechanically complex. This paper adds one extend-retract actuator to a four-wheel wheelchair. Climbing backwards, one wheel pair is held still on a tread by self-locking worm gears while extending or retracting the chassis rolls the other pair up over the stair edge; alternating the two climbs one step at a time. A half-scale, one-tenth-mass prototype is analyzed statically and tested on wooden stairs carrying an 8 kg anthropomorphic dummy.

## How it works
- **Mechanism.** Each wheel pair (107 mm loaded radius, larger than the riser but smaller than the tread) is driven by a 60 W motor through a 40:1 worm gear. A ball screw rated at 968 N telescopes the chassis, varying the wheelbase from 225 to 503 mm.
- **Static analysis.** Equilibrium at the moments the upper or lower wheels lift off gives the friction the stationary wheels need. With zero wheel torque and the seat at its best position, the required friction coefficient falls from 0.72 on a 24° stair to 0.48 on a 40° stair, because on steeper stairs the chassis pushes the stationary wheels down rather than back.
- **Sliding seat.** A rack-and-pinion seat moves the center of mass to the analysis-derived position for each stair, and forward at stair entry so the rear wheels can mount the first step.

## Results
- On 24°, 32° and 40° stairs the peak chassis force was about 10% of its rating and barely changed with inclination; wheel torques stayed small next to their 3.42 Nm rating.
- Work per metre climbed was 284, 289 and 203 J/m, with the chassis contributing over 94% each time; the steepest stair needed the least.
- With 3 kg and 11 kg dummies on the steepest stair, ascent stayed stable once the seat was set. On nine staircases (every pairing of three risers and three treads), 27 consecutive climbs succeeded with no failures.

## Limitations
The prototype is half scale, externally powered and teleoperated by an operator watching it, and the operator also sets the seat position. Only ascent is measured, and turning is outside the paper's scope. Automatic seat control and stopping at riser contact via load cells are future work.
