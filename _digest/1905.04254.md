## What they do
State-of-the-art legged robots take years to develop and are usually expensive custom designs. Stanford Doggo is a small quadruped (42 cm long, 4.8 kg, 8 degrees of freedom) built around a quasi-direct-drive (QDD) belt transmission. The CAD files, bill of materials, wiring and software are open-source, and the robot needs only hand tools to assemble.

## How it works
- **Coaxial belt drive.** Each leg is a parallel linkage driven by two motors through 3:1 belt drives with 3D-printed plastic pulleys. The two drive shafts nest coaxially, so the leg can rotate continuously, unlike Minitaur's.
- **Why QDD.** By the paper's motor scaling laws, a transmission with ratio N saves mass over a larger direct-drive motor if it weighs less than (sqrt(N) - 1) times the motor mass. The belt drive weighs 27% of the motor mass.
- **Electronics.** A Teensy 3.5 sends leg position commands at 100 Hz to four ODrive controllers, which run field-oriented control at 10 kHz on T-Motor MN5212 motors.
- **Control.** Walking, trotting, bounding and pronking come from open-loop piecewise-sinusoidal foot trajectories, tracked with PD control on leg angle and extension that acts as virtual stiffness and damping.

## Results
- Against Minitaur's direct-drive actuator: 76% higher continuous torque (1.51 vs 0.86 Nm) and 37% higher peak torque for 8% more mass and about three times the reflected inertia, at $120 instead of $280.
- Foot force estimated from motor currents tracks a load cell with 2.8% mean absolute error on steps and 5.3% on sine waves; control bandwidth is about 150 Hz.
- Maximum jump height is 1.14 m, more than twice that of any other quadruped compared. Vertical jumping agility is 2.23 m/s, against 1.83 m/s for Salto-1P, matching the galago.
- Steady velocity is 0.9 m/s with a cost of transport of 3.2.

## Limitations
All gaits are open-loop. The authors say instability of the open-loop tracking limited the top speed, and the cost of transport is higher than Minitaur's (2.3). The QDD actuator has 2.7 times Minitaur's reflected inertia, and the belts needed occasional re-tensioning. Closed-loop gait controllers are left to future work.
