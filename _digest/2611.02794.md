abstract: 'Robust multimodal perception for soft robotic grasping is challenged by modality-dependent noise, semantic ambiguity, and limited coordinated multisensory data. We introduce Soma, a dataset containing 100 objects, 1,000 continuous grasping trajectories, and 74,914 multimodal samples grouped by software acquisition cycle, spanning remote RGB, hand RGB, depth, tactile, IMU, and structured text observations. We also collect Soma-Success, an independent grasp-outcome dataset with 1,000 trials (998 retained after quality control), to evaluate frozen-encoder transfer for post-hoc grasp outcome classification and early grasp success prediction. Built on Soma, SomaAlign learns multimodal representations in three stages: masked reconstruction estimates relative modality reliability; reliability-aware contrastive alignment uses pair-specific adaptive temperatures to adjust alignment sharpness; and conflict-aware semantic constraints improve intra-sample cross-modal consistency while preserving global feature geometry. SomaAlign achieves an average Recall@10 of 90.66% across 30 directed cross-modal retrieval tasks. The learned representations also support physical attribute recognition, multimodal question answering, cross-modal generation, and cross-dataset transfer to grasp outcome prediction. On Soma-Success, frozen SomaAlign encoders with lightweight probes achieve 94.59% post-hoc classification accuracy and 84.07% early prediction accuracy. Together, Soma, Soma-Success, and SomaAlign provide a data and representation-learning foundation for robust and transferable multimodal perception in soft robotic manipulation.'

## What they do
Soft grippers deform, vibrate and slip, so their tactile and IMU signals are noisier than camera images, and most visuo-tactile datasets use rigid grippers with optical tactile sensors. The authors collect Soma, a soft-gripper grasping dataset with six synchronized modalities, and Soma-Success, a separate grasp-outcome set. They then propose SomaAlign, which embeds all six modalities in one space while accounting for how reliable each modality is.

## How it works
- **Data.** Pneumatic two- and three-finger grippers carry a hand camera, one IMU per finger and 8×14 pressure arrays on the finger pads, next to a remote RealSense D435i. A motorized rod pulls each grasped object down on a fishing line until it slips or drops. Ten trajectories per object give 74,914 samples labelled stationary or sliding, with templated text.
- **Stage I.** Masked reconstruction per modality; modalities that are harder to reconstruct get a lower reliability score.
- **Stage II.** Pairwise InfoNCE on UniTouch-initialized encoders, with a temperature per modality pair set by its reliability, so reliable pairs are aligned more sharply.
- **Stage III.** A fuzzy score of cross-modal conflict, averaged over the batch, scales how strongly each sample's modalities are pulled together, while a penalty keeps sample centers near their Stage-II positions.

## Results
- Retrieval over 30 modality directions: R@1 63.04%, against 44.55% for ImageBind-style hub alignment and 42.00% for pairwise InfoNCE with the same encoders.
- Vision-tactile-language retrieval: average R@1 69.71% vs 55.64% for adapted TVL, the strongest published baseline.
- Soma-Success with frozen encoders and object-grouped 5-fold probes: 94.59% post-hoc outcome accuracy (MLP). Early prediction before lifting reaches 84.07%, below hub alignment's 86.48%.
- Removing Stage III drops R@1 to 22.72%.

## Limitations
The reliability score is a reconstruction proxy, not a measure of sensor noise. Text annotations are templated, so language is limited. Grasp-outcome results use trained probes rather than zero-shot transfer, and other platforms, sensors and end-effectors are untested. The representations are not yet used for closed-loop control.
