abstract: 'Performing agile navigation with four-legged robots is a challenging task due to the highly dynamic motions, contacts with various parts of the robot, and the limited field of view of the perception sensors. In this paper, we propose a fully-learned approach to train such robots and conquer scenarios that are reminiscent of parkour challenges. The method involves training advanced locomotion skills for several types of obstacles, such as walking, jumping, climbing, and crouching, and then using a high-level policy to select and control those skills across the terrain. Thanks to our hierarchical formulation, the navigation policy is aware of the capabilities of each skill, and it will adapt its behavior depending on the scenario at hand. Additionally, a perception module is trained to reconstruct obstacles from highly occluded and noisy sensory data and endows the pipeline with scene understanding. Compared to previous attempts, our method can plan a path for challenging scenarios without expert demonstration, offline computation, a priori knowledge of the environment, or taking contacts explicitly into account. While these modules are trained from simulated data only, our real-world experiments demonstrate successful transfer on hardware, where the robot navigates and crosses consecutive challenging obstacles with speeds of up to two meters per second. ## What they do
A fully learned pipeline gets a quadruped to a target across parkour-like obstacles (boxes, gaps, tables, stairs) within a time limit. It uses no expert demonstrations, offline planning or prior map. Everything is trained in simulation and deployed on ANYmal D.

## How it works
- **Perception.** Two encoder-decoder 3D CNNs work on voxelized point clouds from six depth cameras and a LiDAR. A coarse network (12.5 cm voxels, 4 m map) feeds its previous output back in to remember occluded surfaces. A fine network (6.25 cm voxels, 2 m map) refines the area near the robot.
- **Locomotion.** Five RL skills run at 50 Hz: walk, jump, climb up, climb down and crouch. Each must reach a commanded position and heading within a given time, using a 2.5D map taken from the reconstruction. Training adds front-back and left-right symmetry augmentation.
- **Navigation.** A 5 Hz RL policy runs with the frozen skills in the loop. It sees the goal, the remaining time and the perception latent. A modified PPO gives it a hybrid output: a categorical skill choice plus Gaussian position, heading and time commands. Reward is sparse, given at the end of the episode.

## Results
- On hardware the robot reaches 2 m/s and climbs a 0.9 m platform. It also stands up after falling off a box and re-routes when obstacles are pulled away.
- Every skill holds up to about 90% of its training difficulty: gaps and boxes up to 1 m, and passages down to 0.4 m.
- In simulation over 1000 roll-outs per terrain, the learned navigation succeeds 98.2/96.3/97.6% of the time. Hand-coded skills and targets reach 95.3/60.9/75.3%. Removing the heading and time outputs drops success to as low as 71.9%.
- The policy climbs down directly from boxes up to 1 m high. Above that it increasingly takes a longer, safer route.

## Limitations
Tested on a limited set of box-based scenarios, and generalization to new obstacles is untested. The eight networks are tuned separately and depend on each other, so changing one skill means retraining perception and navigation. Navigation only converges with a dedicated curriculum.
