abstract: We present DOPER, an RGB pose estimation framework that shares a visual backbone across object detection, segmentation, and keypoint prediction to recover the 6D pose of known objects through Perspective-n-Point. Starting from synthetic training, DOPER adapts to real observations using partial supervision while retaining synthetic keypoint labels. Calibrated multi-view observations provide geometric constraints for keypoint supervision. During inference, optional heatmap refinement fits a rigid pose directly to the predicted keypoint distributions, resolving ambiguous correspondences without depth input or a learned pose refiner. Starting from underspecified CAD models, DOPER progressively refines the object representation and pose estimator. Results on SPEED+ and SHIRT further demonstrate effective adaptation to illumination changes and accurate temporal pose estimation. Across the evaluated single-view and multi-view configurations, DOPER achieves state-of-the-art detection AP results on HANDAL, HOPEv2, HOT3D, and T-LESS while using only RGB and running in real time (35 Hz per object model on one GB300). These results demonstrate the effectiveness of jointly learning detection and pose keypoints, with real observations and geometric constraints.

## What they do
DOPER revisits DOPE-style keypoint pose estimation for known objects. One network per object is trained on domain-randomized renders of an approximate CAD model, which may lack texture or parts but must have correct metric scale. It then improves from real, unannotated images of the deployment scene and recovers 6D pose from RGB with Perspective-n-Point.

## How it works
- **Shared backbone.** A DINOv3-pretrained ConvNeXt feeds a full-image branch (CenterNet-style detection plus foreground mask) and a higher-resolution crop branch that predicts one heatmap per keypoint (64 in the main experiments). Robust PnP turns the keypoints into a pose.
- **Monocular adaptation.** Confident detections on real images prompt a segmentation model for masks, and an optional head gives coarse orientations. Real images supply only boxes, masks and orientations; keypoint labels still come from synthetic images.
- **Multi-view supervision.** With calibrated stereo or moving cameras, one pose is fit across views and its reprojected keypoints become targets. Fine-tuning and relabeling repeat in rounds.
- **Heatmap refinement.** Optionally, the rigid pose is optimized so all projected keypoints together maximize heatmap likelihood, resolving multi-peak heatmaps caused by symmetry.

## Results
- BOP detection AP from RGB: 81.4 on HANDAL (best listed baseline 65.7) and 65.5 on HOT3D (55.3). Most baselines handle unseen objects; the seen-object baseline HccePose scores 33.2 on HANDAL. With RGB only and no separate pose refiner DOPER leads on two of four datasets, and with optional depth correction on all four.
- Adaptation adds up to 7.5 AP points. Single-view inference takes 28.6 ms per object model (35 Hz) on one GB300.
- SPEED+ spacecraft photos: error falls from 0.2013 to 0.0472 (Lightbox) with extra renders and adaptation up to generation 7, and gross failures from 461 to 14.
- For CAD models with texture or 20% of geometry removed, training on reconstructions from onboarding videos improves validation AR by an estimated 23.6 and 16.3 points.

## Limitations
One model is trained per object, and the authors name per-object training and refinement cost as open problems, along with making reconstruction-based adaptation independent of annotated poses. Multi-view supervision needs calibrated cameras. Evaluation is on image benchmarks; the paper reports no robot experiments.
