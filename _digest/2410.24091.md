abstract: Tactile and visual perception are both crucial for humans to perform fine-grained interactions with their environment. Developing similar multi-modal sensing capabilities for robots can significantly enhance and expand their manipulation skills. This paper introduces \textbf{3D-ViTac}, a multi-modal sensing and learning system designed for dexterous bimanual manipulation. Our system features tactile sensors equipped with dense sensing units, each covering an area of 3$mm^2$. These sensors are low-cost and flexible, providing detailed and extensive coverage of physical contacts, effectively complementing visual information. To integrate tactile and visual data, we fuse them into a unified 3D representation space that preserves their 3D structures and spatial relationships. The multi-modal representation can then be coupled with diffusion policies for imitation learning. Through concrete hardware experiments, we demonstrate that even low-cost robots can perform precise manipulations and significantly outperform vision-only policies, particularly in safe interactions with fragile items and executing long-horizon tasks involving in-hand manipulation. Our project page is available at \url{https://binghao-huang.github.io/3D-ViTac/}.

## What they do
Bimanual manipulation of fragile objects or tools held in the hand needs contact information that cameras miss under occlusion. 3D-ViTac adds dense, flexible tactile pads to soft fin-shaped grippers on a low-cost bimanual teleoperation rig. Touch and vision go into one 3D point cloud for a diffusion policy trained by imitation.

## How it works
- **Sensor.** Each finger carries a 16x16 piezoresistive array (Velostat between orthogonal conductive threads), under 1 mm thick, with 3 mm² per sensing point. It reads out at up to about 32.2 FPS through an Arduino, and a pad plus reading board costs about $20, excluding the Arduino. Four pads give 1024 units.
- **Representation.** Three RealSense views are merged, cropped and downsampled to 512 visual points. Each tactile unit becomes a 3D point, placed by forward kinematics, carrying its continuous reading as a feature. A one-hot flag marks each point as visual or tactile.
- **Policy.** A PointNet++ encoder conditions a diffusion policy that outputs sequences of robot joint states. During teleoperation, tactile readings are shown to the operator on a screen.

## Results
- Whole-task success over 20 episodes, from 30-50 demos: egg steaming 0.85, fruit preparation 0.80, hex key collection 0.90, sandwich serving 0.85. Point-cloud-only policies score 0.55, 0.45, 0.65 and 0.65. The best tactile-image baselines reach 0.70, 0.70, 0.60 and 0.75.
- With a single camera, adding tactile points raises success from 0.50 to 0.80 (egg steaming) and from 0.30 to 0.70 (hex key). Tactile points alone reach only 0.10 and 0.15.
- Ablations: 16x16 resolution slightly beats 8x8 and clearly beats 4x4 and binary contact.
- Single-arm peg insertion: 0.85 versus 0.65 for point cloud only.

## Limitations
Collecting real multimodal demonstrations is expensive. The tactile sensor has no simulation model, so the data cannot be randomized or scaled up in simulation.
