abstract: 'To manipulate a moving object, a policy must predict the scene at the moment its action executes, yet every millisecond spent predicting pushes that moment further away. Scene-level forecasts are computationally expensive, whereas target-centric forecasts can omit nearby objects that affect contact. Our key insight is that what a policy must predict decouples by role: only the target''s pose, which determines contact, requires millimeter precision, while the surroundings matter primarily through the clearance they leave and the motion they impart. We present FoveaWAM, a native 4D world-action model that allocates precision according to these roles. Specifically, it models the target''s past and future motion as a metric SE(3) trajectory, the surroundings as sparse 3D flow, and relies on the current frame for everything static. This compact representation lets FoveaWAM act in 15.1 ms on one RTX 5090, about 10 times faster than video world-action baselines, leaving substantially less time for the scene to evolve before action execution. To evaluate each policy under its own latency, we introduce OnTimeBench, a dynamic manipulation benchmark in which the scene keeps moving while the policy computes. FoveaWAM reaches 82.1% success on OnTimeBench, 20.8 points above the strongest baseline, and also leads on DOMINO and real-robot tasks. Project page: https://foveawam.github.io/'

## What they do
To grasp a moving object, a policy must act on where the object will be when the command executes, and slow inference makes that point further away. FoveaWAM predicts only what contact needs. The target gets a precise metric 6-DoF pose trajectory, the surroundings get coarse sparse 3D flow, and the static scene is read from the current frame. The authors also build OnTimeBench, 14 simulated dynamic tasks where physics keeps running while the policy computes.

## How it works
- **Inputs.** One RGB-D frame becomes 196 frozen MAE patch tokens, each tagged with the metric 3D point behind it. The target's pose history (last 350 ms, 8 samples) and the robot's end-effector history add 16 small tokens.
- **Two streams.** A 12-layer transformer holds a world stream and an action stream that share masked attention. The action stream generates 8-step command chunks by flow matching.
- **Training-only prediction.** Linear heads predict the target's future pose and the sparse flow up to 400 ms ahead. They shape the world features and are dropped at inference.
- **Real robot.** FoundationPose tracks the target online, and the model starts from a base pretrained on DROID.

## Results
- OnTimeBench at native latency: 82.1% success versus 61.4% for PUMA, the best baseline. Given the same pose history, π0.5 reaches 58.7%. With the world paused, FoveaWAM scores 90.3%.
- Pausing the world during inference reorders 9 of 11 methods. OpenWAM-α falls from 58.7% paused to 46.1% at native latency.
- DOMINO (24 single-arm tasks, evaluated paused): 46.8% versus 26.9% for DynamicWAM.
- Real robot (Flexiv Rizon 4s, 6 tasks, 20 trials each): 48.3% average versus 30.8% for π0.5 and 1.7% for ACT. The largest gains are turntable (60% vs 30%) and conveyor grasping (70% vs 45%).
- Removing the pose history costs 19.6 points, the largest drop in the ablation.

## Limitations
The target must be rigid and trackable online, and articulated or deformable targets are left to future work. Real-robot success stays modest: 35% on toy car and ball intercept, 10% on ramp cart grasp.
