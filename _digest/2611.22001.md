## What they do
DreamFAR is an action-conditioned video world model for a bimanual robot, trained from scratch on teleoperation data with no internet-video pretraining. It streams frames at 10 Hz, so a policy or a human on leader arms can act inside it in closed loop, and keeps contact-rich rollouts coherent for a minute or more.

## How it works
- **Tokenizer.** A fine-tuned SSDD encoder maps each of three 256x256 camera views to a 16x16 grid of latents projected onto the unit sphere, which limits scale drift.
- **Contact-based memory.** An auxiliary head predicts one contact bit per arm; when one flips (a grasp or a release), that frame enters a keyframe bank. Up to eight keyframes, retrieved by end-effector-pose similarity, join a 5-s window of recent frames.
- **Streaming transformer.** 1.9B parameters with a learned, never-evicted sink frame, trained with per-frame noise levels (diffusion forcing). At inference 16 frames at staggered noise levels are denoised together over a rolling KV cache, so cost per step stays constant.
- **Data.** An internal teleoperation dataset on YAM arms; 256 H200 GPUs for six days.

## Results
- Against baselines retrained on the public ABC dataset (DreamFAR and WEAVER both at about 0.8B), 60-s rollouts on 1,000 held-out episodes average 16.4 dB PSNR, versus 15.8 for Cosmos-Predict 2.5, 14.5 for WEAVER and 11.0 for IWS. FID rises from 15 to 32 over the minute, versus 30 to 73 for Cosmos-Predict 2.5.
- Policy evaluation with two π0 checkpoints on three tasks (32 episodes each): mean task progress inside the model and on the real robot correlates at Pearson r = 0.71.
- Training π0 on 75 real plus 75 demonstrations collected inside DreamFAR nearly doubles real-only task progress, to 63% over 10 real trials.
- Contact memory beats fixed-stride and no memory on wrist-view PSNR (19.25 vs 18.99 vs 18.66).

## Limitations
One YAM station, one embodiment and parallel grippers only. The model is optimistic: it reports higher progress than the real robot and preserves the policy ranking on two of three tasks. It is trained on mostly successful demonstrations, so the authors warn it should not be the only check before deploying a policy.
