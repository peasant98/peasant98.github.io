abstract: In this paper, we propose a locomotion training framework where a control policy and a state estimator are trained concurrently. The framework consists of a policy network which outputs the desired joint positions and a state estimation network which outputs estimates of the robot's states such as the base linear velocity, foot height, and contact probability. We exploit a fast simulation environment to train the networks and the trained networks are transferred to the real robot. The trained policy and state estimator are capable of traversing diverse terrains such as a hill, slippery plate, and bumpy road. We also demonstrate that the learned policy can run at up to 3.75 m/s on normal flat ground and 3.54 m/s on a slippery plate with the coefficient of friction of 0.22.

## What they do
RL locomotion controllers usually need an accurate state estimate, but analytical estimators become unreliable on ice or sand, and many assume a fixed gait schedule that learned policies don't follow. This paper replaces the estimator with a small network trained at the same time as the policy. The estimator outputs interpretable states (base linear velocity, foot height, contact probability) and feeds them to the policy, using only IMU and joint encoders. Everything is trained in RaiSim and transferred to a Mini Cheetah.

## How it works
- **Concurrent training.** After each batch of simulated trajectories, the actor and critic are updated with PPO and the estimator with supervised regression to ground truth, so the policy learns to cope with the estimator's errors, e.g. under foot slip.
- **Networks.** All three are MLPs; the inputs include joint-state history at 0.02, 0.04 and 0.06 s back. The policy outputs joint position targets for a PD controller, with policy and estimator both running at 100 Hz.
- **Foot height estimate.** A reward term alone did not keep enough foot clearance at low speeds, so the estimated foot height is given directly to the policy.
- **Sim-to-real.** A forward-velocity curriculum (up to 3.5 m/s) plus randomized observation noise, motor friction, PD gains, foot geometry and ground friction (0.4-1.0).

## Results
- Simulation: lowest velocity tracking error (Vx RMS 0.111 m/s, vs 0.168 implicit and 0.136 sequential). Average time to fall on rough terrain is 85.7 s, vs 25.0 s without the estimator and 30.0 s for implicit.
- Real-robot top speed: 3.75 m/s on normal ground and 3.54 m/s at friction 0.22, vs 1.72 / 1.34 m/s for the built-in MPC.
- The slope-trained model climbs a 19.1° hill (trained on ±10°) and a slippery 9.0° slope; the other controllers manage at most 12.4°.
- The estimator forward pass takes 7 µs, against 34 µs for the robot's linear Kalman filter.

## Limitations
Blind, proprioceptive locomotion only. On hardware, estimated contacts lag true contacts by 0.04 s, which the authors attribute to chain and rubber-foot compliance and sparse joint history. Foot height is inferred assuming even terrain. The slope-trained model has slightly higher tracking error than the flat-trained one, which the authors attribute to the latter being fit to simpler terrain.
