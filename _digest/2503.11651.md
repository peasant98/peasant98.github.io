abstract: We present VGGT, a feed-forward neural network that directly infers all key 3D attributes of a scene, including camera parameters, point maps, depth maps, and 3D point tracks, from one, a few, or hundreds of its views. This approach is a step forward in 3D computer vision, where models have typically been constrained to and specialized for single tasks. It is also simple and efficient, reconstructing images in under one second, and still outperforming alternatives that require post-processing with visual geometry optimization techniques. The network achieves state-of-the-art results in multiple 3D tasks, including camera parameter estimation, multi-view depth estimation, dense point cloud reconstruction, and 3D point tracking. We also show that using pretrained VGGT as a feature backbone significantly enhances downstream tasks, such as non-rigid point tracking and feed-forward novel view synthesis. Code and models are publicly available at https://github.com/facebookresearch/vggt.

## What they do
Classical 3D reconstruction relies on iterative optimization such as bundle adjustment (BA), and learned methods like DUSt3R and MASt3R process only two images at a time, fusing pairs in post-processing. VGGT is one feed-forward transformer that takes one, a few or hundreds of images of a scene and predicts, for every image, camera intrinsics and extrinsics, a depth map, a point map in the first camera's frame, and features for point tracking.

## How it works
- **Backbone.** DINOv2 patchifies each image; each frame gets a camera token and four register tokens, with separate learned tokens marking the first frame as the reference. 24 blocks alternate frame-wise and global self-attention, with no cross-attention; about 1.2B parameters.
- **Heads.** A camera head regresses rotation quaternion, translation and field of view. A DPT head outputs depth and point maps with uncertainty, plus features for a CoTracker2-style tracker.
- **Training.** One multi-task loss (camera, depth, point map, tracking) on 16 public 3D datasets plus synthetic artist-made assets, 2-24 frames per scene, 64 A100s for nine days.
- At inference, unprojecting predicted depth with predicted cameras gives better points than the point-map head.

## Results
- Camera pose (10 frames, AUC@30): 85.3 on RealEstate10K (not trained on) and 88.2 on CO3Dv2 in about 0.2 s, vs 78.9 / 83.4 for VGGSfM v2 at about 10 s. Adding BA gives 93.5 / 91.8 in about 1.8 s.
- DTU depth without ground-truth cameras: Overall 0.382 vs 1.741 for DUSt3R, close to MASt3R's 0.374 with ground-truth cameras. ETH3D point maps: 0.677 vs 0.826 for MASt3R.
- Not trained for two-view matching, it still beats RoMa on ScanNet-1500 (33.9 vs 31.8 AUC@5). As CoTracker's backbone it lifts TAP-Vid RGB-S δ_avg^vis from 78.9 to 84.0.
- Alternating attention beats global-only and cross-attention (ETH3D Overall 0.709 vs 0.827 and 1.061). On an H100, 100 frames take 3.12 s; 200 frames take 8.75 s and 40.6 GB.

## Limitations
No support for fisheye or panoramic images. Accuracy drops under extreme input rotations, and it fails on scenes with substantial non-rigid deformation. Memory grows quickly with the number of frames, and BA post-processing still improves the feed-forward result.
