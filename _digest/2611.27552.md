abstract: 'General-purpose whole-body trackers can execute diverse humanoid motions, but they require dense reference trajectories. However, tasks usually specify only where, in which posture, and when the robot should arrive, leaving the motion in between to a separate planner. Inspired by human motor coordination, our key idea is that a low-level controller can organize the intervening motion and its pace by itself from sparse goals with arrival times. In this paper, we propose S-BFM, a novel behavioral foundation model superior in its autonomy: it generates whole-body transitions from target postures, poses, and arrival times alone. Specifically, we first introduce Spatial Grounding, which adds world-frame position and heading to a forward-backward behavior representation through spatial reconstruction. Then, Time-Conditioned Distillation is designed to learn transitions and pace from real motion through closed-loop imitation of a dense-reference teacher. Lastly, Generative Fine-Tuning is introduced to broaden the covered distances and arrival times with a distilled motion generator. In simulation, S-BFM lowers global joint-position error by over 32% relative to the best baseline under fixed and mixed horizons, and raises fixed-goal reach from 43.5% to 74.6% while cutting terminal position error from 1.67 to 0.26 m. We further deploy S-BFM on the full-size AGIBOT A3 humanoid. Sparse spatiotemporal goals thus suffice for natural, well-timed whole-body motion.'

## What they do
General-purpose humanoid trackers follow dense reference motions, so another module has to supply every frame between task goals. S-BFM is a single low-level controller that receives only a target posture, a world-frame position and heading, and an arrival time, and produces the whole-body motion in between, including its pace. It is evaluated on a simulated 29-DoF Unitree G1 and deployed on the full-size AGIBOT A3 humanoid.

## How it works
- **Spatial grounding.** Builds on BFM-Zero's forward-backward behavior representation. World-frame root position and heading are added to the encoder input, and a readout must reconstruct them from the embedding, so encoding a goal state gives a code that moves the robot to that place as well as that posture.
- **Time-conditioned distillation.** With encoder and actor frozen, a controller that sees only the sparse goal and the remaining time imitates a dense-reference teacher in closed loop (DAgger-style), learning both the transition and its pace.
- **Generative fine-tuning.** A compact motion generator distilled from Kimodo creates references for the same endpoint postures at other distances and horizons. Those that pass tracking checks are mixed with real references to fine-tune the controller.
- At deployment there is no intermediate reference: observations, goal, remaining time and a hold/continue flag map to PD joint targets.

## Results
- Sparse timed keyframes (0.1-3.5 s horizons): global joint-position error of 38.35 cm against 59.35 cm for BeJEPA and 70.45 cm for PLAT; arrival rate 85.5% against 77.9% for BeJEPA.
- Fixed goals 0.4-4 m away: reach rises from 43.5% (BeJEPA) to 74.6% and holding the goal for 5 s from 7.4% to 35.2%. At 3-4 m under strong lateral and downward pushes, it still reaches 68.0% of goals against 23.6%.
- Ablation: spatial grounding alone arrives on time in only 4.6% of commands, 17.4% with distillation added. Generative fine-tuning raises 5 s holding under short time commands from 13.6% to 92.0% at 2 m.
- On the AGIBOT A3 the robot tracks sparse goals, walks or runs to the same target depending on the time left, and gets up from the ground, shown qualitatively.

## Limitations
All quantitative comparisons are in simulation, and BeJEPA and PLAT are the authors' reproductions (BeJEPA without its online MPC). Training needs three successive stages. Connecting the controller to vision-language-action or world-action models that would issue the goals is left open.
