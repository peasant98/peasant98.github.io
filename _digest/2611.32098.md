abstract: Robotics foundation models are now widely used for manipulation, and egocentric videos offer rich data for scaling. However, existing methods overlook the human active priors captured in egocentric videos, which limits performance in unstructured scenes. They act on given observations instead of actively seeking information as humans do. We propose ActiveBridge, a training framework that learns these human active priors and transfers them to robots through world-action modeling. We design a robot that actively controls its viewpoint through head actions to acquire the visual information needed for manipulation using a single head camera, without wrist cameras. During human training, an instruction-conditioned model jointly predicts future egocentric videos and synchronized head-wrist action trajectories, with head actions providing explicit supervision for active perception. Experiments show that ActiveBridge outperforms baselines on active manipulation tasks, while ablations demonstrate the benefit of joint head-wrist action supervision.

## What they do
People move their heads to see what they need while they manipulate. ActiveBridge learns this from human egocentric video. Before robot fine-tuning, a pretrained world-action model (LingBot-VA) is trained to predict future egocentric video together with head and wrist trajectories, so head motion gets explicit supervision. The robot matches human sensing: one movable head camera and no wrist cameras.

## How it works
- **Data engine.** 387 hours of EgoVerse video. Qwen3-VL-8B writes task instructions, Depth Anything 3 gives metric camera (head) trajectories and HaWoR gives wrist poses, all expressed in the first head frame as a 21D head-plus-two-wrists action.
- **Human training.** A flow-matching loss on future video latents and on head and wrist actions, with a separate weight on the head term.
- **Robot post-training.** The human-trained model is fine-tuned on robot demonstrations in the robot's own head, arm and gripper actions. The platform has two AgileX NERO arms and a PiPER arm as an actuated neck carrying one RealSense D455.

## Results
- TAVIS-Head simulation benchmark (5 active-perception tasks): 77.1% in-distribution on Reachy-2 and 63.8% on GR1T2, the best of the methods tested. The largest gains over LingBot-VA come under spatial shifts (50.2% to 55.9% on Reachy-2, 44.5% to 47.9% on GR1T2).
- Removing the head-action target lowers TAVIS-Head success by 0.7-2.4 points in every condition. On RoboTwin 2.0 the average is 91.4% against 91.2% for LingBot-VA.
- Real robot, 4 tasks, 64 demonstrations and 10 trials each: 25/40 successes against 17/40 for LingBot-VA and 27/40 for π0, which does better on table tidying (6/10 vs 3/10).

## Limitations
The paper does not discuss limitations. Gains over LingBot-VA without human training are small in simulation (0.3-5.7 points on TAVIS-Head, 0.2 on RoboTwin 2.0), real-robot results come from 10 trials per task, and π0 matches or beats ActiveBridge on three of the four real tasks.
