abstract: 'This paper presents Proactive Human-in-the-Loop (PHIL), a framework for proactive policy correction and preference-based post-training. PHIL enables operators to preview policy-predicted future motion and intervene before anticipated undesirable actions. During takeover, PHIL continues querying the policy and displaying its predicted trajectories in the physical workspace through an extended reality (XR) interface. PHIL pairs rejected policy predictions with corrective action chunks subsequently executed from the same physical state over the same horizon, and preference-based post-training encourages the policy to favor these corrections. ## What they do
Human-in-the-loop imitation learning usually collects corrections reactively: the operator takes over after the robot has started to go wrong, so the correction data are full of recovery motions. PHIL (Proactive Human-in-the-Loop) shows the operator the policy's next action chunk as a 3D gripper path in an XR headset, so they can take over *before* the mistake. The rejected predictions and the corrections that followed become preference pairs for post-training a diffusion policy or a pretrained VLA (X-VLA).

## How it works
- **XR preview.** A Meta Quest 3 overlays a virtual Franka Panda synchronized with the real one and draws the predicted gripper-tip trajectory of each 30-step chunk (queried every 10 steps at 20 Hz). Holding the controller trigger takes over; releasing it hands control back.
- **Shadow predictions.** During takeover the policy keeps running on the same schedule. Each displayed but unexecuted prediction is labeled rejected and paired with the next 30 executed actions from the same state.
- **Preference post-training.** A pairwise logistic loss on the policy's own imitation loss favors the corrective chunk over the rejected one, alongside imitation on the base demonstrations.

## Results
Four real tasks (Pick Up Banana, Towel Folding, Cook the Carrot, Lemon in the Drawer), five correction episodes and 25 evaluation rollouts per condition.
- Averaged over tasks and demo budgets, success goes from 44.0% (HG-DAgger, reactive) to 70.3% for the diffusion policy and from 57.8% to 76.9% for X-VLA. Cook the Carrot with 10 demos: 52% vs 24% for HG-DAgger and Residual Policy, 4% for the base policy.
- Ablations: proactive data alone gives no consistent gain under behavior cloning; on the same proactive data the preference loss lifts Towel Folding from 40% to 68% and Cook the Carrot from 28% to 52%.
- Across 12 HG-DAgger settings, policies trained on proactive corrections show 21 unnecessary recovery behaviors versus 214 for reactive ones.

## Limitations
The headline comparison changes both the collection mode and the training objective; only the ablations separate them. Mixing two proactive and three reactive episodes drops PHIL to 0-20% success, below either data source alone. Experiments cover one arm and four tasks, and the recovery analysis counts only successful episodes.
