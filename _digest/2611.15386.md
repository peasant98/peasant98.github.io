abstract: Industrial manipulators offer reach and precision but can damage nearby structures in confined spaces. Soft robots provide compliant contact but typically have limited reach. Rigid-soft systems combine their advantages, although they often require dedicated actuation and control hardware. We propose actuating a passive tendon-driven soft extension through manipulator joint motion. As the robot moves, the exterior spans of tendons anchored to upstream links change, altering tendon tension and deforming the extension. Inspection through narrow apertures, for example, constrains the motion of the end effector (EE). We develop a controller that regulates the soft tip while preserving such EE constraints. A differential model separates rigid transport from soft deformation, combining analytic arm and tendon kinematics with a locally identified span-to-deformation map. We also derive a local upper bound on the number of independent deformation directions transmitted by the tendons under the EE constraint, and conditions for the exponential convergence of the tip error. A quadratic program with control barrier functions imposes obstacle clearance, fulcrum limits, and model-validity conditions. Simulations and real experiments show tip and shape regulation under free, position-constrained, and fulcrum-constrained EE motion.

## What they do
A soft extension on an industrial arm combines the arm's reach with compliant access to confined spaces, but usually needs its own tendon motors. Here the extension is passive: each tendon runs from an anchor on an upstream arm link to the base of the extension. As the arm moves, the exterior span between anchor and entry point changes, which tensions the tendon and bends the extension. The controller uses this to steer the soft tip while the end effector (EE) obeys a task constraint, such as holding a fixed position or pivoting through an aperture.

## How it works
- **Differential model.** Tip velocity is split into rigid transport with the EE and tendon-driven deformation. Arm and span kinematics are analytic; the span-to-deformation map is fitted from probing motions and refined online with Broyden's rank-one update.
- **Null-space control.** The EE constraint is the primary task, and a damped least-squares law regulates the tip and body points in its null space. They prove the weighted tip error does not increase, give conditions for exponential convergence, and bound how many deformation directions the tendons can transmit.
- **Safety.** A QP with control barrier functions enforces obstacle clearance, the fulcrum's aperture, cone and insertion limits, and keeps the spans inside the identified domain.

## Results
- Simulation (UR10 with a Cosserat-rod extension): every feasible target reached within 4 mm, with the EE constraint held to 0.2 mm. Without the obstacle barrier the extension penetrated a sphere by 18.8 mm; with it, clearance stayed above 10.7 mm.
- Real UR10, six experiments with handmade foam extensions: spans changed by up to 35 mm, the position-constrained EE stayed within 0.5 mm, and all barrier values stayed nonnegative.
- 6 of the 16 targets predicted feasible were reached, all under the fulcrum. The rest stopped at active barriers or at stationary points of the controller, 14-42 mm from the target.

## Limitations
The identified model is local to the probed span domain, which limited several real targets; marker predictions had 9-14 mm RMS error. Tendon count and anchor placement were not optimized, and the controller regulates shape only, not tension, contact force or stiffness.
