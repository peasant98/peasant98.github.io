abstract: Robot policies act in real time but fail without knowing why, whereas a reasoning agent that watches the robot can often tell what went wrong. Existing systems either hand the agent control, which takes minutes per episode, or let it send instructions. Yet a policy follows a new instruction only from a fresh start, since a half-finished task leaves it in states outside its training data. Nor can an instruction fix how the robot moves. If a drift keeps pulling the hand to the left, "push right, slightly" is not a task the policy has learned. We propose RoboThink, in which the agent thinks and the policy learns to act on the agent's guidance. An instruction channel tells the policy what to do. When the user says "the milk, not the butter" after the robot has started, the agent rewrites the instruction and the robot undoes its own motion, with no training. A feedback channel tells it how to move. It is taught from the agent alone with agent-labelled DAgger, role tokens and counterfactual feedback, and is read only by the policy's action module. On LIBERO, RoboThink with Claude Opus 5.5 raises success from 0.09 to 0.87 under an unknown constant drift and from 0.42 to 0.84 under a random one. It also completes misheard and changed requests in 0.91 to 1.00 of the episodes. Acting alone, GPT-6 Astra needs 2.1 min per success and over a million tokens per episode, whereas RoboThink needs 55 s and 8k tokens. The same recipe works across policies, for both vision-language-action models and world action models, and across robots, from one arm to two arms and a mobile manipulator. On all 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks with two arms, success rises from 0.12 to 0.74 under the constant drift and from 0.31 to 0.56 under the random one. Finally, the agent is a plug-in. A policy taught by one agent works with other agents, including open-weight ones, and grows stronger with a stronger agent, without retraining.

## What they do
A robot policy (a VLA or a world action model) acts in real time but cannot tell why it fails; a VLM agent watching it often can, but is slow when it controls the robot itself. RoboThink keeps the policy in control and calls the agent only after chunks 1, 2 and 3 and then every four chunks. The agent's conclusions reach the policy through an instruction channel (what to do) and a feedback channel (how to move). All experiments are in simulation: LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0 (two arms) and RoboCasa (a mobile manipulator).

## How it works
- **Agent.** A frozen VLM sees the user's request, recent frames and a table of commanded versus actual end-effector motion. It answers in JSON with a per-axis drift estimate and, if needed, a corrected instruction; a template turns the estimate into feedback text.
- **Undo before redo.** When the instruction was misheard or changed, the robot replays its past commands in reverse with the gripper open, then follows the new instruction. This needs no training.
- **Feedback channel.** Only the action module reads the feedback, behind learned role tokens; the world module keeps the plain instruction. It is trained with agent-labelled DAgger (the agent's corrected plans are the targets, so the true drift is never used) plus counterfactual feedback, which pairs one state with different feedback and targets so the policy must read the text.

## Results
- LIBERO with Fast-WAM under constant/random drift: 0.09/0.42 alone, 0.00/0.16 with the feedback simply appended to the prompt, 0.87/0.84 with RoboThink. Without drift, false alarms lower success from 0.96 to 0.89.
- Acting alone through code, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 reach 0.78/0.84 and 0.53/0.58, at 2.1 and 2.3 min per success and 1.2 M and 759 k tokens per episode; RoboThink takes 55 s and 8 k tokens.
- On all 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, success rises from 0.12/0.31 to 0.74/0.56, unchanged without drift (0.91). Misheard and changed requests succeed in 0.91-1.00 of episodes, against 0.00-0.71 without the agent.
- Across ten agents, success tracks the accuracy of the agent's drift estimate (r = 0.96); an all open-weight setup with Kimi K3 reaches 0.82/0.82.

## Limitations
Simulation only. The feedback channel is trained on drifts that stay constant within an episode, and the agent cannot supply a missing skill: under LIBERO-MAX scene events success reaches only 0.20. The robot waits about 8 s per agent call, and undo assumes the motion can be retraced.
