tldr: PointPilot predicts the 3D point flow of whatever matters in the current subtask (the gripper before a grasp, the held object after) and turns it into end-effector poses with a non-learned rigid fit. abstract: Generalization remains a central challenge in robotics, calling for policies that address domain variations from multiple dimensions such as diverse embodiments, camera poses, and/or scene layouts. In order to approach this problem, we introduce PointPilot, a task-focused and 3D aligned manipulation framework built on a 3D space point-flow prediction. Rather than modeling the global scene, PointPilot only represents task-focused objects (robot grippers treated as objects), avoiding the prediction of potentially redundant scene parts unrelated to the manipulation tasks. Moreover, PointPilot aligns 3D spatial representation in one unified coordinate and supports closed-loop and fully automatic action execution across different manipulation modes from object point flow without a separately trained action expert, while existing works typically need an extra action head or are (semi-)automatic and single-arm mode specific. Given sensor observations and language instructions, our model jointly learns subtask planning, task-relevant object grounding, and 3D point flow prediction in an end-to-end way. By processing heterogeneous data in a shared 3D point flow space, PointPilot is able to absorb demonstrations from different embodiments. Extensive simulative and real world experiments validate our method. Notably, we demonstrate zero-shot embodiment generalization with PointPilot. A single trained policy can be deployed on previously unseen embodiments and successfully complete manipulation tasks without any additional fine-tuning.

## What they do
PointPilot is a manipulation policy whose output is the motion of 3D points on a "task-focused object": the gripper itself while reaching, then the object rigidly held by it after the grasp. Because it predicts object motion rather than joint commands, demonstrations from different robots share one supervision space, and a single model handles single-arm and bimanual tasks.

## How it works
- **Planning.** A Qwen2.5-VL planner reads the main camera image, the instruction and a memory of earlier predicted subtasks, and outputs the current and next subtask for each arm.
- **3D grounding.** Image tokens get 3D positional embeddings from camera frustums, with no depth input. A transformer decoder predicts a 3D box for each task-focused object and the gripper it belongs to.
- **Point Flow DiT.** Points sampled in each box are denoised with flow matching into 30-step future displacements, together with gripper open/close states. Auxiliary camera views are aligned into the main-camera frame.
- **Execution.** A learning-free adapter fits a rigid transform to the predicted flow and applies it to the assigned end effector through IK. The policy replans after every 30-step chunk.

## Results
- One checkpoint trained jointly on LIBERO and RoboTwin scores 85.8% on LIBERO (96.0% Spatial, 97.6% Object, 77.2% Long). On RoboTwin 2.0 Clean it reaches 56.6%, against 55.2% reported for DP3.
- Zero-shot transfer from ALOHA to unseen arms in RoboTwin costs 10.4 points of average success on ARX and 16.6 on Piper. X-VLA loses 11.4 and 64.7.
- On a real CobotMagic bimanual robot (50 demonstrations, 20 trials per task): QR-code scanning 10/20, cube-to-basket 18/20, pouring 14/20.
- Without planning memory, LIBERO average falls to 75.2% (LIBERO-Long to 55.0%).

## Limitations
The method assumes locally rigid objects and gripper-based execution; dexterous hands and human-hand data are left to future work. Only the current subtask's objects are predicted, not other objects they affect. Three friction-driven LIBERO-Goal tasks, where the object is not coupled to the gripper, pull its score down. Cross-embodiment transfer is shown only in simulation, and the real-world policies are trained per task.
