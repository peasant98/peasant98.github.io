abstract: Most robotic handwriting rigidly mounts the pen and reduces writing to trajectory tracking. A freely held pen instead couples grasp stability, tip motion, and contact force. We present Grip, Trace, Write, a force-aware simulation environment and learning framework for high-DoF handwriting. A two-branch actor separates pen retention from arm-wrist writing while preserving their closed-loop coupling. Training learns a grasp controller, initializes writing from inverse-kinematics demonstrations by behavior cloning, and improves writing through reinforcement learning with rewards on ink, force, time, and retention. Across diverse tasks, the policy improves completion, precision, force regulation, and efficiency over behavior cloning and inverse kinematics, and generalizes without fine-tuning to unseen English and Chinese content. These results establish force-aware handwriting as a testbed for dexterous tool use.

## What they do
Robot handwriting usually bolts the pen to the arm and tracks a path. Here the pen is a free rigid body held in a tripod grasp by a multi-fingered hand, so the fingers must keep hold of it while the arm and wrist move the tip and press with a requested force. The authors build a MuJoCo environment for this and train one policy that traces shapes and writes digits, capital letters, English words and Chinese characters.

## How it works
- **Setup.** A hand with 25 finger joints and a 2-joint wrist sits on a UR5e, controlled at 25 Hz. The policy sees 137 state entries (proprioception, tactile, task state) plus 16 upcoming trajectory samples giving tip displacement, height, requested force and time. No images.
- **Two-branch actor.** One MLP outputs finger actions (grasp), another outputs arm, wrist and stop actions (writing). Both read the same state.
- **Three stages.** The grasp branch is cloned from 240 teacher episodes, then trained with 8M PPO steps to hold the pen while still, moving and scribbling. An inverse-kinematics controller then writes with that grasp to produce demonstrations for behavior cloning. Finally PPO refines the writing branch with the grasp frozen.
- **Outcome reward.** Ordered ink coverage within 1 mm, off-target ink, force error, elapsed time and pen drops; no term for imitating IK.

## Results
- Mean completion over eight tasks, 50 seeds each: 94.8% for RL, 91.3% for BC, 92.0% for IK. Ink precision rises from 79.0% (BC) to 87.6%.
- Force error falls 53%, from 0.773 N to 0.362 N; time from 8.19 s to 7.67 s.
- Zero-shot on unseen content, completion is 91.6% on English words, 92.9% on Chinese idioms and 91.6% on Chinese characters with seven or more strokes.
- Final RL used 39.85M transitions in 193.8 minutes.

## Limitations
Everything is in simulation; there is no real-robot test. The pen starts already seated in the grasp. The line task is the exception where IK (98.2%) beats RL (97.5%). RL training is non-monotone, and the learning curves are snapshots of a single run, not independent seeds.
