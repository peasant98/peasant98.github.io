abstract: Contact transitions allow humanoid robots to redistribute object support across the whole body, enabling manipulation beyond hand-only grasps. Human videos provide a scalable source of such transitions for robots to learn from. However, body occlusion disrupts object pose tracking and corrupts retargeted motion. Moreover, accurately reproducing the object trajectory does not ensure that the robot applies stabilizing forces at the intended contacts, risking slips or drops in later phases. To address these challenges, we propose ReContact, which reconstructs and retargets human-object interactions through an object-centric contact plan. The plan specifies contact phases, contact pairs, anchors, pressing directions, and sticking or sliding modes. Contact priors stabilize object pose reconstruction through occlusion. A residual policy over a frozen whole-body tracker is rewarded for making contact at planned anchors with forces aligned to the specified directions. In simulation, on a thigh-supported regrasp task, ReContact achieves the planned two-palm bottom hold in 83.5 ± 5.2% of episodes over three training seeds, versus 0.16% for the best adapted baseline. One policy trained on 247 references re-solved for new placements raises in-workspace tube-transfer success from 20.4% (source reference only) to 97.8%. On a real Unitree G1, policies execute seven contact-rich manipulation and two loco-manipulation tasks.

## What they do
Humanoids often pass an object's support between body parts, e.g. resting a box on a raised thigh while the palms slide underneath. Copying such contact transitions from human video fails when the body hides the object, and a policy that tracks the object trajectory can still hold it at the wrong points. ReContact makes the contacts explicit in an object-centric contact plan.

## How it works
- **Contact plan.** Per phase, the active robot-link/object pairs, each with an anchor on the object, a pressing direction and a stick or slide mode. GPT-6 Astra drafts it from exocentric video; egocentric plans come from reconstructed hand-object contacts.
- **Reconstruction and retargeting.** Contact priors tie the object pose to the supporting body part through occlusion; whole-body optimization fits the G1 while keeping the anchors and directions.
- **Control.** A residual policy over the frozen SONIC whole-body tracker, trained with PPO, is rewarded for contact near each anchor with force along the planned direction.
- **Task families.** The placement-dependent segment is re-solved at new object placements, and one policy trains on all of them.

## Results
- Thigh-supported regrasp in simulation: 83.5 ± 5.2% two-palm success over three seeds. Adapted baselines complete 24.3-65.7% of episodes but form the hold in at most 0.16%, squeezing the box between the hands instead.
- Tube transfer: one policy on 247 re-solved references reaches 97.8% success across the workspace, against 20.4% for the best single-reference policy.
- Real Unitree G1: seven contact-rich manipulation and two loco-manipulation tasks, including a chain of table pickup, regrasp, about 3 m of loaded walking, reverse regrasp and floor placement.

## Limitations
The paper reports no success rates for the hardware tasks, where object poses come from fiducial markers, the head camera or poses latched at start. Each task family needs its own residual policy.
