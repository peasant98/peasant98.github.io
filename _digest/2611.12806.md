abstract: 'Tactile sensing is an indispensable modality for contact-rich dexterous manipulation, a capability that hints at the ultimate frontiers of embodied intelligence. However, commercially available tactile sensors are still at a relatively early stage of development, with each type of sensor offering irreplaceable strengths alongside its own drawbacks. A key challenge is thus to free tactile understanding from sensor-specific physics, so that embodied models can effectively leverage heterogeneous tactile data. To this end, we develop LingBot-Touch, a tactile foundation model characterized by the following properties. (i) Our model yields a unified representation across different tactile sensors (i.e., piezoresistive, magnetic, and visuotactile sensors), and is easily extensible to new sensor types. (ii) The resulting tactile tokens are plug-and-play: they can be seamlessly integrated into policy models across paradigms, from diffusion policy to vision-language-action (VLA) models such as $π_{0.5}$ and LingBot-VLA, consistently enhancing manipulation dexterity. (iii) Extensive experiments, spanning simulation and real-robot deployments across multiple hardware platforms, demonstrate that our tactile representation substantially benefits complex manipulation tasks while maintaining strong computational efficiency. We believe the findings in this work demonstrate the potential of leveraging cross-sensor data to scale tactile learning, and offer encouraging insights for the design of future hardware and embodied models.'

## What they do
Tactile sensors differ in physics, layout, resolution and rate, so data from one cannot be pooled with data from another. LingBot-Touch is a tactile encoder trained once on 4,983 hours of fingertip recordings from three sensing principles (piezoresistive and magnetic gloves worn by people, visuotactile Sharpa Wave hands under teleoperation). The frozen encoder is then added to existing policies as extra conditioning tokens.

## How it works
- **Canonical fingertip space.** Every taxel gets a 3D position in a shared, unit-radius fingertip frame plus a finger identity and time index. This 5D address enters only through rotary position embeddings, so no fixed taxel count is needed.
- **Shared backbone.** A two-layer MLP tokenizer per sensing principle feeds one Haptic Transformer.
- **Tactile-JEPA.** The model predicts the latent representation of masked taxels from visible ones, with an EMA target encoder, instead of reconstructing the mostly-zero signal.
- **Use in policies.** The 5.46M-parameter Tiny encoder stays frozen; trainable cross-attention readouts produce 40 tokens per hand. It adds under 0.2% to the VLA parameter counts.

## Results
- Real robots, 26 tasks (20 on Sharpa Wave, 6 on XHand1), 20 trials each: mean success rises from 21.6% to 35.6% for Diffusion Policy, 48.1% to 69.9% for pi-0.5, 48.3% to 73.1% for LingBot-VLA and 45.4% to 68.6% for T-Rex. Raw tactile input gives at most about half that gain.
- Frozen-feature probing on Sharpa Wave: fingertip force within 0.81 N and contact center within 0.12 mm, against 2.31 N and 2.19 mm for FTP-1. It also beats FTP-1 on XHand1, whose magnetic sensor model was not in pretraining.
- RL in simulation, twelve tasks: 69.0% success, against 45.6% with a CNN trained with the policy and 21.4% without touch.
- In the same LingBot-VLA policy on five tasks: 82.2% with LingBot-Touch, 67.5% with the FTP-1 encoder, 55.9% without touch.

## Limitations
Experiments cover two hand embodiments. Capacitive sensors are absent from pretraining because the available recordings did not meet quality requirements. The canonical geometry covers only fingertips, so contact on the palm and the rest of the fingers is underrepresented.
