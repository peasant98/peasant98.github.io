tldr: Train a depth-image trajectory planner for fast quadrotor flight by backpropagating episode losses through its own planned trajectories, using differential flatness instead of a dynamics simulator. It trains in about 30 minutes on one GPU, keeps 92-94% success at 7.5-8.2 m/s mean speed in simulated forests where PPO gets 60-64% at about 5 m/s, and flies real forests at up to about 11 m/s.
abstract: Vision-based high-speed navigation requires a trajectory planner that can reactively generate smooth, collision-free trajectories from onboard visual observations. Deep reinforcement learning offers a promising approach to vision-based trajectory planning, as it enables the optimization of sequential decision-making policies in complex environments. However, model-free policy gradient methods rely on sample-based policy gradient estimation, which yields noisy gradient estimates and inefficient training. To this end, we propose a framework for learning trajectory planning policies using the analytic policy gradient (APG) method and use it to train APG-Planner, a vision-based high-speed trajectory planner. The proposed framework leverages differential flatness to analytically reconstruct vehicle states from planned trajectories, thereby enabling differentiable trajectory-level state transitions without requiring an explicit low-level dynamics model. By iteratively performing trajectory planning and differentiable state transitions, we construct a differentiable episode rollout and analytically compute policy gradients from episode-level losses, improving both training efficiency and policy performance. After approximately 30 minutes of training on a single GPU, APG-Planner demonstrates comparable or superior performance to existing baseline methods in simulation and achieves speeds of up to 11 m/s in real-world flight experiments. We further validate the proposed framework on a skid-steer ground robot, demonstrating its applicability to differentially flat robotic systems beyond quadrotors.

## What they do
Learned planners for vision-based high-speed flight are usually trained by imitating an expert, which is limited by the demonstrations, or by guidance from a planning cost that stays local to each decision. Model-free RL optimizes whole episodes but estimates its gradients from noisy samples. APG-Planner makes an entire receding-horizon episode differentiable and trains the planner with analytic policy gradients. Differential flatness supplies the state transitions, so no low-level dynamics model or calibrated simulator is needed.

## How it works
- **Planner.** A CNN encodes a 40x64 depth image. An MLP adds velocity, acceleration, roll, pitch and a goal vector and outputs a terminal position, velocity and acceleration, joined to the current state by a 1 s quintic polynomial.
- **Flatness-based rollout.** The state 0.1 s along each plan is reconstructed analytically through the quadrotor's flatness map, and a new depth image is rendered there. Fifty such steps form one differentiable episode.
- **Loss and training.** Goal progress, jerk and obstacle clearance along each planned segment. Gradients flow across steps through position, velocity and acceleration. Training takes about 30 minutes on one RTX 4080 SUPER.
- **Deployment.** A geometric controller tracks the plan; replanning runs at 10 Hz, about 13 ms per plan.

## Results
- In Flightmare forests (50 maps per density, 40 m courses), it reaches competitive or higher success than EGO, AGILE and YOPO, with lower snap, especially at high speed.
- Full APG reaches 94%/92% success (sparse/dense) at 8.16/7.47 m/s mean speed. Cutting gradients between replanning steps keeps success (94%/88%) but slows flight to about 2 m/s; PPO on the same objective gets 60%/64% at about 5 m/s.
- On a 25 cm quadrotor with onboard visual-inertial odometry and stereo depth, it flew collision-free through two forests at up to about 11 m/s. With a unicycle flatness model, the same framework drove a Clearpath Jackal through a forest.

## Limitations
Training assumes the controller tracks the plan exactly. Real-world runs are shown as demonstrations, with a peak speed but no success rates or baseline comparisons. The method needs a differentially flat system.
