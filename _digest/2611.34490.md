abstract: The Vehicle Routing Problem with Floating Targets (VRPFT) is a generalization of the capacitated vehicle routing problem where targets may actively move to locations ''convenient'' for the vehicles. In particular, each target may leave its home location at a bounded speed. A solution to the VRPFT is a sequence of targets for each vehicle to visit and a meeting point in space-time for each target. In this paper, we solve the VRPFT exactly with a branch-price-and-cut algorithm. Our key contribution is a dominance criterion for the pricing labeling algorithm that utilizes closed-form lower and upper bounds on a vehicle's trajectory cost given a fixed sequence of targets. We prove that the criterion preserves optimality, and the algorithm has smaller computation time than the exact dynamic program of prior work on identical hardware. We also provide a relaxed criterion that prunes more aggressively; the resulting heuristic is faster than our exact algorithm and matched every certified optimum on the benchmark.

## What they do
In the vehicle routing problem with floating targets (VRPFT), capacity-limited vehicles must meet targets that can themselves move at bounded speed from their home toward a meeting point, as when a passenger walks toward a ride-share car. The visiting order is discrete but the meeting points and times are continuous. The paper solves the problem exactly with branch-price-and-cut.

## How it works
- **Master problem.** Pick a set of routes that serves every target once. Routes are generated lazily, and each route's cost (its meeting points and times) is a convex second-order cone program.
- **Pricing by labeling.** A labeling search looks for routes with negative reduced cost. The usual pruning rule fails here, because the cost of appending targets depends on the whole partial route, not only its last target (Figure 2).
- **New dominance criterion.** Closed-form lower and upper bounds on each meeting time, from the disk of places a target can reach, give a pruning test that provably keeps optimality. Rounded-capacity and subset-row cuts tighten the relaxation.
- **Relaxed variant.** Crediting a label's full time advantage prunes more but is no longer exact, so this version is a heuristic.

## Results
- Tested on the 18-instance benchmark of Gambella et al. (up to 20 targets) with a 16 h limit, against the dynamic program of Zhang et al. on identical hardware.
- The exact version certifies 12 optima and is faster on each: 5.3x geometric mean (1.6x to 34x). The relaxed version matches all 12 optima at 14.7x (up to 131x) and finishes 4 more without a certificate.
- The certified values show the dynamic program returned suboptimal routes on 4 of the 12 instances.
- On instances no prior work closed, the relaxed version improves the best known cost on 18-4-7 (87.57 to 80.54) and 20-4-7 (139.59 to 110.08).

## Limitations
The exact version does not close 6 of the 18 instances within 16 h, and two stay unsolved by both variants; Wang et al.'s branch-and-price certifies one instance this method does not. The relaxed variant has no optimality guarantee. The bounds assume no target is faster than the vehicles. Evaluation is computational only, on one benchmark.
