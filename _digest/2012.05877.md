abstract: We present iNeRF, a framework that performs mesh-free pose estimation by "inverting" a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF). NeRFs have been shown to be remarkably effective for the task of view synthesis - synthesizing photorealistic novel views of real-world scenes or objects. In this work, we investigate whether we can apply analysis-by-synthesis via NeRF for mesh-free, RGB-only 6DoF pose estimation - given an image, find the translation and rotation of a camera relative to a 3D object or scene. Our method assumes that no object mesh models are available during either training or test time. Starting from an initial pose estimate, we use gradient descent to minimize the residual between pixels rendered from a NeRF and pixels in an observed image. In our experiments, we first study 1) how to sample rays during pose refinement for iNeRF to collect informative gradients and 2) how different batch sizes of rays affect iNeRF on a synthetic dataset. We then show that for complex real-world scenes from the LLFF dataset, iNeRF can improve NeRF by estimating the camera poses of novel images and using these images as additional training data for NeRF. Finally, we show iNeRF can perform category-level object pose estimation, including object instances not seen during training, with RGB images by inverting a NeRF model inferred from a single view.

## What they do
Render-and-compare pose estimation normally needs a watertight mesh of the object; iNeRF uses a NeRF instead. Given an RGB image, known intrinsics, an initial pose guess and a trained NeRF, it freezes the NeRF and runs gradient descent on the camera pose to minimize the photometric error between rendered and observed pixels. The same loop poses unlabeled images for NeRF training and, with pixelNeRF, estimates the pose of objects unseen in training.

## How it works
- **SE(3) optimization.** The pose is parameterized in exponential coordinates as a correction applied to the initial estimate, so it stays on SE(3). Putting the center of rotation at the initial estimate reduces coupling between rotation and translation. Adam with an exponentially decaying learning rate.
- **Ray sampling.** Backpropagating through every pixel does not fit in GPU memory, so each step renders a batch of rays. Random rays mostly hit textureless background; sampling only detected interest points converges faster but gets stuck in local minima. *Interest region* sampling draws rays from dilated regions around the interest points.
- **Self-supervised NeRF.** Train NeRF on posed images, pose the remaining images with iNeRF, then retrain NeRF on all of them.
- **Category level.** pixelNeRF predicts a NeRF from one image of an unseen object; iNeRF aligns it to a second image.

## Results
- 2048 rays per step fit in one forward/backward pass, 150x faster per step than full-image rendering on a 640x480 image. Larger ray batches and interest-region sampling both raise the fraction of poses within 5° or 5 cm.
- At batch size 1024, the fraction under 5° rotation error falls from 71% on synthetic scenes to 55% on real LLFF scenes, and under 5 cm from 73% to 39%.
- Fern with 25% of poses labeled: PSNR 21.85, rising to 23.89 when iNeRF poses the rest (100% labeled: 24.94).
- ShapeNet cars vs. SuperGlue: mean rotation error 4.39° vs. 9.27°, outliers 8.7% vs. 33.3%. On 10 real toy cars, with pixelNeRF trained only on ShapeNet, tracking needs under 10 iterations per frame (about 1 Hz).

## Limitations
Lighting changes and occlusion are not modeled and can severely hurt accuracy. 100 optimization steps take about 20 s, too slow for real-time use. It needs an initial pose estimate, and self-supervision needs enough labels: with only 10% of Fern poses, adding iNeRF-posed images lowers PSNR from 18.5 to 15.64.
