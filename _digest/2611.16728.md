abstract: 'Generalist manipulation policies can benefit from the complementary experience of humans, real robots, and simulated agents. However, differences in morphology, sensing, and control interfaces make it difficult to learn effectively from their combined demonstrations. We introduce EgoX, an egocentric cross-embodiment learning framework that organizes heterogeneous demonstrations through semantically corresponding observation and action modalities. EgoX pretrains a Modularized Cross-Embodiment Transformer with Embodiment Dreaming (MXT+) on this mixture and fine-tunes it on target-embodiment demonstrations. Within each embodiment, MXT+ assigns a dedicated tokenizer to each observation modality and a dedicated expert to each action modality, with a Transformer trunk shared across embodiments. A training-only embodiment-dreaming objective predicts latent representations of future visual and proprioceptive states, encouraging the shared trunk to model each embodiment''s dynamics. Across 13 task-embodiment pairs spanning five tasks and six robot embodiments, including five physical robots and one simulated humanoid, cross-embodiment pretraining raises MXT+''s average normalized task progress from 0.63 to 0.80 over training from scratch. The gains are particularly pronounced under out-of-distribution conditions, where average task progress increases from 0.53 to 0.77. Ablations demonstrate the benefits of broader pretraining mixtures, modular interfaces, and embodiment dreaming, with dreaming producing more similar latent geometry across embodiments. ## What they do
Humans, robots and simulated agents produce demonstrations with different bodies, cameras and action spaces. EgoX treats them all as embodiments of equal standing: it pretrains one policy, MXT+, on their combined egocentric data and then fine-tunes it on a target robot and task. The data cover a human, a Unitree G1 humanoid with five-finger hands, an xArm7 with a LEAP Hand, a LocoMan quadruped, a Galaxea R1 Lite, a Unitree Z1 with a fixed scoop and a simulated G1: 4,851 trajectories, 34.38 hours.

## How it works
- **Shared modalities.** Observations and actions are split into corresponding parts (egocentric image, torso pose, left/right wrist poses, end-effector keypoints) in common root, egocentric and wrist frames. Fingertips, gripper contacts and tool points become comparable keypoints.
- **Modular Transformer.** Each embodiment has its own tokenizer per observation modality and its own expert per action modality, joined by a shared encoder-decoder trunk that predicts 2 s action chunks.
- **Embodiment dreaming.** A training-only head predicts latent tokens of future images and joint states, with targets from EMA copies of the tokenizers. Deployment does not use it.
- Pretraining draws an equal-sized batch from every embodiment and holds out the target robot's target-task data for fine-tuning.

## Results
- Pretraining raises average task progress from 0.63 to 0.80 (out of distribution 0.53 to 0.77). Pretrained MXT+ ranks first among four methods, including ACT and pi0.5, on 12 of 13 pairs, with 20 trials per real-robot pair.
- Broader mixtures transfer better: the full mixture adds 0.12 in distribution and 0.23 out of distribution over no pretraining.
- Dreaming raises task progress from 0.63 to 0.78 over seven settings. On Scoop it makes the trunk's latents more alike across embodiments (mean Gromov-Wasserstein distance down 38.52%).
- A coarse variant that merges camera views, pose streams and action outputs scores zero on simulated Kitchen-Cleanup after pretraining.

## Limitations
The team collected all data themselves, so larger datasets are untested. Simulation covers one humanoid and two tasks. Adding a robot needs hand-specified modality correspondences and frames. Equal-sized batches reuse small datasets such as LocoMan's more often, which may cause overfitting.
