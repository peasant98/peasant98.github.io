abstract: 'Dexterity is, at its core, finger-level control, yet existing dexterous manipulation and human-object interaction (HOI) generation methods rarely let one specify how an interaction is performed: five-fingered hands are often reduced to grippers, or finger usage is copied from individual human demonstrations. We present FineDex, a framework that makes finger-level intent both expressible and executable. FineDex learns coordinated human body, hand, and object motions that follow fine-grained instructions on hand selection, individual fingers, and contacts with articulated objects. To bridge generation and execution, FineDex leverages generated interactions as actionable priors for an agent that discovers physically feasible contact strategies through practice in a physics-based digital twin. By adapting at test time without per-instance retraining, FineDex generalizes across fine-grained instructions and object configurations. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art HOI generation quality and fine-grained controllability, substantial improvements in dexterous manipulation success, and successful deployment on a real dexterous humanoid robot.'

## What they do
Most dexterous-manipulation and human-object interaction (HOI) generators judge success by task completion alone. They cannot be told which hand, finger or contact to use. FineDex adds that control. A generator produces body, hand and articulated-object motion that follows instructions such as "use only the left middle finger". An agent then turns that motion into something a robot can execute through practice in simulation, with no per-instance training.

## How it works
- **Generator.** A Transformer diffusion model trained on ARCTIC with new fine-grained annotations (hand roles, finger actions, contacts). Hand selection uses guidance during denoising. A small adapter on a frozen backbone edits single fingers, and dual-trajectory sampling changes only the commanded fingers. A post-hoc optimization enforces designated or exclusive finger contacts.
- **Contact programs.** Generated motion is retargeted to the robot hand. Candidate contacts come from the generated HOI, geometric push or hook synthesis, and a library of human grasps.
- **Practice.** A multimodal LLM agent (GPT-6 Astra by default) proposes programs, tests them in parallel digital twins with randomized dynamics, and refines them. The most reliable program runs open-loop.
- **FingerBench**, a new benchmark that scores instruction following in both generation and execution.

## Results
- HOI generation on ARCTIC: FID drops from 1.51 to 0.65 (bimanual) and from 2.32 to 1.61 (whole-body) against the best baselines.
- Hand switching succeeds 83.9% of the time; no baseline exceeds 8.0%. Baselines score 0% on finger-motion tasks, against 37.0-92.5% for FineDex.
- Execution with floating hands: direct replay of the generated motion succeeds 6.2% of the time, and practice raises this to 75.0% (42.2% on G1). Generated priors add a few points on shifted object configurations (70.3% vs 63.4% floating, 39.4% vs 36.6% G1).
- Real G1 with Wuji hands: with a fixed object placement, the left-middle-finger program succeeded in 5 of 5 consecutive trials.

## Limitations
Enforcing designated fingers lowers task success. Limited arm reach and imperfect wrist tracking are the main failure modes on the humanoid. Real-robot programs run open-loop, without feedback.
