These scenarios often require highly proactive strategies, simultaneously demanding interpretability and quick adaptability, which challenge end-to-end and data-driven methods. An effective strategy requires: scalability to multiple agents, adaptability to changes in other agents'' behavior, and harmony with surrounding traffic without freezing or becoming overly conservative. ## What they do
In a narrow street or a packed merge there is often no gap that is safe on its own, so the ego vehicle has to negotiate: push in when others will yield, back off when they won't. SIGMO adds this to a standard sampling-based planner (MPPI) without a learned interaction model. An analytic estimate of each agent's probability of yielding scales down that agent's risk in the cost.

## How it works
- **Polygate.** Five sigmoid scores feed the estimate: time-to-collision along the predicted trajectories, the agent's recent acceleration (braking suggests yielding), closing speed, path priority (who reaches the conflict point first) and an elliptical body-frame proximity gate. Each sigmoid's inflection point and sharpness has a physical meaning. The scores are fused into one yield probability, which is pulled toward 0.3 for adjacent agents.
- **Gated risk.** For every MPPI sample, the overlap between Gaussian footprints of the ego and an agent gives a spatial risk. A multiplier that falls as the yield probability rises scales it, but the risk is never removed entirely. A separate one-second collision penalty does not depend on the gate.
- The features come from each candidate rollout's geometry, so any predictor works (constant velocity here) and no prediction is re-run per sample.

## Results
- Narrow passage with pedestrians and cars (100 seeds): 73% success against 69% for MPPI, with 25% against 26% collisions, and stall time down 24.3%. An oracle given the agents' true cooperativeness reaches 70%.
- Packed merge: 38% success against 26%, both with 2% collisions. Nudging lowers the minimum distance from 0.82 m to 0.62 m. An oracle with true reactive trajectories reaches 69%.
- MetaDrive, 150 scenarios initialized from the Waymo Open Dataset: 92.7% success against 89.3% for IANN-MPPI, which uses a learned Social GAN interaction model.
- F1Tenth car with lidar: 15 Hz with 2000 samples. Merging from a ramp past a pedestrian, naive MPPI fails to merge, while SIGMO merges or surrenders behind the pedestrian. Shown qualitatively only.

## Limitations
The simulated agents follow simple behavior models that may not reflect real human behavior. Because SIGMO builds on MPPI, it has no hard collision constraints. The hardware tests are a few qualitative runs on a scale car.
