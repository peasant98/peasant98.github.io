## What they do
Simulators for robot learning at scale are strongest on rigid bodies, and adding cloth, cables or liquids usually means a second engine plus hand-written glue. Alloy puts all of these materials in one scene and runs it in many parallel worlds on one GPU, or on a CPU. It is built on Newton, MuJoCo Warp and Warp, and is designed to be driven by coding agents.

## How it works
- **Routing.** Each object is a shape plus a material card (density, stiffness, friction). The router assigns it a solver and records why: MuJoCo Warp for rigid bodies and robots, Newton's VBD for cloth, soft volumes and rods, implicit MPM for sand and pastes, Alloy's SPH for liquids.
- **Coupling.** Solvers exchange poses and contact forces through proxy bodies inside every control step. The step size comes from the interface's effective mass and contact stiffness. If a world's interface work looks wrong, that control period is rerun with a finer step.
- **Health grades.** Every world is graded OK, DEGRADED or INVALID after each exchange. Invalid worlds are reset and frozen, separating physics failures from policy failures.
- **Agent interface.** The docs ship inside the package, every error names its fix, and the engine is exposed over the Model Context Protocol.

## Results
- Coding agents on one NVIDIA L4, reading only the shipped docs, trained Go2 walking to 97% held-out success in 15.5 min (97-98% under 150 N pushes). Cable lifting reached 95.7% in about 30 min, pouring 92% in 38.5 min, and behavior-cloned pick-and-place 256/256.
- From 1 to 64 worlds, throughput grows about 39x for a Franka scene, 20x for a cloth sheet and 13x for a 200 ml pour.
- All ten closed-form checks (e.g. cantilever bending, catenary sag, hydrostatic pressure) fall within tolerances set in advance. A ten-block tower had no errors in any of 1,024 worlds.

## Limitations
Alloy is an early 0.x release whose interfaces may change. The policy results come from a pre-release, one training run each. Everything is in simulation, with no transfer to real robots.
