abstract: In this work, we explore self-supervised visual pre-training on images from diverse, in-the-wild videos for real-world robotic tasks. Like prior work, our visual representations are pre-trained via a masked autoencoder (MAE), frozen, and then passed into a learnable control module. Unlike prior work, we show that the pre-trained representations are effective across a range of real-world robotic tasks and embodiments. We find that our encoder consistently outperforms CLIP (up to 75%), supervised ImageNet pre-training (up to 81%), and training from scratch (up to 81%). Finally, we train a 307M parameter vision transformer on a massive collection of 4.5M images from the Internet and egocentric videos, and demonstrate clearly the benefits of scaling visual pre-training for robot learning.

## What they do
They test whether a single vision encoder, pre-trained with self-supervision on in-the-wild images, can serve as the perception module for many real robot tasks. The encoder is trained once as a masked autoencoder (MAE), frozen, and reused unchanged across six xArm tasks and two Allegro hand tasks, each learned by behavior cloning from at most 80 demonstrations.

## How it works
- **Data.** 4.5M images: 2.6M frames from Ego4D, 1.2M from ImageNet, and 700k hand-object interaction images from Epic Kitchens, Something-Something and 100 Days of Hands.
- **Pre-training.** MAE masks random patches (e.g. 75%) and reconstructs them. Encoders are ViT-S, ViT-B and ViT-L (22M, 86M and 307M parameters).
- **Policy.** A four-layer MLP takes the frozen image features from a wrist-mounted RGB camera plus joint positions and outputs delta joint angles at 5 Hz. 80 demonstrations per task, mostly from HTC Vive VR teleoperation.
- **Evaluation.** Each task uses the same 16 object placements for every model, run back to back to hold lighting and robot condition fixed.

## Results
- Over 981 real trials, the MAE ViT-B beats CLIP (the strongest baseline), supervised ImageNet and from-scratch ViT-B encoders on reach, push and pick, and matches CLIP's 80-demo performance with half the demonstrations. It also beats CLIP on the fridge, fruit and sink tasks.
- On PickFruit: CLIP ViT-B 18.8%, R3M ResNet-50 31.3%, MAE ViT-S 68.8%, ViT-B 93.8%, ViT-L 100%.
- Scaling the model from ViT-S to ViT-B helps only when the data grows too; ViT-L improves further, most on PickSink. A wrist camera beats a third-person one, and dropping either images or proprioception gives zero success.
- Allegro hand: about 50% success reaching 8 seen and 45 unseen objects, and 50% over 30 cube-flipping trials.

## Limitations
Mostly toy objects in clean lab scenes. Each task involves a single object and needs neither feedback control nor multi-step planning. The authors also note that robot up-time can affect results.
