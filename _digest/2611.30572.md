abstract: Scaling visuo-tactile policy learning is constrained by the high cost of real-world data collection and the difficulty of building realistic simulations. Generative models offer a scalable source of training data, yet useful demonstrations require plausible tactile feedback, temporal alignment with visual contact, and accurate actions. To this end, we present VeriTouch, a framework for generating and verifying synthetic visuo-tactile trajectories for policy learning. Conditioned on initial images and task instructions, our world model, VeriGen, jointly generates high-fidelity dual-view videos and realistic tactile sequences over complete manipulation trajectories. By integrating a dedicated tactile expert with a pretrained video model, it captures realistic visual details and tactile deformation dynamics through joint visuo-tactile modeling. Our multimodal verifier VeriEval then checks visual quality while focusing on tactile reliability, assessing tactile realism, contact-force plausibility, and temporal alignment between visible contact and tactile signals, thereby directly filtering out unreliable sequences. Finally, our inverse dynamics model, VeriIDM, combines global scene information from both camera views with fine-grained gripper details and motion across neighboring frames to extract actions from verified videos. Experiments on five real-world tasks show that synthetic data improves all four evaluated policies, with further scaling synthetic trajectories substantially boosting policy performance without additional real-world data collection.

## What they do
Visuo-tactile demonstrations are expensive to collect: they need tactile sensors and teleoperation. VeriTouch synthesizes more of them from a small real set. From the first main- and wrist-camera images and a task instruction, a world model generates the whole trajectory, both videos plus the deformation of the two fingertip tactile sensors. A verifier then rejects unreliable samples, and an inverse dynamics model recovers the actions, with no physical or simulated replay.

## How it works
- **VeriGen.** Extends the Cosmos3-Nano video model to generate both views jointly and adds a tactile diffusion-transformer expert that generates 3D displacement fields on each sensor, linked to the video by joint attention over a shared time axis. It is pretrained on 16,593 OmniViTac trajectories and fine-tuned on 200 real ones from the target tasks.
- **VeriEval.** A VLM fine-tuned on about 900 human-labeled generated videos scores instruction following and physical plausibility. Tactile checks require the tactile contact interval to match contact seen in the wrist video, tactile images to reconstruct well through the AnyTouch2 autoencoder, and the contact force estimated by FeelAnyForce to suit the task.
- **VeriIDM.** Predicts end-effector pose and gripper closure from the two RGB streams, using local gripper features and 17-frame temporal context.

## Results
- Setup: xArm7 with two Xense tactile sensors; five tasks (chip pick, egg pick, charger press, pullback car, whiteboard erase), 10 unseen layouts x 3 trials each.
- Averaged over ACT, π0.5, N0-VTLA and FTP-1, success rises from 19.4% (real only) to 33.8% with verified synthetic data, versus 22.9% with unfiltered synthetic data.
- For N0-VTLA, success climbs from 33.0% to 67.0% at a 4:1 synthetic-to-real ratio. Replacing generated touch with no-contact input cuts success from 51.7% to 26.0%; visual-only verification gives 43.3%.
- Replaying extracted actions on the robot succeeds 94% of the time, versus 84% for the EVA inverse dynamics model.

## Limitations
The paper states none. All real tests use one arm, one sensor type and five tasks, and the generator is fine-tuned on demonstrations of those same tasks. On a constructed test set the tactile verifier has 72% precision, so some corrupted samples get through.
