abstract: Personalization of powered prosthesis control to individual user needs remains a significant challenge. To address this challenge, policy iteration with constraint embedded (PICE), a reinforcement learning (RL) tuning approach, has been developed and validated. This feasibility study investigated whether the developed approach can personalize a commercial powered knee prosthesis under clinically realistic conditions. To reduce the time for personalization, tuning was initialized with a policy learned offline from one non-disabled participant walking on a treadmill and was then applied to three other non-disabled participants walking with the knee through a bypass adapter on a 5-meter walkway. All participants reached the goal within 8-18 tuning steps (1.7-4.2 minutes of walking), reducing the knee-kinematic feature error by 64-90% on average. These results support the feasibility of implementing the RL-based tuning approach in clinically realistic conditions with space and equipment constraints.

## What they do
Powered knee prostheses must be tuned to each user, and prosthetists do it by hand. This study tests whether PICE, an RL tuner previously validated only on treadmill walking, can personalize a commercial Össur Power Knee under clinic-like conditions: self-paced walking on a 5 m walkway with stops and turns, using only the knee's onboard sensors and the four parameters a prosthetist already adjusts. Three non-disabled participants walked on the knee through a bypass adapter.

## How it works
- **Black-box interface.** The proprietary controller is left untouched; a laptop reads knee angles at about 50 Hz and sets the four normalized clinical parameters.
- **State and reward.** Every 6 gait cycles, peak stance and swing flexion and extension are taken from the last 4 steady-state cycles; their normalized errors from a target form the state. Cycles with stopping, turning or starting are excluded. The action is a bounded parameter increment, and the reward penalizes feature error and adjustment size.
- **PICE.** A constrained LSTD-Q method: the Q-function is quadratic, with its weights constrained so that choosing the action is a convex problem. It starts from a policy learned offline from 150 treadmill tuning steps of one other participant, and refits every 12 steps.

## Results
- All four trials met the success criterion: 8, 10 and 18 steps (1.7, 2.3 and 4.2 min of walking) for target T1, and 8 steps (1.7 min) for a second target T2.
- Errors fell on average by 90%, 81%, 86% and 64% for stance max, stance min, swing max and swing min. Swing max flexion error went from 17.2-21.0° to 1.3-4.3° in the T1 trials.
- Three trials converged on the offline prior alone. In the one online update, RMS TD error fell 47%, measured on six samples. No falls occurred.

## Limitations
Non-disabled participants on a bypass adapter, one to three per condition, and no statistical tests. Success means hitting experimenter-set kinematic targets, not clinical benefit or user preference. Each condition was tuned once on level ground, so repeatability across days and other terrains are untested.
