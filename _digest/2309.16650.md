abstract: 'For robots to perform a wide variety of tasks, they require a 3D representation of the world that is semantically rich, yet compact and efficient for task-driven perception and planning. Recent approaches have attempted to leverage features from large vision-language models to encode semantics in 3D representations. However, these approaches tend to produce maps with per-point feature vectors, which do not scale well in larger environments, nor do they contain semantic spatial relationships between entities in the environment, which are useful for downstream planning. In this work, we propose ConceptGraphs, an open-vocabulary graph-structured representation for 3D scenes. ConceptGraphs is built by leveraging 2D foundation models and fusing their output to 3D by multi-view association. The resulting representations generalize to novel semantic classes, without the need to collect large 3D datasets or finetune models. We demonstrate the utility of this representation through a number of downstream planning tasks that are specified through abstract (language) prompts and require complex reasoning over spatial and semantic concepts. (Project page: https://concept-graphs.github.io/ Explainer video: https://youtu.be/mRhNkQwRYnc )'

## What they do
Dense maps with a vision-language feature per point scale poorly and hold no relations between objects. ConceptGraphs builds an object-centric 3D scene graph from posed RGB-D frames using only off-the-shelf 2D foundation models. Nodes are objects with a point cloud, a CLIP feature and a caption; edges are spatial relationships. The graph is written out as JSON so an LLM can plan over it.

## How it works
- **Mapping.** SAM gives class-agnostic masks; each gets a CLIP feature and is projected to 3D and denoised with DBSCAN. New detections are greedily matched to map objects by the sum of a point-overlap score and a feature-similarity score, then fused by averaging features and merging point clouds.
- **Captioning.** LLaVA captions crops from each object's 10 best views; GPT-4 summarizes them into one caption or marks the object invalid.
- **Edges.** 3D bounding-box IoU gives a dense graph, pruned to a minimum spanning tree; GPT-4 labels each edge (e.g. "a on b") from captions and positions.
- **Planning.** GPT-4 reads the object list, returns the object most relevant to a query, and its 3D pose goes to navigation or grasping.

## Results
- Replica scene graphs, rated by Mechanical Turk workers: node captions are correct about 70% of the time (most errors come from LLaVA), edges about 90%, with 0-5 duplicate objects per scene.
- Zero-shot 3D semantic segmentation on Replica: 40.63 mAcc and 35.95 F-mIoU, against 31.53 and 38.70 for ConceptFusion+SAM.
- Text-query retrieval: LLM retrieval beats CLIP on complex queries, e.g. Replica negation R@1 0.80 vs 0.26; in a lab scan (10 queries each) affordance 1.00 vs 0.40 and negation 1.00 vs 0.00.
- Robots: a Jackal navigates to objects named by abstract or visual queries, searches likely containers when objects have moved, and pushes objects an LLM deems traversable; a Spot Arm picks and places a duck plush for "cuddly quacker".

## Limitations
LLaVA captions are error-prone (many small objects become toothbrushes or scissors), and the graph misses small or thin objects and has duplicates, which hurts planning when that object matters. Building and querying the graph needs many LVLM calls and proprietary LLM inference, which can be costly.
