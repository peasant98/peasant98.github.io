## What they do
PECNet forecasts pedestrian trajectories in two steps: infer where each pedestrian will be at the end of the 4.8 s prediction window, then predict the path to a sampled endpoint jointly for everyone in the scene. The premise is that people head toward a destination, so the endpoint is easier to predict than the points in between.

## How it works
- **Endpoint VAE.** Past positions and the ground-truth endpoint are encoded into a Gaussian latent. At test time a latent sampled from N(0, σT I) is decoded with the past encoding into an endpoint.
- **Social pooling.** Past and sampled-endpoint features of all pedestrians go through rounds of non-local attention, masked to neighbours close in space and overlapping in time. An MLP then outputs the remaining positions.
- **Training.** End to end with KL, endpoint and trajectory losses. Downstream modules see sampled rather than ground-truth endpoints; using ground truth was about 8.8% worse.
- **Truncation trick.** For small K, sample with σT = 1 and truncate; for large K, use σT > 1 without truncation. No retraining is needed.

## Results
- Stanford Drone, K = 20: 9.96 / 15.88 px ADE/FDE, against 12.58 / 22.07 for P2TIRL. Without social pooling or truncation: 10.56 ADE. It matches the previous best ADE with K = 10.
- ETH/UCY, K = 20, no truncation: average 0.29 / 0.48 m, against 0.49 / 0.97 for CGNS. The gain is largest on HOTEL (74.2%) and smallest on ETH (12.9%).
- Truncation at K = 1 lowers ADE from 22.85 to 17.29 (about 24.7%).
- The error in predicting a future point grows up to about the 7th future position, then plateaus and drops slightly.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. As K grows, FDE approaches 0 but ADE stays at 6.49 px, so the remaining error is in the path to the endpoint. Social pooling adds only 0.33 ADE on Stanford Drone, and the truncation constant (c ≈ 1.2) is chosen empirically. Only pedestrian datasets are tested; there are no robot experiments.
