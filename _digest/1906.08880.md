abstract: 'Reinforcement Learning (RL) of contact-rich manipulation tasks has yielded impressive results in recent years. While many studies in RL focus on varying the observation space or reward model, few efforts focused on the choice of action space (e.g. joint or end-effector space, position, velocity, etc.). However, studies in robot motion control indicate that choosing an action space that conforms to the characteristics of the task can simplify exploration and improve robustness to disturbances. This paper studies the effect of different action spaces in deep RL and advocates for Variable Impedance Control in End-effector Space (VICES) as an advantageous action space for constrained and contact-rich tasks. We evaluate multiple action spaces on three prototypical manipulation tasks: Path Following (task with no contact), Door Opening (task with kinematic constraints), and Surface Wiping (task with continuous contact). We show that VICES improves sample efficiency, maintains low energy consumption, and ensures safety across all three experimental setups. Further, RL policies learned with VICES can transfer across different robot models in simulation, and from simulation to real for the same robot. Further information is available at https://stanfordvl.github.io/vices.'

## What they do
An empirical study of how the action space affects RL for manipulation. It compares joint torques, velocities and positions, variable joint impedance, and end-effector impedance with low, medium or high fixed gains, and advocates variable impedance control in end-effector space (VICES), where the policy also outputs the controller's stiffness and damping along all six axes.

## How it works
- **Controller as action interface.** The policy runs at 20 Hz. An analytic controller turns each action into joint torques at 500 Hz, interpolating linearly between consecutive actions.
- **VICES.** The policy outputs a desired end-effector pose change plus position and orientation gains. An operational-space impedance controller maps these to end-effector wrenches and then to joint torques through the Jacobian.
- **Tasks.** Free-space path following through four via-points, opening a door to 60°, and wiping a table at an unknown height from 48x48 RGB images. All are trained with PPO on a simulated Panda, five seeds per action space.

## Results
- Path following: every action space except joint torques and low fixed impedance passes all four via-points. Among end-effector policies, only VICES consistently cut energy use without losing task performance once an energy penalty was added.
- Door opening: medium, high and variable end-effector impedance reach close to 100% success. Joint velocity reaches up to 75%, while joint torques and positions fail.
- Wiping: VICES converges faster, reaches the highest reward and wipes the largest fraction of the stain, at a mean force of 28 N (below the 40 N payload).
- Zero-shot transfer: in simulation, only end-effector policies transfer from Panda to Sawyer. On the real robot, without force sensing, the VICES policy wipes a whiteboard in 8 of 10 trials.

## Limitations
Only PPO was tested. Transferred end-effector policies still lose performance: they do not account for the new robot's joint limits, and when wiping, the other robot produces different forces. For sim-to-real, camera images are replaced by simulator renders with a color-segmented overlay.
