abstract: 'World action model (WAM) is a promising paradigm for visuomotor control by jointly modeling world dynamics and actions. Notably, existing WAMs often rely on large generative models and iterative sampling which incur substantial latency for action generation, limiting real-time deployment. In particular, there are two root causes of inefficiency in conventional flow matching or diffusion based action models in WAMs: 1) transporting uninformative Gaussian noise into precise actions necessitating multiple ODE steps, and 2) repeatedly injecting task context through expensive cross-attention modules. Our key insight is that jointly learning and aligning the source and action manifolds streamlines the transport between them, substantially accelerating action generation. Building on this insight, we introduce VITA-WAM, a WAM architecture with manifold-aligned flow-matching (VITA flow matching) that directly transports latent observation representations containing visual, language, and proprioceptive information into latent actions. More specifically, a flow latent alignment (FLA) mechanism is employed to facilitate manifold alignment between latent observation and action representations, enabling accurate generation with few ODE steps. The resulting architecture also eliminates costly attention for observation conditioning during repeated denoising steps. Notably, with a single ODE step, VITA-WAM outperforms 10-step FastWAM by 21.47 pp on LIBERO-Plus and by 1.65 pp on LIBERO, achieving these gains through the FLA objective without distillation, while inferring at 55.67~ms latency, a $2.10\times$ speedup compared to FastWAM.'

## What they do
World-action models (WAMs) pair a video model with an action model, but their flow-matching action heads start from Gaussian noise, need many ODE steps, and attend to the observations again at every step. VITA-WAM scales the authors' earlier VITA flow matching to a 6B WAM. Actions flow from a learned observation latent instead of noise, so one ODE step is enough and the action model needs no observation conditioning while integrating.

## How it works
- **Backbone.** A pretrained Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video DiT, co-trained with video flow matching as in Fast-WAM.
- **Source and target.** A Q-Former fuses current-frame video features, language and proprioception into one 512-D source token. An action autoencoder maps a 32-step action chunk to a 512-D target latent. The action DiT operates only on the evolving action latent.
- **Flow Latent Alignment (FLA).** During training the ODE is integrated from the source with the same number of Euler steps used at inference, decoded, and given an L1 loss against the true actions, with gradients through solver, decoder and source encoder. Their theory links this alignment to lower transport cost and less curved flows.

## Results
- LIBERO: 98.85% with one step, vs 97.20% for Fast-WAM with 10 steps (95.30% with one).
- LIBERO-Plus, with no training on its perturbations: 70.80% (1 step) and 73.05% (2 steps) vs 49.33% for 10-step Fast-WAM. Faster-WAM (SparseMoT) reaches 73.57%.
- Real Franka Research 3, four tasks, 180 demonstrations each: 77/80 successes vs 74/80 for 10-step Fast-WAM.
- On an RTX 4090 an action chunk takes 62.51 ms vs 211.41 ms (55.67 vs 116.65 ms compiled).

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. The real-robot test is four tasks with 20 trials each against one baseline, and the margin there is 3 of 80 episodes. On LIBERO-Plus a single step stays below Faster-WAM (SparseMoT), and compiled latency ties Faster-WAM (DoT), which is 0.39 ms faster.
