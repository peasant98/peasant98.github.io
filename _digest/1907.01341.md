abstract: 'The success of monocular depth estimation relies on large and diverse training sets. Due to the challenges associated with acquiring dense ground-truth depth across different environments at scale, a number of datasets with distinct characteristics and biases have emerged. We develop tools that enable mixing multiple datasets during training, even if their annotations are incompatible. In particular, we propose a robust training objective that is invariant to changes in depth range and scale, advocate the use of principled multi-objective learning to combine data from different sources, and highlight the importance of pretraining encoders on auxiliary tasks. Armed with these tools, we experiment with five diverse training datasets, including a new, massive data source: 3D films. To demonstrate the generalization power of our approach we use zero-shot cross-dataset transfer, i.e. we evaluate on datasets that were not seen during training. The experiments confirm that mixing data from complementary sources greatly improves monocular depth estimation. ## What they do
Depth datasets disagree in form: metric depth, depth up to scale (SfM), or disparity up to scale and shift (stereo with unknown calibration). This paper trains one monocular depth network on five such datasets, including a new one from 23 3D films, and tests it only on datasets never seen in training.

## How it works
- **3D Movies dataset.** Stereo frames from Blu-rays; the horizontal component of optical flow serves as disparity, since film disparities can be negative. A left-right consistency check and automatic filters remove bad frames, leaving 75,074 training frames from 19 films.
- **Loss.** The network predicts disparity (inverse depth up to scale and shift). Prediction and ground truth are aligned in scale and shift per image, then compared with a trimmed absolute error that discards the 20% largest residuals, plus a multi-scale gradient-matching term.
- **Mixing.** Each dataset is treated as a task, and training seeks a Pareto optimum across them, instead of sampling datasets in equal parts per minibatch.
- **Encoder.** ResNeXt-101 pretrained on weakly-supervised data, then ImageNet.

## Results
- The trimmed loss gives the lowest validation error of all losses tested. The best encoder improves up to 15% over ResNet-50; a randomly initialized ResNet-50 is on average 35% worse than a pretrained one.
- Each extra dataset used alone generalizes worse on average than ReDWeb alone. Mixing all five improves the mean by 19.5% over ReDWeb with naive mixing and 22.4% with Pareto mixing.
- Zero-shot, the final model reaches WHDR 12.46 on DIW, AbsRel 0.129 on ETH3D and 0.327 on Sintel. The three best prior zero-shot models are 65-69% worse on average; a ResNet-50 version is 16% worse but still beats them.

## Limitations
Depth is relative: errors are measured after aligning scale and shift to ground truth per image. The model learned that lower image regions are closer, which breaks on an image rotated by 90 degrees. It predicts the depicted content for paintings, photos and mirrors, hallucinates discontinuities at strong edges, misses thin structures and blurs the background.
