abstract: 'Pretrained video diffusion models (VDMs) offer a powerful foundation for World Action Models (WAMs), which jointly predict robot actions and future observations. Building such models requires injecting low-dimensional robot signals (e.g. proprioception, actions, and values) into a latent space shaped by natural videos, and a key design choice is the interface through which these signals enter: existing schemes treat it as a formatting detail, spending a separate latent frame on each signal or bolting on dedicated encoders and decoders. We argue that signal injection is instead a distribution-alignment problem: how well a representation matches the prior of the pretrained VDMs during denoising. Using the relative reconstruction error of a frozen VDM denoiser as a probe, we find that conventional spatial encodings sit far off this prior, whereas frequency-domain packing yields latents that the denoiser corrects far less. Motivated by this diagnostic result, we propose MetaWAM, which folds all robot signals into a single unified meta-frame: each signal is assigned to a disjoint frequency-domain channel band and lifted to the spatial latent by an inverse DCT, yielding a decodable interface aligned with the pretrained VDM prior without any added module or architectural change. A fixed band layout together with slot-aware routing lets the same meta-frame support policy, world-model, and value prediction over a shared set of robot variables. On RoboCasa, MetaWAM reaches 68.9% success while collapsing the per-signal frames into one and cutting inference latency by 39%, and attains 85.0% success on three real-world Franka tasks. Code and models are available at https://github.com/showlab/MetaWAM'

## What they do
World Action Models adapt a pretrained video diffusion model to predict robot actions and future observations together. Low-dimensional signals (proprioception, action chunks, value) must enter a latent space shaped by natural video; prior work spends a full latent frame on each or adds dedicated encoders and decoders. MetaWAM writes all of them into one "meta-frame" in the frequency domain, which a frozen denoiser treats as closer to its prior.

## How it works
- **Diagnostic.** The relative reconstruction error of a frozen Cosmos-Predict2 denoiser measures how far an injected latent sits from the video prior. DCT-encoded signals are corrected less than spatially tiled or VAE-encoded ones.
- **Meta-frame.** Each signal gets a disjoint channel band; its values go into fixed DCT coefficient slots and a per-channel 2D inverse DCT lifts them to a spatial latent. Readout is a DCT plus slot lookup, with no learned decoder.
- **Sequence.** One input meta-frame (task type, current proprioception), six visual frames from three cameras, and one output meta-frame (actions, future proprioception, value): 8 frames instead of 11.
- **Slot-aware routing.** Band-level masks mark each signal as clean conditioning or denoising target, so one model trains as policy, world model and value function.

## Results
- RoboCasa, 24 tasks with 50 demos each: 68.9% average success against 67.1% for Cosmos Policy; inference takes 0.291 s per query instead of 0.479 s.
- LIBERO: 98.1% average (Cosmos Policy 98.5%). LIBERO-Plus: 83.4% overall against 82.2%.
- Real Franka Panda (Sweep, Place Cup, Build Block; 80 demos and 20 trials per task): 85.0% in distribution and 68.3% out of distribution, against 78.3% and 63.3% for Cosmos Policy and 71.7% and 46.7% for pi0.5.
- Among the four packings, the unified meta-frame also gives the best future-view PSNR, future-proprioception error and value error.

## Limitations
The fixed band layout and loss weights may need adapting when signal scales or task importance change. Instruction following is limited by the Cosmos-Predict2 backbone. On LIBERO-Plus, background (72.7%) and robot initial-state (59.9%) perturbations score below Cosmos Policy.
