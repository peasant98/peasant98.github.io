abstract: We introduce EgoDepth-Bench, comprising 941,233 synchronized samples collected across 37 distinct physical environments. Each sample combines six calibrated RGB views with metric depth in the native fisheye view, reconstructed from five active-infrared stereo modules. A calibrated ball-bar confirms millimeter-level geometric accuracy, with a median error of 1.27 mm on a known sphere spacing. Environment-held-out splits enable evaluation of generalization to unseen environments. Across monocular, stereo, and multi-view models, the benchmark measures both depth accuracy and prediction coverage, including on hands and objects. Fine-tuning MapAnything on EgoDepth-Bench reduces metric AbsRel from 0.708 to 0.091 and scale-aligned AbsRel from 0.327 to 0.072 on unseen environments. These results demonstrate that EgoDepth-Bench provides effective supervision and enables rigorous evaluation of metric and relative depth estimation in egocentric manipulation. The project page provides the evaluation code and interactive RGB-depth sequences.

## What they do
Egocentric human demonstrations are a growing source of robot training data, and recovering the metric geometry of hands, objects and workspace can help learning from them. No existing dataset combines native-fisheye metric depth of real dynamic manipulation with many physical sites. EgoDepth-Bench records 941,233 synchronized samples (8.72 h, 1,155 recordings) at 37 sites with a head-worn rig, and benchmarks 18 public depth models on sites held out from training.

## How it works
- **Rig.** Six global-shutter fisheye RGB cameras (camera 2 sees about 163° x 136°) and five Gemini 336L active-IR stereo modules, hardware-triggered at 30 Hz. Five participants did freely chosen activities such as folding clothes, tidying and mopping.
- **Ground truth.** FoundationStereo runs on each rectified IR pair. Its depth is kept where it agrees within 5% with the module's onboard depth, or, where onboard depth is missing, only at near-range disparities above 128 px. Points are projected into camera 2's fisheye image and fused with a z-buffer.
- **Validation.** A certified 500 mm ball-bar, captured static and moving at different distances and angles, checks the reconstructed geometry.
- **Benchmark.** Site-disjoint splits (24 train, 5 validation, 8 test sites). Scores report accuracy and coverage, with separate hand and object masks.

## Results
- Ball-bar spacing error: median 1.27 mm (P95 6.66 mm), rising from 1.11 mm at 0.25-0.8 m to 9.79 mm at 1.2-2.0 m.
- Zero-shot, UniDepth v2 is the best metric monocular model (AbsRel 0.201, 15.44 cm MAE). FoundationStereo reaches 7.35 cm MAE but covers only 35.4% of the image.
- For MapAnything, six views give no clear gain over one, while metric rig poses cut six-view AbsRel from 3.168 to 0.708.
- Fine-tuning MapAnything lowers test AbsRel from 0.708 to 0.091 and object error from 33.81 to 5.21 cm; scale-aligned AbsRel drops from 0.327 to 0.072.

## Limitations
Ground truth covers 66.9% of image pixels on average, and its error grows past 1.2 m. The protocol scores single frames, not temporal consistency. Fine-tuning uses one seed, and the motion comparisons are observational. Even after fine-tuning, only 60.0% of object pixels fall within 5 cm.
