abstract: While recent vision-language-action (VLA) models increasingly incorporate 3D geometry, how to effectively exploit it for precise and generalizable action prediction remains a central challenge. We introduce Hi3R-VLA, a 3D policy that grounds heterogeneous modalities - vision, language, and action - in explicit 3D geometry for robotic manipulation. Our model integrates spatially-aligned visual features and language conditioning into point cloud representations, enriching scene geometry with fine-grained, task-relevant semantics. It then anchors low-level control commands in the same 3D coordinate frame and uses 3D-RoPE enhanced action-to-point attention over coarse-to-fine point features to model geometric relationships between action targets and observed points. Our analysis demonstrates the importance of semantic fusion within 3D and spatial alignment between observations and actions. With fewer than 250M trainable parameters, Hi3R-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on RoboCasa, RLBench, RoboTwin2, DexJoCo and DexSuite across diverse manipulation tasks and embodiments, from single-arm and dual-arm grippers to dexterous hands. Real-robot evaluations further demonstrate its effectiveness in the physical world. The model, code and dataset are publicly released at https://cshizhe.github.io/projects/hi3r_vla.html.

## What they do
Hi3R-VLA asks which design choices make explicit 3D geometry useful for a language-conditioned manipulation policy. The answer is a compact model that puts vision, language and actions in one metric point-cloud frame. It is tested on five simulation benchmarks, covering single arms, dual arms and dexterous hands, and on a real UR5.

## How it works
- **Multimodal point encoder.** A Point Transformer v3 backbone encodes the RGB-D point cloud. Frozen SigLIP2 image features are projected onto the points and fused at every encoder stage, and a pooled instruction embedding modulates the point features through adaptive layer normalization.
- **Geometry-aware action expert.** A flow-matching transformer attends to point features from coarse to fine scales, plus image and text tokens, and outputs action chunks.
- **Spatial anchors.** Each action token also carries an end-effector position in the point-cloud frame, so 3D RoPE can encode the displacement between action targets and scene points.
- **Pretraining.** An RGB-D version of DROID (35.2k demonstrations), rebuilt from existing 3D reconstructions and curated instructions.

## Results
- RoboCasa24 (50 demos per task): 78.8%, against 76.6% for SA-WAM and 67.1% for Cosmos Policy, with about 10x fewer trainable parameters.
- RoboTwin2 (50 tasks): 90.1% clean and 50.5% randomized, versus 81.5% / 41.8% for 4D-WAM and 70.7% / 46.0% for pi0.5. Over 90% on RLBench, 77.3% on DexJoCo with Allegro hands and 86% on DexSuite.
- Ablations on RoboCasa24: an auxiliary spatial anchor adds 9.2 points over none, and image-point fusion at every stage adds 5.8.
- Real UR5, 5 tasks with 50 demos each: ahead of pi0.5 on every task in both partial and full success; Prepare coffee is fully completed in 3 of 10 trials.

## Limitations
The policy needs calibrated RGB-D cameras, so noisy depth or calibration hurts it. Real-robot failures come from noisy, incomplete point clouds, no recovery after a failed sweep and poor object-orientation understanding. Pretraining used only a subset of DROID.
