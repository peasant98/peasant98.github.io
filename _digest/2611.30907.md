abstract: World models facilitate planning and control in robotic manipulation by enabling agents to anticipate the consequences of their actions. However, they typically rely on action-labelled interaction data that is costly to acquire and difficult to scale. These models are also often tied to the specific embodiment they were trained on, limiting transferability to previously unseen robot morphologies. We introduce a 3D latent-action world model that learns continuous action representations directly from point-cloud observations of retargeted human manipulation demonstrations. A contrastive objective is used to encourage consistent representations of motion across embodiments. The resulting unified latent action space serves directly as the search space for model-predictive control, where sampled latent actions condition particle-based future predictions. Executable robot commands are then recovered from the predicted geometric motion without requiring an embodiment-specific action decoder. Through extensive evaluations, we demonstrate that the learned latent actions exhibit controllable and compositional motion, transfer to unseen robot embodiments, and support zero-shot manipulation with unseen gripper geometries. Following task-agnostic adaptation, our model achieves 90%, 92%, and 60% success on reaching, grasping, and placing, respectively, compared with DexWM's reported 72%, 58%, and 28%. Finally, we demonstrate zero-shot real-world manipulation with an unseen dexterous hand, achieving an 86% success rate across 50 trials on five diverse tasks involving pushing, grasping, and lifting everyday objects. These results highlight the potential of geometry-grounded latent actions as a unified, transferable interface for world-model-based robotic manipulation. Project materials, including demonstration videos and source code, are available at https://vinaymohan0409.github.io/DexLatentWorld/.

## What they do
World models for manipulation usually need action-labelled data and stay tied to the robot they were trained on. DexLatentWorld learns actions without labels: it infers a compact latent action from each pair of 3D scene snapshots, trained on human hand-object demonstrations (H2O) retargeted to eight robot hands. Planning then happens directly in that latent space, and the robot's joint targets are fitted to the predicted hand motion, so a new hand needs no learned action decoder.

## How it works
- **Scene as particles.** Hands, object, table and distractors are sampled as 1,024 points each. A shared Point Transformer V3 encoder feeds both models.
- **Latent actions.** An inverse dynamics model turns two consecutive scenes (0.2 s apart) into a 32-dimensional latent. A contrastive loss pulls together latents of the same transition performed by different embodiments.
- **Forward dynamics.** Given the scene and five latents, a causal decoder predicts per-point displacements over 1 s in one pass, trained with a movement-weighted Huber loss.
- **Control.** The Cross-Entropy Method searches latent sequences against a geometric goal cost with receding-horizon replanning. Palm pose comes from Kabsch alignment of predicted hand points, finger joints from inverse kinematics.

## Results
- Correct latents give 7.94 mm hand and 13.59 mm object error, versus 53.17/43.43 mm with zeroed latents. A linear probe identifies the embodiment from latents only 26.6% of the time, versus 85.3% from encoder features.
- Unseen grippers in RoboSuite, no fine-tuning: 100% on cube stacking and milk transport, 80% push cube, 50% reach and push puck.
- RoboCasa, Franka with Allegro hand, after task-agnostic LoRA fine-tuning: 90/92/60% on reach/grasp/place versus DexWM's reported 72/58/28%, with 256 instead of 5,120 candidates per planning step.
- Real UR7e with an unseen Sharpa Wave hand, no fine-tuning: 86% over 50 trials on five push and grasp tasks (70% to 100% per task).

## Limitations
The model learns correlations rather than explicit interaction mechanics, retargeting introduces contact inconsistencies, and grasps are less stable without tactile or force feedback. Real-world goals are hand poses captured by manually posing the robot, which also requires consistent object placement.
