abstract: Reinforcement learning has made quadrupedal locomotion remarkably robust. To handle unmodeled dynamics and external disturbances, domain randomization is usually employed to make a policy hedge against every condition it might meet. However, maintaining accurate tracking across varying dynamics and generalizing beyond the sampled range remain challenging. Here we show that explicitly estimating the disturbance and feeding the resulting estimation back into both the policy and the controller can relax this compromise. We introduce Policy Augmentation and Compensation with Explicit disturbance estimation (PACE). The framework lumps payload variations, center-of-mass (CoM) shifts, actuator degradation, and external forces into one joint-space disturbance. A nonlinear observer subsequently recovers this disturbance online without requiring joint acceleration measurements. The disturbance estimation provides feedforward torque compensation as the foundational control pathway and further augments the policy observation to enable disturbance-aware action generation. Considering the learning difficulty of fast-varying contact effects, we further provide an analytical contact estimation approach from joint and inertial measurements, without demanding force sensors. Across eleven simulated dynamics conditions, PACE reduces velocity-tracking error by 18.1% to 57.0% against state-of-the-art baselines, and maintains improved tracking under out-of-distribution (OOD) commands. Notably, in real-world experiments, the deployed real robot carried an 11.5 kg payload, nearly equal to its own mass. It also negotiated unseen 16 cm step-downs and crossed five outdoor terrains. The policy was trained exclusively on flat ground in simulation with at most 3 kg of added mass.

## What they do
RL locomotion policies usually cope with payloads, shifted centers of mass and weak motors through domain randomization or a learned latent, so the policy must hedge across every condition and can only correct as fast as it runs. PACE adds an explicit estimate of the disturbance, in joint-torque units, to a teacher-student locomotion policy. The estimate is applied directly as feedforward torque and also given to the policy as an observation, and both paths stay in the loop during training and deployment.

## How it works
- **Disturbance observer.** Payload, CoM shift, actuator degradation, external forces and model mismatch are lumped into one 12-D joint-space disturbance. A nonlinear observer with a constant diagonal inertia matrix estimates it without joint accelerations, and its error is shown to be uniformly ultimately bounded.
- **Two pathways.** The estimate is added to the PD torque as feedforward compensation and appended to the policy and value inputs. PACE0, the ablation, keeps only the compensation.
- **Sensorless foot forces.** The observer needs contact forces, which the Lite3 cannot measure reliably. They are reconstructed from joint states, measured joint torques and the IMU through inverse dynamics, a damped SVD inverse and a friction-cone projection; in MuJoCo the vertical-force RMSE is 2.7 N.

## Results
- Simulation, eleven payload, CoM and motor-weakening conditions: average velocity-tracking error is 57.0% lower than BASELINE and 18.1% lower than HIMLOCO.
- After an 80 N lateral push, recovery takes 72 ms, against 204 ms for BASELINE and 300 ms for HIMLOCO.
- Real DeepRobotics Lite3, trained with at most 3 kg of added mass: it walks with 11.5 kg (robot mass 11.6 kg), against 7.5 kg for BASELINE, and succeeds 5/5 on 16 cm step-downs, where BASELINE fails every trial from 10 cm.
- Five unseen outdoor terrains, 5 trials each: 100% on flagstone, lawn, sand and gravel and 60% on large rocks; BASELINE gets 0-60%.

## Limitations
The observer uses one fixed scalar gain for all joints and conditions; the authors suggest adaptive or joint-dependent gains. Feeding the estimate to the policy did not help on large rocks, where PACE0 succeeded 80% and PACE 60%. Step-downs higher than 16 cm failed for all methods.
