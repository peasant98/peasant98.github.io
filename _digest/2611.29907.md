abstract: 'Underactuated grippers can form enveloping grasps through passive compliance, while the terminal pose and single width predicted by generic grasp generators do not fully capture the approach-closure process. Conventional collision filtering also struggles to distinguish harmful interference from acceptable contact. We present UnderGrasp, a post-hoc grasp refinement and evaluation framework for an underactuated gripper that reuses grasp candidates and quality priors from a frozen generator. The model jointly encodes the target, local environment, support surface, and approach-path geometry to propose diverse combinations of insertion depth, pre-grasp width, and target closing width for each candidate. These actions are ranked by combining separately predicted envelopment and interaction quality with upstream priors, balancing grasp feasibility, envelopment, and scene disturbance rather than rejecting all environmental contact. The back end is trained solely on configuration and interaction labels generated by gripper-specific simulations, without real-robot grasp labels. For trials involving target or environmental contact during approach, the conditional success rates increase from 0.36 to 0.89 and from 0.25 to 0.86, respectively, demonstrating more reliable execution under contact. ## What they do
Grasp generators such as AnyGrasp and GraspNet output a pose and one width for an ideal parallel-jaw gripper. An underactuated gripper can instead wrap its fingers around an object, which holds better, but only if the approach depth and closing command suit the object and its surroundings. UnderGrasp keeps the generator frozen and adds a back end that turns each candidate into a gripper-specific approach-and-close action. Instead of rejecting all contact, it scores each contact by its expected effect on the grasp.

## How it works
- **Inputs.** For each candidate, PointNet++ encodes four point clouds in the grasp frame: the target, the nearby environment, the support surface and the scene along the approach path.
- **Proposals.** A mixture density network proposes several actions per candidate, each an insertion offset along the approach axis, a pre-grasp width and a target closing width.
- **Evaluation.** A second network predicts how far each finger wraps, contacts and disturbance during approach, support force, capture probability and over-closing risk. These become envelopment and interaction scores, which are multiplied with the generator's calibrated score for ranking.
- **Supervision.** All labels come from 66,560 simulated MuJoCo grasps on 12 training objects, with no real-robot grasp labels.

## Results
Dobot CR5 with an OmniPicker gripper, 10 unseen objects, 60 trials per method:
- With AnyGrasp, success goes from 0.55 to 0.82 and the share of successful grasps that envelop the object from 0.12 to 0.55. With GraspNet: 0.42/0.08 to 0.65/0.36.
- Simply inserting 40 mm deeper raises the enveloping share to 0.35 but lowers success to 0.52.
- In trials with target or environment contact during approach, success is 0.89 and 0.86, vs 0.36 and 0.25 for AnyGrasp, even though UnderGrasp makes contact more often.

## Limitations
Enveloping poses that look good can still fail: closing can push a large, light object away before the fingers surround it, and some support contacts still cause failure. The authors attribute this to sim-to-real differences in mass, friction and contact. Training uses only 12 objects, and with 60 trials per method a single failure noticeably shifts the success rate.
