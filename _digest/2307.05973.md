abstract: Large language models (LLMs) are shown to possess a wealth of actionable knowledge that can be extracted for robot manipulation in the form of reasoning and planning. Despite the progress, most still rely on pre-defined motion primitives to carry out the physical interactions with the environment, which remains a major bottleneck. In this work, we aim to synthesize robot trajectories, i.e., a dense sequence of 6-DoF end-effector waypoints, for a large variety of manipulation tasks given an open-set of instructions and an open-set of objects. We achieve this by first observing that LLMs excel at inferring affordances and constraints given a free-form language instruction. More importantly, by leveraging their code-writing capabilities, they can interact with a vision-language model (VLM) to compose 3D value maps to ground the knowledge into the observation space of the agent. The composed value maps are then used in a model-based planning framework to zero-shot synthesize closed-loop robot trajectories with robustness to dynamic perturbations. We further demonstrate how the proposed framework can benefit from online experiences by efficiently learning a dynamics model for scenes that involve contact-rich interactions. We present a large-scale study of the proposed method in both simulated and real-robot environments, showcasing the ability to perform a large variety of everyday manipulation tasks specified in free-form natural language. ## What they do
LLM-based robot planners usually call a library of hand-made motion primitives. VoxPoser instead has GPT-4 write Python that composes 3D voxel value maps in the robot's observation space: where to go, what to avoid, how to rotate, how fast to move and when to close the gripper. These maps are the objective for a motion planner, so trajectories come out zero-shot, with no training of the LLM or the perception models.

## How it works
- **Perception.** For each object or part the LLM asks about, OWL-ViT finds a box, Segment Anything makes a mask and XMem tracks it; with RGB-D this gives a point cloud.
- **Value maps.** A planner prompt splits the instruction into sub-tasks. A composer prompt then calls one prompt per map type, each returning a 100x100x100 voxel grid built with NumPy and the perception API.
- **Planning.** Greedy search finds collision-free end-effector positions on a weighted sum of affordance and avoidance maps; the other maps set rotation, speed and gripper at each waypoint. The plan is redone at 5 Hz.
- **Online learning.** For contact-rich tasks, noisy copies of the zero-shot trajectories guide exploration while an MLP dynamics model is learned.

## Results
- Real Franka Panda, 5 tasks, 10 trials each: 88% success on static scenes and 70% with disturbances, against 24% and 0% for an LLM-plus-primitives baseline in the style of Code as Policies.
- Simulation, 13 tasks with 2766 instructions: on unseen instructions and attributes, 65.0% on object interaction and 76.7% on spatial composition, against 17.5% and 25.0% for LLM plus primitives and 0% for a learned U-Net costmap.
- Opening a door, window and fridge in simulation: zero-shot success is 3-18%. Using those trajectories as an exploration prior reaches 80-92% after under 3 minutes of online interaction; exploring without the prior runs past the 12-hour limit.

## Limitations
VoxPoser depends on external perception, which limits tasks that need holistic visual reasoning or fine object geometry. Most real-world errors came from the detector, which is sensitive to initial object poses and weaker on object parts. Contact-rich tasks still need a learned dynamics model, the planner handles only the end effector rather than the whole arm, and the prompts are engineered by hand.
