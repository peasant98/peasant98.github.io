abstract: This paper presents and experimentally demonstrates a novel framework for variable assistance on lower body exoskeletons, based upon safety-critical control methods. Existing work has shown that providing some freedom of movement around a nominal gait, instead of rigidly following it, accelerates the spinal learning process of people with a walking impediment when using a lower body exoskeleton. With this as motivation, we present a method to accurately control how much a subject is allowed to deviate from a given gait while ensuring robustness to patient perturbation. This method leverages control barrier functions to force certain joints to remain inside predefined trajectory tubes in a minimally invasive way. The effectiveness of the method is demonstrated experimentally with able-bodied subjects and the Atalante lower body exoskeleton.

## What they do
Crutch-less exoskeletons normally track a fixed gait rigidly, which is not ideal for patients who are recovering muscle function and should do part of the work themselves. This paper adds a variable assistance layer to the Atalante exoskeleton (12 actuated joints): the wearer controls the sagittal hip and knee of the swing leg, while a safety filter keeps those joints inside a tube, the virtual guide, around the nominal trajectory. An assistance factor sets the tube width, from 1 (the baseline rigid controller) to 0 (lowest assistance).

## How it works
- **Baseline walking.** Gaits generated with Partial Hybrid Zero Dynamics over a grid of patient and gait parameters are fitted by a neural network, which outputs joint trajectories tracked by PID. All non-assisted joints stay under this controller.
- **Transparency.** On the assisted joints, joint idealization compensates gravity and friction, and feedforward torques from gait generation partly compensate the exoskeleton's inertia.
- **Virtual guide filter.** Each assisted joint is treated independently. A control barrier function is evaluated online by integrating the joint dynamics under a backup PD law for the two extreme user torques, so the joint stays in the tube despite user perturbations and the filter acts only when needed.
- **Haptic feedback.** Eight vibration motors on the thighs and shanks vibrate harder as a joint approaches the guide boundary.

## Results
- On the empty exoskeleton, joint angles stayed inside the bounds over 30 steps for every tube shape tested.
- Eight able-bodied subjects (58-91 kg, 1.62-1.93 m) walked five-minute trials at full assistance and at an assistance factor of 0.5 (a tube of plus or minus 4 degrees). Trajectories always stayed within the guides. Metabolic rate rose only when subjects actively tracked the gait under partial assistance; active subjects at full assistance spent about as much as passive ones.
- One subject swept assistance factors 1.0, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0.0 and back on three consecutive days: metabolic rate and tracking error both increased as assistance decreased, symmetrically around 0.0, so the increase is not due to exhaustion.

## Limitations
Tested only with able-bodied subjects, not in clinical rehabilitation. At low assistance factors an operator standing behind the exoskeleton had to help keep it stable. Subjects found the gaits hard to track because they are not very anthropomorphic, and the haptic feedback was sometimes hard to interpret.
