abstract: We present Human to Humanoid (H2O), a reinforcement learning (RL) based framework that enables real-time whole-body teleoperation of a full-sized humanoid robot with only an RGB camera. To create a large-scale retargeted motion dataset of human movements for humanoid robots, we propose a scalable "sim-to-data" process to filter and pick feasible motions using a privileged motion imitator. Afterwards, we train a robust real-time humanoid motion imitator in simulation using these refined motions and transfer it to the real humanoid robot in a zero-shot manner. We successfully achieve teleoperation of dynamic whole-body motions in real-world scenarios, including walking, back jumping, kicking, turning, waving, pushing, boxing, etc. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first demonstration to achieve learning-based real-time whole-body humanoid teleoperation.

## What they do
H2O teleoperates a full-sized Unitree H1 humanoid, whole body including the legs, in real time from a single RGB webcam. No exoskeleton, markers or force sensors on the operator. The main obstacle they identify is data: many human motions are infeasible for the robot, so they clean a large mocap dataset in simulation before training a sim-to-real RL tracking policy on it.

## How it works
- **Retargeting.** Fit an SMPL body shape to the H1 using 12 corresponding joints, then retarget AMASS motions by matching those joints; 13k sequences become 10k.
- **Sim-to-data.** Train a privileged imitator (full rigid-body state, no domain randomization) on all 10k and discard what it fails to track, leaving about 8.5k feasible sequences.
- **Teleoperation policy.** PPO policy outputs 19 joint targets for a PD controller. Its goal input is 8 keypoints (shoulders, elbows, hands, ankles), their offset from the robot's own keypoints, and their velocities. Trained with domain randomization (friction, mass, CoM, PD gains, 20-60 ms delay, pushes, rough terrain).
- **Deployment.** HybrIK estimates the human pose at 30 Hz from a 1080p webcam. Robot linear velocity comes from motion capture.

## Results
- On the 10k retargeted motions in simulation, H2O succeeds on 72.5%, versus 67.9% without sim-to-data and 53.2% with a goal of keypoint positions only. The privileged policy, which cannot transfer to the real robot, reaches 85.5%.
- Global MPJPE is 166.7 mm for H2O versus 176.6 mm without sim-to-data. Training on 0.1% of the clean data still gives 52.0% success.
- On the real robot: walking, back jumping, ball kicking, catching and binning a box, stroller pushing and boxing, and it keeps its balance when kicked.

## Limitations
The real system needs motion capture for the robot's linear velocity. RGB pose estimation adds latency and error, and the operator gets only visual feedback. There is no systematic way to decide which motions are feasible for a given robot, and richer goal representations cost RL sample efficiency.
