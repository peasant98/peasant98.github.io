abstract: Tactile information plays a critical role in human dexterity. It reveals useful contact information that may not be inferred directly from vision. In fact, humans can even perform in-hand dexterous manipulation without using vision. Can we enable the same ability for the multi-finger robot hand? In this paper, we present Touch Dexterity, a new system that can perform in-hand object rotation using only touching without seeing the object. Instead of relying on precise tactile sensing in a small region, we introduce a new system design using dense binary force sensors (touch or no touch) overlaying one side of the whole robot hand (palm, finger links, fingertips). Such a design is low-cost, giving a larger coverage of the object, and minimizing the Sim2Real gap at the same time. We train an in-hand rotation policy using Reinforcement Learning on diverse objects in simulation. Relying on touch-only sensing, we can directly deploy the policy in a real robot hand and rotate novel objects that are not presented in training. Extensive ablations are performed on how tactile information help in-hand manipulation.Our project is available at https://touchdexterity.github.io.

## What they do
Touch Dexterity rotates an object in the palm of a multi-finger hand without vision. Instead of a few high-resolution fingertip sensors, it covers the palm, finger links and fingertips of an Allegro hand with 16 force-sensing resistors (about $12 each), each read as touch/no-touch. An RL policy trained in IsaacGym transfers directly to the real hand.

## How it works
- **Binary touch.** Real readings are thresholded; in simulation each sensor is a fixed link whose net contact force is thresholded at 0.01 N. This avoids matching noisy, nonlinear force values between sim and real; calibration is just picking the threshold.
- **Policy.** PPO with an MLP. Input: joint positions, the 16 touch bits, the previous joint target and the rotation axis, stacked over 4 steps. Output: relative joint targets, smoothed by an exponential moving average and tracked by a PD controller at 10 Hz. Only the value network sees contact forces, object pose and physics parameters.
- **Reward and sim2real.** Rotation is a finite-difference angle around the axis (simulator angular velocity was too noisy), plus penalties on velocity, falling, work and torque. Mass, friction, PD gains and sensor dropout and delay are randomized; PD gains are matched by system identification.

## Results
- In simulation, multi-object training fails completely without touch or with low-sensitivity (0.2 N) sensors. Disabling touch at test time drops cumulative rotation reward from 976 to 352 on seen objects and from 594 to 187 on unseen ones.
- Real hand, 30 s trials, 3 seeds: the cuboid turns 4.91 rounds and never falls (no-sensor policy: 0.25). Unseen objects: apple 2.67, orange 3.00, soup can 4.25, rubber duck 1.42 rounds. Continuous-force input does worse and varies widely across objects.
- Removing the fingertip or the palm sensors cuts cuboid rotation to 0.17 or 0.42 rounds.
- From policy rollouts, a shape predictor reconstructs unseen column-shaped objects with MSE 0.22 using touch, 0.45 without.

## Limitations
The task is rotation about a fixed axis in the palm, a simplified form of in-hand reorientation. Rotation about x and y is weaker (1.68 and 1.88 rounds on seen objects, versus 3.43 about z); the authors attribute this to contacts at the sides of the finger links, which the sensor layout does not cover.
