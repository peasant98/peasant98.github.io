## What they do
They build one embedded pipeline for a pick-and-insert task on a Franka Research 3. A wrist RealSense D435i and a Jetson AGX Orin turn a segmented object or hole into a metric target in the robot base frame. On top of that shared front end they compare two ways to move: small PPO reaching policies trained in simulation and run with fixed weights, and a scripted Cartesian sequence that uses contact force to find the hole.

## How it works
- **Perception.** YOLO11-seg (TensorRT, trained on about 100 labeled images of four shapes) gives a mask. Depth at the mask centroid is back-projected and moved into the base frame with Tsai-Lenz eye-in-hand calibration, and the target updates as the wrist camera moves.
- **Learned hybrid.** Two PPO policies trained in RLBench for 10M steps each: Policy A reaches the object, Policy B the hole region. Each sees a 15-number state (joints, end-effector position, class, target, one orientation term) and outputs joint increments. The actor-critic pair has 10,895 parameters. Grasp, lift and release are scripted.
- **Force-guided analytical.** Scripted approach, grasp and transport, then a descent to about 2 N normal contact and a planar spiral search. A drop in height while force relaxes signals entry and triggers release.
- Both branches run through the same Cartesian impedance controller.

## Results
- Simulation, 1000 episodes each: Policy A 87.2% success (5.2% collisions), Policy B 83.1% (no collisions, 16.9% timeouts).
- Real robot, 50 trials each: learned hybrid 35/50 (70.0%), force-guided 37/50 (74.0%). The 95% confidence intervals overlap (56.2-80.9% vs 60.4-84.1%).
- Segmentation macro mAP50 is 0.953. Perception takes 34.55 ms per frame on the Jetson (about 28.9 FPS), with postprocessing the largest share (15.3 ms).

## Limitations
The physical study uses one square object roughly aligned with its hole and reports only end-to-end completion, not per-stage failures. Policy B never sees force or insertion state. The authors name gripper occlusion, detector false positives that make the target jump, and depth or calibration error as failure mechanisms, but did not measure how often each occurs.
