## What they do
Domain randomization needs hand-tuned parameter ranges, and ranges that are too wide include infeasible scenes or yield overly conservative policies. SimOpt starts from a conservative distribution over simulation parameters and adapts it with a few real-world roll-outs, until the trained policy behaves the same in simulation and on the real robot. It does not try to replicate the real scene exactly, and rewards are computed only in simulation.

## How it works
- **Loop.** Train a policy with PPO on the current parameter distribution in NVIDIA Flex, run it a few times on the real robot, update the distribution, and repeat. Policy weights carry over between iterations.
- **Discrepancy.** Weighted L1 and L2 distances between simulated and real observation trajectories (joint angles plus an object position tracked with DART), with Gaussian smoothing for misalignment. Only partial real-world observations are needed.
- **Update.** The parameters follow a full-covariance Gaussian. The simulator is not differentiable, so a sampling-based method built on relative entropy policy search updates it under a KL-divergence bound, which keeps the new distribution close to what the current policy was trained on.
- **Scale.** 64 GPUs with 150 simulated agents each.

## Results
- Simulated drawer opening: randomizing the cabinet position with a 2 cm standard deviation gives a policy that opens the drawer; with 10 cm it reaches the handle but does not open it. SimOpt transferred to a cabinet offset by 15 cm in 3 RL+SimOpt iterations, and by 22 cm in 5.
- Swing-peg-in-hole (ABB Yumi, peg on a soft rope): each iteration is about 7 min of RL, 3 real roll-outs and 3 updates of 9600 samples. After two iterations: 90% success over 20 trials. The largest changes were in rope parameters and action scaling.
- Drawer opening (Franka Panda): before the update, one finger pushes the handle until the gripper opens; after one update, the drawer opens in all 20 trials.

## Limitations
The parameter distribution is a single Gaussian; multi-modal distributions are left to future work. Real-world tracking with DART needs depth images and 3D articulated object models. Policies observe only joint angles and object positions, with no vision or touch. On the real robots, results are only compared before and after SimOpt updates; the comparison to standard domain randomization is done in simulation.
