tldr: A flying wing with no control surfaces, steered only by an internal sliding mass, a momentum wheel and thrust, is proven small-time locally accessible with a Lie-bracket analysis. In simulation, a nonlinear MPC tracks a figure-8 with 65 m lobes at 0.99 m cross-track error.
abstract: This paper presents a rigorous geometric accessibility and controllability analysis for a fixed-wing Uncrewed Aircraft System (UAS) actuated solely by an internal moving mass, a momentum wheel, and external thrust. This configuration eliminates traditional control surfaces, which in turn reduces the combinatorial set of computational fluid dynamics (CFD) studies necessary for high-fidelity modeling of flight through unsteady flow fields. Using Lie bracket computations and the strong Lie Algebra Rank Condition, it is shown that the full nonlinear dynamics are generically small-time locally accessible, with singular configurations explicitly characterized and shown to be immediately escapable by direct mass actuation. The stronger property of small-time local controllability is shown to hold for the attitude subsystem at equilibrium points, using Sussmann's condition. A key contribution is the explicit treatment of the time-varying inertia tensor via the Huygens-Steiner Theorem, which introduces nontrivial coupling between the control vector fields. The momentum wheel provides direct yaw authority that, combined with inertia variation from the moving mass, generates a full-rank accessibility Lie algebra. A nonlinear Model Predictive Controller (MPC) is derived with consideration of flight envelope constraints and is applied to track different trajectories under unperturbed and perturbed flow field conditions.

## What they do
Control surfaces (ailerons, elevators, rudders) multiply the CFD runs needed to build a data-driven aerodynamic model, since every surface setting has to be swept. The authors propose a flying wing with a symmetric airfoil and no control surfaces. An internal mass slides along chord and span to shift the center of gravity (pitch and roll), a momentum wheel supplies yaw torque, and a propeller supplies thrust. They prove which states this underactuated design can reach, then test a nonlinear MPC on it in simulation.

## How it works
- **Dynamics.** A 15-state model with the mass velocities, wheel torque and thrust as inputs. The inertia tensor changes as the mass moves and is handled with the Huygens-Steiner (parallel axis) theorem; aerodynamics come from lifting-line theory.
- **Accessibility.** Lie brackets show the reduced 12-state system meets the Lie Algebra Rank Condition, so it is small-time locally accessible. The one realistic exception, level trim with zero roll and pitch rate and a centered mass, is escaped as soon as the mass moves.
- **Controllability.** The full system is not small-time locally controllable (bounded inputs cannot reverse forward velocity), but the attitude subsystem is, at every trim equilibrium, by Sussmann's condition.
- **Control.** NMPC (30 steps of 0.1 s, solved with MATLAB fmincon) under flight-envelope limits derived from stall speed, bank angle and maximum thrust.

## Results
Simulated wing with 1.2 m span:
- Figure-8 at 10 m/s (2.0 kg, 0.4 kg moving mass): 0.99 m cross-track RMSE without wind (1.5% of the lobe width), 1.52-2.38 m under four constant winds of at least 1.5 m/s.
- Level-climb-level at 15 m/s (3.0 kg, 0.5 kg moving mass): 0.17 m RMSE undisturbed, 1.06-1.26 m averaged over three random-gust scenarios of 10 runs each, and 1.04 m (SD 0.47 m) over 25 Dryden-turbulence runs.
- In turns, spanwise mass motion does most of the roll work; the momentum wheel is used sparingly.

## Limitations
All results are simulations with a nominal lifting-line model; the paper reports no physical prototype. The largest wheel that fits in the 0.3 m chord cannot supply the yaw moment the controller requests, so Euler-angle tracking deviates. The solver fails more often once peak turbulence exceeds 20% of airspeed.
