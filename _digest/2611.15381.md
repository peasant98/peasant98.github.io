abstract: 'Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown promising capabilities for robot manipulation, but their performance on accessible mobile bimanual platforms remains underexplored. We present a low-cost and reproducible mobile bimanual platform integrating two LeRobot SO-101 manipulators with a TurtleBot 4 and a LeRobot-ROS 2 software stack. Using long-horizon toilet cleaning as a case study, we collect 250 real-world demonstrations and evaluate $π_0$, $π_{0.5}$, SmolVLA, and GR00T under the same embodiment and task conditions. We further evaluate variants using SARM-derived task-progress signals and short-term MEM observation history. Among the evaluated models, $π_0$ performs best, achieving 60% strict and 70% relaxed autonomous success, increasing to 90% with limited human intervention. In contrast, the evaluated progress- and memory-augmented variants do not improve their corresponding baselines. Our results demonstrate the feasibility of long-horizon mobile bimanual manipulation on accessible hardware while highlighting remaining challenges in reliable VLA deployment. ## What they do
They build a low-cost, reproducible mobile bimanual robot and use it to compare open vision-language-action (VLA) models on the same robot, data and task. The task is long-horizon toilet cleaning in five stages: grab a sponge and drive to the stain, scrub with the rough side, flip the sponge with both arms, wipe with the soft side, then put the sponge away and return.

## How it works
- **Hardware.** Two 5-DoF SO-101 arms sit on a 3D-printed triangular base on a redesigned TurtleBot 4 top cover, with a mast-mounted top camera and a wrist camera on each arm. The platform costs about €3200, of which the arm extension is about €500.
- **Software.** A LeRobot–ROS 2 stack runs policy inference on a workstation, while an onboard Raspberry Pi 4 handles low-level control. Two operators teleoperate, one with leader arms and one driving the base by keyboard.
- **Data and policies.** 250 demonstrations at 30 Hz, 200 teleoperated and 50 DAgger-style corrections. They fine-tune π0, π0.5, SmolVLA and GR00T N1.7, plus π0 with SARM task-progress inputs and π0.5 with short-term MEM memory.

## Results
Each policy gets 20 rollouts (GR00T 5), with toilet and table orientation, sponge position and stain randomized:
- π0: 60% strict, 70% relaxed, and 90% with at most one human repositioning. It mostly failed by getting stuck under the toilet seat or not moving on to the next subtask.
- π0 with SARM: 40% / 55% / 65%, worse than π0. π0.5 reached 25% strict success; π0.5 with MEM, 10%.
- SmolVLA and GR00T: 0%. In most rollouts SmolVLA never reached the sponge; GR00T could not pick it up.

## Limitations
One task in one setting, with lighting, background, toilet and tools held fixed. The 5-DoF arms limit reach and dexterity, and the robot relies on external power and an off-board workstation. The released π0.5 includes only the flow-matching action head, and why the augmented variants did worse was not isolated by ablations.
