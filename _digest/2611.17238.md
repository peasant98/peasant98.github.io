abstract: For robots to assist, serve, and collaborate with humans, they must not only interpret human requests conveyed through language and multimodal intentional cues, but also execute long-horizon tasks that interleave navigation and manipulation. In this paper, we introduce HEIR, a resource for studying how to Harness Egocentric Intent for Human-Robot Interaction. HEIR pairs human speech, measured gaze, and egocentric video with robot observations and teleoperated navigation-manipulation trajectories, while each session preserves successive episodes, with each request fulfilled through multiple execution subtasks. The captured data, together with human-authored annotations, support the study of intent grounding, execution planning, and task completion within a unified interaction setting. To demonstrate the value of HEIR, we conduct extensive offline experiments to evaluate how models benefit from additional human-centric context across these tasks, together with real-robot experiments that assess execution task completion. HEIR thus provides a valuable resource for studying user-centric robot interaction in naturalistic household environments. Our demo and dataset are available online.

## What they do
A home robot has to work out what a person means ("bring me that thing") and then carry it out over several navigation and manipulation steps. HEIR is a dataset for this. A participant wearing eye-tracking glasses makes spoken requests while an operator teleoperates a Galaxea R1 Lite mobile dual-arm robot to fulfil them. The authors benchmark intent inference, subtask planning, navigation and manipulation on it, offline and on the real robot.

## How it works
- **Recording.** Egocentric video, gaze and speech are synchronized with four robot camera streams and arm, gripper, torso and base states and actions. Requests follow one another in a changing scene, so later ones can refer to earlier ones.
- **Annotation.** Humans label each request's intent (task, objects, goal, nominal plan), whether the target was visible to the robot, and the subtask boundaries. GPT-5.6 adds subgoals and completion conditions, and humans review every episode.
- **Scale.** 17 participants, 300 sessions, 38.56 h, 1,836 requests and 6,416 subtasks, with 60 sessions held out for testing.
- **Deployed system.** A VLM intent reasoner, a planner choosing Continue, Switch or Done, a VLN model for navigation and a VLA for manipulation.

## Results
- Intent inference with Gemma-4-31B: object-location accuracy is 20.5% from text alone, 54.9% with the robot view, 71.1% with the human view added and 77.7% with gaze. Plan exact match rises from 30.3% to 78.1%.
- Fine-tuning on HEIR raises planner decision accuracy by 25-37 points and InternVLA-N1's navigation action accuracy from 49.1% to 90.7%.
- On 20 real pick-and-place tasks (3 trials each), Lingbot-VLA2, FastWAM and π0.5 succeed 68.3%, 60.0% and 36.7% of the time, matching their offline ranking.
- The full system completes 81.25% of 32 navigation subtasks but only 18.52% of 27 end-to-end requests, which the authors attribute to errors accumulating across components.

## Limitations
All sessions come from one staged room, so the test split varies furniture and object placement, not the environment. The real-robot benefit of gaze is shown only in a few qualitative cases, and some planning metrics rely on LLM judges.
