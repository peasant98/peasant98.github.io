## What they do
VLAs that take thermal images usually treat them as one more camera, which helps little when an instruction depends on temperature ("the coldest cup", "the one with no heat signature"). Thermo-VLA adds a ThermoRes Encoder to pi_0.5 that turns the thermal image into cold and hot attribute tokens and injects them into the head-camera RGB tokens. It is evaluated on six real tasks on a dual-arm Unitree G1, trained on 1,307 demonstrations.

## How it works
- **Cold/hot decomposition.** The thermal image is calibrated to a background reference estimated from patches near the top of the frame, then split into two views that emphasise responses below and above the background. A shared SigLIP-based encoder and the RGB projector turn both into tokens with the RGB token layout.
- **Gated residual injection.** Text tokens cross-attend to the cold and hot tokens, and a small gate MLP predicts one weight per branch. The weighted thermal tokens are added to the RGB tokens as residuals, so the RGB path stays an identity path.
- **Action generation.** The modulated head tokens, wrist-camera tokens and text form the prefix for the Gemma backbone and the flow-matching action expert, unchanged from the base model.

## Results
- Four temperature-dependent tasks, 20 trials per instruction: average success 0.86, 18 points above pi_0.5 with RGB plus thermal input. On the task of finding the target with an abnormal thermal response it succeeds in every trial, while the other RGB-thermal methods are around 0.5.
- Generalisation: 0.79 on unseen objects (0.64 for pi_0.5 RGBT, 0.38 for ThermoAct), 0.76 under red illumination and 0.79 on unseen tablecloths.
- Forcing the thermal residual to zero drops temperature-dependent tasks to about 0.5: the robot still reaches and grasps but picks targets at chance.
- The same residual design on colour channels raises pi_0.5 from 0.572 to 0.775 average success on colour-conditioned tasks in LIBERO and on the real robot.

## Limitations
On the two RGB-only tasks Thermo-VLA is slightly below plain pi_0.5; the authors plan to pretrain the thermal encoder on more thermal data. Relative temperature ranking stays hard (0.58 on the coffee task), since the cold/hot split carries no temperature magnitude, and reassigning temperatures to seen objects drops success to 0.60.
