abstract: A key technical challenge in performing 6D object pose estimation from RGB-D image is to fully leverage the two complementary data sources. Prior works either extract information from the RGB image and depth separately or use costly post-processing steps, limiting their performances in highly cluttered scenes and real-time applications. In this work, we present DenseFusion, a generic framework for estimating 6D pose of a set of known objects from RGB-D images. DenseFusion is a heterogeneous architecture that processes the two data sources individually and uses a novel dense fusion network to extract pixel-wise dense feature embedding, from which the pose is estimated. Furthermore, we integrate an end-to-end iterative pose refinement procedure that further improves the pose estimation while achieving near real-time inference. Our experiments show that our method outperforms state-of-the-art approaches in two datasets, YCB-Video and LineMOD. We also deploy our proposed method to a real robot to grasp and manipulate objects based on the estimated pose.

## What they do
RGB-D pose estimators either process color and depth separately or rely on slow post-processing such as ICP. DenseFusion encodes the image with a CNN and the depth points with a PointNet, fuses them pixel by pixel, and lets each pixel predict a pose, so occluded objects can be located from their visible part. A learned refinement module replaces ICP.

## How it works
- **Segmentation.** PoseCNN's segmentation network masks each known object; the masked depth becomes a point cloud and the mask's box crops the image.
- **Dense fusion.** A ResNet-18 encoder-decoder gives a 128-d color feature per pixel; a PointNet variant with average pooling gives a 128-d feature per point. Each point is paired with the pixel it projects to, and a pooled global feature is appended to every pair.
- **Per-pixel voting.** Each fused feature predicts a pose and a confidence. The loss is the confidence-weighted distance between model points under predicted and true pose (closest-point distance for symmetric objects), plus a term penalizing low confidence. The most confident pose is the output.
- **Refinement.** The point cloud is moved into the frame of the current estimate, re-fused with the same image features, and a 4-layer network predicts a residual pose. Two iterations, trained after the main network converges.

## Results
- YCB-Video: ADD-S AUC 93.1 and 96.8% under 2 cm, against 93.0 and 93.2% for PoseCNN+ICP and 83.9 and 74.1% for PointFusion. Refinement lifts the bowl from 69.5% to 98.8% under 2 cm.
- As occlusion increases, PointFusion and PoseCNN+ICP degrade sharply while the per-pixel and iterative variants drop only 2%.
- 0.06 s per frame versus 10.6 s for PoseCNN+ICP; 16 FPS with about 5 objects per frame.
- LineMOD ADD: 94.3 (86.2 without refinement) versus 79 for SSD-6D+ICP.
- On a Toyota HSR with a different camera and no finetuning, 73% of 60 grasps on 5 YCB objects succeed; the banana only 7/12.

## Limitations
It needs a 3D model of every object and relies on PoseCNN's segmentation as its first stage. The large and extra-large clamps stay the weakest objects (79.2% and 76.3% under 2 cm). In the grasping test objects were never stacked, and the authors attribute the banana failures to their plain-yellow banana differing from the dataset model.
