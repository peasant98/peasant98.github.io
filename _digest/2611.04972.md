tldr: A safety layer sits between a VLM/VLA navigation planner and a legged robot. It turns the model's actions into goal poses and certifies every command with a sampling-based MPPI planner under barrier constraints on a live signed distance field. On long missions with a Unitree G1, collisions dropped from 60 of 60 runs to 0 of 60 across four language front ends.
abstract: By harnessing the inherent commonsense capabilities of pretrained Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), current Vision-Language Action(VLA) models for legged robots excel at interpreting language-based navigation commands but lack the physical grounding and explicit safety assurance required for real-world operation. Therefore, this paper aims to cage Legged-Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) through a safety architecture that provides physical grounding and ensures navigation safety. The key idea is not to abandon the powerful capabilities of end-to-end VLA planning, but rather to augment and cage them. In particular, we integrate a high-level VLA planner with a low-level physical-grounding layer that leverages efficient state estimation, in-situ 3D spatial perception, and an Signed Distance Field (SDF) representation to generate executable trajectories. We provide a navigation planner with provable collision-avoidance guarantees under the stated assumptions by combining a low-dimensional Bezier-parameterized MPPI planner with Discrete Exponential Barrier Function (dEBF) constraints. The proposed Legged-VLN system is modular, making it compatible with various VLM/VLA models and agnostic to the robot platform. The proposed system is validated with extensive real-world experiments on humanoid and quadrupedal robots, demonstrating versatile, efficient, and safe vision-language driven navigation capabilities.

## What they do
Vision-language models can turn spoken instructions into navigation commands for legged robots, but those commands ignore the robot's footprint and the obstacles around it. This paper "cages" the model: its outputs are treated only as proposals, and a geometric planning layer checks every motion for collisions before the robot executes it. The same layer runs on a Unitree G1 humanoid and a Ghost Robotics Vision 60 quadruped.

## How it works
- **Semantic layer.** A VLM picks discrete actions (turns of up to 45°, steps of up to 75 cm). A goal projector moves each resulting waypoint to a free spot with at least 0.3 m clearance. An optional Grounding-DINO + SAM2 stage then approaches a named object.
- **Physical layer.** An incrementally updated signed distance field (SDF) of the surroundings. The robot is modeled as a few spheres, and a Bézier-parameterized MPPI planner samples 200 trajectories over a 2.5 s horizon.
- **Certification.** Discrete exponential barrier constraints limit how fast clearance may shrink between steps. Only a rollout that satisfies them is executed, otherwise the robot stops. Under the stated assumptions this provably keeps it collision-free.

## Results
- Controlled G1 ablations across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and NaVILA: collision rate falls from 80% to 0% without a global planner and from 100% to 0% with one. End-to-end success rises from 15% to 40% and from 0% to 70%.
- G1, 75 trials per configuration: collision rate falls from 0.33 to 0 on simple tasks and from 0.50 to 0 on complex ones, with success unchanged at 0.75. On the Vision 60 it falls from 0.67 to 0.20 and from 0.91 to 0.08.
- Long G1 missions: 60/60 uncaged runs collide, 0/60 caged.
- Object grounding cuts Vision 60 endpoint error from 1.50 m to 0.29 m.

## Limitations
The guarantee only covers geometry already in the map. Thin wires, table edges and LiDAR blind spots caused the remaining Vision 60 collisions. The model ignores state-estimation error, latency and tracking error, and only static obstacles are handled. The cage cannot fix wrong semantic goals.
