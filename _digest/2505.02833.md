abstract: 'Teleoperating humanoid robots in a whole-body manner marks a fundamental step toward developing general-purpose robotic intelligence, with human motion providing an ideal interface for controlling all degrees of freedom. Yet, most current humanoid teleoperation systems fall short of enabling coordinated whole-body behavior, typically limiting themselves to isolated locomotion or manipulation tasks. We present the Teleoperated Whole-Body Imitation System (TWIST), a system for humanoid teleoperation through whole-body motion imitation. We first generate reference motion clips by retargeting human motion capture data to the humanoid robot. We then develop a robust, adaptive, and responsive whole-body controller using a combination of reinforcement learning and behavior cloning (RL+BC). Through systematic analysis, we demonstrate how incorporating privileged future motion frames and real-world motion capture (MoCap) data improves tracking accuracy. TWIST enables real-world humanoid robots to achieve unprecedented, versatile, and coordinated whole-body motor skills--spanning whole-body manipulation, legged manipulation, locomotion, and expressive movement--using a single unified neural network controller. ## What they do
TWIST teleoperates a humanoid by having it imitate the operator's whole-body motion in real time. OptiTrack MoCap captures the human, an online retargeter converts the pose into robot joint positions and root velocities, and a single controller tracks them while keeping balance. It runs on a 29-DoF, 1.3 m Unitree G1.

## How it works
- **Data.** Over 15,000 AMASS and OMOMO clips (~42 h) retargeted offline, plus 150 in-house MoCap clips (~0.5 h) retargeted online, so training sees real teleoperation noise. The online retargeter jointly optimizes 3D joint positions and orientations.
- **Teacher.** PPO policy that sees 2 s of future reference frames and tracks targets in the robot's local frame, with penalties for jitter and foot slip.
- **Student.** Sees only proprioception and the current frame; trained with PPO plus a decaying KL term toward the teacher (RL+BC). Large end-effector perturbations in training teach it to exert force.
- **Deployment.** Zero-shot sim-to-real: MoCap at 120 Hz, retargeting and policy at 50 Hz, PD control at 1000 Hz.

## Results
- One controller handles uprighting a trash can, lifting a box from the floor, kicking a door shut while carrying objects, kicking a soccer ball, crouching under obstacles, boxing and waltzing.
- On 50 unseen MoCap clips, RL+BC beats pure RL on all six tracking metrics (joint position 0.083 vs 0.090 rad). Feet error is 23.4 mm, vs 26.5 for RL, 33.7 without in-house MoCap data and 35.1 for DAgger.
- In-house clips cut summed tracking error from 2.09 to 1.52 with the improved retargeter (2.36 to 2.09 with the original).
- Total delay is about 0.9 s: 0.7 s to generate tracking goals, 0.2 s for the policy.

## Limitations
No egocentric video or touch feedback reaches the operator, so occlusions and grasp success are hard to judge. The G1's motors overheat after 5-10 minutes, especially when crouching, causing most failures. The MoCap setup is not portable.
