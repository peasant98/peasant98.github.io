abstract: 'Learning to predict the effects of contact-rich manipulation skills requires models that represent distinct outcome modes rather than a single expected result. Even for basic skills such as pushing, picking, and placing, small variations in contact conditions or object geometry can produce qualitatively different object motions under nearly identical states and actions. Deterministic and Gaussian models tend to average over these alternatives or represent them as a single broad distribution, obscuring distinct modes such as sliding, toppling, and rotating. We study probabilistic skill-effect models that learn multimodal distributions over object pose changes given partial point clouds and actions. We instantiate this abstraction with three representative model classes: mixture density networks, state tokenization, and flow matching, each paired with latent dynamics for next-step prediction. Across diverse objects and skills, explicitly multimodal models predict which outcome occurs more accurately than deterministic and Gaussian baselines. In planning, flow matching significantly improves task success over the deterministic baseline on every skill and achieves the highest success on every multi-step sequence. On a real robot, probabilistic models succeed at pick about twice as often as the deterministic baseline, providing evidence that probabilistic skill effects offer a practical abstraction for reasoning and planning under uncertainty.'

## What they do
A skill-effect model predicts where an object ends up after a whole push, pick or place, so a planner can chain skills. Near a contact boundary the same action can slide, topple or miss the object. A deterministic model averages those outcomes into one pose, often one floating above or sinking into the table. This paper predicts a multimodal distribution over the object's SE(3) pose change from partial point clouds and compares three ways to output it against deterministic and Gaussian baselines.

## How it works
- **Model.** Point Transformer v3 encodes the object and table point clouds. A transformer latent dynamics model adds the skill's start and end gripper poses and predicts a latent change. There is one model per skill.
- **Output heads.** A five-component mixture density network (MDN), state tokenization (512 translation and 512 rotation tokens) or flow matching on SE(3).
- **Data.** Push, pick and place on 94 objects (44 YCB, 50 primitive shapes) in Isaac Sim. Perturbed poses and grasps supply failed executions. Each skill has 49k-104k training episodes.
- **Planning.** The cross-entropy method searches skill parameters and scores each candidate by its expected goal cost over sampled outcomes.

## Results
- All three multimodal heads predict which outcome occurs (Brier score) significantly better than both baselines on every skill.
- In simulated single-step planning, flow matching is the only model that significantly beats the deterministic one on every skill, unseen objects included. Over multi-step sequences it has the highest success on all four plans.
- On the real robot (5 objects, 15 tasks per skill), the deterministic model predicted success on 12 of its 15 picks but succeeded on 7. Every probabilistic model picked about twice as often.

## Limitations
Real-robot push and place success was low for every model. Multi-step plans run open-loop, and friction and mass are fixed in simulation. With many candidates, flow matching and tokenization plan an order of magnitude slower than the MDN.
