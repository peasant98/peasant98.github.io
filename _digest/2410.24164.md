tldr: π0 adds a flow-matching action expert to the PaliGemma vision-language model and pre-trains it on about 10,000 hours of data from 7 robot configurations plus open datasets, giving one 3.3B-parameter policy that controls robots at up to 50 Hz. Without fine-tuning it beats OpenVLA and Octo on every tested task, and after fine-tuning it folds laundry from a crumpled bin and assembles boxes.
abstract: Robot learning holds tremendous promise to unlock the full potential of flexible, general, and dexterous robot systems, as well as to address some of the deepest questions in artificial intelligence. However, bringing robot learning to the level of generality required for effective real-world systems faces major obstacles in terms of data, generalization, and robustness. In this paper, we discuss how generalist robot policies (i.e., robot foundation models) can address these challenges, and how we can design effective generalist robot policies for complex and highly dexterous tasks. We propose a novel flow matching architecture built on top of a pre-trained vision-language model (VLM) to inherit Internet-scale semantic knowledge. We then discuss how this model can be trained on a large and diverse dataset from multiple dexterous robot platforms, including single-arm robots, dual-arm robots, and mobile manipulators. We evaluate our model in terms of its ability to perform tasks in zero shot after pre-training, follow language instructions from people and from a high-level VLM policy, and its ability to acquire new skills via fine-tuning. Our results cover a wide variety of tasks, such as laundry folding, table cleaning, and assembling boxes.

## What they do
π0 is a generalist vision-language-action (VLA) model built on the 3B-parameter PaliGemma VLM that outputs continuous actions with flow matching instead of discrete action tokens. One model is pre-trained on about 10,000 hours of the authors' dexterous data (7 robot configurations, 68 tasks) plus OXE, Bridge v2 and DROID. It is then prompted directly or fine-tuned on curated data for long tasks such as laundry folding and box assembly.

## How it works
- **Action expert.** A separate 300M-parameter set of transformer weights handles robot state and actions and talks to the VLM only through self-attention. It predicts a 50-step action chunk with 10 flow-matching steps, reusing cached observation keys and values; on-board inference takes 73 ms on an RTX 4090.
- **Cross-embodiment data.** Single-arm, bimanual and mobile robots share one 18-D state/action vector, zero-padded for smaller robots. Each task-robot combination is weighted by n^0.43 to down-weight over-represented data.
- **Pre-train, then post-train.** Diverse, lower-quality pre-training data teaches recovery from mistakes; 5 to 100+ hours of curated task data teach fluent execution. A high-level VLM can split "bus the table" into language subcommands.

## Results
- Without fine-tuning, π0 is near-perfect on shirt folding and easy bussing and beats OpenVLA and Octo, trained on the same mixture, on all five tasks. A version trained for 160k instead of 700k steps still beats every baseline.
- π0 follows language commands significantly better than π0-small (470M parameters, no VLM initialization), which carries over to higher task progress with human or VLM step commands.
- Fine-tuned on new tasks, π0 generally beats OpenVLA, Octo, ACT and Diffusion Policy; pre-training improves on π0 trained from scratch by up to 2x.
- On multi-stage tasks lasting 5 to 20 minutes (laundry, dryer unloading, table bussing, box building, egg packing), the full recipe scores best on every task; the authors could not solve these tasks with other methods.

## Limitations
The authors pooled all available data without knowing which data helps or how to weight it. Not all tasks work reliably, and how much data near-perfect performance needs is unclear. The comparison with π0-small mixes VLM initialization with model size, and transfer to distant domains such as driving or legged locomotion is untested.
