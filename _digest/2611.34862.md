abstract: We introduce BOP-Refer, the first benchmark for monocular 3D and 2D grounding of everyday and industrial objects sourced from 9 diverse BOP datasets. We pair images from these datasets with VLM-generated and human-verified referring expressions, and derive accurate ground-truth 3D and 2D bounding boxes of the referred objects from their 3D models and 6DoF pose annotations. Our systematic evaluation of 17 frontier VLMs across the major proprietary and open-weight families (Gemini, GPT, Claude, Qwen, Gemma) shows that the best model, Gemini 4 Carbon, reaches 72.6% AP_IoU2D in 2D but only 12.4% AP_IoU3D in 3D, despite the more lenient IoU thresholds in 3D. The predicted 3D boxes are usually centered on the target objects in the image but often off in depth by more than the object's size, suggesting that metric scale estimation, which needs reliable object size priors, is currently by far the main source of error.

## What they do
BOP-Refer tests monocular 3D object grounding: given one image with known camera intrinsics and a free-form text query, predict oriented 3D boxes of the one or more objects it refers to. The same images and queries are also scored in 2D, which separates failing to find an object from failing to place it in 3D. The authors evaluate 17 VLMs and four specialist 3D pipelines, none fine-tuned on the benchmark.

## How it works
- **Data.** 1,400 validation and 1,400 test real images from 9 BOP datasets (six everyday-object, three industrial), covering 225 objects, with one query per image. Amodal 3D and 2D ground-truth boxes come from the objects' 3D models and annotated 6DoF poses. Test ground truth stays private behind an online evaluation server.
- **Queries.** GPT-5.4 and Gemini 3.1 Pro write candidate expressions from the image, a scene graph of attributes and spatial relations, and object appearance descriptions. Claude Opus 4.7 checks each against 14 criteria, then human reviewers rewrite or discard ambiguous ones.
- **Metrics.** AP over a symmetry-aware IoU3D and over a new Normalized Corner Distance (NCD), the mean corner error divided by the box diagonal, which still ranks near misses above far ones when boxes no longer overlap.

## Results
- 3D: Gemini 4 Carbon leads with 12.4% IoU3D AP and 60.3% NCD AP; Claude Opus 5.5 reaches 5.3% IoU3D AP, and WildDet3D and Boxer 1.6% and 1.0%. The best open-weight model gets 26.1% NCD AP.
- 2D: Gemini 4 Carbon 72.6% AP, Gemini 4 Argon 69.1%, Claude Opus 5.5 69.0%.
- Adding each target's camera distance to the query lifts NCD AP of three strong VLMs to 87.5-90.6%; adding the ground-truth 2D box gains only 2-4 points.
- Predicted box centers point in almost the right direction (median error at most 0.6 degrees), but distance and size are off by a median 7-18% and 7-15%. The 2D boxes miss 1-2% of targets, the 3D boxes 35-62%.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. The benchmark is evaluation-only, with no training split, and its splits are kept small so that VLM evaluation stays affordable. Each VLM ran in a single prompt configuration, and removing the intrinsics or in-context examples shifts NCD AP by up to 11.7 points. No evaluated model was trained for this setting, so the scores measure zero-shot transfer.
