tldr: A prompt-only recipe that turns an off-the-shelf VLM into a robot reward model by first describing the video without seeing the task, then scoring it against an explicit success criterion. Its rewards lift real-robot online RL on a DROID Franka from 10% to 100% success.
abstract: 'Vision-Language Models (VLMs) hold promise for zero-shot video task-progress estimation in robot learning, yet existing frameworks frequently suffer from confirmation bias, hallucinating progress in failed executions due to implicit success assumptions or relative temporal ranking designs. Naive prompting of frontier models collapses into a binary success detector that fails to capture nuanced, non-linear progress and failures. While post-training approaches can mitigate this through explicit training, they demand expensive real-world robotics data and require computationally intensive post-training for every individual base model. To resolve these limitations, we introduce D&A (Describe & Assess): our approach decouples progress estimation into an objective, task-grounded textual description of scene dynamics followed by an assessment phase using explicit rubrics. By compelling the VLM to synthesize an explicit, text-based definition of absolute success before scoring, the framework anchors evaluations to an external, global criterion rather than the video''s internal timeline. Empirical results demonstrate that our method calibrates zero-shot estimates and mitigates confirmation bias, providing fine-grained, partial progress metrics that accurately penalize failed executions while correctly interpolating successful runs. Being purely prompt-based, D&A generalizes across tasks, embodiments, and benchmarks---matching or surpassing post-trained reward models---and its reward estimates enable efficient downstream reinforcement learning.'

## What they do
Asked directly for per-frame task progress, VLMs tend to assume the robot succeeded and hallucinate progress on failed runs. D&A (Describe & Assess) is a zero-shot, purely prompt-based reward model that splits the job in two so the model cannot read the task into the video. No robot data or finetuning is needed, so it works on any promptable VLM.

## How it works
- **Describe.** An entity extractor lists every object in the scene, task-relevant or not. A describer then narrates the whole video, downsampled to about 8 key frames, using those entities but without seeing the instruction.
- **Assess.** A separate call gets the instruction and the description, first writes what the world looks like when the task is done, then scores each timestep against that criterion. Scores in [0,1] are interpolated back to every frame.
- **Feedback.** The assessor can also output a failure analysis with corrections, used as feedback between attempts of a code-as-policies planner.
- Rewards drive online RL (DSRL on π0) or offline RL by advantage conditioning or filtering on π0.5. The default base model is Gemini 3 Flash.

## Results
- Online RL, DROID Franka, "put the fruit in the bowl": success goes from 10% to 100% in about 90 minutes; with TOPReward rewards it is 5%.
- Offline RL on a bimanual YAM moving a book to a box: 100% vs 20% for BC, 80% for TOPReward and 85% for Robometer. On a book-extraction task, 95% vs 80% for BC, finishing in 18.8 ± 1.8 s vs 28.5 ± 9.2 s.
- On their 54-trajectory, 14-task D&A-Bench, D&A ranks trajectories best (Kendall τ 0.67 vs 0.56 for Robometer, 0.24 for TOPReward).
- Rich feedback lifts code-as-policies on LIBERO-Goal from 40.6% to 51.4%.

## Limitations
It inherits the latency, token cost, context limits and occasional hallucinations of closed frontier VLMs. Rewards are only as fine as language can describe, which is hard for precise spatial relations or distances.
