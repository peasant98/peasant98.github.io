abstract: Deep reinforcement learning is a promising approach to learning policies in uncontrolled environments that do not require domain knowledge. Unfortunately, due to sample inefficiency, deep RL applications have primarily focused on simulated environments. In this work, we demonstrate that the recent advancements in machine learning algorithms and libraries combined with a carefully tuned robot controller lead to learning quadruped locomotion in only 20 minutes in the real world. We evaluate our approach on several indoor and outdoor terrains which are known to be challenging for classical model-based controllers. We observe the robot to be able to learn walking gait consistently on all of these terrains. Finally, we evaluate our design decisions in a simulated environment.

## What they do
The paper asks how fast fully model-free deep RL can learn quadruped locomotion directly on hardware. Using no new algorithm, only existing components implemented carefully and a well-chosen task setup, a Unitree A1 learns to walk from scratch in about 20 minutes, on flat ground, a memory foam mattress, mulch, a lawn and a hiking trail.

## How it works
- **Algorithm.** SAC with critic regularization in the style of DroQ (layer norm, dropout), taking 20 critic updates and one actor update between every pair of actions. Training is synchronous, per step, after 1000 random-action steps.
- **Speed.** JAX with the 20 critic updates jit-compiled into one call gives 2400 critic updates per second on a laptop RTX 2070. Their best PyTorch version allowed only 7.5 Hz control.
- **MDP.** Proprioceptive state only. Actions are PD targets for 12 joints at 20 Hz, kept within a fixed offset of a default pose. The reward is constant within a target-velocity band, using forward velocity projected onto the ground plane from an onboard Kalman-filter estimator.
- **Resets.** Episodes end when roll or pitch exceed 30 degrees; a learned reset policy from prior work stands the robot up.

## Results
- On every terrain the robot learns to walk in under 20,000 samples, about 17 minutes of data and 20 minutes of wall-clock time; flat ground was repeated four times.
- In their comparison table, prior work that learned joint PD targets from scratch on real robots used 60,000-160,000 samples and 1-2 hours.
- In simulation, plain SAC at 20 updates per step needs about 30k samples to reach the target velocity; REDQ saves about 5k, and layer norm alone does better still. An unconstrained action space makes no progress, and low-pass filtering the actions slows learning.

## Limitations
The task is forward walking at a target velocity. A person lifts and reorients the robot to keep it in the training area. The onboard velocity estimator is significantly less accurate than VR tracking and systematically underestimates velocity. Real-time training depends on a fast, jit-compiled implementation.
