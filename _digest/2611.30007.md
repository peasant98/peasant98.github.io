abstract: LiDAR world models generate and predict metric 3D observations in the form of LiDAR scans. They typically synthesize scans following the scan pattern of their training sensor or render them from an intermediate 3D surface or field. However, following one scan pattern limits transfer to LiDARs with other patterns, and rendering from intermediate geometry can cause incorrect depths and false returns. In this paper, we propose a scan pattern agnostic 4D LiDAR world model that decouples the scene from sensor observation, so one spatial latent can be observed by heterogeneous LiDARs at the same mounting position without regeneration. Our decoder predicts the hit probability and depth of each ray directly from the latent, combining global scene context with local features retrieved at a preliminary depth. Without an intermediate surface or field, it reduces incorrect depths and false returns, and as each ray is decoded independently from one latent, LiDARs that share a ray receive the same return and observe one consistent scene. Finally, a diffusion transformer generates scenes in this latent and predicts 4D sequences from observed latents warped by the given ego and object motion. On KITTI-360 and nuScenes, our model generates realistic scenes and reduces the registration error between predicted frames by about 70% relative to the best compared world model. Its decoder yields 19 to 28% lower depth error than the rendering used by other world models, and generated scans benefit several downstream tasks. Our code will be open-sourced. More details are available at https://minwoo0611.github.io/OneLatentWorld.

## What they do
LiDAR world models usually generate range images tied to one sensor's raster, or render scans from intermediate occupancy, meshes or fields, which can add false or too-early returns. This model separates the scene from the sensor: scans from any LiDAR are encoded into one spatial latent, a diffusion transformer generates or predicts scenes there, and a decoder reads returns along whatever rays the target sensor fires, including non-repetitive patterns.

## How it works
- **Shared spatial encoding.** Scans are voxelized on a radius-azimuth-elevation grid whose cells store occupancy, point count and mean radial offset, then encoded into an 80x80x32 latent spanning 80 m.
- **Depth-guided ray decoding.** Each ray attends to all latent tokens to predict a hit probability and a preliminary depth, then refines the depth with local features sampled there. Rays are decoded independently, so a ray shared by two sensors gets the same return. Training uses labeled hits and misses, partly seen through random scan patterns.
- **Latent world model.** A rectified-flow DiT generates latents, optionally conditioned on 3D boxes and HD maps. For prediction it warps three observed latents by the given ego and object motion and predicts five future frames.

## Results
- Unconditional generation on nuScenes: FPVD drops from 48.43 to 10.57 and BEV MMD from 1.90 to 0.11 versus the best baseline per metric, at under 1 s per scan.
- Prediction: lowest registration error between predicted frames on both datasets, 66-72% below the compared world models. Removing the latent warp more than doubles it.
- Reading returns from measured scenes gives 19-28% lower depth error than rendering from occupancy, meshes or a render head. A scene plus scans for four unseen HeLiPR sensors takes 1.3 s, versus 16 s for LidarDM.
- Scans decoded for sensors absent from training improve place recognition, moving object segmentation and PandaSet car detection over training on raw source scans. For place recognition on Roundabout they reach 92.5% of the mean R@1 of the released weights trained on the real sensors.

## Limitations
The decoder only reads rays from the latent's own origin, so it cannot simulate a sensor at another mounting position. It predicts no return intensity. It rarely fills large unobserved regions and can leave them without returns, and on KITTI-360 it may predict no returns above the training sensor's vertical field of view.
