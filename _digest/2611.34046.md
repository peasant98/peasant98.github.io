abstract: Image-goal navigation asks a robot to reach the viewpoint of a target photograph without a map or goal pose. In three dimensions, this requires not only recognizing the goal but also estimating 3D displacement and generating continuous control actions to approach and settle at the target view. This is difficult because the same visual difference between current and goal image can require very different actions depending on the robot's velocity, yet monocular images alone reveal neither metric scale nor body motion. As a result, existing image-goal policies are limited to planar action spaces and rely on a state estimator for flight control. Policies that map directly from images to low-level control commands have been demonstrated for agile flight, but not toward goal images in unseen environments. We present the first image-goal navigation policy that directly commands thrust and angular rates for agile quadrotor flight in unseen 3D environments, without relying on state estimation (e.g., SLAM, VO, or SfM) or a separate velocity controller. We train a recurrent vision-based policy through a four-stage pipeline that combines geometric and motion supervision with privileged-teacher distillation and end-to-end reinforcement learning fine-tuning. On 1000 held-out flights in unseen environments, the policy achieves an 82.3% success rate with a mean terminal position error of 0.10m across successful flights. Deployed zero-shot on a real quadrotor, the policy reaches an 84% success rate over 25 trials in an unseen indoor environment.

## What they do
Given a photograph taken somewhere in a room, a quadrotor must fly to that viewpoint and hover there. Earlier aerial image-goal methods output planar velocity commands and rely on a separate state-estimation-based controller. This policy sees only the current onboard image, the goal image and its own previous command, and outputs collective thrust and body rates directly. It has no access to position, velocity, attitude or IMU.

## How it works
- **Stage 1, teacher.** PPO trains a policy on the privileged 12-D goal-relative pose and motion state, with a 20-level curriculum and randomized dynamics.
- **Stage 2, visual encoder.** Frozen DINOv3 patch features of the current and goal images, plus camera-ray embeddings, pass through cross-attention and an LSTM that also sees the previous command. It is trained to predict the privileged state and to match the teacher's latent.
- **Stages 3-4.** The student reuses the teacher's actor, is trained with DAgger-style imitation, then fine-tuned with PPO and a goal-reprojection penalty near the goal.
- Trained on 800 HM3D buildings in an Isaac Lab / Warp simulator of a 0.21 kg quadrotor.

## Results
- HM3D, 1000 flights in 100 unseen buildings: 82.3% success, versus 74.6% for Oracle IBVS (ground-truth correspondences and state) and 40.8% for SIFT IBVS. Its mean final position error (0.10 m) and flight time are larger than IBVS's.
- Gibson, zero-shot: 93.0% success.
- Real quadrotor in an unseen arena: 21/25 flights succeed (3 crashes, 1 timeout), with 0.118 m mean final position error, despite 30.51% of video frames dropped.
- Imitation alone reaches 64.8%; RL fine-tuning lifts it to 82.3%. PPO from scratch never leaves the first curriculum level.

## Limitations
The goal image must partly overlap the starting view; the policy does not explore to find goals it cannot see.
