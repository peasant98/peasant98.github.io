abstract: Vision-language models (VLMs) exhibit strong semantic understanding but remain limited in their ability to reason about metric 3D space in the physical world. Existing VLMs typically infer 3D scene structure from one or more images without known camera calibration or cross-view geometric constraints. This makes metric 3D inference inherently ambiguous and leaves it unclear whether their spatial predictions are grounded in visual evidence or driven primarily by semantic priors. In contrast, calibrated stereo provides explicit cross-view geometry that resolves this ambiguity and offers a more reliable foundation for metric 3D perception and geometric generalization. We investigate how VLMs can learn transferable 3D representations directly from calibrated binocular observations. To this end, we introduce StereoVLM, the first geometry-aware binocular VLM for metric 3D spatial reasoning. StereoVLM follows a hierarchical learning paradigm spanning from cross-view correspondence and disparity estimation to calibration-conditioned metric depth perception and semantic spatial reasoning. We further find that disparity supervision generalizes substantially better than direct binocular depth supervision on unseen environments. To evaluate these capabilities, we introduce Stereo3D-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark across three levels, from point-level geometry to object-level spatial reasoning. StereoVLM-8B achieves 95.17% depth accuracy ($δ_1$) and a 66.98 average object-level score on Stereo3D-Bench. Intervention results further indicate that the model's performance on Stereo3D-Bench benefits from valid cross-view geometry and cannot be explained solely by additional visual tokens or semantic priors. Hierarchical geometric supervision also yields average gains of 3.54 points across five spatial benchmarks and 4.3 percentage points in RoboCasa manipulation success.

## What they do
Vision-language models guess metric 3D structure from single images, so their answers can come from semantic priors rather than geometry. StereoVLM gives a VLM a calibrated stereo pair and teaches it stereo geometry explicitly. Stereo3D-Bench scores its answers against sensor or simulator geometry.

## How it works
- **Model.** Qwen3-VL-Instruct (2B and 8B) with full-parameter instruction tuning on 800x320 stereo pairs. Metric questions state the focal length and baseline in the prompt; every answer is text.
- **Hierarchical supervision.** Point-level questions ask for a pixel's right-image match, its disparity and its metric depth. Object-level questions ask the same for a named object, then for its oriented 3D box, and for the 3D box of the object a manipulation instruction refers to. Training targets come from FoundationStereo, kept only where it agrees with the dataset's own depth.
- **Controls.** Each benchmark query is rerun with the right image missing, black, replaced by the left image, or taken from another scene.

## Results
- Supervising disparity generalizes better than supervising depth directly: on an unseen in-house split, depth δ1 is 74.92% versus 45.89% (monocular input: 51.00%).
- On the main split, StereoVLM-8B cuts correspondence error from 9.27 to 3.80 px relative to GPT-6 Astra and lifts the object-level average from 47.61 to 66.98; task-target 3D grounding goes from 12.82% to 51.28%.
- The right view matters: with only the left image, the 2B model's δ1 falls from 94.51% to 80.82%; with the left image duplicated, to 7.40%.
- A GR00T policy built on StereoVLM-2B succeeds on 42.4% of 24 RoboCasa tasks, versus 38.1% without stereo tuning and 34.9% from plain Qwen3-VL-2B.

## Limitations
Evaluation is limited to benchmarks and simulated kitchen tasks, with no real-robot test, and the model needs calibrated, synchronized cameras. On the unseen split the 8B model's depth δ1 drops to 70.16%, close to GPT-6 Astra's 69.27%. When the right camera is perturbed, correspondence error grows from 2.48 to as much as 7.13 px: the model does not fully adapt its matches to the changed geometry.
