abstract: 'Physics-based human-object interaction (HOI) has enabled multiple characters to cooperatively transport objects, but existing cooperative controllers largely assume homogeneous embodiments and predefined interaction tasks. We ask whether a single physics-based policy can enable cooperation across diverse embodiments, team compositions, and interaction skills. We present MorphHOI, a decentralised framework built on a key insight: effective heterogeneous cooperation requires a unified representation across embodiments while accounting for differences in their physical capabilities. To realise this insight, we introduce a canonical body interface that unifies observation and action spaces, together with an embodiment-conditioned transformer that adapts cooperative decisions to the physical capabilities of each agent and its teammates. To extend cooperation to new interaction skills, we reuse the learned cooperative policy through task-specific tokens and lightweight adaptation, preserving its original capabilities. Our framework achieves success rates of up to 96.7\% on seen embodiments, 95.6\% on unseen embodiments, and 91.4\% on novel payloads. Furthermore, the learned cooperation extends to diverse interaction skills with high success rates, including box carrying, transport on stairs, and waypoint following, while preserving previously acquired cooperative capabilities.'

## What they do
Physics-based controllers for characters that carry an object together usually assume every agent has the same body. MorphHOI trains one decentralised policy that runs on every member of a team whose members differ in height, proportions, mass, joint layout and PD gains. All bodies are simulated humanoids in Isaac Gym: procedurally generated digital humans mixed with robot models that use their makers' kinematics, masses and joint limits. The base task is to walk to a table, lift it, carry it to a target and put it down.

## How it works
- **Canonical body interface.** Every body has the same 15 links but zero to three hinges between them. The policy observes link states and outputs 28 canonical joint targets, which damped Gauss-Newton IK maps onto each body's own hinges; missing DoFs are masked.
- **Embodiment-conditioned transformer.** A description of each body (stature, limb ratios, mass, strength, DoF mask, stiffness ratios) enters the agent's and its teammates' tokens and modulates the layers and a low-rank action head. Self, team and task attention are separate, and an optimal-transport step assigns agents to grasp points on the rim.
- **Training.** PPO with an adversarial motion prior: stage I on teams of identical bodies, stage II on mixed teams drawn from 320 training bodies, with contact thresholds and style reward adapted per body.
- **New skills.** Stage III adds a tokenizer and per-agent gated LoRA for box carrying, waypoint following and stairs; agents without the skill run exactly the stage II policy.

## Results
- Training bodies, teams of 2/4/8: 96.7%, 94.5% and 87.8% success (average 93.0%, versus 88.3% for retrained TeamHOI and 78.3% for retrained CooHOI). The released TeamHOI policy gets 0%.
- Unseen embodiments: 95.6%, 93.9% and 80.0%; unseen table shapes: 90.0%, 91.4% and 84.9%. Trained on 2-4 agents, it reaches 80.0% with twelve and 51.0% with sixteen.
- Gated LoRA reaches harmonic-mean success of 88.6% (box), 92.2% (path) and 88.5% (stairs); full fine-tuning gets 0% on the box.
- Removing body descriptions drops average success from 93.0% to 80.5%.

## Limitations
Everything runs in simulation. Deployment on physical humanoids is left to future work, with actuator limits, state estimation, delays and contact dynamics named as open challenges. Coordination gets harder as teams grow, and larger teams fail more often on longer carries.
