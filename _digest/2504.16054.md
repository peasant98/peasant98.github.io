abstract: In order for robots to be useful, they must perform practically relevant tasks in the real world, outside of the lab. While vision-language-action (VLA) models have demonstrated impressive results for end-to-end robot control, it remains an open question how far such models can generalize in the wild. We describe $π_{0.5}$, a new model based on $π_{0}$ that uses co-training on heterogeneous tasks to enable broad generalization. $π_{0.5}$\ uses data from multiple robots, high-level semantic prediction, web data, and other sources to enable broadly generalizable real-world robotic manipulation. Our system uses a combination of co-training and hybrid multi-modal examples that combine image observations, language commands, object detections, semantic subtask prediction, and low-level actions. Our experiments show that this kind of knowledge transfer is essential for effective generalization, and we demonstrate for the first time that an end-to-end learning-enabled robotic system can perform long-horizon and dexterous manipulation skills, such as cleaning a kitchen or bedroom, in entirely new homes.

## What they do
π0.5 tests how far a vision-language-action model generalizes in homes it has never seen. Building on π0, it trains on about 400 hours of mobile-manipulator data from about 100 homes, plus a much larger mixture of other sources: static arms in other homes, lab data from many robot types, subtask annotations and web data. From a high-level prompt such as "place the dishes in the sink", the policy cleans kitchens and bedrooms in new homes.

## How it works
- **One model, two levels.** The model first outputs a text subtask ("pick up the cup"), then predicts a 50-step action chunk conditioned on it. A 2B PaliGemma backbone plus a 300M action expert.
- **Pre-training (280k steps).** Plain next-token prediction of text, bounding boxes and FAST-tokenized actions. 97.6% of examples in this phase do not come from mobile manipulators doing household tasks.
- **Post-training (80k steps).** Adds a flow-matching action expert, trains on successful mobile and static-arm episodes plus web and subtask data, and adds verbal instructions: experts steering the robot step by step with language commands.
- **Control.** Target poses for arms and torso and base velocities at 50 Hz, tracked by PD controllers with no planner or collision checking.

## Results
- In three unseen real homes it completes multi-stage kitchen and bedroom tasks lasting about 2-5 minutes.
- With data from 104 locations it matches a control trained on the test homes themselves; without the co-training pre-training, both baselines are significantly worse.
- Removing other-robot data (ME or CE) significantly hurts. Removing web data mainly hurts unseen object categories and subtask inference.
- It beats π0 and π0-FAST+Flow. Its own subtask inference beats a human oracle and GPT-4 as the high-level policy; verbal instructions are about 11% of the high-level examples, yet removing them significantly hurts.

## Limitations
Unfamiliar drawer handles and hard-to-open cabinets, partial observability (an arm hiding a spill), and a high-level policy that gets distracted, opening and closing a drawer repeatedly. Prompts are simple and context is modest, which the authors flag for tasks spanning rooms or needing memory of where things are stored.
