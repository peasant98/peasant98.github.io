abstract: This paper presents a personalized gait optimization framework for lower-body exoskeletons. Rather than optimizing numerical objectives such as the mechanical cost of transport, our approach directly learns from user preferences, e.g., for comfort. Building upon work in preference-based interactive learning, we present the CoSpar algorithm. CoSpar prompts the user to give pairwise preferences between trials and suggest improvements; as exoskeleton walking is a non-intuitive behavior, users can provide preferences more easily and reliably than numerical feedback. We show that CoSpar performs competitively in simulation and demonstrate a prototype implementation of CoSpar on a lower-body exoskeleton to optimize human walking trajectory features. In the experiments, CoSpar consistently found user-preferred parameters of the exoskeleton's walking gait, which suggests that it is a promising starting point for adapting and personalizing exoskeletons (or other assistive devices) to individual users.

## What they do
The Atalante exoskeleton walks without crutches using gaits generated by partial hybrid zero dynamics, but nothing in that optimization captures user comfort, and users struggle to give reliable numerical scores. CoSpar instead learns which gait in a precomputed gait library a user prefers, from pairwise comparisons and suggested improvements.

## How it works
- **Utility model.** A preference-based Gaussian process over the finite set of gaits, with the posterior estimated by a Laplace approximation.
- **Gait selection.** Extends the Self-Sparring dueling-bandit algorithm: draw n samples from the posterior and run the best gait under each. The user compares these with each other and with the b trials before them.
- **Coactive feedback.** The user can also suggest a change (e.g. a longer step). The suggested gait is added to the data as preferred over the tested one, with a weight that can be set lower than pairwise preferences.

## Results
- Simulation: on a compass-gait biped, preferences derived from cost of transport make the posterior peak at the lowest-cost step length. On 100 random 2D functions (30x30 grid, 150 evaluations), adding coactive feedback improved learning in every (n, b) setting.
- Step length (15 values, 0.08-0.18 m), three able-bodied subjects, 20 trials each with n=1, b=1: after five trials CoSpar had found a compact preferred region. Blind rankings of the posterior's maximum, mean and minimum gaits matched the posterior for every subject.
- Two features at once (step length with duration, and with width) for one subject: estimated preferences were consistent with a 3-gait blind ranking.
- Metabolic expenditure was recorded but did not reflect preferences, since users do not have to exert effort to walk.

## Limitations
CoSpar only selects from a precomputed gait library over one or two discretized features. Larger parameter sets would likely need high-dimensional learning methods, and generating new gaits is left to future work. Human tests cover three able-bodied subjects, with the 2D experiments run on one of them.
