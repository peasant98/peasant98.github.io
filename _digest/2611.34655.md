abstract: 'Dexterous mobile manipulation is essential for robots to perform useful tasks in complex, unstructured environments. However, existing systems face two challenges. First, navigation remains too coarse for downstream manipulation, which often requires a precise base position and approach orientation rather than merely reaching a meter-scale goal region. Second, navigation and manipulation are commonly trained in isolation, lacking a feedback loop that enables the two capabilities to co-evolve. These limitations prevent current systems from reliably executing long-horizon dexterous mobile-manipulation tasks in real-world environments. To address them, we introduce Nav4Manip, a bidirectionally coupled co-evolution system for long-horizon dexterous mobile manipulation. It comprises two complementary architectures: Nav4Manip-N learns long-horizon navigation and refines its policy through manipulation feedback, while Nav4Manip-M trains a world-action model (WAM) for downstream dexterous manipulation. We train Nav4Manip-N in three stages: multi-task co-tuning learns navigation behavior and manipulation-aware affordance prediction; rejection sampling fine-tuning (RFT) rejects low-quality policy-generated paths and fine-tunes the model on retained trajectories; and reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF) uses counterfactual preferences to further improve navigation decisions. To support this framework, we establish the Nav-N-Manip benchmark and construct Nav4Manip-Pairs from benchmark rollouts, providing large-scale paired navigation--manipulation trajectories and affordance supervision. Extensive experiments on public vision-language navigation (VLN) benchmarks and Nav-N-Manip demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, while real-world evaluations show that Nav4Manip completes diverse dexterous mobile-manipulation tasks. Project website: https://nav4manip.github.io/.'

## What they do
Vision-language navigation (VLN) benchmarks count a stop within 3 m of the goal as success, but a dexterous grasp needs a precise base position and approach direction. Nav4Manip pairs a navigation model (Nav4Manip-N) with a dexterous manipulation world-action model (Nav4Manip-M) and uses manipulation outcomes to teach the navigator to stop where grasping works. The authors also build the Nav-N-Manip benchmark and a 233-hour paired navigation-manipulation dataset.

## How it works
- **Data.** In 221 EmbodiedGen v2 houses and 176 VLNVerse scenes, candidate base poses are scored by repeated manipulation rollouts in MuJoCo, paired with collision-checked paths, and rendered in Isaac Sim.
- **Nav4Manip-N.** A Qwen3-VL-based model on head-camera RGB predicts discrete navigation actions and a bird's-eye affordance map over base positions and yaws. It is co-tuned on 4.9M samples from R2R, RxR, HM3D ObjectNav, rendered EQA and the paired data.
- **Manipulation feedback.** Rejection-sampling fine-tuning keeps the policy's own rollouts whose endpoints let grasping succeed. RLAIF has an agent propose corrections (move closer, change yaw), keeps those that execution confirms, and distills them into the navigator.
- **Nav4Manip-M.** A world-action model (Qwen3-VL-4B encoder, Wan2.2 video branch, 1B ActionDiT), trained on simulated trajectories and post-trained on teleoperated data from a Galbot G3 with Sharpa hands.

## Results
- Best single-view SR@3/SPL@3 on R2R (60.03/56.79) and RxR (62.06/52.60) val-unseen.
- Nav-N-Manip: co-tuning lifts end-to-end success from 4.07% to 31.01%; RFT plus two RLAIF rounds reach 52.13%, vs 34.30% for alternating optimization. Distilling the agent cuts navigation latency per episode from 50.33 s to 13.80 s.
- Real shelf tasks, 10 trials each: 30%, 20% and 40% end-to-end success with three manipulation policies; Uni-NaVid reaches 0%.
- Post-training Nav4Manip-M on the navigator's actual stopping poses raises real-robot success from 17% to 30% over 30 trials.

## Limitations
Tested on one robot (Galbot G3 with Sharpa hands) and indoor scenes only. Navigation and manipulation remain separate models. Real-robot end-to-end success stays at 40% or below.
