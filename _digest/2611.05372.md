abstract: 'Inverse kinematics (IK) converts end-effector pose commands into joint motion, and many vision-language-action (VLA) policies rely on it to act on a given arm and to transfer across embodiments. Precise IK remains laborious: redundancy and singularities complicate the inverse mapping, execution depends on hand-tuned controllers, and training data on physical robots are costly to collect. We present PolicyIK, a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning policy for high-precision IK. It updates joint-position references from pose feedback and observes its accumulated reference relative to the measured joints, so it can retain the offset that the position controller needs at rest. Two-scale pose errors with an error-dependent action gain resolve millimeter residuals while preserving large motions. Progress and dwell rewards teach it to both reach the tolerance and stay within it. In simulation, a single attempt by the trained PPO policy keeps the mean errors over the final second within 1 mm and 0.2° on 93.5% of held-out tasks, and a deployment protocol raises success to 98.9%. When trained directly on a physical arm with an off-policy RL learner, PolicyIK achieves higher reaching and holding success than all four classical IK controllers under the same time budgets. In simulated pick-and-place, PolicyIK executes the end-effector commands of the $π_{0.5}$ VLA policy with 95.7% success. These results show that reinforcement learning can provide high-precision inverse kinematics for robot arms. Code is available at https://github.com/Tinaliu0123/PolicyIK.'

## What they do
Many VLA policies output end-effector poses and leave the conversion to joint commands to an IK controller, so IK accuracy limits every policy built on top of it. On a position-controlled arm, gravity, finite gains and joint limits make the arm settle away from the goal. PolicyIK learns closed-loop IK with goal-conditioned RL. Its target is full-pose precision of 1 mm and 0.2°, which the arm must reach and then hold.

## How it works
- **Reference accumulation.** Each action is an increment to a joint-position reference that a PD controller tracks. The policy also sees the reference minus the measured joints, so a zero action keeps the offset needed to hold a pose against gravity.
- **Two-scale pose errors.** The pose error is observed clipped at a coarse (1 cm, 20°) and a fine (0.5 mm, 0.25°) scale, and an error-dependent gain shrinks steps near the goal.
- **Reward.** A progress term pays only when the best error so far improves; a dwell term pays for every step inside the tolerance.
- **Training.** PPO in 4,096 Isaac Sim environments, or FlashSAC directly on hardware with hindsight goal relabeling, near-goal starts, 150 scripted demonstrations and a latent-alignment critic for large-rotation detours.

## Results
- Simulated 7-DoF OpenArm, 4,120 held-out tasks: 93.5% strict success in one attempt (median 0.25 mm, 0.04°), 98.9% with retries. DLS, QP and MPC score 43, 37 and 37 points lower, and an adapted UMI on Legs RL recipe gets no strict successes.
- Physical OpenArm, trained on hardware for 120,000 transitions: reaches 54 of 60 tasks and holds 43 through the final 5 s.
- Physical xArm7: 60/60 on standard and 60/60 on large-rotation tasks, for both simulation- and hardware-trained policies.
- Simulated LIBERO-Object pick-and-place driven by π0.5: 95.7% success, against 96.7% for hand-tuned DLS and 90.8% for default DLS and hand-tuned QP.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. Hardware errors come from joint encoders through forward kinematics, not from an external measurement, and the VLA pick-and-place test is simulation-only. On the OpenArm, a 15 s budget leaves only 27 of 60 tasks held for 5 s, and a separate policy is trained for each arm and mounting.
