abstract: Reinforcement learning (RL) requires large amounts of interaction data or a reliable simulator, both hard to obtain for nonlinear soft robotic hardware. Recent work shows that a time-scale separation inherent to soft robot dynamics justifies their reduction to low-dimensional invariant manifolds, known as adiabatic spectral submanifolds (aSSMs). We demonstrate that an aSSM-reduced model, a simple polynomial ordinary differential equation learned purely from pre-collected data, can serve as an RL training environment. On a pneumatically actuated soft arm, we train a trajectory-tracking policy in 3 hours on a six-dimensional aSSM-reduced model and deploy it without fine-tuning, whereas reproducing this policy training extrapolates to 3 years of hardware interaction. Our policy tracks dynamic targets with 40% lower error than otherwise identical policies trained on a 4.8-million-parameter recurrent neural network (RNN) or a 1.7-million-parameter transformer, which take 13 and 100 hours to identify, against 5 minutes for our mathematically justified 6,924-parameter model.

## What they do
Soft robots lack fast, faithful simulators, and model-free RL on delicate hardware is impractical. This paper trains an RL policy entirely offline on a tiny data-driven model: the arm's dynamics restricted to a six-dimensional adiabatic spectral submanifold (aSSM), which exists because the arm's vibrations decay faster than the control actions change. The policy is deployed on a pneumatic soft continuum arm (SoPrA) without fine-tuning.

## How it works
- **Data.** A 2.2-hour sweep of 400 random static pressure commands, each held for 20 s, with motion capture recording the tip and mid-section positions.
- **Model.** A cubic polynomial maps static actions to static states. Quadratic polynomials give the manifold parametrization and reduced dynamics, fit by least squares in a delay-embedded observable space.
- **Policy.** Standard policy optimization (Schulman et al., 2017) on parallel model rollouts; the policy sees the current and previous state, the previous action and the next target, and tracks random 2D tip trajectories.
- **Baselines.** The same RL procedure on RNN and transformer world models trained on the same amount of data, plus an MPC controller using the aSSM model.

## Results
- The aSSM model has 6,924 parameters and takes 5 minutes to learn, against 13 hours for the 4.8M-parameter RNN and 100 hours for the 1.7M-parameter transformer. MLP models diverged on some unseen action sequences.
- On hardware lemniscate and square tracks, aSSM-RL has 40% lower tracking error than the best neural-model policy and 20% lower than aSSM-MPC.
- The policy runs at 100 Hz, twice the rate of the aSSM-MPC controller, which falls behind on the fastest square.

## Limitations
The commanded and measured chamber pressures do not match (about 0.3 s valve lag), which adds wiggles to every closed-loop result and prevented learning the higher-order model correction. The model is only valid when targets move slowly relative to the arm's decay, and accuracy degrades as targets speed up. Evaluation covers 2D tip tracking with external motion capture and 5 of the 6 chambers active.
