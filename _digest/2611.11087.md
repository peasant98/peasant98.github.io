tldr: A text-to-3D scene pipeline whose physics stage simulates cloth, pillows and ropes instead of treating them as rigid, by building each object's rest shape from how it is manufactured. abstract: We present SoftPAT3D, a physics-augmented framework for text-to-3D scene generation in which deformable objects, such as cloth, stuffed toys and ropes, are generated as physically valid, simulation-ready bodies and placed in intersection-free contact with the furniture that supports them, with one another and with themselves. Existing physics-augmented pipelines simulate rigid bodies only and cannot accommodate a generated deformable object, which text-to-3D generation returns as a geometrically invalid mesh, sometimes self-intersecting, that has no rest shape, no material model and no admissible initial state. These are the inputs a deformable simulation requires, and we construct each from information already in the pipeline. The rest shape comes from how the object is manufactured rather than from the generated physically inconsistent deformed configuration, and a material library assigns each deformable object its initial elastic parameters, which can then be optimized. The occluded support is regenerated and converted into a watertight collision mesh, each object is released from an optimizable placement above the supporting part that the prompt specifies, and the complete scene is driven to static equilibrium under one unified contact model, with completion verified by geometric checks and a vision-language model. When a scene fails the prompt, a grid search over placements corrects it, and the adjoint of a differentiable simulation of the same contact energy reaches the targets the grid does not, places several bodies jointly and recovers and optimizes material parameters. Multi-body scenes equilibrate in a single simulation, and the pipeline also runs from the text prompt alone. On all sixty-five prompts, SoftPAT3D produces no undesired interpenetration of any kind, whereas every other system does, and its scenes directly support scene editing and robotic manipulation.

## What they do
Text-to-3D scene pipelines settle their layouts with a physics engine, but every object in that stage is rigid. A generated tablecloth or pile of clothes arrives already draped, as a closed and sometimes self-intersecting surface with no rest shape, no material and no valid starting state, so it ends up floating above or cutting into the furniture. SoftPAT3D builds those missing inputs on top of the PAT3D pipeline and simulates rigid supports, cloth and stuffed objects together to static equilibrium.

## How it works
- **Rest shapes from manufacturing.** Garments are sewn from parametric sewing patterns, linens are flat panels, and pillows are two seamed panels with a tetrahedralized interior, each clamped to the real-world size range of its class.
- **Initial state.** The cloth is inpainted out of the support, which is regenerated and converted into a watertight collision mesh. A language model picks the part named in the prompt (seat, backrest, arm), and the object is dropped from the lowest intersection-free height above it.
- **One contact model.** All bodies share an incremental-potential-contact barrier, so a state that starts intersection-free stays so.
- **Completion check and repair.** Two geometric checks and a vision-language model judge the result. Failures are re-placed by grid search or by the adjoint of a differentiable simulation.

## Results
- Zero crossings and zero penetration on all 65 prompts. The rigid pipeline leaves cloth 14.8 mm inside the furniture, and Blender's cloth solver crosses at 196 edges per thousand on the generated asset.
- 21 of 34 scenes are complete, against 8, 7, 2 and 0 for the baselines.
- Held-out prompts: 19 of 32 complete with placement optimization, 14 from the physics stage alone.
- Simulated manipulation: in all 23 pull, lift and drag episodes the gripped patch reaches 90% of its commanded displacement, median grip error 5.7 mm.

## Limitations
Prompts that ask for an arrangement, such as a row of pillows or a folded towel, are treated as placements and fail the prompt check. A support generated as a solid block has no part to place onto. The sewn panel keeps the generated object's kind in 48 of 60 scenes and its colour in 34. Materials are effective values, not measured ones, and plasticity is not modelled. Manipulation is simulated with a position constraint; the robot arm in the figures is for visualization only.
