abstract: Coordinating locomotion and dexterous manipulation is fundamental to humanoid robots operating in human environments. A key challenge is learning motion priors from diverse data and using them effectively for coordinated robot control. We present CHAMP, a unified framework for humanoid loco-manipulation that combines whole-body motion pretraining with adaptive feature extraction. CHAMP pretrains a vision-language model to predict future body and hand motion from approximately 2,298 hours of heterogeneous human and robot data. A shared motion representation enables unified whole-body motion learning across embodiments from datasets with body annotations, hand annotations, or both. To ground these motion priors in robot control, we introduce a Coordination-oriented Action Expert (CAE) and train it on replay data generated using the target controller. Through sparse routing, CAE adaptively selects, transforms, and fuses features from multiple layers of the pretrained model to jointly generate body and hand actions. In experiments, CHAMP achieves overall success rates of 84.8% on seven simulated tasks and 91.4% on seven real-world tasks with a Unitree G1, outperforming the evaluated baselines in both settings. In particular, CHAMP achieves 70.0% success on the long-horizon popcorn-serving task, compared with 40.0% for the best-performing baseline.

## What they do
CHAMP is a vision-language-action policy for humanoid loco-manipulation, where walking, bending and both hands have to work together. A VLM is first pretrained to forecast whole-body and finger motion from mixed human and robot data. An action expert then turns its internal features into body and hand commands for a Unitree G1, with body motion executed by the SONIC whole-body controller.

## How it works
- **Motion pretraining.** Qwen3-VL predicts the next second of pelvis pose, 12 body-joint positions, wrist poses and fingertip positions from one image, an instruction and the current pose. A shared motion representation lets nine datasets with body-only, hand-only or full annotations train one model.
- **Robot action grounding.** With the VLM frozen, a Coordination-oriented Action Expert (CAE) is trained on 254.5 h of robot trajectories, replayed through SONIC in simulation to filter out falls and drift. Per token, a router picks two of four VLM layers and two projection experts in each, and fuses them to condition a flow-matching DiT that outputs SONIC motion latents and hand commands.
- **Task fine-tuning.** CAE is fine-tuned on 100 teleoperated demonstrations per task.

## Results
- Real world (10 trials per task): 91.4% overall, first on all seven tasks; GR00T N1.6 reaches 74.3%, ω-0 61.4%, Ψ0 57.1% and π0.5 47.1%.
- Long-horizon popcorn serving: 7/10, versus 4/10 for GR00T N1.6 and 3/10 for Ψ0 and ω-0.
- Simulation (seven SIMPLE tasks, 30 trials each): 84.8% versus 61.9% for GR00T N1.6.
- Ablations in simulation: 71.9% without motion pretraining, 73.8% without action grounding, and 62.4-78.6% when reading a single fixed layer.

## Limitations
Pretraining covers body and hand kinematics only, with no supervision of object dynamics or contact forces; two of the three popcorn failures came from excessive scoop-bucket contact force that made the robot lose its grip. Control is evaluated on one humanoid platform with task-specific fine-tuning.
