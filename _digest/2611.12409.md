abstract: 'Robots are increasingly expected to execute natural-language manipulation tasks in real-world environments. This is particularly relevant in household assistance and logistics, where clutter requires reasoning about both task objectives and execution feasibility. This challenge becomes even more pronounced for bimanual humanoid platforms, where coordinating two manipulators introduces additional interaction and kinematic constraints. We present a zero-shot vision-language framework for execution-aware bimanual manipulation that jointly reasons about task objectives and physical feasibility. Planning is grounded in a structured scene representation that combines semantic object relations with execution constraints, enabling the selection of obstacle relocations and execution choices while preserving task semantics. Compatible actions are organised into parallel bimanual stages with explicit pre- and post-conditions. A hierarchical validator evaluates these conditions under perceptual uncertainty and uses stage-level execution history to support local recovery or targeted replanning. Experiments on the CENTAURO hybrid wheeled-legged humanoid robot across nine language-conditioned manipulation scenarios show higher task success than a non-execution-aware baseline. In dedicated failure scenarios, hierarchical validation recovers from execution failures with fewer model queries than always replanning. These results demonstrate the benefits of combining execution reasoning directly into task planning. Videos and additional material are available on the Project Webpage: https://advrhumanoids.github.io/bridging-planning-execution/.'

## What they do
VLM task planners reason about what to do but usually leave implicit how each action can physically be carried out, so a box wedged between two others looks graspable to them. This zero-shot framework (no training, fine-tuning or demonstrations) makes those choices explicit for a bimanual humanoid: which side of an object is free, which arm can reach it, and what must hold before and after each step.

## How it works
- **Scene graph.** A VLM lists objects and relations from one RGB image; FoundationPose with CAD models gives 6D poses. Each of an object's six sides is marked accessible (enough clearance for the end-effector, with the blocker recorded otherwise) and reachable (an IK solution exists).
- **Planning.** A VLM outputs actions as (grasp/place/push/release, target, left/right/both arms, contact type, side), relocating blocking objects when no side is free.
- **Scheduling.** Compatible actions are grouped into parallel two-arm stages, each with a local goal and atomic pre- and post-conditions.
- **Validation.** A VLM rates each condition satisfied, uncertain or violated. Uncertain triggers re-perception; a failure is handled by repeat, modify (change arm, contact or side) or full replan, in that order.

## Results
- Real CENTAURO robot, nine tabletop scenarios, 20 runs each, Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 as the VLM.
- Boxes Aside, Fragile Box and Long Horizon: 85%, 70% and 50% success, against 0% for a planner given only symbolic relations. Push Failure rises from 15% to 95%, Bottles on Box from 70% to 95%.
- Hierarchical validation mainly saves queries: a recovery decision costs 1.1 queries in Bottle Failure and 2.1 in Push Failure, against 3 for always replanning.
- Parallel scheduling cuts robot motion time by 40% in Bottles on Box and 11% in Long Horizon.
- Swapping the VLM: GPT-o3 reaches 80.0% at 91.4 s latency, Gemini 73.3% at 41.9 s, GPT-5.2 5.0%.

## Limitations
Objects are oriented boxes grasped on opposing faces, so handles and other instance-specific grasps are out of scope, and poses need CAD models. Motions come from inverse kinematics with a fixed base; unreachable sides are discarded. Long-horizon planning is the weak module (67% correct), and perception suffers from the head camera's viewpoint.
