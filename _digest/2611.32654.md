abstract: 'Robot demonstrations provide more than actions to imitate: they reveal how actions change the world and how those changes contribute to task success. Recent world-action models (WAMs) extend vision-language-action (VLA) models with the first signal by jointly predicting actions and future observations, yet this predictive ability serves only as an auxiliary loss for policy learning. Meanwhile, most policy improvement methods learn dynamics and values in separate models, increasing training overhead and limiting representation sharing between these complementary objectives. We introduce DexWAC, a unified world-actor-critic model that jointly learns an actor, an action-conditioned world model, and a bootstrapped critic in a single robot pretraining stage. Our key insight is that visual prediction encourages control-relevant features, while value supervision promotes task-relevant representations; together, they shape a representation suited to policy learning. Built on an image-editing backbone, DexWAC predicts, for each action chunk, the visual latent and proprioception at its endpoint together with its reward and continuation, so the world model alone efficiently yields complete imagined transitions without generating intermediate video frames. After adapting the world model and critic on real rollouts, we improve the pretrained actor through either PPO on imagined rollouts or critic-guided best-of-$N$ selection. DexWAC surpasses the large-scale pretrained VLA and WAM baselines in simulation and achieves 75% average success on two real-world tasks, compared with 45% for $π_{0.5}$. Its world model is more accurate and faster than video-based baselines, and ablations confirm that learning dynamics and value together improves the policy.'

## What they do
World-action models predict future observations alongside actions, but only as an auxiliary loss, and improving a pretrained policy usually takes a separately trained world model and value model. DexWAC learns an actor, an action-conditioned world model and a temporal-difference critic in a single pretraining stage on shared features. It then reuses the world model and critic to improve the actor without more real-world interaction.

## How it works
- **Shared backbone.** A Mixture-of-Transformers couples a world backbone, initialized from the FLUX.2-klein 4B image-editing model, with a compact actor-critic expert. The expert reads the backbone's keys and values for the instruction, image latents and proprioception.
- **Chunk-endpoint world model.** For each 16-step action chunk it predicts only the image latent and proprioception at the chunk's end, plus reward and continuation, so one prediction gives a complete imagined transition.
- **Value from the start.** The critic learns by chunk-level TD from the start of pretraining, so value targets shape the shared representation.
- **Improvement.** After the world model and critic are adapted on successful and failed rollouts, the actor is improved by PPO on imagined rollouts, or a Q-critic picks the best of the actor's proposals.

## Results
- Pretraining alone gives 65.5% success on eight DexJoCo tasks, versus 57.9% for π0.5 and 66.8% for Motus, and the best average on RoboTwin 2.0's 50 tasks (88.1%).
- The world model has the best scores on all six image metrics (wrist-view LPIPS 0.085 vs 0.137 for FACT) and is about 8.5x faster than FACT.
- With 50 rollouts per task, imagined PPO lifts DexJoCo success from 65.5% to 67.8%, mostly on bimanual tasks (47.8% to 52.9%).
- On real robots (30 trials per task, pretrained policy only), egg transfer succeeds 50% vs 20% for π0.5 and bimanual cup extraction 100% vs 70%.

## Limitations
Policy-improvement gains are small and uneven, with task-specific regressions that the authors attribute to hard credit assignment and conflicting gradients across tasks. The real-robot tests cover two tasks and skip the improvement stage. The model has not been tried with large-scale robot pretraining.
