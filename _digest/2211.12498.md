## What they do
Earlier visuo-tactile datasets were collected by robots on lab tabletops or in simulation, so they cover a small number of graspable, mostly rigid objects. In Touch and Go, people collect the data instead. One person presses a GelSight sensor onto objects in classrooms, hallways, apartments, hiking trails, playgrounds and streets while a second person films the hand with a webcam. The authors then use the dataset for three tasks: learning tactile features, restyling images to match a touch signal, and predicting future touch.

## How it works
- **Collection.** A GelSight sensor about 1.5 cm across and a webcam both record at about 27 Hz to one laptop. Collectors press each object about 3 times, for 0.7 s on average, and aim for parts with complex geometry. No separate force sensor is used.
- **Annotation.** A ResNet-18 press detector, trained on 10k hand-labeled frames, finds the frames in contact (97% test accuracy, about 66% chance), and crowd workers correct its errors. Five workers label each pressed frame with one of 20 material categories.
- **Applications.** Contrastive Multiview Coding learns matched image and touch embeddings. For stylization, a CUT-based generator restyles an image conditioned on a GelSight frame, and a discriminator judges whether the resulting image-touch pair would co-occur. For touch prediction, a 3D-convolution residual network uses separate touch and vision encoders.

## Results
- The dataset holds about 13.9k touches of about 3971 objects (estimated from counts in 10% of the videos), split 52.2% indoor and 47.8% outdoor. That is about 4x the object instances of ObjectFolder 2.0.
- Linear probes on frozen features: 78.1% grasp-outcome accuracy on Feeling of Success data (ImageNet 73.0%, VisGel-trained 75.6%), 54.7% on material (ImageNet 46.9%), 77.3% on hard/soft and 79.4% on rough/smooth.
- Stylization: 0.197 visuo-tactile similarity, vs 0.178 for a CycleGAN variant and 0.165 for unedited images.
- Touch prediction: adding vision helps both models. For example, LPIPS for Geng et al. drops from 0.103 to 0.091.

## Limitations
The data comes mostly from around the University of Michigan campus and from a few collectors, so it may not generalize to other places. Collectors may probe unrepresentative parts of objects and do not press with consistent force. The robot result is offline: a linear classifier predicts grasp outcomes on a previously recorded dataset.
