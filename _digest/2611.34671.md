abstract: Scaling robot learning requires demonstrations that combine realistic visual observations with reliable action supervision. Simulation-based synthesis faces a substantial visual gap relative to real environments, while video-only generation struggles to maintain physically consistent interactions and provide aligned action labels. We present DemoFoundry, an automated framework that couples coding agents with generative rendering to synthesize photorealistic and physically grounded robot demonstrations. Coding agents both construct aligned real--proxy video pairs from existing robot episodes to train a video renderer and write executable interaction programs to generate new demonstrations. Given a scene reference and task instruction, an agent reconstructs task-relevant geometry and writes a control program without requiring target-task human demonstrations. Program execution in a physics simulator produces proxy videos with aligned robot states and actions. The video renderer translates these proxy videos into scene-consistent, photorealistic observations, reducing the need for detailed appearance modeling in each scene. Automated task-execution checks select successful trajectories for rendering and policy learning. Experiments show improved visual quality and action--video consistency. Policies trained on the generated demonstrations outperform raw-simulation training and approach the performance of training on an equal number of real demonstrations.

## What they do
Simulation gives exact action labels but looks unlike the real scene; video generators look real but lack action labels and often get contact wrong. DemoFoundry splits the two jobs: a coding agent writes a task program that runs in a physics simulator, and a video model trained on real–simulated pairs renders the plain simulator video as a photorealistic one of the target scene. Generation needs no target-task demonstrations and no human operator.

## How it works
- **Paired data.** An agent (GPT-6 Astra) reconstructs episodes from DROID, AgiBot World, RoboMIND and RH20T, replays the recorded actions in MuJoCo or DexSim, and keeps a pair only if every visible object reaches silhouette IoU ≥ 0.80 within three refinement rounds. This gives 1,884 pairs, 1,522 minutes summed across views.
- **Renderer.** LoRA fine-tuning of the LTX-2.5 and MiniMax-H3 video models maps a proxy video plus a first RGB frame to the real video.
- **New scenes.** From a reference image, robot model, camera calibration and instruction, the agent rebuilds the task geometry and writes a program with grasp, inverse-kinematics and motion-planning tools. It runs under randomized layouts; only successful rollouts are rendered, with their states and actions as labels. Editing the rendering prompt varies the appearance.

## Results
- On held-out pairs the fine-tuned H3 renderer reaches 23.3 dB PSNR and FVD 258.8, against 15.5 dB and 808.7 for the released model.
- Real dual-arm robot, three tasks, 30 trials each: 50 generated demonstrations per task give 46.7% (Diffusion Policy) and 60.0% (π0.5) mean success, versus 12.2% and 27.8% from raw simulation and 48.9% and 62.2% from 50 real demonstrations. Real plus 100 generated reaches 72.2% and 82.2%.
- π0.5 success rises from 42.2% with 25 generated demonstrations to 85.6% with 200. Estimated cost is $0.855 per demonstration at 100, against $2.42 for teleoperation.
- With a green bottle, a new background colour or altered lighting, prompt-varied data gives 21, 22 and 18 of 30 successes, against 5, 10 and 2 for simulator domain randomization.

## Limitations
The authors name multi-view consistency and drift when long videos are rendered window by window. Only 39.7% of source episodes pass the reconstruction check after three refinement rounds. Real-robot tests cover three tabletop tasks on one platform.
