abstract: Learning shared action representations is central to cross-embodiment manipulation. However, identical joint values can produce different spatial configurations across robots, limiting representations learned from joint values alone. We introduce RoboMorpher, a morphology-conditioned action tokenizer that jointly reconstructs joint angles and link poses to associate control coordinates with their kinematic effects. MorphGen supplies scalable kinematic supervision by generating serial-arm descriptions from survey-derived geometric priors. We pretrain RoboMorpher on 40 million morphology-state pairs and integrate the frozen tokenizer into a vision-language-action policy for discrete prediction and continuous refinement. We also introduce EmbodiBench, a ten-task benchmark with 30 generated URDFs per task. With downstream URDFs excluded from tokenizer pretraining, the policy achieves 56.40% success on RoboTwin 2.0 and 23.27% on EmbodiBench, exceeding continuous joint-space control by 9.73 and 15.34 percentage points. Removing link-pose supervision reduces EmbodiBench success by 17.94 percentage points, supporting morphology-aware geometric supervision for joint-action representation learning.

## What they do
In cross-embodiment manipulation, the same joint values put the links of different arms in different places, so a policy trained on raw joint actions cannot tell what an action does. RoboMorpher is an action tokenizer conditioned on the robot's URDF that learns this relation from kinematics alone, before any task demonstrations. It is pretrained on 40 million generated morphology-state pairs and then frozen inside a vision-language-action (VLA) policy.

## How it works
- **MorphGen.** A survey of 33 arm descriptions gives per-joint link-length priors. The generator samples six- and seven-joint revolute arms in a common local-Z frame convention, picks a home pose with collision-aware IK (cuRobo), and labels each configuration with link poses from forward kinematics.
- **Tokenizer.** URDF features condition a Transformer encoder and decoder through AdaLN, and attention biases encode distance along the chain. Residual vector quantization gives two codes per joint; the decoder reconstructs joint angles plus each link's position and rotation.
- **Policy.** Qwen3-VL-4B feeds a two-branch diffusion Transformer. One branch predicts codes for ten trajectory nodes, the frozen decoder turns them into a coarse 50-frame chunk, and a flow-matching branch refines it.

## Results
All in simulation, with the test URDFs held out of tokenizer pretraining.
- RoboTwin 2.0 (15 tasks, 5 embodiments): 56.40% mean success vs 46.67% for continuous joint control, 51.60% for 256-bin tokens and 50.73% for FAST; better on 12 of 15 tasks.
- EmbodiBench (10 tasks, 30 generated URDFs each): 23.27% vs 7.93%. Lifting gives about 80% of the gain (55.11% vs 14.00%); all three stacking tasks stay at 0%.
- Removing link-pose supervision drops EmbodiBench success to 5.33%.
- An end-effector-control reference still scores higher: 69.93% and 32.00%.

## Limitations
No real-robot experiments. Policy training and evaluation use the same morphologies, so zero-shot transfer to new arms is untested. Stacking fails, reconstruction has large outliers (up to 0.75 m position error), and variation across training seeds is not measured.
