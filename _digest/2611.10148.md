abstract: 'Human demonstrations provide a scalable source of diverse manipulation data, but leveraging them for robot learning remains challenging due to differences in embodiment, viewpoint, action spaces, and temporal structure. We introduce BATCH, a cross-embodiment learning framework that explicitly aligns human and robot behaviors. BATCH learns self-supervised temporal representations with a cycle-consistency objective to establish cross-embodiment correspondences without manual annotation. We further introduce two complementary training objectives that leverage these correspondences within an autoregressive videoaction diffusion model: behavior-aligned co-training and human-to-robot style transfer. On real-world manipulation tasks, BATCH improves generalization to unseen task configurations compared with training on unaligned data and prior cross-embodiment baselines, with additional gains from the style-transfer objective. Our results demonstrate that explicit behavior alignment enables more effective transfer from human demonstrations to robot policies.'

## What they do
Co-training a robot policy on human egocentric video helps, but humans and robots execute the same task at different speeds, and human clips contain segments with no robot counterpart. BATCH first finds which human frames correspond to which robot frames, without labels, and then trains a video-action policy on those aligned pairs so that scenes seen only in human data transfer to the robot.

## How it works
- **Behavior alignment.** An encoder on frozen ResNet-50 features is trained with a multi-cycle temporal cycle-consistency loss (GTCC) on head-camera video from Aria glasses, worn by the person or mounted on the robot. Subsequence DTW in that embedding space retrieves the best-matching human segment for each robot window.
- **Video-action model.** The first 8 blocks of Wan2.1-I2V-14B predict future head-camera video and 16-step action chunks with flow matching. The model is pretrained on robot demos for 20,000 steps, then finetuned for 1,000 steps on aligned pairs.
- **Two finetuning objectives**, alternated with probability 0.5: joint co-training, where human and robot streams share weights but not attention, and style transfer, where the robot's future video is predicted from the aligned human clip.
- **Deployment.** Only the robot stream runs, on ARX5 arms with a head camera and wrist webcams; inference takes 0.35-0.42 s per chunk on one RTX 5090.

## Results
- Alignment on out-of-domain human data: GTCC + DTW gets 57.8% phase overlap on Object in Container and 71.7% on Cup on Saucer; DINOv3 + DTW gets 42.3% and 22.1%.
- Object in Container (20 rollouts, normalized score): BATCH 0.63, without alignment 0.42, EgoVerse co-training 0.40, robot only 0.28.
- Cup on Saucer (30 rollouts, success rate): BATCH 83.3%, EgoVerse 63.3%, without alignment 50.0%, robot only 33.3%.

## Limitations
Alignment assumes humans and robots share a behavioral structure and may struggle when strategies or action orderings differ. Alignment errors feed mismatched supervision into style transfer. Robot demonstrations are still required when finetuning with human data.
