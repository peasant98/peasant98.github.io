abstract: 'Pretrained visual generation models have shown great promise in robotic manipulation. However, a representative line of work still treats future visual prediction as the central link between visual generative pretraining and action learning, retaining it as a training objective even when future scenes are no longer generated at inference time. We argue that the instruction-following, spatial understanding, and visual imagination capabilities acquired through image generation pretraining can support control without future visual prediction: image generation models themselves can be directly adapted into powerful action learners. Building on this perspective, we propose GenPA, which fine-tunes an image generation backbone with a lightweight 19M-parameter action regression adapter and reuses its native generative objective for perception co-training. The native image-editing interface accommodates different visual targets without task-specific perception heads, enabling comparisons of current-scene perception, future prediction, and their combinations within a common policy architecture. At deployment, GenPA requires only a single backbone forward pass and action regression, without visual generation or iterative action denoising. Without additional large-scale robot-data pretraining, GenPA demonstrates strong control performance and robustness to perturbations across simulation benchmarks (99.10% success on LIBERO and 83.79% on LIBERO-Plus) and real-world manipulation tasks. Under compiled inference, GenPA takes 20.9 ms per action chunk on a single H100 GPU, achieving 1.7-3.4x speedups over strong VLA and WAM baselines. Our latency analysis further highlights architectural advantages of visual generation backbones for efficient robot control. Ablations across visual tasks and their combinations identify current-depth supervision as a simple, effective training recipe.'

## What they do
Policies built on image and video generators usually keep future-frame prediction as a training objective. GenPA drops it. It fine-tunes the pretrained image generator FLUX.2 klein Base 4B into a policy that regresses actions in one forward pass, and uses the generator's own image-editing interface for auxiliary perception training on the current scene. There is no extra large-scale robot-data pretraining.

## How it works
- **Action path.** Camera images enter as VAE reference tokens and the instruction as text tokens plus a learned action prefix. A 19M-parameter transformer decoder, with one proprioception-conditioned query per step, reads the final reference features and regresses a 16-step action chunk.
- **Perception path.** A separate forward pass with shared weights trains the backbone to generate image-form targets (depth, normals, segmentation, pointmaps or future frames) with its native rectified-flow loss, with no task-specific heads. Current-frame depth is the default.
- **Deployment.** Only the action path runs: no image generation and no iterative action denoising.

## Results
- Simulation: 99.10% on LIBERO, the best among methods without robot-data pretraining (ImageWAM-4B: 98.40% with a 642M action expert). It scores 83.79% on LIBERO-Plus without fine-tuning and 91.45% on 50 RoboTwin 2.0 bimanual tasks (ImageWAM: 93.38%).
- Real USB transfer, 600 demos, 50 trials per condition: 78.0% mean success vs 70.0% for ImageWAM, 58.0% for π0.5 and 50.0% for Fast-WAM. Under phone-LED lighting it scores 52% vs 38% for ImageWAM.
- Compiled H100 latency is 20.9 ms per chunk, vs 36.6 ms for π0.5, 57.4 ms for ImageWAM and 71.3 ms for Fast-WAM.
- Current-depth supervision raises LIBERO-Plus success from 70.87% to 76.69%; adding more targets does not consistently help.

## Limitations
The paper has no limitations section. By its own results, lighting changes remain hard (52% on the real robot) and robot initial-state perturbations are the weakest LIBERO-Plus category (67.94%). The real-world test is a single one-arm task. Native depth supervision needs depth labels and costs 1.294 s per training update, vs 0.517 s for a DPT depth head.
