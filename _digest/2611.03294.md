tldr: ForceLDM distills future force/torque and optical-flow features from a privileged teacher into a π0-based student policy that sees only current observations. On five real-robot contact-rich tasks it averages 87.1% success, 14.9 points above the strongest baseline.
abstract: Contact-rich manipulation requires fine-grained control and complex physical interactions with the environment, posing significant challenges for robot policy learning. Vision-only policies struggle with such tasks, as contact states and interaction forces are difficult to infer from visual observations alone. Existing methods mitigate this issue by incorporating force or tactile sensing. However, they often treat these signals as auxiliary observations without fully exploiting their role in modeling future interaction dynamics. To address this, we propose ForceLDM, a force-aware latent dynamics model that learns future contact and motion dynamics to guide action generation. Rather than directly predicting future observations, which can entangle task-relevant dynamics with irrelevant sensory details, ForceLDM learns a compact representation of future dynamics through knowledge distillation. Specifically, a teacher network with privileged future force/torque and optical flow signals extracts future dynamic features, which are then distilled into a student network trained only on current observations. This enables the student model to reason about upcoming contacts and physical dynamics without relying on future sensory inputs at deployment. To improve robustness and generalization, we further introduce a curriculum-based progressive noise injection strategy to mitigate over-reliance on future features. Experiments on five real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks demonstrate that ForceLDM outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, generalizes effectively to novel objects, and remains robust to environmental perturbations. Project page and code are available at https://tpy001.github.io/ForceLDM/.

## What they do
Force-aware VLAs usually feed force/torque in as one more input and learn a direct observation-to-action mapping, while video world models predict future frames and mix control-relevant dynamics with appearance details. ForceLDM instead teaches a π0 policy to anticipate upcoming contact and motion in latent space. A teacher sees privileged future force and optical flow; a student that sees only current observations learns to match the teacher's internal representations, and only the student is deployed.

## How it works
- **Future dynamics encoding.** An MLP turns the next 32 steps of 6D force/torque into one force token. RAFT optical flow between the current and future frame is colorized, compressed with SDXL-VAE and pooled by 16 learnable queries into motion tokens.
- **Teacher-student distillation.** Two action experts share π0's PaliGemma backbone and get the robot state and a 10-step force history. The student's 17 learnable queries are aligned with the teacher's force and motion representations at layers 8, 12 and 16 (cosine loss, stop-gradient on the teacher); both experts train with flow matching.
- **Progressive noise injection.** Gaussian noise on the student's queries ramps up between 30% and 70% of training to curb overfitting.

## Results
xArm 7 with a wrist force/torque sensor, 80-100 SpaceMouse demonstrations and 20-30 trials per task.
- In distribution: 87.1% average success vs 72.2% for Seer with force, 67.7% for TA-VLA and 41.1% for π0 without force; 85% on VGA plugging, where the best baseline reaches 50%.
- Out of distribution (occlusion, unseen objects, color, height, material, size; 15 trials each): 68.9% vs 48.9% for the best baseline.
- Ablations: force plus flow reaches 90.7%, force alone 68.0%, flow alone 70.7%. Removing the distillation loss drops 85.1% to 42.8%; removing noise injection drops it to 74.1%.
- On a Piper-X with GR00T N1.5 and motor-current torque estimates: 70% vs 62.5% for TA-VLA and 20% for GR00T N1.5.

## Limitations
Datasets are small (3.08 hours on the xArm), so whether the gains hold at scale is open. Optical-flow errors from occlusion or motion blur can corrupt the learned dynamics, and 2D flow carries no depth. ForceLDM trails TA-VLA on gear assembly (83.3% vs 93.3%) because of grasp failures near the workspace boundary.
